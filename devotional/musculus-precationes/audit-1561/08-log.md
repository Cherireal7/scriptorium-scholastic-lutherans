## Doubtful

1. 137v, *reddet* / *reddes*. Followed 1561 as a wording variant ("when he shall render"), as the collation lists it. It may be no more than a misprint of 1561: the prayer speaks to God in the second person throughout, and 1573's *reddes* is the reading of the Chrysostom text. If the editor prefers, restore "when thou shalt render" and turn fn 12 into a misprint note.
2. 134v, *pietatis functiones fidei, gratia*. Translated as *fidei gratia* ("by the grace of faith") with the comma taken as misplaced; this is how the prayer reads in its source (Erasmus's evening prayer). Read strictly by the 1561 comma it would be "the offices of the godliness of faith, … by [thy] grace", which is harsh. 1573 smoothed it with *et*.
3. 138v, *ac ut*. 1561 has no verb of asking. "[we pray]" is supplied in square brackets so that the English can be read; *ac* could also be a misprint for *fac* ("grant that"), but that is a guess and is not put in the text.
4. 132v, *qui est benedictus*. Treated as a misprint for *es* (the prayer addresses God in the second person in the same clause: *custodi me … qui est*). A strict rendering would be "who is blessed".
5. "God of Abraham …": lower-cased because 1561 does not set *Deus* in small capitals here. If the edition wants GOD wherever 1573 has it, this is the one place in this part.
6. Markers placed one or two words off where English word order differs from the Latin: `{f.135r}` (Latin breaks inside *An-geli*; marker stands before "the dead shall be raised at the trumpet of thine Angel"), `{f.135v}` (the page begins with *periculo*; marker stands after "peril of soul and body."), `{f.131r}` (break inside *vi-vum*; marker after "the living fountain:").
