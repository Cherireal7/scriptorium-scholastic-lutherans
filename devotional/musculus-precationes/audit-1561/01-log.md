## Changes

1. **f.A2v — kind: mistranslation (construction)**
   - Latin: *sed quod tamen non omnino nihil me fecisse putem, ut quum ad structuram Tabernaculi Domini aurum, argentum et lapides pretiosos quos offeram non habeam, si vel pilos saltem caprorum ac pelles arietum rubras conferam, atque talentum … mensariis committam, potius quam … abscondam.*
   - Was: “that I have not done altogether nothing; so that, whereas for the building of the Tabernacle of the Lord I have not gold, silver and precious stones to offer, I may at the least bring goats' hair and rams' skins dyed red …, and may commit the talent … rather than hide it”
   - Now: “that I have not done altogether nothing, if, as one who for the building of the Tabernacle of the Lord hath not gold, silver and precious stones to offer, I bring at the least goats' hair and rams' skins dyed red …, and commit the talent … rather than hide it”
   - Why: the *si* was dropped and the clause turned into a purpose clause. The Latin is a condition: “I have not done nothing, if I bring at least goats' hair”.

2. **f.A2v — kind: reference**
   - Latin: *aurum, argentum et lapides pretiosos … pilos caprorum ac pelles arietum rubras*
   - Was: `[Exodus 35:5–7]`
   - Now: `[Exodus 35:5–9]`
   - Why: the precious stones stand in verse 9.

3. **f.A2v — kind: footnote untrue of the print (removed)**
   - Latin: *esse in ea sententia quosdam*
   - Was: footnote “The print has *in ea sententii*, read *in ea sententia*.”
   - Now: footnote removed; text unchanged.
   - Why: enlarged, the last letter is a badly inked *a* (the bowl is faintly there), not a second *i*. The print reads *sententia*.

4. **f.A3v — kind: wrong Greek and footnote untrue of the print; construction**
   - Latin: *Ut ergo pii hac commonefactione aerumnarum ac miseriarum ἱερὰν ἄγκυραν χαλάζει, ac precibus omnia praesentia et imminentia quoque mala propulsare studeant*
   - Was: “should ἱερὰν ἄγκυραν χαλᾶν, “let down the sacred anchor”, and should endeavour by prayers to drive away all evils”; footnote: “The print has ἱερὰν ἄγκυραν χαλάζει, which is not a possible form here; the infinitive χαλᾶν (or χαλάσαι), depending on *studeant*, is meant.”
   - Now: “should endeavour ἱερὰν ἄγκυραν χαλάζειν, “to let down the sacred anchor”, and by prayers to drive away all evils”; footnote: print has χαλάζει without the final ν; the phrase is Erasmus's adage *Sacram ancoram solvere*, ἱερὰν ἄγκυραν χαλάζειν (*Adagia* 1.1.24).
   - Why: the intended word is χαλάζειν, the form in which Erasmus prints the proverb; only a ν is missing. χαλᾶν was an invention of the translator. Both infinitives depend on *studeant*, which the English now shows.

5. **f.A4r — kind: footnote (alternative added)**
   - Latin: *hanc consuetudinem Ecclesiae nunc pene collapsa*
   - Was: “The print has *collapsa*, read *collapsam*, agreeing with *consuetudinem*.”
   - Now: the same, with the second possibility *collapsae* (agreeing with *Ecclesiae*) named. Text unchanged.
   - Why: the print has plain *collapsa* with no stroke; either correction is possible, *collapsam* the more likely.

6. **f.A1r — kind: footnote (overstated)**
   - Latin: *NUNC RECENS COGNItae et auctae*
   - Was: “The print has *recens cognitae*, “newly known”, an evident misprint for *recens recognitae* …”
   - Now: “… A syllable has most probably dropped out, though *cognitae* can itself mean “examined”.” Text (“newly revised”) unchanged.
   - Why: *cognoscere* also means to examine; the misprint is probable, not evident.

7. **f.A1r — kind: footnote (small precision)**
   - Was: “The title page prints the place only.”
   - Now: “The title page prints the place only, without a year.”

8. **Whole file — kind: structure**
   - Was: one `# Appendix` with `## Title Page` and `## Dedication`; markers `{img.5}`, `{img.7}`–`{img.14}`.
   - Now: `# Title Page` and `# Dedication`, each with its Latin line in italics; the Latin address of the dedication added as the heading line; markers `{f.A1r}`, `{f.A2r}`–`{f.A5v}`; footnotes renumbered 1–8 (old 3 removed).

## Checked and left as they were

- Homer, first quotation: *Iliad* 19.290 (Briseis over Patroclus) — correct.
- Homer, second quotation: *Iliad* 17.446–447 (Zeus to the horses of Achilles) — correct.
- Johann Friedrich I of Saxony died 3 March 1554 — correct.
- Scripture references Matthew 25:25–27, 24:12, 21:13, Luke 17:28, 2 Thessalonians 2:3, Psalm 2:2, Isaiah 49:23, 1 Thessalonians 3:13 — correct.
- No omission or addition of a sentence or clause found.
- “GOD” in capitals kept: it is the house style of the 1573 preface files, though in this dedication *Deus* is printed in ordinary roman capitals-and-lower-case, not small capitals.

## Doubtful

- *ille apud Homerum* (A3v): the Latin is masculine, but the speaker in Homer is Briseis. “that person” kept.
- Greek as printed has small slips that are silently normalised, as the old file did: ὑὸς for υἱὸς and ἀπωλεας for ἀπωλείας (A4v), τῆς abbreviated twice, the verb πνείει badly set (A3v), breathings and accents irregular.
- *T. C. DD.* (A5v): *Tuae Celsitudini deditissimus* is a likely expansion, not a certain one (*dedicat* is also possible).
- *exulceratissimo* (A4r) is rendered “most corrupt”; literally “most festered”. Left.
- Erasmus's form χαλάζειν was confirmed from a quotation of the adage on the web, not from an edition of the *Adagia*.
