# Audit log — part 05 (Sections VII and VIII, pp. 122–164, images 0180–0222)

Second reader. Every page image was read with the OCR beside it and compared with the English sentence by sentence (verse line by line).

## Changes

- p.135 (img 0193) — Latin: "Ut nos in fide Filii / Pura semper retineat" — was: "in the faith of the Son, / pure, may ever keep us." — now: "in the faith of the Son, / the pure faith, may ever keep us." — kind: mistranslation (*pura* is ablative with *fide*; the loose "pure" read as if it described "us" or the Spirit)
- p.135 (img 0193) — Latin: "ORATIO." over the collect "Deus qui solennitate paschali" — was: "## Prayer" — now: "## Collect" — kind: mistranslation (agreed term: *Oratio* over a collect = Collect; heading level unchanged)
- p.136 (img 0194) — Latin: "Spiritum nobis Domine tuae charitatis infunde" — was: "the Spirit of thy love" — now: "the Spirit of thy charity" — kind: mistranslation (agreed term: *charitas* = charity)
- p.138 (img 0196) — Latin: "pro dilectae sobolis charitate" — was: "for the love of thy beloved offspring" — now: "for the charity of thy beloved offspring" — kind: mistranslation (agreed term)
- p.139 (img 0197) — Latin: "charitas lucrifaciat crudelitatem" — was: "his love my cruelty" — now: "his charity my cruelty" — kind: mistranslation (agreed term)

## Doubtful, not changed

- p.135 (img 0193) — "Et consolator pavidos / Apostolorum animos": the print reads *consolator* (noun), and the English "and is the Comforter of the fearful souls" follows it. The hymn elsewhere reads *consolatur* ("and comforteth the fearful souls of the Apostles"), which is probably what was meant; a footnote could say so.
- p.142 (img 0200), p.150 (img 0208), p.154 (img 0212) — the three readings above (*desperando*, *confidendo*, *consignato corpore*) are almost certainly misprints for *sperando*, *confitendo*, *configurato corpori*. The English keeps the print without a footnote; the file footnotes other misprints (notes 1, 2, 3, 5), so for consistency an editor may want footnotes here, or the emended sense ("but that in hoping I may breathe again"; "so often confessing"; "our body being fashioned like unto the body of thy glory", Phil. 3:21). Left as the translator had it.
- p.146 (img 0204) — "quam difficiles exitus habetis, dum iniungitis": rendered "when ye have laid hold". The print does read *iniungitis*; the sense is uncertain (the source text is usually given with *ungitis* / *pungitis* wordplay). Defensible, left.
- p.138 (img 0196) — "meque per hoc redderet tuae pietati pacabilem": rendered "render me acceptable to thy lovingkindness"; closer would be "reconciled to". Defensible, left.
- p.129 (img 0187) — "ros generationis" rendered "the dew of thy generation": "thy" is not in the Latin (comes from the KJV shape of the verse). Left.
- p.139 (img 0197) — "eius quaeso pietati mea impietas" rendered "his godliness"/"mine ungodliness" rather than the agreed "lovingkindness", to keep the wordplay. Left.
- Page markers: where a heading stands at the top of a printed page (pp. 124, 136, 141, 150) the marker is placed at the start of the paragraph under the heading, not before the heading. The other parts of the book do the same, so left.
