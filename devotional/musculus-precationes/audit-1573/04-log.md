# Audit log — 04-VI.md (Section VI, the Passion of Christ)

Printed pp. 84 (from the heading "VI.") to 122 (to the heading "VII."), images 0142–0180. Every page image read beside its OCR text and compared with the English sentence by sentence, verse line by line.

## Changes

- p.117 (img 0175) — Latin: "in cruce dixisti: Sitio secundum salutem humani generis" — was: "I thirst, that is, for the salvation of mankind" — now: "I thirst, as touching the salvation of mankind" — kind: misreading (the English rendered *scilicet*, the reading of the usual text of the prayer; the print clearly has *secundum*)

## Doubtful, not changed

Places where the English silently follows the usual text of a hymn or prayer against an evident misprint in the 1573 print. The sense given is right in each case, so nothing was changed; but the conventions ask for a footnote where a reading departs from the print, and none is given here. Footnotes were not added, so that the footnote numbering stays intact. Whoever owns the file may want to add them.

- p.99 (img 0157) — print: "Vt qui velatus facie, / Fuisti sol iusticiae" — English: "Thou who, with thy face veiled" (= *Tu qui*, the usual text). "Vt" is not translated.
- p.102 (img 0160) — print: "Tuae mortis supplicium" — English: "by the torment of thy death" (= *supplicio*).
- p.107 (img 0165) — print: "Electa digna stipite" — English: "chosen, with thy worthy trunk" (= *digno stipite*). Also "Vt nos lauret crimine" read as *lavaret*, and "tulit tartaris" as "from hell"; both fine.
- p.111 (img 0169) — print: "Inferni claustra penetras." — English: "Piercing the bars of hell" (= *penetrans*).
- p.111 (img 0169) — print: "Nos tuo vultu facies" — English: "satisfy us with thy countenance" (= *saties*). This is the most substantial of the silent emendations.
- p.91 (img 0149) — print: "per factionem huius mereatur veniam" (the usual text of the Meditations has *satisfactionem*) — English: "through this one's doing", which translates the print. Left as it stands.
- p.98 (img 0156) and p.109 (img 0167) — the hymn *In passione Domini* is printed twice; the first time line 3 reads "Fit nostrum refrigerium", the second time "Sit nostrum refrigerium". The English distinguishes them ("is found" / "let ... be"), which matches the print. "Is found" for *fit* is loose but defensible.
- p.85 (img 0143) — "vbique vsque ad mortem obediens factus" — English: "made in all things obedient even unto death". *Ubique* is literally "everywhere"; "in all things" is a fair late-Latin sense. Left.
- p.88 (img 0146) — "Me ad illicitam concupiscentiam rapuit ardor" — English: "Me the heat of desire carried away to unlawful lust". "Of desire" is a small expansion of *ardor*. Left.
- p.98 (img 0156) — print: "intra iudicium tuum et animam meam" — English: "between thy judgment and my soul" (= *inter*). Right in sense.
- Page markers at pp. 93, 109 and 122: the printed page begins with a heading (Avgvsti. Meditat. Cap. 14 / Hymnvs / Oratio), and the marker stands in the first line of text after that heading, not before the heading. This is consistent through the file and looks like the format's rule, so it was left.
