## Doubtful, not changed

- p.214 (img 0272) — Latin: "Ipse enim pater tenebrarum laqueos suos abscondit in tenebris suis, qui sunt filii tenebrarum harum, non videntes lumen tuum" — English: "hideth his snares in his darkness from them that are the children of this darkness". The print's sentence is abridged (the Soliloquies have a clause between "suis" and "qui sunt": that those who are in his darkness may be caught in them), so the relative clause has no antecedent. "from them" is the translator's bridge; it is not in the Latin, but some bridge is needed and the sense is defensible. A footnote could say the print is elliptical here.
- p.225 (img 0283) — Latin: "preciosique corporis et sanguinis tui mysterium reficere" — the print has "mysterium" where the sense needs "mysterio"; the English "to refresh me with the mystery" silently reads the ablative. Correct sense; a footnote for the misprint is optional under the conventions.
- p.240 (img 0298) — Latin: "in manus pietatis commendo animam meam" — English: "into the hands of thy lovingkindness". No "tuae" is printed (the fold hides only a letter of "pietatis"); "thy" is a natural supply, left.
- p.235 (img 0293) and p.242 (img 0300) — headings "Hiero. in agone" and "Precatio in gravi morbo vel agone" are given as "in the agony of death"; "of death" is supplied from the section title ("in agone mortis"). Left as a defensible expansion.
- p.245 (img 0303) — heading "Oratio" is given as "Prayer"; the conventions say *Oratio* over a collect = "Collect". The piece is a short prayer without a collect's ending, so "Prayer" was left; the editor may wish to make it agree with other parts.
- p.229 (img 0287) and p.230 (img 0288) — "psalterio" is "harp" in Psalm 149:3 and "psaltery" in Psalm 150:3. Both follow the KJV wording of the verse; left.
- Print misprints read silently in the English and correct in sense (no note in the file): p.213 "de oro eius" (ore); p.217 "tot corde meo" (toto); p.233 "a longo" (longe); p.237 "turris fortitudines" (fortitudinis); p.242 "refugescat" (refrigescat).
