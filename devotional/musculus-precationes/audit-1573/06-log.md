## Doubtful, not changed

- p.199 (img 0257) — "doces me & regis me": *doces* could be a misprint for *duces* / *ducis* (Vulgate *deduces me*; KJV "lead me"). I translated what is printed. If the editor judges it a misprint, restore "leadest" and add a footnote.
- p.167 (img 0225) — "vt sano palato possis manducari à me" is rendered "that with a sound palate I may be able to eat thee": the voice is turned round (Latin: "that thou mayest be eaten by me"). Sense unchanged; left.
- p.169 (img 0227) — "posuisti mysterium in virtute Spiritus sancti" is rendered "didst establish this mystery": "this" is not in the print (the usual text of the prayer has *mysterium istud*). Left as a defensible article choice.
- p.174 (img 0232) — "calix meus inebrians est" is rendered "my cup overfloweth unto drunkenness": "overfloweth" comes from the KJV, the Latin says only "my cup is inebriating". Left.
- p.178 (img 0236) — "Custodiens paruulos Dominus" is rendered with KJV "preserveth the simple" (Latin: "little ones"). Left as intended KJV wording.
- p.201 (img 0259) — "ab ipso spes mea" rendered with KJV "my expectation is from him" (Latin: "hope"); "in inferno" rendered "in the grave". Left as intended KJV wording.
- p.179 (img 0237) — The print's Lauda Sion lacks the stanza "Dogma datur Christianis…" (between "Consecramus hostiam" and "Quod non capis"). The English rightly follows the print; footnote 6 does not mention the omission. Not an error; a note could be added if wanted.
- pp.174, 183, 195, 202 — the page marker stands after the headings that open the page, at the first word of the body text, not before the headings. Consistent throughout the file; left.
