## Doubtful, not changed

- img 0047 — the `[?]` on the second dedicatee's surname. Enlarged, the print reads "Iacobo Iokiſlo": I, o, k, i, long s, then a tall stroke with no cross-bar (so not the "ſt" ligature, compared with "noſter" and "ſtertentes" on the same page, and not the "ſi" ligature, compared with "ſit" and "inſidias"), a stray speck above the line, then o. So "Jokisl" is what the type shows. It is not a known form of a name; it may be a misprint for "Iokisco" (Jociscus / Jokisch). A library authority record for a "Jakob Jociscus" exists, but I found nothing that ties him to Frankfurt on the Oder in 1570, so the identification is not proved. `[?]` left in place.
- preface (img 0050) — "et vera etiam magis charitas refrigescit" is rendered "and yet true charity waxeth the more cold". The Latin has no adversative ("and true charity also grows the more cold"); "yet" is a light interpretive touch. Defensible, left.
- p. 22 (img 0080) — "Aufer a me concupiscentias" is rendered "Take away from me evil desires". "Evil" is not in the Latin, but it is a usual way to render *concupiscentiae*. Left.
- p. 15–16 (img 0073–0074) — the "Prosa de Sancta Trinitate" is printed as short prose paragraphs, one per versicle; the English sets each as a verse line (`| `). Each English line matches one printed paragraph. This is a layout choice, not a translation matter. Left.
- p. 33 (img 0091) — "Generatio generationis laudabit opera tua" is given in the KJV wording ("One generation shall praise thy works to another"), as the conventions direct. Left.
