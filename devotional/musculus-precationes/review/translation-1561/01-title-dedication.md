# Title Page
*Precationes ex veteribus orthodoxis Doctoribus, ex Ecclesiae hymnis et Canticis, ex Psalmis denique Davidis collectae, et in certos locos digestae. Nunc recens cognitae et auctae. Per Andream Musculum Doctorem. Francofordiae ad Oderam.*

{f.A1r} Prayers gathered out of the ancient orthodox Doctors, out of the hymns and canticles of the Church, and lastly out of the Psalms of David, and arranged under certain heads. Now newly revised[^1] and enlarged. By Andreas Musculus, Doctor.

At Frankfurt on the Oder.[^2]

# Dedication
*Illustrissimo Principi ac Domino, Domino Ioanni Alberto, Duci Mechelburgensi, Principi Vandalorum, Comiti Schuerinensi, Regionum Rochstocii ac Starchart Domino, Domino suo clementissimo.*

{f.A2r} To the most illustrious Prince and Lord, the Lord Johann Albrecht, Duke of Mecklenburg, Prince of the Wends, Count of Schwerin, Lord of the lands of Rostock and Stargard, his most gracious Lord.

Grace, peace, mercy, and the Spirit of prayers, from God the Father and Jesus Christ our Lord.

This little book of prayers I have twice put forth; not, however, with that diligence which was indeed fitting, but rather gathered together with a certain hasty pains and labour. But since the booksellers, the copies of the former and of the later edition being sold off, ask and earnestly demand a new printing of the little book, I thought that I should undertake a labour not so very unprofitable, if I should once more bring those {f.A2v} forms of prayers back to the anvil, and deliver them to the printer to be struck off more fitly dressed in their due adornment. Now whether I have in very deed also performed this in this third edition, whosoever shall compare this with the former ones will easily be able to perceive and to judge. Yet I say not this because I esteem this labour of mine to be a thing of so great moment, but because I think, nevertheless, that I have not done altogether nothing, if, as one who for the building of the Tabernacle of the Lord hath not gold, silver and precious stones to offer, I bring at the least goats' hair and rams' skins dyed red [Exodus 35:5–9], and commit the talent entrusted to me by the Lord (though it be a very small one) to the exchangers, rather than hide it in the earth with the wicked and slothful servant [Matthew 25:25–27].

Neither am I ignorant that there are some of this opinion, that the truly godly, the saints and elect of God, have no need of any {f.A3r} prescribed forms of prayer, as being men who, having received the Spirit of prayers, are able with no very great labour to form and fashion forms of prayers for themselves, which, drawn forth out of the inmost recesses of the heart in the time of tribulation, do the more easily pierce heaven, as being much more ardent and vehement, and come the more swiftly to the ears of God. Which although I myself also confess to be most true, yet neither will any man be able to deny this, that these same prescribed prayers do very greatly stir up, and the more enkindle, that ardour and vehemency of the heart. Much less will any man be able to deny that these same little prayers, gathered out of the writings of the godly and holy men of old, do also lay open very many things that are to be asked for; seeing there is no man who knoweth not that mankind, after the fall of our first parents, is oppressed with greater calamities, troubles and miseries than that even the holiest {f.A3v} men, and those most exercised in spiritual things, are able sufficiently to know and to mark the multitude of them. So that most truly did that person say in Homer:

ὥς μοι δέχεται κακὸν ἐκ κακοῦ αἰεί, “so for me doth evil ever follow upon evil.”[^3]

True also is that saying of Homer:

οὐ μὲν γάρ τί πού ἐστιν ὀϊζυρώτερον ἀνδρὸς πάντων, ὅσσα τε γαῖαν ἔπι πνείει τε καὶ ἕρπει, “for there is surely nothing anywhere more wretched than man, of all things that breathe and creep upon the earth.”[^4]

To the end therefore that the godly, by this putting in mind of troubles and miseries, should endeavour ἱερὰν ἄγκυραν χαλάζειν, “to let down the sacred anchor”,[^5] and by prayers to drive away all evils both present and impending, this hath always been received in the Church in all ages, that assemblies should be held daily, in which, with prescribed hymns, canticles and psalms, and with certain set short prayers, the worship of invocation (the one only refuge in evils) was rendered unto God. {f.A4r} Therefore, if it is not so greatly to be praised, yet certainly neither is it rashly to be condemned, the zeal of those who endeavour by these same prescribed forms of praying to preserve and to further this custom of the Church, now well nigh fallen down;[^6] especially in this old age of the world, and in this age which, as it is the last, so is it also the most corrupt, wherein, according to the prophecy of Christ, iniquity aboundeth, but charity toward God and our neighbour hath not so much waxed cold [Matthew 24:12] as it is almost wholly quenched. Insomuch that, a horrible security now reigning, the House of God, the House of prayer, is become a very den of thieves [Matthew 21:13], who under the pretext of the Christian name, in the Church of Christ, as rotten members, buy, sell, plant and build [Luke 17:28]; but the worship of invocation, most acceptable to God, and in this our age restored anew by the light of the Gospel to its former dignity and purity, the horrible darkness being driven away of him who is now revealed, ὁ ἄνθρωπος {f.A4v} τῆς ἁμαρτίας, ὁ υἱὸς τῆς ἀπωλείας, “that man of sin, the son of perdition” [2 Thessalonians 2:3], they neglect and lay aside most carelessly; so that not a few are found, not so much among men of the lowest condition and the dregs of the common people, as even in the number of those who, set upon the highest pinnacle, sit at the helm of the commonwealth, who either pray seldom, and that with a cold and sluggish spirit, or, which is more to be lamented, are little or nothing careful about prayers, being nothing better than the swine of Epicurus' herd.

But to you, most illustrious Prince, I have desired to dedicate this little book for this cause most of all, that I have learned from the speech and the letters of many good men that Your Highness holdeth not the lowest place among those princes who, whilst the rest partly rage and rise up against the Lord and against his Christ [Psalm 2:2], and partly spoil the churches, seize the goods of the Church for themselves and turn them to {f.A5r} profane uses, do the office of a good, godly and truly Christian prince, cherishing and defending the Church and godly ministers; to the end that this your goodwill also, your bounty, your love toward the ministry, and lastly your most godly endeavours, wherewith you diligently endeavour to cherish and preserve the Church, now truly wretched and afflicted, may by this token, such as it is, and mark of our love and gratitude toward you in return, be the more stirred up; so that, whereas of your own accord, though no man admonish you, you do most readily and freely offer the breasts to the hungering and thirsting Church, you may not cease hereafter to be a nursing father [Isaiah 49:23] of the ministry of the word. And that you may do this with your wonted cheerfulness and liberality, I pray and beseech with my whole heart the eternal God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, that he would keep you and your illustrious and renowned house safe and unharmed, and would make Your Highness to grow day by day more and more in the knowledge of his grace and in faith unfeigned, {f.A5v} that you may be found unblameable and without fault in the day of our redemption, and at the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ with all his saints [1 Thessalonians 3:13]. Amen. 1561, on the 3rd day of March, the day on which Johann Friedrich, Elector, Duke of Saxony, died, who deserved most highly of the Church.[^7]

Most devoted to Your Highness,[^8]

Andreas Musculus, Doctor.

[^1]: The print has *recens cognitae*; the title of the 1573 edition reads *recens recognitae*, “newly revised”, which is the usual phrase and is followed here. A syllable has most probably dropped out, though *cognitae* can itself mean “examined”.

[^2]: The title page prints the place only, without a year. In the Munich copy the year “1. 5. 6. 1.” is written beneath it by hand; the year is that of the dedication, which is dated 3 March 1561.

[^3]: Homer, *Iliad* 19.290 (the words of Briseis).

[^4]: Homer, *Iliad* 17.446–447.

[^5]: The print has χαλάζει, without the final ν. The phrase is the proverb as Erasmus gives it, *Sacram ancoram solvere*, ἱερὰν ἄγκυραν χαλάζειν (*Adagia* 1.1.24): to let down the sacred anchor, the largest one, kept for the last, is to take to one's last refuge.

[^6]: The print has *collapsa*, read *collapsam*, agreeing with *consuetudinem* (or possibly *collapsae*, agreeing with *Ecclesiae*: “this custom of the Church, which is now well nigh fallen down”).

[^7]: Johann Friedrich I of Saxony died on 3 March 1554; the dedication is dated on the anniversary of his death.

[^8]: The print has only the abbreviation *T. C. DD.*, most probably *Tuae Celsitudini deditissimus*.
