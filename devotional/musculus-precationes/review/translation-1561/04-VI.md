# VI. Forms of Prayer from Meditation on the Passion of Christ, the Son of the Living God
*Formulae precandi ex meditatione Passionis Christi Filii Dei viventis*

## Augustine, Meditations, chapters 5 and 6

Almighty, everlasting God, I pray thee that me, whom mine own deserts threaten with the sentence of death, thou wouldst vouchsafe to restore unto life by the intercession of thy most precious offspring, who sitteth at thy right hand. For what other intercessor I should send {f.46v} unto thee I know not, save him who is the propitiation for our sins. Behold my Advocate with thee, God the Father. Behold the High Priest, who needeth not to be cleansed by the blood of another, who shineth bedewed with his own blood. Behold the holy sacrifice, well-pleasing and perfect, offered and accepted for a sweet-smelling savour. Behold the Lamb without spot, who before his shearers was dumb, who, smitten with buffets, besmeared with spittle, laden with reproaches, opened not his mouth. Lo, he who did no sin hath borne our sins, and with his stripes hath healed our sicknesses. Look, loving Father, upon thy most loving Son, who hath suffered for me things so cruel. Behold, most merciful King, who it is that suffereth, and graciously remember for whom he suffereth. Is not this, my Lord, that innocent one, whom, a Son, thou didst deliver up that thou mightest redeem a servant? Is not this the Author of life, who, led as a sheep to the slaughter, and made in all things obedient even unto death, feared not to undergo the most dreadful kind of death? Call to mind, O Dispenser of all salvation, {f.47r} that this is he whom, though thou didst beget him of thine own power, thou didst yet will to be made partaker of my weakness. Truly this is thy Godhead, which put on my nature, which ascended the gibbet of the cross, which in the flesh it had taken bore the sorrowful punishment. Turn back, O Lord my God, the eyes of thy Majesty upon the work of thine unspeakable lovingkindness. Behold thy sweet Son, stretched out in his whole body; see his guiltless hands dripping with holy blood, and, being appeased, forgive the wicked deeds which my hands have wrought. Consider his defenceless side pierced through with the cruel spear-point, and renew me in that sacred fountain which I believe to have flowed thence. See his unspotted feet, which stood not in the way of sinners, but walked always in thy law, pierced through with dreadful nails, and make perfect my goings in thy paths, and graciously make me to hate all the ways of iniquity; remove from me the way of iniquity, and mercifully make me to choose the way of truth. I beseech thee, O King of saints, by this Holy One of holy ones, by this my Redeemer, make me {f.47v} to run the way of thy commandments, that I may be able to be united in spirit to him who abhorred not to be clothed with my flesh. Dost thou not mark, loving Father, the head of thy dearest young Son, his snow-white neck bowed down, sunk in most precious death? Look, most gentle Maker, upon the manhood of thy beloved offspring, and have mercy upon the feebleness of thy weak creature. His naked breast gleameth white, his bloody side is red, his stretched bowels are parched, his comely eyes grow dim, his royal countenance is pale, his long arms are stiff, his legs hang like marble, a stream of blessed blood watereth his pierced feet. Look, glorious Father, upon the torn limbs of thy most dear offspring, and graciously remember what my substance is. Behold the punishment of God made man, and release the misery of man whom thou hast made. See the torment of the Redeemer, and forgive the offences of the redeemed. This is he, my Lord, whom thou hast smitten for the sins of thy people. This is that innocent one in whom no guile was found, and yet he was numbered with the transgressors. Who liveth and reigneth with thee {f.48r} in the unity of the Holy Ghost, God, world without end. Amen.

## Augustine, Meditations, chapter 7

What hast thou done, sweetest Child, that thou shouldest be so judged? What hast thou done, most loving Youth, that thou shouldest be so dealt with? What is thy crime, what thine offence, what the cause of thy death, what the occasion of thy condemnation? I am the stroke of thy pain, the fault for which thou wast slain. I am the desert of thy death, the wickedness that was avenged upon thee. I am the bruise of thy Passion, the travail of thy torment. O wondrous manner of judgment, and unspeakable ordering of the mystery! The unrighteous sinneth, and the righteous is punished; the guilty offendeth, and the innocent is beaten; the ungodly transgresseth, and the godly is condemned. What the evil deserveth, the good suffereth; what the servant committeth, the Lord payeth. What man doeth amiss, God beareth. How far, O Son of God, how far hath thy humility come down? {f.48v} How far hath thy charity burned? How far hath thy love reached? How far hath thy compassion come? For I have done wickedly, thou art visited with the penalty. I have committed the crime, thou art smitten with the vengeance. I have been proud, thou art humbled. I have been puffed up, thou art brought low. I have been disobedient, thou, being obedient, payest for the crime of disobedience. I have obeyed my appetite, thou art afflicted with hunger. Me the heat of desire carried away to unlawful lust, thee perfect charity led to the cross. I presumed to take that which was forbidden, thou didst undergo the rack.[^1] I delight in food, thou labourest on the gibbet. I enjoy pleasures, thou art torn with nails. I taste the sweetness of the apple, thou the bitterness of the gall. With me Eve laugheth and rejoiceth, with thee Mary weepeth and suffereth. Behold, O King of glory, behold, my ungodliness and thy godliness are made plain. Lo, my unrighteousness and thy righteousness are manifest. What, O my King and my God, what shall I render unto thee for all that thou hast rendered unto me? For nothing can be found in the heart of man that may worthily be returned for such rewards. Can the wit of man {f.49r} devise anything to which the divine compassion may be compared? Nor is it for the creature to contrive a service wherewith justly to repay the Creator’s help. Yet there is, O Son of God, in this thy wondrous dispensation, there is that wherein my frailty may in some measure suffice: if my mind, pricked by thy visitation, crucify its flesh with the affections and lusts. And that this may be done, do thou, O Lord Jesus Christ, rule and govern me by thy Spirit. Amen.

## Augustine, Meditations, chapter 8

Almighty God, Father of my Lord, I beseech thee, have mercy upon me; for whatsoever I have found most precious I have devoutly offered, whatsoever I have discovered most dear I have humbly presented. I have left nothing to myself which I have not set before thy Majesty; nothing now remaineth that I may add, for I have committed unto thee my whole hope. I have sent unto thee my Advocate, thy beloved Son; I have sent[^2] thy glorious offspring as Mediator[^3] between {f.49v} me and thee; I have sent, I say, an Intercessor, through whom I trust for pardon; I have sent in words the Word, whom I have said to have been sent for my deeds; and I have counted out unto thee the Passion of thy most holy offspring, which I believe to have been endured for me. I believe that the Godhead sent by thee took upon it my manhood, in which he thought it meet to endure buffets, bonds, spitting and mockery, and moreover to undergo the cross, the nails and the spear. This manhood, once brought low with the wailings of infancy, bound with the swaddling bands of childhood, vexed with the sweats of youth, wasted with fastings, afflicted with watchings, wearied with journeyings, afterwards visited with scourges, torn with torments, numbered with the dead, and endowed with the glory of the resurrection, he hath brought into the joys of heaven and hath set at the right hand of thy highness. This is my atonement and thy propitiation. Here graciously consider what a Son thou hast begotten, and what a servant thou hast redeemed. Here look upon the Maker, and despise not the thing that he hath made. Embrace the serene Shepherd, and mercifully look upon the sheep brought home {f.50r} upon his own shoulders. This is that most faithful Shepherd who long sought with many and divers labours the sheep that wandered over the steep places of the mountains and the precipices of the valleys; and who, when it was now dying, now fainting through long exile, yet having found it, rejoicing set himself beneath it, and with a wondrous striving of charity lifted it up to himself out of the deep abyss of confusion, and, holding it fast in loving embraces, carried back to the ninety and nine the one that was lost. Behold, my Lord and King, almighty God, behold, the good Shepherd bringeth back to thee that which thou didst commit unto him. By thine appointment he undertook the saving of man, whom he hath restored unto thee free from every stain. Behold, thy most glorious Son[^4] hath reconciled unto thee thy creature, which had strayed far from thee. Lo, the gentle Shepherd bringeth back to thy flock that which the violent robber had driven away. He restoreth to thy sight the servant whom his own conscience had made a fugitive; that he who of himself hath deserved punishment may, through this one’s doing, deserve pardon; and that he who for his faults looked for hell may now, with so great a Leader, trust {f.50v} to be called back to his own country. Of myself, holy Father, I was able to offend thee, but of myself I was not able to appease thee. My God, thy beloved Son, was made my helper, partaking of my manhood that he might heal my weakness; that whence the cause of offence had arisen, thence he might offer unto thee the sacrifice of praise, and might thereby render me acceptable to thy lovingkindness, in that, sitting at thy right hand, he should always shew himself to be a partaker of my substance. Behold my hope, behold my whole confidence. If thou despisest me, as is meet, for mine iniquity, yet mercifully look upon me for the charity of thy beloved offspring. Consider in the Son that for which thou mayest be gracious to the servant. See the mystery of the flesh, and forgive the guilt of the flesh. As often as the wounds of thy blessed offspring lie open before thee, let my wicked deeds, I beseech thee, be hidden. As often as the precious blood is red from his holy side, let the stain of my defilement, I beseech thee, be washed away. And because flesh hath provoked thee to anger, let flesh, I beseech thee, bend thee to mercy; and as flesh hath led me astray into fault, let flesh lead me unto pardon. {f.51r} For great is that which mine ungodliness deserveth, but far greater that which the godliness of my Redeemer rightfully claimeth. For great is mine unrighteousness, but much greater the righteousness of my Redeemer. For by as much as God is higher than man, by so much is my wickedness less than his goodness, both in quality and in quantity. For what offence hath man committed which the Son of God made man hath not redeemed? What pride could be so swollen that so great humility should not lay it low? What dominion of death so great that the torment of the cross of the Son of God should not destroy it? Truly, my God, if the offences of sinning man and the grace of the redeeming Author be weighed in an even balance, they are parted not merely as the east is from the west, but as the lowest hell from the highest pole of heaven. Now, O most excellent Creator of light, now forgive my faults for the measureless labours of thy beloved Son. Now, I pray thee, let my ungodliness be forgiven for his godliness, my perverseness for his modesty, and my fierceness for his meekness. Now let his humility win over my pride, {f.51v} his patience my impatience, his kindness my hardness, his obedience my disobedience, his calm my unquietness, his sweetness my bitterness, his gentleness my wrath, his charity my cruelty. Through the same thy Son, who liveth and reigneth with thee, world without end. Amen.

## Augustine, Meditations, chapter 14

I might indeed have despaired because of my exceeding sins and my infinite negligences, had not thy Word, O God, been made flesh and dwelt among us. But now I dare not despair, because when we were enemies we were reconciled by the death of thy Son; how much more, being reconciled, are we saved through him. For all my hope, and the assurance of my whole confidence, is in his precious blood, which was shed for us and for our salvation. In him I breathe again, and trusting in him I long {f.52r} to come unto thee, not having mine own righteousness, but that which is of thy Son, our Lord Jesus Christ. Wherefore we give thanks unto thee, O God, most merciful and most gracious lover of men, who through Jesus Christ thy Son our Lord, when we were not, didst mightily make us, and when we had been lost through our own fault, didst wonderfully deliver and recover us. I give thee thanks for his Passion and cross, for his death and resurrection, for his taking up into heaven and the seat of his Majesty at thy right hand. I give thee thanks for that most sacred shedding of his precious blood, whereby we are redeemed, and likewise for the most holy and life-giving mystery of his body and blood, whereby we are daily fed and given to drink in thy Church, are washed and sanctified, and are made partakers of the one supreme Godhead. With whom unto thee, and likewise unto the Holy Ghost, be honour, praise and glory, world without end. Amen.

## Augustine, Manual, chapter 13

{f.52v} Almighty God, holy Father, I might have despaired because of my exceeding sins and vices, my faults and infinite negligences, which I have committed and daily without ceasing do commit in heart, in word and in deed, and in all the ways in which human frailty can sin, had not thy Word, O my God, been made flesh[^5] and dwelt among us. But now I dare not despair, because he, being subject unto thee even unto death, yea, the death of the cross, took away the handwriting of our sins, and, nailing it to the cross, crucified sin and death. In him, then, I breathe again in safety, who sitteth at thy right hand and maketh intercession for us; trusting in him I long to come unto thee, in whom we are already risen, have already ascended into heaven, and sit together in heavenly places. To thee be praise, to thee glory, to thee honour, to thee thanksgiving. Amen.

## Augustine, Manual, chapter 21

{f.53r} Through love God came unto men and was made man. Through love the invisible God was made like unto his servants. Through love he was wounded for our transgressions. Safe and sure rest is there for the weak and for sinners in the wounds of the Saviour. There I dwell secure; through the wounds his bowels lie open to me. Whatsoever is wanting to me of myself, I take to myself from the bowels of my Lord; for they overflow with mercy, nor are there wanting openings through which they may flow. Through the openings of his body the secrets of his heart lie open to me; the great mystery of godliness lieth open; the bowels of the mercy of our God lie open, whereby the dayspring from on high hath visited us. The wounds of Jesus Christ are full of mercy, full of lovingkindness, full of sweetness and charity. They pierced his hands and his feet, and with a spear they pierced his side. Through these clefts it is given me to taste how gracious the Lord my God is; {f.53v} for truly he is gracious and gentle, and of great mercy unto all that call upon him in truth, unto all that seek him, and most of all unto them that love him. Plenteous redemption is given unto us in the wounds of Jesus Christ our Saviour. I glorify thee, O Lord Jesus Christ, my Redeemer and Deliverer, and I will praise thy name as long as I have my being. Amen.

## Augustine, Manual, chapters 22 and 23

When any foul thought assaileth me, I run back to the wounds of Christ. When my flesh presseth me down, by the remembrance of the wounds of my Lord I rise again. When the devil layeth snares for me, I flee to the bowels of the mercy of my Lord, and he departeth from me. If the heat of lust stir my members, it is quenched by the remembrance of the wounds of our Lord, the Son of God. In all adversities I have found no remedy so effectual as {f.54r} the wounds of Christ. In them I sleep secure and rest without fear. Christ died for us; there is nothing so bitter unto death that it is not healed by the death of Christ. All my hope is in the death of my Lord. His death is my merit, my refuge, my life and my resurrection. My merit is the compassion of the Lord; I am not poor in merit so long as that Lord of compassions faileth not. And if the mercies of the Lord are many, many am I in merits. The mightier he is to save, the more secure am I. I have sinned a great sin, and am conscious to myself of many offences; yet not even so do I despair. For where offences abounded, grace did much more abound. He that despaireth of the pardon of his sins denieth that God is merciful. He doeth God great wrong who distrusteth his mercy. As much as in him lieth, he denieth that God hath charity, truth and power, in which my whole hope standeth: {f.54v} namely, in the charity of adoption, in the truth of the promise, and in the power of redemption. Let my foolish thought now murmur as much as it will, saying: “For who art thou, and how great is that glory, or by what merits dost thou hope to obtain it?” And I will answer with confidence: “I know whom I have believed; for in exceeding charity he hath adopted me to be a son, who is true in promising, mighty in performing, and it is lawful for him to do what he will.” I cannot be terrified by the multitude of my sins, if the death of the Lord come into my mind, for my sins cannot overcome him. The nails[^6] and the spear cry unto me that I am truly reconciled to Christ, if I love him. Longinus[^7] opened unto me the side of Christ with his spear, and I have entered in, and there I rest secure. He stretcheth out his arms upon the cross and spreadeth forth his hands, ready for the embraces of sinners. Between the arms of my Saviour I desire both to live and to die; there, secure, I will sing: I will extol thee, O Lord, for thou hast lifted me up, {f.55r} and hast not made my foes to rejoice over me.

## The Church

O Lord Jesus Christ, Son of the living God, set thy Passion, cross and death between thy judgment and my soul, now and in the hour of my death. And vouchsafe always to grant to the living mercy and grace, to thy Church peace and concord, and to us sinners life and glory everlasting. Who livest and reignest with God the Father in the unity of the Holy Ghost, God, world without end. Amen.

## Psalm 22

{f.58v} My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me? Thou art far from helping me, and from the words of my roaring.

O my God, I cry in the daytime, and thou answerest not; and in the night season also I am not silent.

But thou art holy, O thou that inhabitest the praises of Israel.

Our fathers trusted in thee: they trusted, and thou didst deliver them.

They cried unto thee, and were delivered: they trusted in thee, and were not confounded.

But I am a worm, and no man; a reproach of men, and despised of the people.

All they that see me have laughed me to scorn: they have opened the lips, and have shaken the head.

Let him make his complaint unto the Lord; let him deliver him, let him save him, seeing he delighteth in him.

For thou art he that took me out of the womb: my hope from my mother’s breasts.

{f.59r} I was cast upon thee from the womb: thou art my God from my mother’s belly.

Be not far from me; for trouble is near; for there is none to help.

Many oxen have compassed me: fat bulls have beset me round.

They gaped upon me with their mouths, as a ravening and a roaring lion.

I am poured out like water, and all my bones are scattered: my heart is become like melting wax in the midst of my bowels.

My strength is dried up like a potsherd; and my tongue cleaveth to my jaws; and thou hast brought me into the dust of death.

For dogs have compassed me: the assembly of the wicked have inclosed me: they pierced my hands and my feet.

I may tell all my bones: but they look and stare upon me.

They parted my garments among them, and cast lots upon my vesture.

{f.59v} But be not thou far from me, O Lord: O my strength, haste thee to defend me.

Deliver my soul from the sword; my darling from the power of the dog.

Save me from the lion’s mouth, and my lowliness from the horns of the unicorns.

| God made man, was taken at the hour of Matins.
| By his own disciples quickly forsaken,
| he was delivered to the Jews, sold and afflicted.

| At the first hour Jesus was led to Pilate,
| much accused by false witnesses;
| they smite him on the neck, his hands bound;
| they spit upon the face of God, the pleasant light of heaven.

| “Crucify,” they cry at the third hour;
| mocked, he is clothed in a purple robe;
| his head is pierced with a crown of thorns;
| {f.60r} he beareth the cross on his shoulders to the place of punishment.

| At the sixth hour Jesus was nailed to the cross,
| and, hanging, was numbered with the thieves;
| thirsting by reason of his torments, he was filled with gall;
| the Lamb washeth away guilt, being thus made a mockery.

| At the ninth hour the Lord Jesus gave up the ghost;
| crying “Eli,” he commended his soul to the Father;
| a soldier pierced his side with a spear;
| then the earth quaked, and the sun was darkened.

| He is taken down from the cross at the evening hour;
| his might lay hidden in the divine mind;
| such a death did the medicine of life undergo;
| alas, the crown of glory lay prostrate.

| At the hour of Compline he is given to burial,
| the noble body of Christ, the hope of the life to come;
| he is embalmed with spice, the Scriptures are fulfilled;

| {f.60v} These canonical hours with devotion
| I recall unto thee, O Christ, in godly meditation,

| mayest be my comfort in the agony of death. Amen.
