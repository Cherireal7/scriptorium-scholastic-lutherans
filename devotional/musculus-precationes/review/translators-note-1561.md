# Translator’s Note

## 1. The book

Andreas Musculus (1514–1581), professor of theology at Frankfurt on the Oder and later one of the authors of the Formula of Concord, gathered this book of Latin prayers for the use of students, pastors and households. Its matter is not his own. As the title says, the prayers are taken *ex veteribus orthodoxis doctoribus* — out of the ancient teachers of the Church, above all the books of meditations, soliloquies and the manual that went under Augustine’s name, with Bernard, Jerome, Cyprian, Ambrose and others; *ex Ecclesiae hymnis et canticis* — out of the hymns, collects and canticles of the Church’s services; and *ex Psalmis Davidis* — out of the Psalter. Musculus arranged them *in certos locos*, under eighteen heads, from the invocation of the Holy Trinity to the prayers at table, so that a reader could find a form of prayer for the chief articles of the faith and for the chief needs of a Christian life.

## 2. The text-base

This working English translation follows the edition printed at Frankfurt on the Oder in 1561 (VD16 ZV 26230; 5 unnumbered and 139 numbered leaves, in octavo). Its dedication, to Duke Johann Albrecht of Mecklenburg, is dated 3 March 1561 and calls this the third edition. The title page of the copy used prints the place but no year. The copy is that of the Bayerische Staatsbibliothek, Munich, shelf-mark Asc. 5561 x, in the library’s digital copy bsb10173614.

The translation was made from the page images. The prayers were first translated from the enlarged Leipzig edition of 1573, which contains them all, and that translation was read a second time against the 1573 print. For this volume every page was then read again against the page images of the 1561 edition: what the 1561 edition does not have was taken out, and wherever its wording differs from that of 1573 the English follows 1561, with a note giving the later reading. The library’s machine-read text was used as an aid only. The printer’s abbreviations have been expanded silently. The handwritten entries of former owners in the Munich copy, among them a register of hymns on the last leaves, are not part of the book and are not translated.

## 3. What is translated

Everything the printer set is here: the title page, the dedication, and the eighteen sections of prayers. The 1561 edition has no calendar and no printed index. Running heads, signature marks and catchwords are left out.

## 4. Editorial conventions

**Leaf numbers.** The 1561 edition numbers its leaves, not its pages. The small red marks in square brackets in the text show where each page of the print begins: [12r] is the front of leaf 12, [12v] its back. The leaves of the dedication are marked by their signatures.

**Section numbers.** The sections are numbered I to XVIII throughout. The print gives no numeral to the first, the seventeenth and the eighteenth.

**Headings.** Each main section carries its English title with the Latin title beneath it. Within a section, each prayer stands under the name the book gives it — the Father or the book it is taken from (“Augustine, Meditations, chapter 12”), or “The Church” for hymns, collects and canticles of the services, or the number of the Psalm.

**Scripture.** Psalms and other passages of Scripture are conformed to the King James Version (1611), which is in the public domain. The book numbers the Psalms as the Hebrew Bible and the King James Version do, and its Latin Psalter is not the Vulgate but a newer version made closer to the Hebrew. Where that Latin plainly says something other than the King James Version, the Latin is translated. The pieces headed “gathered out of the Psalms” are chains of single verses from many Psalms; they are given verse by verse as printed, without references.

**God and Lord.** The 1561 print sets *Deus* and *Dominus* in ordinary type, so this volume writes “God” and “Lord” throughout, in the Psalms also. (The 1573 print sets them in small capitals in many places, and the companion volume shows this.)

**Readings of 1573.** In forty-three places the wording of this edition differs from that of 1573 in more than spelling. The English follows 1561 there, and a note gives the later reading. In a few of these places it is not certain whether the 1561 reading is what the compiler meant or a slip of the press that the later edition put right; the notes say so.

**Register.** The prayers are rendered in the traditional English of prayer — *thou*, *thee*, *thy* — so that the patristic prayers, the collects and the Psalms stand in one voice, as they do in the Latin. The Duke, in the dedication, is addressed as *you*.

**Hymns and verse.** Hymns and sequences are translated line for line in plain prose lines, without rhyme or metre. The Latin first line is given in italics above each hymn.

**Attributions.** The attributions are those of the print. Many of the prayers that the sixteenth century read under the names of Augustine, Bernard or Jerome are now known to be the work of later, mostly medieval, writers; the book’s own ascriptions are kept as printed, without correction. Where a prayer has no name over it in the print, none has been added.

**Misprints.** Where the print is plainly wrong and the right reading is not in doubt, the right reading is translated and the printed one is given in a note. Notes stand at the end of the section they belong to.

## 5. The edition of 1573

Musculus enlarged the book again after 1561. The Leipzig edition of 1573 keeps every prayer of this edition in its place, and adds a calendar, a new preface dated 1570, an index, and ninety-four further pieces at the ends of ten sections. It is translated in full in the companion volume. The appendix to this volume lists what it adds.

## 6. Status

This is a working edition. The other printings of the book (among them Frankfurt on the Oder 1559, Wittenberg 1562, Leipzig 1571 and 1580) have not been compared, and the sources of the single prayers have not been traced beyond what the book itself names.
