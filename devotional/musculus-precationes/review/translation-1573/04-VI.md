# VI. Forms of Prayer from Meditation on the Passion of Christ, the Son of the Living God
*Formulae precandi ex meditatione Passionis Christi Filii Dei viventis*

## Augustine, Meditations, chapters 5 and 6

Almighty, everlasting God, I pray thee that me, whom mine own deserts threaten with the sentence of death, thou wouldst vouchsafe to restore unto life by the intercession of thy most precious offspring, who sitteth at thy right hand. For what other intercessor I should send {p.85} unto thee I know not, save him who is the propitiation for our sins. Behold my Advocate with thee, God the Father. Behold the High Priest, who needeth not to be cleansed by the blood of another, who shineth bedewed with his own blood. Behold the holy sacrifice, well-pleasing and perfect, offered and accepted for a sweet-smelling savour. Behold the Lamb without spot, who before his shearers was dumb, who, smitten with buffets, besmeared with spittle, laden with reproaches, opened not his mouth. Lo, he who did no sin hath borne our sins, and with his stripes hath healed our sicknesses. Look, loving Father, upon thy most loving Son, who hath suffered for me things so cruel. Behold, most merciful King, who it is that suffereth, and graciously remember for whom he suffereth. Is not this, my Lord, that innocent one, whom, a Son, thou didst deliver up that thou mightest redeem a servant? Is not this the Author of life, who, led as a sheep to the slaughter, and made in all things obedient even unto death, feared not to undergo the most dreadful kind of death? Call to mind, O Dispenser of all salvation, that this is he whom, though thou didst beget him of thine own power, thou didst yet will to be made partaker of my weakness. Truly this is thy Godhead, which put on my nature, which ascended the gibbet of the cross, which in the flesh it had taken bore the sorrowful punishment. Turn back, O Lord my God, the eyes {p.86} of thy Majesty upon the work of thine unspeakable lovingkindness. Behold thy sweet Son, stretched out in his whole body; see his guiltless hands dripping with holy blood, and, being appeased, forgive the wicked deeds which my hands have wrought. Consider his defenceless side pierced through with the cruel spear-point, and renew me in that sacred fountain which I believe to have flowed thence. See his unspotted feet, which stood not in the way of sinners, but walked always in thy law, pierced through with dreadful nails, and make perfect my goings in thy paths, and graciously make me to hate all the ways of iniquity; remove from me the way of iniquity, and mercifully make me to choose the way of truth. I beseech thee, O King of saints, by this Holy One of holy ones, by this my Redeemer, make me to run the way of thy commandments, that I may be able to be united in spirit to him who abhorred not to be clothed with my flesh. Dost thou not mark, loving Father, the head of thy dearest young Son, his snow-white neck bowed down, sunk in most precious death? Look, most gentle Maker, upon the manhood of thy beloved offspring, and have mercy upon the feebleness of thy weak creature. His naked breast gleameth white, his bloody side is red, his stretched bowels[^1] are parched, his comely eyes grow dim, his royal countenance is pale, his long arms are stiff, his legs hang like marble, a stream of blessed blood watereth {p.87} his pierced feet. Look, glorious Father, upon the torn limbs of thy most dear offspring, and graciously remember what my substance is. Behold the punishment of God made man, and release the misery of man whom thou hast made. See the torment of the Redeemer, and forgive the offences of the redeemed. This is he, my Lord, whom thou hast smitten for the sins of thy people. This is that innocent one in whom no guile was found, and yet he was numbered with the transgressors. Who liveth and reigneth with thee in the unity of the Holy Ghost, God, world without end. Amen.

## Augustine, Meditations, chapter 7

What hast thou done, sweetest Child, that thou shouldest be so judged? What hast thou done, most loving Youth, that thou shouldest be so dealt with? What is thy crime, what thine offence, what the cause of thy death, what the occasion of thy condemnation? I am the stroke of thy pain, the fault for which thou wast slain. I am the desert of thy death, the wickedness that was avenged upon thee. I am the bruise of thy Passion, the travail of thy torment. O wondrous manner of judgment, and unspeakable ordering of the mystery! The unrighteous sinneth, and the righteous is punished; the guilty offendeth, and the innocent is beaten; the ungodly transgresseth, and the godly is condemned. What the evil deserveth, the good suffereth; {p.88} what the servant committeth, the Lord payeth. What man doeth amiss, God beareth. How far, O Son of God, how far hath thy humility come down? How far hath thy charity burned? How far hath thy love reached? How far hath thy compassion come? For I have done wickedly, thou art visited with the penalty. I have committed the crime, thou art smitten with the vengeance. I have been proud, thou art humbled. I have been puffed up, thou art brought low. I have been disobedient, thou, being obedient, payest for the crime of disobedience. I have obeyed my appetite, thou art afflicted with hunger. Me the heat of desire carried away to unlawful lust, thee perfect charity led to the cross. I presumed to take that which was forbidden, thou didst undergo the sting. I delight in food, thou labourest on the gibbet. I enjoy pleasures, thou art torn with nails. I taste the sweetness of the apple, thou the bitterness of the gall. With me Eve laugheth and rejoiceth, with thee Mary weepeth and suffereth. Behold, O King of glory, behold, my ungodliness and thy godliness are made plain. Lo, my unrighteousness and thy righteousness are manifest. What, O my King and my God, what shall I render unto thee for all that thou hast rendered unto me? For nothing can be found in the heart of man that may worthily be returned for such rewards. Can the wit of man devise anything to which the divine compassion may be compared? Nor is it for the creature to contrive a service wherewith justly to repay the Creator’s help. Yet there is, O Son of God, in {p.89} this thy wondrous dispensation, there is that wherein my frailty may in some measure suffice: if my mind, pricked by thy visitation, crucify its flesh with the affections and lusts. And that this may be done, do thou, O Lord Jesus Christ, rule and govern me by thy Spirit. Amen.

## Augustine, Meditations, chapter 8

Almighty God, Father of my Lord, I beseech thee, have mercy upon me; for whatsoever I have found most precious I have devoutly offered, whatsoever I have discovered most dear I have humbly presented. I have left nothing to myself which I have not set before thy Majesty; nothing now remaineth that I may add, for I have committed unto thee my whole hope. I have sent unto thee my Advocate, thy beloved Son; I have sent[^2] thy glorious offspring as Mediator between me and thee; I have sent, I say, an Intercessor, through whom I trust for pardon; I have sent in words the Word, whom I have said to have been sent for my deeds; and I have counted out unto thee the Passion of thy most holy offspring, which I believe to have been endured for me. I believe that the Godhead sent by thee took upon it my manhood, in which he thought it meet to endure buffets, bonds, spitting and mockery, and moreover to undergo the cross, the nails and the spear. This manhood, once {p.90} brought low with the wailings of infancy, bound with the swaddling bands of childhood, vexed with the sweats of youth, wasted with fastings, afflicted with watchings, wearied with journeyings, afterwards visited with scourges, torn with torments, numbered with the dead, and endowed with the glory of the resurrection, he hath brought into the joys of heaven and hath set at the right hand of thy highness. This is my atonement and thy propitiation. Here graciously consider what a Son thou hast begotten, and what a servant thou hast redeemed. Here look upon the Maker, and despise not the thing that he hath made. Embrace the serene Shepherd, and mercifully look upon the sheep brought home upon his own shoulders. This is that most faithful Shepherd who long sought with many and divers labours the sheep that wandered over the steep places of the mountains and the precipices of the valleys; and who, when it was now dying, now fainting through long exile, yet having found it, rejoicing set himself beneath it, and with a wondrous striving of charity lifted it up to himself out of the deep abyss of confusion, and, holding it fast in loving embraces, carried back to the ninety and nine the one that was lost. Behold, my Lord and King, almighty God, behold, the good Shepherd bringeth back to thee that which thou didst commit unto him. By thine appointment he undertook the saving of man, whom he hath restored unto thee free from every stain. Behold, thy dearest Son hath reconciled unto thee thy creature, which had strayed far from thee. Lo, the gentle Shepherd {p.91} bringeth back to thy flock that which the violent robber had driven away. He restoreth to thy sight the servant whom his own conscience had made a fugitive; that he who of himself hath deserved punishment may, through this one’s doing, deserve pardon; and that he who for his faults looked for hell may now, with so great a Leader, trust to be called back to his own country. Of myself, holy Father, I was able to offend thee, but of myself I was not able to appease thee. My God, thy beloved Son, was made my helper, partaking of my manhood that he might heal my weakness; that whence the cause of offence had arisen, thence he might offer unto thee the sacrifice of praise, and might thereby render me acceptable to thy lovingkindness, in that, sitting at thy right hand, he should always shew himself to be a partaker of my substance. Behold my hope, behold my whole confidence. If thou despisest me, as is meet, for mine iniquity, yet mercifully look upon me for the charity of thy beloved offspring. Consider in the Son that for which thou mayest be gracious to the servant. See the mystery of the flesh, and forgive the guilt of the flesh. As often as the wounds of thy blessed offspring lie open before thee, let my wicked deeds, I beseech thee, be hidden. As often as the precious blood is red from his holy side, let the stain of my defilement, I beseech thee, be washed away. And because flesh hath provoked thee to anger, let flesh, I beseech thee, bend thee to mercy; and as flesh hath led me astray into fault, let flesh lead me unto pardon. {p.92} For great is that which mine ungodliness deserveth, but far greater that which the godliness of my Redeemer rightfully claimeth. For great is mine unrighteousness, but much greater the righteousness of my Redeemer. For by as much as God is higher than man, by so much is my wickedness less than his goodness, both in quality and in quantity. For what offence hath man committed which the Son of God made man hath not redeemed? What pride could be so swollen that so great humility should not lay it low? What dominion of death so great that the torment of the cross of the Son of God should not destroy it? Truly, my God, if the offences of sinning man and the grace of the redeeming Author be weighed in an even balance, they are parted not merely as the east is from the west, but as the lowest hell from the highest pole of heaven. Now, O most excellent Creator of light, now forgive my faults for the measureless labours of thy beloved Son. Now, I pray thee, let my ungodliness be forgiven for his godliness, my perverseness for his modesty, and my fierceness for his meekness. Now let his humility win over my pride, his patience my impatience, his kindness my hardness, his obedience my disobedience, his calm my unquietness, his sweetness my bitterness, his gentleness my wrath, his charity my cruelty. Through the same thy Son, who liveth and reigneth with thee, world without end. Amen.

## Augustine, Meditations, chapter 14

{p.93} I might indeed have despaired because of my exceeding sins and my infinite negligences, had not thy Word, O God, been made flesh and dwelt among us. But now I dare not despair, because when we were enemies we were reconciled by the death of thy Son; how much more, being reconciled, are we saved through him. For all my hope, and the assurance of my whole confidence, is in his precious blood, which was shed for us and for our salvation. In him I breathe again, and trusting in him I long to come unto thee, not having mine own righteousness, but that which is of thy Son, our Lord Jesus Christ. Wherefore we give thanks unto thee, O God, most merciful and most gracious lover of men, who through Jesus Christ thy Son our Lord, when we were not, didst mightily make us, and when we had been lost through our own fault, didst wonderfully deliver and recover us. I give thee thanks for his Passion and cross, for his death and resurrection, for his taking up into heaven and the seat of his Majesty at thy right hand. I give thee thanks for that most sacred shedding of his precious blood, {p.94} whereby we are redeemed, and likewise for the most holy and life-giving mystery of his body and blood, whereby we are daily fed and given to drink in thy Church, are washed and sanctified, and are made partakers of the one supreme Godhead. With whom unto thee, and likewise unto the Holy Ghost, be honour, praise and glory, world without end. Amen.

## Augustine, Manual, chapter 13

Almighty God, holy Father, I might have despaired because of my exceeding sins and vices, my faults and infinite negligences, which I have committed and daily without ceasing do commit in heart, in word and in deed, and in all the ways in which human frailty can sin, were not thy Word, O my God, flesh, and did he not dwell among us. But now I dare not despair, because he, being subject unto thee even unto death, yea, the death of the cross, took away the handwriting of our sins, and, nailing it[^3] to the cross, crucified sin and death. In him, then, I breathe again in safety, who sitteth at thy right hand and maketh intercession for us; trusting in him I long to come unto thee, in whom we are already risen, have already ascended into heaven, and sit together in heavenly places. {p.95} To thee be praise, to thee glory, to thee honour, to thee thanksgiving. Amen.

## Augustine, Manual, chapter 21

Through love God came unto men and was made man. Through love the invisible God was made like unto his servants. Through love he was wounded for our transgressions. Safe and sure rest is there for the weak and for sinners in the wounds of the Saviour. There I dwell secure; through the wounds his bowels lie open to me. Whatsoever is wanting to me of myself, I take to myself from the bowels of my Lord; for they overflow with mercy, nor are there wanting openings through which they may flow. Through the openings of his body the secrets of his heart lie open to me; the great mystery of godliness lieth open; the bowels of the mercy of our God lie open, whereby the dayspring from on high hath visited us. The wounds of Jesus Christ are full of mercy, full of lovingkindness, full of sweetness and charity. They pierced his hands and his feet, and with a spear they pierced his side. Through these clefts it is given me to taste how gracious the Lord my God is; for truly he is gracious and gentle, and of great mercy unto all that call upon him in truth, unto all that seek him, and most of all unto them that love him. Plenteous redemption {p.96} is given unto us in the wounds of Jesus Christ our Saviour. I glorify thee, O Lord Jesus Christ, my Redeemer and Deliverer, and I will praise thy name as long as I have my being. Amen.

## Augustine, Manual, chapters 22 and 23

When any foul thought assaileth me, I run back to the wounds of Christ. When my flesh presseth me down, by the remembrance of the wounds of my Lord I rise again. When the devil layeth snares for me, I flee to the bowels of the mercy of my Lord, and he departeth from me. If the heat of lust stir my members, it is quenched by the remembrance of the wounds of our Lord, the Son of God. In all adversities I have found no remedy so effectual as the wounds of Christ. In them I sleep secure and rest without fear. Christ died for us; there is nothing so bitter unto death that it is not healed by the death of Christ. All my hope is in the death of my Lord. His death is my merit, my refuge, my life and my resurrection. My merit is the compassion of the Lord; I am not poor in merit so long as that Lord of compassions faileth not. And if the mercies of the Lord are many, many am I in merits. The mightier he is to save, {p.97} the more secure am I. I have sinned a great sin, and am conscious to myself of many offences; yet not even so do I despair. For where offences abounded, grace did much more abound. He that despaireth of the pardon of his sins denieth that God is merciful. He doeth God great wrong who distrusteth his mercy. As much as in him lieth, he denieth that God hath charity, truth and power, in which my whole hope standeth: namely, in the charity of adoption, in the truth of the promise, and in the power of redemption. Let my foolish thought now murmur as much as it will, saying: “For who art thou, and how great is that glory, or by what merits dost thou hope to obtain it?” And I will answer with confidence: “I know whom I have believed; for in exceeding charity he hath adopted me to be a son, who is true in promising, mighty in performing, and it is lawful for him to do what he will.” I cannot be terrified by the multitude of my sins, if the death of the Lord come into my mind, for my sins cannot overcome him. The nail and the spear cry unto me that I am truly reconciled to Christ, if I love him. Longinus[^4] opened unto me the side of Christ with his spear, and I have entered in, and there I rest secure. He stretcheth out his arms upon the cross and spreadeth forth his hands, {p.98} ready for the embraces of sinners. Between the arms of my Saviour I desire both to live and to die; there, secure, I will sing: I will extol thee, O Lord, for thou hast lifted me up, and hast not made my foes to rejoice over me.

O Lord Jesus Christ, Son of the living God, set thy Passion, cross and death between thy judgment and my soul, now and in the hour of my death. And vouchsafe always to grant to the living mercy and grace, to thy Church peace and concord, and to us sinners life and glory everlasting. Who livest and reignest with God the Father in the unity of the Holy Ghost, God, world without end. Amen.

## Psalm 22

My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me? Thou art far from helping me, and from the words of my roaring.

O my God, I cry in the daytime, and thou answerest not; and in the night season also I am not silent.

But thou art holy, O thou that inhabitest the praises of Israel.

Our fathers trusted in thee: they trusted, and thou didst deliver them.

They cried unto thee, and were delivered: they trusted in thee, and were not confounded.

But I am a worm, and no man; a reproach of men, and despised of the people.

All they that see me have laughed me to scorn: they have opened the lips, and have shaken the head.

Let him make his complaint unto the Lord; let him deliver him, let him save him, seeing he delighteth in him.

For thou art he that took me out of the womb: my hope from my mother’s breasts.

I was cast upon thee from the womb: thou art my God from my mother’s belly.

Be not far from me; for trouble is {p.105} near; for there is none to help.

Many oxen have compassed me: fat bulls have beset me round.

They gaped upon me with their mouths, as a ravening and a roaring lion.

I am poured out like water, and all my bones are scattered: my heart is become like melting wax in the midst of my bowels.

My strength is dried up like a potsherd; and my tongue cleaveth to my jaws; and thou hast brought me into the dust of death.

For dogs have compassed me: the assembly of the wicked have inclosed me: they pierced my hands and my feet.

I may tell all my bones: but they look and stare upon me.

They parted my garments among them, and cast lots upon my vesture.

But be not thou far from me, O Lord: O my strength, haste thee to defend me.

Deliver my soul from the sword; my darling from the power of the dog.

Save me from the lion’s mouth, and my lowliness from the horns of the unicorns.

## Prayer. Bonaventure

{p.112} We adore thee, O Christ, and we bless thee, who by thy cross hast redeemed the world. I give thanks unto thee, O Lord Jesus Christ, by whose grace I am what I am, by whose mercy I live, move and have my being. Vouchsafe that I may praise thee, most gracious Jesus Christ, and sing praises to thy most sacred Passion.

## Another

O God, who didst will that thy Son should undergo for us the gibbet of the cross, that thou mightest drive away from us the power of the enemy: grant unto us thy servants that we may obtain the grace of the resurrection. Through, etc.

## Another

O God, who in the humility of thy Son hast raised up the fallen world: grant unto thy faithful people perpetual gladness, that those whom thou hast delivered from the perils of perpetual death thou mayest make also to enjoy everlasting joys. Through the same.

## Another

O Lord Jesus Christ,[^7] eternal sweetness and jubilation of them that love thee, surpassing all joy and all desire, salvation and lover of penitent sinners, {p.113} who hast testified that thy delights are with the sons of men, being made man for man’s sake in the end of the ages: remember all the forethought and inward sorrow which thou didst bear in thy human nature from the beginning of thy conception, and most of all when the time drew near of thy most saving Passion, foreordained from eternity in the divine heart. Remember the sadness and bitterness which, by thine own witness, thou hadst in thy soul when thou saidst: My soul is exceeding sorrowful, even unto death; and when at the last supper thou didst deliver thy body and blood to thy disciples, didst wash their feet, and, sweetly comforting them, didst foretell thy Passion that was at hand. Remember the trembling, anguish and pain which thou didst endure in thy whole tender body before the Passion of thy cross, when after thy threefold prayer thou didst pour forth a bloody sweat, wast betrayed by thine own disciple, wast taken by the chosen people, wast accused by false witnesses, wast unjustly judged by three judges in the chosen city. In the time of the Passover, in the flowering youth of thy body, thou wast condemned being innocent, wast delivered up, wast spit upon, wast stripped of thine own garment, wast clothed in the garments of another, wast buffeted, wast veiled in thine eyes and face, wast smitten with blows, wast bound to the pillar, {p.114} wast scourged, wast crowned with thorns. O sweetest Jesus, grant me, I beseech thee, for the remembrance of these pains and sufferings before the Passion of thy cross, true contrition before my death, pure confession, and the remission of all my sins. Amen.

## Another

O Jesus, true liberty of angels and Paradise of delights, remember the terror and horror which thou didst bear when all thine enemies, like most fierce lions, stood round about thee, and troubled thee with buffetings, spittings, tearings and other unheard-of torments; and by all the insulting words, the hard stripes and the most hard torments wherewith all thine enemies afflicted thee, I beseech thee that thou wouldst deliver me from all mine enemies, visible and invisible, and grant me to find under the shadow of thy wings the protection of eternal salvation. Amen.

## Another

O Jesus, Framer and Creator of the world, whom no measure meteth with a true bound, who holdest the earth in the hollow of thy hand, call to mind thy most bitter pain which thou didst bear {p.115} when they first fastened thy most holy hands to the cross with blunt nails, and, to pierce thy most tender feet, since thou wast not fitted to their will, added pain upon pain to thy wounds, and so cruelly drew thee asunder and stretched thee out upon the length and breadth of thy cross that they loosed all the joints of thy limbs: I pray thee, by the remembrance of this most holy and most bitter pain upon the cross, that thou wouldst give me thy fear and thy love. Amen.

## Another

O Jesus, heavenly Physician, call to mind the faintness and pain and bruising which, lifted up on the high gibbet of the cross, thou didst suffer in all thy torn limbs, of which not one had remained rightly in its place, so that no pain was found like unto thy pain; for from the sole of the foot even unto the crown of the head there was no soundness in thee. And then, unmindful of all thy pains, thou didst lovingly entreat the Father for thine enemies, saying: Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do. By this charity and mercy, and for the remembrance of thy pain, grant that thy Passion may be the full remission of all my sins. Amen.

## Another

{p.116} O Jesus, mirror of eternal brightness, remember that sorrow which thou hadst when in the mirror of thy most serene Majesty thou didst behold the predestination of thine elect, who were to be saved by the merits of thy Passion, and the reprobation of the wicked, who in their multitude were to be damned; and by the abyss of thy compassion, wherewith thou didst then grieve for lost and despairing sinners, and chiefly that which thou didst shew to the thief upon the cross, saying: To day shalt thou be with me in Paradise; I ask thee, gracious Jesus, that thou wouldst shew me thy mercy in the hour of my death. Amen.

## Another

O Jesus, King worthy to be loved and altogether to be desired, remember the sorrow which thou hadst when thou didst hang naked and pitiable upon the cross, and thy friends and acquaintance stood over against thee, and thou didst find no comfort, save only thy beloved mother, standing most faithfully by thee in thine agony, whom thou didst commend to thy disciple, saying: Woman, behold thy son; and to thy disciple: Behold thy mother. I ask thee, gracious Jesus, that thou wouldst have compassion on me in all my tribulations and afflictions, bodily and spiritual; give me comfort in every time of my tribulation. {p.117} Amen.

## Another

O Jesus, fountain of unexhausted lovingkindness, who out of the inmost affection of love didst say upon the cross: I thirst, as touching the salvation of mankind: kindle, we beseech thee, our desire unto every perfect work, and utterly cool and quench in us the thirst of fleshly lust and the heat of worldly loves. Amen.

## Another

O Jesus, sweetness of hearts and exceeding delight of minds, by the bitterness of the vinegar and gall which thou didst bear and taste for us upon the cross, grant unto us wretched sinners worthily to receive thy body and blood at all times, and chiefly in the hour of our death, for the strengthening of our faith concerning the salvation of our souls. Amen.

## Another

O Jesus, royal strength and jubilation of the mind, remember the anguish which thou didst suffer when, through the bitterness of death and the insulting of the Jews, thou didst cry with a loud voice that thou wast forsaken of God the Father, saying: Eli, Eli, lama sabachthani? that is to say, My God, {p.118} my God, why hast thou forsaken me? By this anguish I ask that in the anguish of my death thou forsake me not, O Lord my God. Amen.

## Another

O Jesus, Alpha and Omega, union and power, beginning and end without any mean, call to mind that from the top of thy head even unto the sole of thy foot thou didst plunge thyself for us in the water of thy Passion: for the breadth and length of thy wounds, teach me, who am sunk in sins, to keep through true charity thy commandment, which is exceeding broad. Amen.

## Another

O Jesus, abyss of deepest mercy, I ask thee, for the depth of thy wounds, which passed through thy flesh and the marrow of thy bones and thine inmost bowels, that thou wouldst raise me up, who am sunk in sins, and hide me in the clefts of thy wounds from the face of thy wrath, until thine indignation be overpast, O Lord. Amen.

## Another

O Jesus, mirror of truth, sign of unity and bond of charity, remember thy numberless wounds wherewith {p.119} thou wast wounded from the top of thy head even unto the soles of thy feet, and wast torn by the ungodly Jews and reddened with thy most holy blood: how great a pain thou didst endure for us in thy virgin flesh, gracious Jesus! What more oughtest thou to have done that thou hast not done? Write, I beseech thee, all thy wounds in my heart with thy most precious blood, that in them I may read thy pain and thy love; that the remembrance of them may ever abide in the secret place of my heart; that the pain of thy Passion may be daily renewed in me, and love increased, until I come unto thee, the longed-for treasure, filled with all good things and joys; which vouchsafe to grant me, sweetest Christ, in my life. Amen.

## Another

O Jesus, most mighty Lion, immortal and most unconquered King, remember the pain which thou didst suffer when all the powers of thy body and heart utterly failed, and, bowing thy head, thou saidst: It is finished. By this thine anguish and pain have mercy upon me in the last finishing of my life at my departure, when my soul shall be in anguish and my spirit troubled. Amen.

## Another

{p.120} O Jesus, only begotten of the most high Father, the brightness and express image of his substance, remember the earnest commendation wherewith thou didst commend thy spirit to the Father, saying: Into thy hands I commend my spirit; and, with thy body torn and thy heart broken, with a strong cry, the bowels of thy mercy being laid open, thou didst give up the ghost for our redemption. By this thy most precious death I beseech thee, O King of saints, strengthen me to resist the devil, the world, the flesh and blood, that, being dead to the world, I may live to thee alone; and in the last hour of my departure receive my spirit, an exile and a pilgrim, returning unto thee. Amen.

## Another

O Jesus, true and fruitful Vine, remember the overflowing and abundant shedding of thy blood, which thou didst pour forth most plenteously from thy body, as from a pressed cluster, when thou didst tread the winepress alone upon the cross, and, thy side being pierced with the soldier’s spear, didst give us blood and water to drink, so that not the least drop remained in thee. Then at last thou wast hung up on high as a bundle of myrrh, and thy tender flesh wasted away, and the moisture of thy bowels was dried up, and the marrow of thy bones withered. By this thy most bitter {p.121} Passion and the shedding of thy most precious blood I pray thee, sweetest Jesus, wound my heart, that tears of penitence and of love may be my bread day and night; and turn me wholly unto thee, that my heart may become a perpetual dwelling place for thee, and my conversation be pleasing and acceptable unto thee, and the end of my life so praiseworthy that after the term of my life I may be able to praise thee with all thy saints for ever. Amen.

## Memorial of the Passion
*Memoria Passionis*

There was darkness when the Jews had crucified Jesus; and about the ninth hour Jesus cried with a loud voice, saying: My God, why hast thou forsaken me? And he bowed his head, and gave up the ghost. Then one of the soldiers with a spear pierced his side, and forthwith came there out blood and water.

### Verse

And the veil of the temple was rent from the top to the bottom, and all the earth did quake. And he bowed his head, etc.

### Verse

God spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all.

### Prayer

{p.122} O Lord Jesus Christ, Son of the living God, who for the salvation of the world wast given gall and vinegar to drink upon the cross: as thou, when all things were finished, giving up the ghost upon the cross, didst commend thy spirit into the hands of the Father, so in the hour of my death I commend my soul into the hands of thy lovingkindness, that thou wouldst receive it in peace and command it to be gathered into the choirs of thine elect. Who livest and reignest with God the Father in the unity of the Holy Ghost, God, world without end. Amen.
