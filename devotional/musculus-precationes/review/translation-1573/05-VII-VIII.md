# VII. Forms of Prayer from Meditation on the Resurrection and Ascension into Heaven of the Son of the Living God
*Formulae precandi, ex meditatione Resurrectionis et Ascensionis in coelum Filii Dei viventis*

O Lord Jesus, gracious Jesus, good Jesus, who didst vouchsafe to die for our sins, and didst rise again for our justification, I beseech thee by thy glorious resurrection, raise me up from the sepulchre of all my vices and sins, and give me daily a part in the first {p.123} resurrection, that I may be counted worthy truly to receive a portion in thy resurrection. Most sweet Jesus, thou hast ascended into heaven with the triumph of thy glory, and sittest at the right hand of the Father, O most mighty King: draw me upward unto thee, that I may run after thee in the odour of thine ointments, that I may run and not faint, thou drawing, thou leading, I running; draw the mouth of the soul that thirsteth for thee to the streams above of everlasting fulness. Yea, draw me unto thee, the living fountain, that according to my measure I may drink thence, whereby I may live for ever. O my God, my life, thou fountain of life, fill my mind with the torrent of thy pleasure, and make my heart drunken with the sober drunkenness of thy love, that I may forget the things which are vain and earthly, and have thee alone continually in my remembrance. Give me thy Holy Ghost, whom those waters signified which thou hadst promised to give unto them that thirst. Grant, I beseech thee, that with my whole desire and with all diligence I may press toward that place whither we believe thee to have ascended on the fortieth day after thy resurrection, that in this present misery I may be held by the body only, but in thought may be ever with thee; that where thou art, my treasure, desirable and incomparable and greatly to be loved, there my heart may be also; that I may utter praise to thy name from this time forth and even for ever and ever, Amen.

## Jerome, in his Agony
*Hieronymus in agone*

{p.124} Thou, good Jesus, didst for me undergo in thy body so great a punishment of the cross, that thou mightest destroy the death which I had incurred by my shameful deeds; that thou mightest recover the souls of them of old, which wretched souls the power of the devil had long held in the mansions of hell; that thou mightest call back the whole nature of man, which had fallen into everlasting death, unto life without end, thy Father being reconciled with it, by thy precious blood which thou didst shed for it. Of which life indeed, rising the third day from that pit wherein thou didst lie dead, thou gavest most firm assurance; whereby indeed our faith is made strong and our hope increased, that as thou didst rise incorruptible and impassible and immortal, so likewise shall we also rise. Thou verily, gracious and good Lord, after thy wonderful and singular resurrection, when forty days were fulfilled, wherein by the clear proof of manifold tokens thou didst declare that thou wast risen alive from the dead, and that no darkness of doubt might arise, didst ascend into the heavens by thine own power in the sight of all thy disciples, and, opening unto me the gates of Paradise, didst sit down at the right hand of God the Father almighty, {p.125} where thou sittest without end, and reignest with the Father and the Holy Ghost for ever and ever, Amen.

# VIII. Forms of Prayer of Those Who Repent
*Formulae precandi poenitentiam agentium*

## Augustine, Meditations, chapter 8

Almighty God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, have mercy upon me, and forgive me all my trespasses, for the sake of thy Son, the mediator between me and thee. For of myself I was able, {p.138} holy Father, to offend thee, but of myself I had no power to appease thee. Thy beloved Son, O my God, became my helper, partaking of my manhood that he might heal its infirmity; so that whence the cause of offence had arisen, thence he might offer unto thee the sacrifice of praise, and thereby render me acceptable to thy lovingkindness, in that, sitting at thy right hand, he might ever shew himself to be a partaker of my substance. Behold my hope; behold all my confidence. If thou despisest me, as is meet, for mine iniquity, yet look upon me in mercy for the charity of thy beloved offspring. Regard in the Son that for which thou mayest be gracious to the servant; behold the mystery of the flesh, and remit the guilt of the flesh. As often as the wounds of thy blessed offspring lie open before thee, let my wicked deeds, I beseech thee, be hidden. As often as the precious blood is red from his holy side, let the stain of my defilement, I beseech thee, be washed away. And because flesh provoked thee to anger, let flesh, I implore thee, move thee to mercy; and as flesh led me astray into guilt, so let flesh lead me on to pardon. For much it is that mine iniquity deserveth, but far more is that which the lovingkindness of my Redeemer by right demandeth. Great is mine unrighteousness, but greater by far is the righteousness of my Redeemer. For by how much God is higher than man, by so much is my wickedness less than his goodness, both in quantity and in quality. {p.139} For what trespass hath man committed which the Son of God, made man, hath not redeemed? What pride could swell so high that so great humility would not lay it low? What dominion of death is so great that the punishment of the cross of the Son of God would not destroy it? Truly, O my God, if the trespasses of sinful man and the grace of the redeeming Author be weighed in an even balance, they are parted not only as far as the east is from the west, but as far as the lowest hell is sundered from the highest pole of heaven. Now, O best Creator of light, now pardon my faults, for the sake of the boundless labours of thy beloved Son. Now, I beseech thee, let mine ungodliness be forgiven to his godliness, my perverseness to his modesty, and my fierceness to his meekness. Now let his humility gain over my pride, his patience mine impatience, his kindness my hardness, his obedience my disobedience, his calmness my disquiet, his sweetness my bitterness, his gentleness mine anger, his charity my cruelty. Through the same thy Son, our Lord and Saviour, who reigneth and liveth with thee in the unity of the Holy Ghost, God, world without end, Amen.

## Augustine, Meditations, chapters 13 and 14

Eternal God, most merciful Father, great and many are my {p.140} sicknesses; for the prince of this world hath many things in me, I know and confess it; but I pray thee, deliver me through our Redeemer who sitteth at thy right hand, in whom he could find nothing of his own evil. Justify me through him who did no sin, neither was guile found in his mouth; through him, our Head, in whom is no spot, deliver his member, small and weak though it be; deliver me, I beseech thee, from my sins, vices, faults and negligences. I might indeed have despaired because of my exceeding great sins and mine infirmities, had not thy Word, O God, been made flesh, and dwelt among us. But now I dare not despair; for when we were enemies, we were reconciled by the death of thy Son: how much more, being reconciled, have we been saved by him. For all my hope and the assurance of all my confidence is in his precious blood, which was shed for us and for our salvation. In him I breathe again, and trusting in him I long to come unto thee, not having mine own righteousness, but that which is of thy Son, our Lord Jesus Christ. With whom be praise and glory unto thee, together also with the Comforter, for ever and ever, Amen.

## Augustine, Meditations, chapter 39

{p.141} O Lord Jesus Christ, Son of the living God, who with thy hands stretched out upon the cross didst drink the cup of the passion for the redemption of all mortal men, be merciful to me a miserable sinner. Behold, I come poor to thee who art rich, wretched to thee who art merciful. I confess mine unrighteousness against myself. Behold, O Lord, I was conceived and born in sins, and thou didst wash me and sanctify me, and I afterward defiled myself with greater sins. For I was born in sins of necessity, and afterward I wallowed in sins of mine own will; but thou, O Lord, not unmindful of thy compassion, didst take me from the house of my father after the flesh, and from the tabernacle of sinners. But I, unthankful for so many benefits, have committed many unlawful things, have wrought many abominable things, and where I ought to have amended my sins, I have added sins to sins. These are mine evils, O Lord, wherewith I have dishonoured thee and stained myself, whom thou didst create after thine image and likeness: pride, vainglory, and very many other evils, whereby my unhappy soul is vexed and afflicted, torn and destroyed. Behold, O Lord, mine iniquities are gone over mine head: as an heavy burden they are too heavy for me. And unless thou, whose property is always to have mercy and to spare, put under me the right hand of thy Majesty, {p.142} I am constrained to sink miserably into the deep. But return, O Lord, and deliver my soul: O save me for thy mercy's sake. Have mercy on thy son, whom thou didst bring forth with no small pain, and regard not mine evil so as to forget thine own goodness. What father is there who would not deliver his son? Or what son is there whom his father chasteneth not with the rod of fatherly love? Therefore, O Father and Lord, though I be a sinner, I cannot but be thy son, because thou hast made me and made me anew. Can a mother forget the infant of her womb? And surely, though she should forget, thou, O Father, hast promised that thou wilt not forget. Be appeased therefore, I beseech thee, and have mercy upon me, and turn not thy face away from me, who, to redeem me, didst not turn away thy face from them that rebuked thee and spat upon thee. I confess that I have sinned, and my conscience deserveth damnation; but certain it is that thy mercy surpasseth every offence. Write not, I beseech thee, most gracious Lord, my bitter things against me, so as to enter into judgment with thy servant; but according to the multitude of thy tender mercies blot out mine iniquity. Have mercy, lest I despair, but that in my despairing I may breathe again. Although I have committed that for which thou canst condemn me, thou hast not lost that whereby thou art wont to save. Thou, O Lord, willest not {p.143} the death of sinners, neither rejoicest thou in the destruction of them that die; nay, that the dead might live, thou didst die, and thy death slew the death of sinners. And if they lived when thou wast dying, I beseech thee, O LORD, that I may not die now that thou livest. Behold, sheltered under the shadow of thy mercy, I run to the throne of grace asking pardon, crying and knocking, until thou have mercy upon me. Remember not, most sweet Jesus, thy justice against thy sinner, but be mindful of thy kindness toward thy creature. Remember not wrath against the guilty, but be mindful of thy compassion toward the wretched; forget the proud man who provoketh thee, and look upon the wretched man who calleth upon thee. Who livest and reignest for ever, Amen.

## Augustine, Meditations, chapter 41

Thy goodness, O Lord, created me; thy mercy cleansed me, when created, from original sin; thy patience, after that cleansing of Baptism, hath borne with me, nourished me and waited for me, though I was wrapped in other filth of sins. O most kind Lover of men, the poor is left unto thee; thou wilt be the helper of the fatherless. Have mercy on me, a forsaken orphan; I am become fatherless, without a father, {p.144} my soul is as a widow. Look upon the tears of my bereavement and widowhood; appear unto me, and I shall be comforted; shew me thy presence, and I shall have obtained my desire; reveal thy glory, and my joy shall be full; that I may glorify thee with the Father and the Holy Ghost, world without end, Amen.

## Augustine, Soliloquies, chapter 2

Thy hands, O Lord, have made me and fashioned me; those hands, I say, which were fastened with nails for me. Despise not, O Lord, the work of thine own hands; look, I pray thee, upon the wounds of thy hands. Behold, O Lord God, thou hast graven me upon thy hands: read that writing, and save me. Lo, I thy creature sigh unto thee: thou art the Creator, create me anew. Lo, I thy handiwork cry unto thee: thou art the Life, quicken me. Lo, I, the thing which thou hast formed, look unto thee: thou art he that formed me, restore me. Spare me, O LORD, for my days are nothing. I am sick, I cry to the Physician; I am blind, I hasten to the Light; I am dead, I sigh for the Life. Thou art the Physician, thou the Light, thou the Life. Jesus of Nazareth, have mercy on me; thou Son of David, have mercy on me, thou fountain of mercy. Hear the sick man who crieth unto thee. O Light that passest by, {p.145} wait for the blind man; reach him thy hand, that he may come unto thee, and in thy light see light, Amen.

## Augustine, Soliloquies, chapter 5

I confess unto thee, O Father, Lord of heaven and earth, my misery, that I may be suffered to come unto thy mercy. For I am become wretched, and am brought to nothing; mine iniquities have wounded me, and have brought me to nothing. Mine enemies have done unto me whatsoever they would: they have smitten me, stripped me, defiled me, corrupted me, wounded me and slain me. Alas, O Lord, my life, who hast made me; my light, who hast guided me; defender of my life, have mercy upon me, and raise me up again. O Lord my GOD, my hope, my power, my strength, my consolation in the day of my trouble, thunder, O Lord, from above with a great and mighty voice into the inward ear of my heart; teach me and save me, and I will praise thee, who didst create me when I was nothing, who didst raise me up when I was dead, who hast fed me from my youth with all thy good things; this unprofitable worm, stinking with sins, thou nourishest with all thy best gifts. I beseech thee, forsake me not. I have sinned {p.146} a great sin, and am conscious to myself of many offences. O how bitter and evil a thing is sin! O ye sins, how easy an entrance ye have while ye persuade, how hard a going out ye have when ye have laid hold; but after ye have persuaded, ye sting even unto the death of the soul. Often, when I begin to speak with any man without intent of any evil, straightway I fall into slander; or, speaking unadvisedly of some matter, I swear; or I yield foolishly to the vice of curiosity; or I suffer in mine own inward parts the unlawful and unchaste motions of evil thoughts or of uncleanness. While, forgetting those things which are behind, I would reach forth unto those things which are before, suddenly, by I know not what wretchedness, I perceive myself slipping back to things vain and hurtful; or carnal delight pierceth my mind, and instead of the sweet savour of virtues I smell the intolerable and most foul stench of inward vices. Ah, most sweet Lord Jesus, before thee I spread out all mine iniquities which I bear within, whereby my unhappy soul is ensnared and deceived and afflicted. But thou, good Lord Jesus, why camest thou from heaven? What didst thou do in the world? To what end didst thou give thyself to death, but that thou mightest save sinners, of whom I am chief? Therefore, O Jesus, for thy sweet name's sake, do unto me {p.147} according to thy name. O Jesus, forget the proud man who provoketh thee; look graciously upon the wretched man who calleth upon thee. O Jesus, thy name is a sweet name, a delightful name, a name that strengtheneth the sinner, and a name of blessed hope. For what is Jesus but Saviour? Therefore, for thine own sake, O Jesus, be unto me Jesus, be unto me a kind Saviour. Regard not, O Lord, mine evil so as to forget thine own goodness. Spare me therefore, O Lord, who art the true salvation, and who willest not the death of a sinner; have mercy, O Lord, upon my sinful soul; loose its bonds, heal its wounds, deliver it, O Lord; unburden it, overwhelmed as it is with the weight of sins; loose it, bound with the cords of offences; set it free, entangled in crimes; raise it up where it lieth, and uphold it where it tottereth. Good Jesus, behold my low estate, and wash away all my offences; be thou my helper; leave me not, neither despise me, O God of my salvation, for thy holy name's sake, Amen.

## Augustine, On Contrition, chapter 11
*Augustinus de contritione*

Most just and most kind God, remember that thou art merciful, and art my Redeemer. If thou art a Judge to judge thy guilty one, thou art also GOD to defend thy {p.148} servant, and thou art the Creator to preserve thy work, and thou art God to save him that believeth in thee and is baptized unto thee. Remember not therefore, O LORD, thy justice alone against thy sinner, but be mindful of thy kindness toward thy creature. Remember not thy wrath against thy guilty one, but be mindful of thy compassion toward thy wretched one. Spare me therefore, O Lord, who art my salvation, who willest not the death of a sinner. Spare therefore my sinful soul. Thou art my God; in thee I put my hope, and to thee I commit my soul. O merciful God, have mercy upon me, and have mercy upon thy lowly creature. Deal with me according to thy mercy, who art blessed for ever, Amen.

## Bernard, On the Inner House, chapters 31 and 33
*Bernhardus de interiori domo*[^3]

Most gracious Lord, whence can I be good, who in the midst of good have been evil? I sinned, and thou didst wink at it; I prolonged mine iniquity for a long time, and thou thy lovingkindness, who wast purposing repentance and pardon. Grant mercy therefore to the wretched, thou who hast so long spared the guilty. For I believe that whatsoever thou shalt determine to forgive me shall be as though it had never been. {p.149} My whole life affrighteth me, O my God; for when it is diligently examined, it appeareth to me either sin or barrenness; and if any fruit is seen in it, it is so feigned, or imperfect, or in some way corrupted, that it may either fail to please thee or may displease thee. Wherefore, trembling at the multitude of mine iniquities, yet trusting in thy lovingkindness, I confess unto thee, my Creator and Redeemer, who hast promised pardon after guilt, that I was conceived in sins, and nourished in sins, and through all the time of my life unto this day have had my conversation in sins. I find no sin wherewith I am not in some way defiled. So is my soul filled with evils, as my sins deserve; so, O my God, hath my life drawn nigh unto hell. If thou wilt deliver me, I have cause to give thee thanks; if thou wilt not deliver me thence, I have no cause to reproach thee, for thou art just. Woe is me, what manner of man have I lived! what great evils have I done and said! I am ashamed that I have so lived. My conscience deserveth damnation; but certain it is that thy mercy blotteth out every offence. Blot out therefore, gracious Lord, mine iniquity in the multitude of thy tender mercies, through the merit of thy most bitter passion and death, Amen.

## Bernard, the same work, chapter 34
*Bernhardus ibidem*

{p.150} Alas, wretch that I am, often have I confessed and risen; falling, I am wearied out, so often sinning again, so often trusting. Many a time have I promised to amend, and never have I kept it, but have always returned to sin, and to my former wickednesses have joined new and worse. Behold, most merciful God, I hide not my sins, but shew them; I accuse myself, I excuse not myself, for I acknowledge mine iniquity. I might indeed have despaired because of my exceeding great sins, my vices and my numberless negligences, which I have committed and daily without ceasing do commit, in heart, in mouth and in deed, and in all the ways in which human frailty can sin, had not thy Word, O God, been made flesh, and dwelt among us. But now I dare not despair, because he, being made subject unto thee even unto death, yea the death of the cross, took away the handwriting of our sins, and nailing it to the cross, crucified sin and death. Therefore by the death of thine only begotten Son I beseech thee to blot out all mine iniquities, and to be merciful to me, a most miserable sinner; that being delivered from sins, and justified before thee by the righteousness of thy Son, I may utter praise to thy name, and may glorify thee {p.151} with the same thy Son and the Holy Ghost, from this time forth and for evermore, Amen.

## Jerome, in his Agony
*Hieronymus in agone*

My soul is weary of my life; I will speak in the bitterness of my soul. I am sick; my life is weakened in poverty; my bones are burned as in a frying pan. And therefore I run unto thee, O LORD, the Physician. Heal me, O Lord, and I shall be healed; save me, and I shall be saved; and because I trust in thee, I shall not be ashamed. But who am I, most gracious GOD, that I should speak so boldly unto thee? I am a sinner, wholly born in sins, and begotten and brought up in them, a rotten carcase, a stinking vessel, the food of worms. Woe is me, O Lord; spare me. What victory were it, if in fighting with me thou shouldest overcome me, who am less than stubble before the face of the wind? Forgive all my sins, and lift up the poor out of the dunghill. Surely, O Lord, if it please thee, I will say: Thou oughtest not to flee from him that runneth unto thee, for thou art my God, thy flesh is of my flesh, and thy bones of my bones. For to this end, not leaving the right hand of the Father, thou didst cleave unto my manhood, being made God and Man, and remaining what thou wast before, in one and the same person. And {p.152} why didst thou accomplish this thing, so hard and so unlooked for, but that I might with confidence run back unto thee as unto a brother, and that thou mightest in mercy bestow upon me thy Godhead? Wherefore arise and help me, O Lord; arise, and cast me not off for ever. As the hart panteth after the water brooks, so my thirsting soul seeketh thee, the living fountain, that it may draw water with joy out of the wells of the Saviour, and thirst no more, when it shall come and appear before thy face. Come, thou joy of my spirit, that I may delight in thee. Reveal unto me thy mercy, the gladness of my heart. Let me find thee, my desire. Let my supplication come before thee, O Lord, that thy hand may be upon me to save me. Behold the man who, going down to Jericho, was taken by thieves: I am wounded, I am left half dead; do thou, O gracious Samaritan, take me up. I have sinned exceedingly in my life, and done evil in thy sight; from the sole of the foot even unto the crown of the head there is no soundness in me. Surely, hadst thou not helped me by dying on the tree of the cross, my soul had been worthy to dwell in hell. I, gracious Jesus, am a part of that so great price; for me thou didst sweat precious blood; reject me not. I am the sheep that went astray; seek it, good Shepherd, and add it to thy fold, that thou mayest be justified in thy sayings; for thou hast {p.153} promised me that in whatsoever hour the sinner shall groan, he shall be saved. I am become sorrowful; I acknowledge mine iniquities, and my trespasses are before me. Truly I am not worthy to be called thy son, for I have sinned against heaven and before thee. Make me to hear joy and gladness. Hide thy face from my sins. Blot out mine iniquities according to thy great mercy. Cast me not away from thy presence. Deal not with me after my sins, nor reward me according to mine iniquities; but help me, O God of my salvation, and for the honour of thy name deliver me. Do good in thy good pleasure, that I may dwell in thy house all the days of my life, and for ever and ever praise thee with them that dwell therein, Amen.

## Cyprian

Most merciful Master, Jesus Christ, how often have I transgressed the rule of thy doctrine, how often, O holy Lord, have I despised thy commandments! And when thou saidst unto me, Return, I returned not; when thou didst threaten, I feared not; when thou wast good and gentle, I was provoking; beyond seventy times seven have I sinned against heaven and before thee. Who shall wash away so much filth? Who shall scrape off the dung {p.154} that is heaped together? Whatsoever Peter may say, it must needs be that thou thyself wash us; for we cannot wash ourselves, but in all things that we do we need the indulgence of thy washing. Judas the traitor was admitted to partake of thy table, but being shut out from this saving washing, he could not be washed at the last, who defiled the honour of his Apostleship with detestable covetousness. But forasmuch as with thee is the fountain of life, and the infinite depth of the tender mercies which have been ever of old, those whom thou hast cleansed in Baptism, whom thou hast washed with thy blood, whom thou ever washest by forgiving their daily sins, translate from the marshes of this world and from the mire of this present life to the most pleasant kingdom of thy glory, where is no scab nor blearness of eyes, where no man suffereth an issue of blood, no man is unclean, where there is no more need of any washing, our body being sealed with the body of thy glory, according to thy promises, which thou must needs fulfil; and do thou perfect this good work which thou hast begun in us, and stablish it, through Christ, our only Lord and Saviour, Amen.

## The Church

O most high Maker of all things, when I consider with myself how great a Majesty I have offended by my sins, I shudder at mine own rashness; {p.155} when I consider how kind and wonderful a Father I have forsaken, I abhor mine unthankfulness; when I perceive from how blessed a liberty of spirit into how wretched a bondage I have cast myself, I condemn my madness, and am wholly displeasing to myself, and nothing else is before mine eyes but hell and despair, since thine inevitable justice terrifieth our conscience. But on the other side, when I behold that boundless mercy of thine, which, according to the witness of the Prophet, surpasseth all thy works, and whereby thou art in a manner greater than thyself, though thou art in every way the greatest of all, a certain more cheerful breath of hope shineth upon me and reviveth my soul. For why should I despair of the pardon of sins from him who in the writings of the Prophets so often of his own accord inviteth sinners to repentance, crying that he willeth not the death of a sinner, but rather that he should turn and live? Moreover, how ready pardon is with him for them that repent, thine only begotten Son hath impressed upon us by many parables, as of the piece of silver lost and found, of the sheep brought back from its wandering upon the shoulders, but most plainly of the prodigal son, whose likeness I wholly acknowledge in myself. I wickedly forsook a most loving Father; I basely wasted all his substance; and while I obeyed the lusts of the flesh, {p.156} forgetful of thy commandments, I put upon myself the most shameful bondage of vices, and am brought to utter want; neither do I see whither I may flee, but unto him from whom I fled away. Let thy mercy receive the suppliant whom hitherto thou hast gently borne with. I am not worthy to lift up mine eyes unto thee, or to call thee by the name of Father; but do thou, I beseech thee, vouchsafe to turn thine eyes upon me. For thy look maketh the dead sinner to live again, and the lost to come to himself. For this very thing, that I am now displeasing to myself, I owe to thy regard: thou didst vouchsafe to look upon me as I wandered far from thee, and gavest me back mine eyes, that I might see in how great evils I was; thou didst meet me, breathing into me the remembrance of, and the longing for, the innocence which I had forsaken. I ask not the embrace and the kiss, being a servant worthy of every punishment; I require not the robe and the ring, the tokens of my former dignity, which I cast away. I pray not that thou wouldest receive me into the honour of thy sons; it shall be abundantly great if thou admit me among thy meanest servants of the lowest condition, the branded ones, and those taken back from flight, that at least I may cleave to some part of thy household, seeing that in thy house are many mansions. I am not loth to be reckoned in this life among the outcast, so that I be not separated from thee for ever. I beseech thee, and {p.157} adjure thee by the death of thine only begotten and beloved Son, impart unto me thy Spirit, that he may purify my heart, and strengthen it by his grace, lest through my heedlessness I slip back thither whence I have been called back by thy mercy, Amen.

## A Prayer Gathered out of the Psalms of David, for the Remission of Sins
*Precatio ex Psalmis Davidis collecta, pro remissione peccatorum*

Give ear to my words, O Lord, consider my cry.

Hearken unto the voice of my prayer, my King, and my God: have mercy, and hear my prayer.

In the multitude of thy mercy I will come into thy house: I will worship toward thy holy temple.

O Lord, rebuke me not in thy wrath: neither chasten me in thy hot displeasure.

For thine arrows stick fast in me, and thy hand presseth me sore.

There is no soundness in my flesh because of thine anger; neither is there any rest in my bones because of my sins.

For mine iniquities are gone over mine head: as an heavy burden they are too heavy for me.

Have mercy upon me according to thy great {p.158} mercy: and according unto the multitude of thy tender mercies blot out mine iniquity.

Wash me throughly from mine iniquity, and cleanse me from my sin.

For I acknowledge mine iniquity: and my sin is ever before me.

Against thee only have I sinned, and done evil in thy sight: that thou mightest be justified in thy sayings, and mightest overcome when thou art judged.

Behold, I was shapen in iniquity; and in sin did my mother conceive me.

Hide thy face from my sins, and blot out all mine iniquities.

Create in me a clean heart, O God; and renew a right spirit within me.

Cast me not away from thy presence; and take not thy Holy Spirit from me.

Restore unto me the joy of thy salvation; and uphold me with thy free spirit.

For thou, Lord, art good, and ready to forgive; and plenteous in mercy unto all them that call upon thee.

Thou, O Lord, art a God full of compassion, and gracious, longsuffering, and plenteous in mercy and truth.

I have gone astray like a lost sheep; seek thy {p.159} servant, O Lord; for I do not forget thy commandments.

Enter not into judgment with thy servant: for in thy sight shall no man living be justified.

Who can understand his errors? cleanse thou me from my secret faults, and spare thy servant from the sins of others.

Remember, O Lord, thy tender mercies and thy lovingkindnesses; for they have been ever of old.

Remember not the sins of my youth, nor my transgressions, O Lord.

According to thy mercy remember thou me for thy goodness' sake, O LORD.

For thy name's sake, O Lord, pardon my sin; for it is great.

Shew thy marvellous lovingkindness, O thou that savest them which put their trust in thee.

Hide not thy face from me; put not thy servant away in anger.

For in death there is no remembrance of thee: in the grave who shall give thee thanks?

What profit is there in my blood, when I go down to corruption? Shall the dust praise thee? shall it declare thy truth?

{p.160} Consider and hear me, O my God: lighten mine eyes, lest I sleep the sleep of death.

Withhold not thou thy tender mercies from me: let thy lovingkindness and thy truth continually preserve me.

In thee, O Lord, do I put my trust; let me never be ashamed: deliver me in thy righteousness. I said, Thou art my God.

Make thy face to shine upon thy servant: save me for thy mercy's sake.

Let me not be ashamed, O Lord; for I have called upon thee.

Forsake me not, O Lord my God: be not far from me.

Make haste to help me, O Lord my salvation.

Why art thou cast down, O my soul? and why art thou disquieted in me? hope thou in God: for I shall yet praise him for the help of his countenance.

The Lord is merciful and gracious, slow to anger, and plenteous in mercy.

He will not always chide: neither will he keep his anger for ever.

For as the heaven is high above the earth, so great is his mercy toward them that fear him.

As far as the east is from the west, so far hath he removed our iniquities from us.

{p.161} Like as a father pitieth his children, so the Lord pitieth them that fear him.

For he knoweth our frame; he remembereth that we are dust.

I wait for the Lord, my soul doth wait, and in his word do I hope.

My soul waiteth for the Lord from one morning watch even unto another morning watch.

Let Israel hope in the Lord: for with the Lord there is mercy, and with him is plenteous redemption.

And he shall redeem Israel from all his iniquities.

The Lord is good to all: and his tender mercies are over all his works.

## Hymn
*Christe qui es creator et redemtor*[^4]

| O Christ, who art Creator and Redeemer, the same also Saviour

| of heaven, earth and sea, of angels and of men,

| whom alone we confess to be God and man,

| who camest to save sinners,

| without sin taking upon thee the form of sin;

| out of whose flock, as thou didst the woman of Canaan, thou didst visit Mary Magdalene,

| {p.162} at the same table of the divine Word refreshing her with wondrous cups.

| In the house of Simon the leper, sitting at the figurative feast,

| the Pharisee murmureth, where the woman weepeth, conscious of her guilt.

| The sinner despiseth his fellow sinner; thou, who knowest no sin, hearest the penitent, cleansest her, lovest her being foul, that thou mayest make her fair.

| She embraceth the Lord's feet, washeth them with tears, wipeth them with her hair; washing, wiping, anointing she anointed them, she covered them round with kisses.[^5]

| These are the feasts that please thee, O Wisdom of the Father,

| born of a virgin, who didst not disdain to be touched by a sinful woman.

| By the Pharisee thou wast bidden; with Mary's dishes thou wast filled.

| Much thou forgivest to her that loveth much, and that returneth not afterward to her sin.

| Cleansing her from seven devils by the sevenfold Spirit,

| thou grantest her to see thee rising from the dead before all others.

| By her, O Christ, thou dost signify the proselyte Church, whom, a stranger, thou callest to the children's table;

| whom, between the feasts of the law and of grace, the {p.163} Pharisee's pride despiseth, and the leprosy of heresy vexeth.

| What manner of woman she is thou knowest; she toucheth thee because she is a sinner, because she desireth pardon.

| For what would the sick woman have, had she not received it, were the Physician not at hand?

| King of kings, rich unto all, save us sinners, wiping away all our offences, thou hope and glory of the saints.

## Thanksgiving for Remission of Sins Obtained, Gathered out of the Psalms
*Gratiarum actio, pro consecuta remissione peccatorum, ex Psalmis collecta*

Blessed are they whose iniquities are forgiven, and whose sins are covered.

Blessed is the man unto whom the Lord imputeth not sin, and in whose spirit there is no guile.

Rejoice in the Lord, O ye righteous: for praise is comely for the upright.

O taste and see that the LORD is good: blessed is the man that trusteth in him.

Bless the LORD, O my soul: and all that is within me, bless his holy name.

Bless the Lord, O my soul, and forget not all his benefits:

Who forgiveth all thine iniquities; who healeth all thy diseases;

{p.164} Who redeemeth thy life from destruction; who crowneth thee with lovingkindness and tender mercies;

Who satisfieth thy mouth with good things; so that thy youth is renewed like the eagle's.

The Lord is merciful and gracious, slow to anger, and plenteous in mercy.

He hath not dealt with us after our sins; nor rewarded us according to our iniquities.

For as the heaven is high above the earth, so great is his mercy toward them that fear him.

As far as the east is from the west, so far hath he removed our iniquities from us.

Like as a father pitieth his children, so the Lord pitieth them that fear him.

For he knoweth our frame; he remembereth that we are dust.

Return unto thy rest, O my soul; for the Lord hath dealt bountifully with thee.

For he hath delivered my soul from death, mine eyes from tears, and my feet from falling.

Goodness and mercy shall follow me all the days of my life: and I will dwell in the house of the Lord for ever.

Blessed be the Lord God of Israel from everlasting, and to everlasting.
