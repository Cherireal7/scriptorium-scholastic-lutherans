# III. To the Son
*Ad Filium*

## Augustine, Meditations, chapters 7 and 8

O Lord Jesus Christ, Son of the living God, my most merciful Redeemer and Deliverer, I pray thee by thy mercies, that nothing without thee may grow sweet unto me, nothing please me, nothing precious, nothing beautiful beside {p.37} thee smile upon me; let all things, I beseech thee, grow vile unto me apart from thee, let all things be foul; let that which is contrary to thee be grievous unto me, and thy good pleasure my unfailing desire. Let it weary me to rejoice without thee, and delight me to be made sorrowful for thee. Let thy name be my refreshment, and the remembrance of thee my consolation. I ask thee, my hope, by all thy mercies, that thou be merciful unto mine ungodlinesses: Open mine ears to thy commandments, and incline not (I pray by thy holy name) my heart to words of wickedness, to make excuses in sins. I ask also by thy wonderful humility, that the foot of pride come not against me, and let not the hand of the sinner remove me. My hope, O Christ, O God, thou sweet lover of men, light, way, life, and salvation, I beseech, I supplicate and ask thee, that by thee I may walk, unto thee I may come, in thee I may rest. For thou knowest, O God, knower of hidden things, that not only than the earth, and all things that are therein, art thou dearer unto me, but also than heaven itself, and all things that are therein, thou art more acceptable unto me, thou art more lovely unto me. For I love thee above heaven and earth, and all other things that are in them. I love thee, my God, with a great love, and desire to love thee more; grant me that I may love thee always as much as {p.38} I will, as much as I ought, that thou alone mayest be my whole intent, and all my meditation; let me meditate on thee through the days without ceasing, let me feel thee in sleep by night, let my spirit speak unto thee, let my mind converse with thee, let my heart be enlightened with the light of thy holy vision, that with thee for guide, with thee for ruler, I may walk from strength to strength, and at length may see thee, the God of gods in Sion, now indeed through a glass, darkly, but then face to face. Amen.

## Augustine, Meditations, chapter 35

O King, Christ, Redeemer, I ask thee by all thy mercies, whereby we have been delivered from eternal death, soften my heart, hard and stony, rocky and of iron, with thy most sacred and mighty anointing, and make me through the fire of compunction to become before thee at every hour a living sacrifice; make me in thy sight to have always a contrite and humbled heart with abundance of tears; make me out of desire of thee to be utterly dead unto this world, and to forget the things that pass away, for the greatness of thy fear and love, even so far, that concerning temporal things I neither mourn, nor rejoice, nor fear anything {p.39} temporal, nor love it, neither be corrupted by things pleasant, nor shaken by things adverse: And because thy love is strong as death, let the fiery and honey-flowing power of thy love, I beseech thee, swallow up my mind from all things that are under heaven, that I may cleave unto thee alone, and be fed by the remembrance of thy sweetness alone; fill, I beseech thee, my heart always with thine unquenchable love, with continual remembrance of thee, so that as a burning flame I may wholly burn in the sweetness of thy love; make me, sweetest Lord, to love thee, and through desire of thee to lay down the weight of all carnal desires, and the most heavy burden of earthly lusts, which fight against and weigh down my wretched soul, that running unhindered after thee in the savour of thine ointments, I may be found worthy, with thee for guide, to come even unto the vision of thy beauty, there to be effectually satisfied. Hear, my GOD, hear, O light of mine eyes, gracious and most merciful LORD, become not thou inexorable unto me because of my sins, but for thy goodness’ sake receive the prayers of thy servant. Amen.

## Augustine, Meditations, chapter 36

O Christ, Lord, Word {p.40} of the Father, who camest into this world to save sinners, I ask thee by the most indulgent bowels of thy mercy, amend my life, better my deeds, order my manners, take from me that which hurteth me and displeaseth thee, and give that which thou knowest to please thee and to profit me. Who can make clean that which is conceived of unclean seed, but thou alone? Thou art GOD almighty, of infinite lovingkindness, who justifiest the ungodly, and quickenest them that are dead because of sin, who changest sinners, and they are not. Take therefore from me whatsoever displeaseth thee in me, for thine eyes see my very many imperfections; put forth, I beseech thee, the hand of thy lovingkindness upon me, and take from me whatsoever offendeth the eyes of thy lovingkindness in me. Before thee, O Lord, is my health and mine infirmity; the one, I pray thee, preserve, the other heal. Heal me, O Lord, and I shall be healed; save me, and I shall be saved. O sweetest and kindest Christ, pour, I beseech thee, the multitude of thy sweetness and of thy charity into my breast, that I may desire or think upon nothing earthly, nothing carnal, but may love thee alone, and have thee alone in my heart and in my mouth. Write with thy finger in my breast the sweet remembrance of thy honey-flowing name, never to be blotted out by any forgetfulness. Write upon the tables of {p.41} my heart thy will and thy statutes, that I may have thee, the Lord of immeasurable sweetness, and thy precepts always and everywhere before mine eyes. Kindle my mind with that fire of thine, which thou didst send upon the earth, and didst will that it should be vehemently kindled, that I may daily offer unto thee, with tears springing forth, the sacrifice of a troubled spirit and of a contrite heart. Give me therefore the grace of tears, out of the great sweetness of thy love, and the remembrance of thy mercies. Prepare this table for thy servant in thy sight, and give it into my power, that as often as I will I may be filled from it. Grant of thy lovingkindness and goodness, that this thy cup, which inebriateth and is most excellent, may satisfy my thirst, that my spirit may pant after thee, and my mind may burn in thy love, forgetful of vanity and misery. Hear, my God, hear, O light of mine eyes, hear the things which I ask. Amen.

## Augustine, Meditations, chapter 37

O Jesus, Lord, gracious Jesus, good Jesus, who didst vouchsafe to die for our sins, and didst rise again for our justification, I ask thee by those saving wounds of thine, which thou didst suffer upon the cross for our salvation, out of which flowed that precious blood {p.42} whereby we are redeemed, wound this my sinful soul, for which also thou didst vouchsafe to die; wound it with the fiery and most mighty dart of thine exceeding charity. For the word of God is quick, and powerful, and sharper than any twoedged sword. Thou, the chosen arrow, and the sharpest sword, who art able by thy power to pierce the hard shield of the heart of man, transfix my heart with the javelin of thy love, that my soul may say unto thee, I am wounded with thy charity, so that out of that very wound of thy love most plenteous tears may flow day and night. Smite, O Lord, smite, I beseech thee, this my most hard mind with the gracious and strong point of thy love, and pierce yet deeper unto the inmost parts with mighty power. And so bring forth out of my head water without measure, and out of mine eyes a true fountain of tears flowing continually, from exceeding affection and desire of the vision of thy beauty, that I may mourn day and night, receiving no consolation in this present life, until I be found worthy to see thee in the heavenly bridechamber, my beloved and most beautiful Bridegroom, my God and my Lord, that there, seeing thy glorious, wonderful and most beautiful face, full of all sweetness, together with them whom thou hast chosen, I may humbly adore thy Majesty, and there at length, filled with the heavenly and unspeakable {p.43} jubilation of eternal exultation, may cry out with them that love thee, saying: Behold, that which I longed for I now see, that which I hoped for I now hold, that which I desired I now have. For I am joined in heaven unto him whom, while I was set upon earth, I loved with all my strength, embraced with all my charity, unto whom I clave with all my love; him I praise, bless and adore, who liveth and reigneth, God, world without end. Amen.

## Augustine, Soliloquies, chapters 13 and 15

O Jesus Christ, Son of the living God, I beseech thee by thine own self, forsake me not: thou didst redeem me, when I had perished: I had perished, I was dead; unto the dead thou didst come down, thou tookest mortality upon thee; a King, thou didst come down unto a servant, that thou mightest redeem the servant; thou gavest up thyself, that I might live; thou didst undergo death, thou didst overcome death, thou didst restore me when thou didst humble thyself: I had perished, I had gone away in sins, I was sold; thou camest for me, that thou mightest redeem me, and so greatly didst thou love me, that thou gavest thy blood as the price for me; thou lovedst me, O Lord, more than thyself, because thou wast willing to die for me. Upon such terms, at so dear a price thou broughtest me back from exile, thou redeemedst me from bondage, thou drewest me back from punishment, thou calledst me {p.44} by thy name, thou sealedst me with thy blood, that thy memorial might be always with me, and that he might never depart from my heart, who for my sake departed not from the cross. Thou hast anointed me with thine oil, wherewith thou wast anointed, that from thee, the Christ, I might be called Christian. Unto thee alone therefore be glory, whose is all good, but unto us confusion of face and misery, whose is all evil, except thou be pleased to have mercy. But thou hast mercy, O Lord, thou hast mercy upon all, and hatest nothing of the things which thou hast made, and givest us of thy good things, and enrichest us that are needy, O Lord God, with thy best gifts, because thou lovest the poor, and makest them rich with thy riches. Behold now, O Lord, we that are poor are thy children, and thy little flock; open unto us thy gates, and the poor shall eat and be satisfied, and they shall praise thee that seek thee. My face hath sought thee; thy face, Lord, will I seek, the face of the Lord of hosts, wherein alone abideth all the everlasting glory of the blessed, which to see is indeed life eternal, and the everlasting glory of the saints. Shew us therefore thy face, O Lord, and we shall be saved, who livest and reignest with the Father and the Holy Ghost unto everlasting ages. Amen.

## Augustine, Soliloquies, chapter 31.2 *Cap. 31.2.*

{p.45} O Holy of holies, God of inestimable Majesty, God of gods and Lord of lords, before whom the angelic powers tremble in heaven, whom all dominions adore, and thrones and all virtues quake at thy presence, of whose power and wisdom there is no number. I also, the son of thine handmaid, by faith bow the neck of my heart beneath the feet of thy Majesty, giving thanks, for that thou hast vouchsafed by thy mercy to enlighten me. O true light, holy light, delightful light. Behold, I see, I give thanks; behold, I see the light of heaven; a ray shineth through upon the eyes of my mind from above, from the face of thy light, and maketh glad all my bones. Increase, I beseech thee, O author of light, increase, I beseech thee, that which shineth in me. O light which ever shinest, and art never darkened, enlighten me. O most blessed light, which canst not be seen save by eyes most purified, cleanse me, thou cleansing power, heal mine eyes, that with sound eyes I may behold thee, here darkly and in a glass, but then face to face, when the day of gladness and exultation shall come, wherein I shall enter into the place of the wonderful tabernacle, even unto the house of God, that I may see him that seeth me, face to face, and my desire may be satisfied, and I may speak praise unto thy name, {p.46} world without end. Amen.

## Augustine, On Contrition, chapter 3 *De contritione*

O Lord Jesus Christ, my Redeemer, my life and my salvation, I praise thee, I give thee thanks; thy goodness, O Lord, created me, thy mercy cleansed me from original sin, thy patience, after that cleansing, hath hitherto borne with me in other defilements of sins, hath nourished me and waited for me. O LORD my God, my Creator, my helper, for thee I thirst, for thee I hunger, thee I desire, unto thee I sigh, thee I long for, most kind, most sweet and most serene; thee I will, thee I seek, in thee I hope. My soul refused to be comforted, save by thee, my sweetness; draw near unto me, O Lord, appear unto me and I shall be comforted, shew me thy face, and I shall be saved. Grant me, O Lord my God, thou that art fairer than the children of men, that I may desire thee, that I may love thee, as much as I will and as much as I ought; thou art without measure, and without measure oughtest thou to be loved, most of all by us, whom thou hast so loved, so saved, for whom thou hast done things so great and of such a kind. O sweet Christ, good Jesus, charity, my GOD, kindle me with all thy fire, with thy love, with thy charity, that I may {p.47} love thee, my Lord most sweet and most beautiful, with all my heart, through all my life, and world without end. Amen.

## Augustine, Manual, chapter 14

O most gracious Lord, who hast so loved us and saved us, so quickened and exalted us, O most gracious Lord, how sweet is the remembrance of thee; the more I meditate upon thee, the sweeter and the more lovely art thou unto me. Therefore do thy good things greatly delight me, with the pure beholding of the mind, and the most sweet affection of godly love, in the place of my pilgrimage. Meanwhile, as long as I abide in these frail members, I desire without ceasing to long for and to consider thy wonderful love and beauty. For I am wounded with the dart of thy charity, I burn vehemently with desire of thee, I long to come unto thee, I desire to see thee. Therefore will I stand upon my watch, and with wakeful eyes I will sing with the spirit, I will sing with the understanding also, and with all my strength I will praise thee, my Maker and my Restorer; I will pierce the sky with my mind, and in desire I will be with thee, that in this present misery I may be held by the body only, but with thee in thought and eagerness and all desire I may always be, so that there my heart may be, {p.48} where thou art, my treasure, desirable, incomparable, and greatly to be loved. But behold, my God, most gracious and most merciful, when I would consider the glory of thine immeasurable goodness and lovingkindness, my heart sufficeth not; for thy comeliness, thy beauty, thy power, and thy charity surpass the understanding of the mind of man. As the brightness of thy glory is inestimable, so unspeakable is the kindness of thine eternal charity, whereby thou adoptest as sons those whom thou hast created out of nothing, and joinest them unto thyself; unto thee be honour and glory unto everlasting ages. Amen.

## Jerome, in the Agony of Death *Hieronymus in agone*

O Jesus Christ, thou art my God and my Lord, who didst suffer for me, who, whereas thou wast God alone before all times, and begotten without beginning of God the Father, by an eternal and unsearchable generation, who with the same Father and the Holy Ghost art one God, abiding that same which thou wast and art, didst shut thyself within the little body of one maiden, being made man as I am; truly thou art both God and man. For in such wise didst thou take manhood in the Virgin’s womb, that thou art neither God without man, nor man without God, although neither is the manhood the Godhead, nor the Godhead the manhood: {p.49} The natures are not confounded, although there is one and the same person in thee. Art thou not my flesh and my brother? Truly thus thou didst hunger, thou didst thirst, thou didst weep, thou hadst mine infirmities as I have, yet in thee there was not the infirmity and defect of sinning, as in me. For thou couldest not sin as I; in thee was and is bodily all the fulness of the Godhead and of grace, for grace was not given unto thee by measure. For thy soul was straightway joined inseparably unto the Godhead; it perfectly knew, and was able to do, all things which the Godhead itself knoweth and is able to do. As touching the divine nature which is in thee, thou art equal to the eternal Father, but by reason of that manhood which thou didst take for our redemption, thou art less than it, and yet thou incurrest no reproach thereby: For thou art he upon whom, in the channel of Jordan, John baptizing, the voice of the Father suddenly thundered from heaven, saying, This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased; hear ye him; and the Holy Ghost, descending upon thee as a dove, declared thee to be one with the Father, and the same in substance. Thee therefore, O Lord Jesus Christ, truly GOD and man, I glorify and praise, as my God, and my Lord, my Deliverer and my Saviour; make me always to praise thee as long as I shall be. Amen.

## Bernard

{p.50} O good Jesus, O sweet Jesus, O Jesus, Son of the Virgin Mary, full of mercy and truth, O sweet Jesus, have mercy upon me, according to thy great mercy. O kind Jesus, I beseech thee by that precious blood of thine, which thou didst vouchsafe to shed for us wretched sinners upon the altar of the cross, that thou cast away all mine iniquities, and despise not him that humbly asketh thee, and calleth upon this thy most holy name Jesus. This name Jesus is a sweet name; this name Jesus is a saving name. For what is Jesus but Saviour? O good Jesus, who hast created me and redeemed me with thine own blood, suffer me not to be damned, whom thou hast created out of nothing. O good Jesus, let not mine iniquity destroy me, whom thine almighty goodness hath made. O good Jesus, acknowledge that which is thine in me, and wipe away from me that which is not thine. O good Jesus, have mercy upon me while it is the time of having mercy, lest thou destroy me in the time of thy dreadful judgment. O good Jesus, if I, a wretched sinner, have deserved of thy true justice eternal punishment for my most grievous sins, yet do I appeal from thy true justice unto thine unspeakable mercy; therefore {p.51} thou wilt have mercy upon me as a gracious father and a merciful LORD. O good Jesus, what profit is there in my blood, when I go down into eternal corruption? For the dead shall not praise thee, O LORD, neither all they that go down into hell. O most merciful Jesus, have mercy upon me. O sweetest Jesus, deliver me. O most gracious Jesus, be merciful to me a sinner. O Jesus, receive me, a wretched sinner, into the number of thine elect. O Jesus, the salvation of them that hope in thee. O Jesus, the salvation of them that believe in thee, have mercy upon me. O Jesus, sweet remission of all my sins. O Jesus, Son of the Virgin Mary, pour into me thy grace, wisdom, charity, chastity and humility, and in all mine adversities holy patience, that I may be able perfectly to love thee,[^1] and to glory and delight in thee, world without end. Amen.

## The Church

O Lord Jesus Christ, Son of the living God, who art true and almighty God, the Saviour and the image of the Father, and life eternal, to whom, together with the eternal Father and the Holy Ghost, belongeth equal honour, coeternal Majesty, one substance, I give thee thanks, I adore thee, {p.52} I praise thee, I bless thee, and I glorify thee; suffer me not, I beseech thee, to perish, but save and help me by thy free gift, whom thou hast vouchsafed to redeem with thy precious blood, Who with the same Father, in the unity of the same Holy Ghost, livest and reignest, world without end. Amen.

## Psalm 2

Why do the heathen rage, and the people imagine a vain thing?

The kings of the earth set themselves, and the rulers take counsel together, against the LORD, and against his Christ.

Let us break their bands asunder, and cast away their yoke from us.

He that dwelleth in the heavens shall laugh at them: and the Lord shall have them in derision.

Then shall he speak unto them in his wrath, and vex them in his sore displeasure.

Yet have I set my King upon my holy hill of Zion.

I will declare the decree, which the LORD hath said unto me: Thou art my Son; this day have I begotten thee:

Ask of me, and I shall give thee the heathen for thine inheritance, and the uttermost parts of the earth for thy possession.

{p.53} Thou shalt break them with a rod of iron; thou shalt dash them in pieces like a potter’s vessel.

Be wise now therefore, O ye kings: be instructed, ye judges of the earth.

Serve the LORD with fear, and rejoice unto him with trembling.

Kiss the Son, lest he be angry, and ye perish in the way.

For his wrath shall be kindled in a little while. Blessed are all they that put their trust in him.

## Psalm 45

My heart hath uttered a good matter: I speak of the things which I have made unto the King.

My tongue is the pen of a ready writer.

Thou art fairer than the children of men: grace is poured into thy lips: therefore God hath blessed thee for ever.

Gird thy sword upon thy thigh, O most mighty, with thy glory and thy majesty.

And in thy majesty prosper, and ride upon the word of truth, and of affliction, for righteousness’ sake; and thy right hand shall teach thee wonderful things.

Thine arrows are so sharp, that the people fall under thee, in the midst of the King’s enemies.

Thy throne, O God, is for ever and ever: the sceptre {p.54} of thy kingdom is a right sceptre.

Thou hast loved righteousness, and hated wickedness: therefore God, thy God, hath anointed thee with the oil of gladness above thy fellows.

Myrrh, and aloes, and cassia are all thy garments, out of the ivory palaces, whereby they make thee glad.

Kings’ daughters are in thine honour: upon thy right hand doth stand the queen in vesture of gold.

Hearken, O daughter, and see, and incline thine ear; forget also thine own people, and thy father’s house.

So shall the King greatly desire thy beauty: for he is the Lord God; and thou shalt worship him.

And the daughter of Tyre shall be there with gifts; the rich among the people shall intreat thy favour.

The King’s daughter is all glorious within: her clothing is with fringes of gold.

She shall be brought unto the King in raiment of needlework: the virgins after her, and her companions, shall be brought unto thee.

With gladness and rejoicing shall they be brought: they shall enter into the King’s temple.

Instead of thy fathers sons are born unto thee, whom thou shalt make princes in all the earth.

I will remember thy name in all {p.55} generations: therefore shall the people praise thee for ever and ever.

# IV. To the Holy Ghost
*Ad Spiritum Sanctum*

{p.56} O Love of the divine Godhead, holy communication of the almighty Father and of his most blessed Offspring, almighty Spirit, the Comforter, most merciful consoler of them that mourn, glide now with mighty power into the inmost chambers of my heart, and, as a gracious indweller, make glad with the brightness of thy flashing light all the dark hiding-places of the neglected dwelling, and by thy visitation make fruitful with the abundance of thy dew the things that are withered by the long foulness of drought. Wound the secret places of the inner man with the dart of thy love, and piercing the marrow of my sluggish inward parts kindle it with saving flames, and enlightening them with the fire of holy fervour consume the inmost parts of my mind, and all the parts of my body. Give me to drink of the river of thy pleasure, that it may no more please me to taste anything of the poisoned sweetness of worldly things. Judge me, O Lord, and plead my cause against an ungodly nation; teach me to do thy will, for thou art my God. I believe therefore that in whomsoever thou dwellest, thou foundest there the habitation of the Father and likewise of the Son. Blessed is he who shall be found worthy to have thee for his guest, for through thee the Father and the Son make their abode with him. Come now, come, most kind consoler of the sorrowing soul, protector in times of need, helper in tribulation. Come, cleanser of crimes, healer of wounds. Come, strength of the frail, raiser {p.57} up of them that fall. Come, teacher of the humble, destroyer of the proud. Come, gracious Father of orphans, gentle judge of widows. Come, hope of the poor, reviver of them that faint. Come, star of them that sail, haven of the shipwrecked. Come, singular glory of all the living, only salvation of the dying. Come, most holy Spirit, come and have mercy upon me, fit me for thyself, and graciously condescend unto me, that my littleness may be pleasing unto thy greatness, and my weakness unto thy strength, according to the multitude of thy mercies, through Jesus Christ my Saviour, who with the Father, in thy unity, liveth and reigneth, world without end. Amen.[^2]

## Augustine, Soliloquies, chapter 33

I have known thee to be GOD, and the one Holy Ghost of the Father and of the Son, proceeding equally from both, consubstantial and coeternal with the Father and the Son, our Comforter and Advocate, who didst descend upon the same GOD, our Lord Jesus Christ, in the form of a dove, and didst appear upon the Apostles in tongues of fire, who also from the beginning hast taught all the Saints and Elect of GOD by the gift of thy grace, and {p.58} hast opened the mouths of the Prophets, that they might declare the wonderful things of the kingdom of God; for by all the Saints of GOD thou art worshipped and glorified together with the Father and the Son, among whom I also, the son of thine handmaid, glorify thy name with all my heart, because thou hast enlightened me. For thou art the true light, the light that speaketh truth, the fire of GOD, and the master of spirits, who by thine anointing teachest us all truth, the Spirit of truth, without whom it is impossible to please God, for thou thyself art God of God, and light of light, proceeding from the Father of lights, and from his Son our Lord Jesus Christ, with whom being consubstantial, and coequal, and coeternal, thou art in the essence of the one Trinity superessentially glorified together and reignest together, world without end. Amen.

## The Church

O Lord, Holy Ghost, GOD, who, being coequal, consubstantial and coeternal with the Father and the Son, proceeding ineffably from them, and who didst appear upon the same our Lord Jesus Christ in the form of a dove, and didst descend upon the holy disciples in tongues of fire, I give thee thanks, I adore thee, I praise thee, and I bless and glorify thee; drive away from me, I beseech thee, {p.59} the darkness of all iniquity and unbelief, and kindle in me the light of thy mercy, and the fire of thy most holy love, Who livest and reignest, God, world without end. Amen.

# V. Forms of Prayer from Meditation on the Nativity of Christ
*Formulae precandi ex meditatione nativitatis Christi*

## Augustine, Meditations, chapter 15

O immeasurable lovingkindness, O inestimable charity: that thou mightest deliver the servant, thou gavest up the Son; GOD was made man, that lost man might be plucked out of the power of devils. How most kind a lover of men is thy Son our God, unto whose tender bowels it seemed not enough that he should stoop, being made man of Mary, a true Virgin, except he should also undergo the torment of the cross, his blood being shed for us, and for our salvation. {p.65} The gracious God came, he came of his lovingkindness and goodness, he came to seek and to save that which was lost. He sought the lost sheep, he sought and found it, and upon his shoulders carried it back to the folds of the flock. A gracious Lord, and truly a most gracious Shepherd. O charity, O lovingkindness, who hath heard such things? who is not astonished at such great bowels of mercy? Who doth not marvel? Who doth not rejoice together for thine exceeding charity, wherewith thou hast loved us? Thou didst send thy Son in the likeness of sinful flesh, that for sin he might condemn sin, that we might be made thy righteousness in him. For he is the true Lamb without spot, who hath taken away the sins of the world, who by dying destroyed our death, and by rising again restored our life. But what can we render unto thee, O our God, for such great benefits of thy mercy? What praises, or what thanksgivings? Even if that knowledge and power of the Angels were ours, yet should we be able to repay nothing worthy of so great lovingkindness and goodness of thine. If indeed all our members were turned into tongues to pay thee the praises that are due, our littleness would in no wise suffice. For thine inestimable charity surpasseth all knowledge, which thou hast shewed unto us unworthy, of thy mere {p.66} goodness and lovingkindness. For thy Son our GOD took not on him Angels, but the seed of Abraham, being made like unto us in all things, without sin. Taking therefore the nature of man, not of Angels, and glorifying it with the robe of holy resurrection and immortality, he carried it above all heavens, above all the choirs of Angels, above Cherubim and Seraphim, setting it at thy right hand. This the Angels praise, the dominions adore, and all the powers of the heavens tremble at one above themselves, even at the man who is GOD. For this is all my hope and all my confidence. For there is in the same Jesus Christ our Lord a portion of every one of us, flesh and blood. Where therefore my portion reigneth, there I believe that I reign. Where my flesh is glorified, there I know that I am glorious. Where my blood hath dominion, there I perceive that I have dominion. Although I be a sinner, I despair not of this communion of grace. Though my sins forbid, my substance requireth it. Though mine offences shut me out, the communion of nature doth not thrust me back. For God is not so ungentle, that he should forget man, and not remember him whom he himself beareth, and whom he took for my sake, him whom for my sake he seeketh. {p.67} But gentle and exceeding kind is the LORD our God, and he loveth his own flesh and his own bowels, in the same our God and Lord Jesus Christ, most sweet, most kind and most merciful, in whom we have risen again, and have already ascended into the heavens, and already sit together in heavenly places. Our flesh loveth us. And we have the prerogative of our blood in him. And we are his members, and his flesh, but he is our head, of whom is the whole body, as it is written, Bone of my bones, and flesh of my flesh, and they two shall be one. For this great mystery of godliness I bless and glorify thy holy name. O King, Christ, Son of Mary, Son of the living God, unto thee be honour and glory, with the Father and the Holy Ghost, unto everlasting ages. Amen.

## Augustine, Meditations, chapter 15

I bless thy holy name for ever, and glorify it with all my heart, O almighty and merciful Lord, for the unspeakable and wonderful joining of Godhead and manhood in unity of person, so that there should not be one who is God and another who is man, but one and the same, God and man, man and God. But although by a wonderful condescension the Word was made {p.68} flesh, yet was neither of the two natures changed into the other substance. Unto the mystery of the Trinity no fourth person was added, for the substance of the Word of GOD and of man is one, not confounded, that that which had been taken from us might come unto God, and that which had never been might abide the same that it had ever been. O wonderful mystery. O unspeakable exchange. O marvellous kindness of the divine mercy, ever to be marvelled at, and ever to be loved: we were not worthy to be servants, and behold, we are made the sons of God, heirs indeed of God, and joint-heirs with Christ. Whence is this to us? and who hath brought us unto this? But I ask thee, most merciful Father, O God, by this thine inestimable lovingkindness, goodness and charity, that thou make us worthy of the many and great promises of the same thy Son, our Lord Jesus Christ. Command thy strength, and strengthen that which thou hast wrought in us; perfect that which thou hast begun, that we may be found worthy to come unto the full grace of thy lovingkindness. Make us through the Holy Ghost to understand, and to be found worthy of, and always with due honour to reverence this great mystery of godliness, which was manifest in the flesh, justified in the Spirit, seen of Angels, preached unto the Gentiles, believed on in the world, received {p.69} up into glory, through the same thy Son our Redeemer. Amen.

## The Church

## Psalm 72

Give the king thy judgments, O God, and thy righteousness unto the king’s son.

That he may judge thy people with righteousness, and thy poor with judgment.

The mountains shall bring peace to the people, and the little hills righteousness.

{p.70} He shall judge the poor of the people, he shall save the children of the needy, and shall break in pieces the oppressor.

They shall fear thee as long as the sun and moon endure, throughout all generations.

He cometh down like rain upon the fleece: and as showers that drop upon the earth.

In his days shall the righteous flourish; and abundance of peace, until the moon be taken away.

He shall have dominion also from sea to sea, and from the river unto the ends of the earth.

They that dwell in the wilderness shall bow before him; and his enemies shall lick the dust.

The kings of the sea and of the isles shall bring presents: the kings of the Arabians and of Sheba offer gifts.

Yea, all kings of the earth shall worship him: all nations shall serve him.

For he shall deliver the needy when he crieth; the poor also, that had no helper.

He shall spare the poor and needy, and shall save the souls of the needy.

He shall redeem their soul from deceit and violence: and precious shall their blood be in his sight.

And he shall live, and to him shall be given of the gold of Arabia: and they shall worship him continually; all the day long shall they bless him.

{p.71} And there shall be abundance of corn in the earth; upon the top of the mountains the fruit thereof shall shake like Lebanon: and they shall flourish in the city like grass of the earth.

His name shall endure for ever: his name shall be continued as long as the sun: and men shall be blessed in him: all nations shall magnify him.

Blessed be the LORD God, the God of Israel, who only doeth wondrous things.

And blessed be the name of his majesty for ever: and the whole earth shall be filled with his majesty; Amen, and Amen.

## Psalm 132

The LORD hath sworn in truth unto David, and will not fail him; Of the fruit of thy body will I set upon thy throne.

If thy children will keep my covenant and these my testimonies that I shall teach them, their children shall also sit upon thy throne for evermore.

For the LORD hath chosen Zion; he hath chosen it for his habitation.

This is my rest for ever: here will I dwell; for I have chosen it.

I will abundantly bless her provision: I will satisfy her poor with bread.

There will I make the horn of David to bud: I have ordained a lamp for my Christ.

His enemies will I clothe with shame: but upon himself shall his crown flourish.
