# XII. Forms of Prayer under Persecution, against Enemies and Foes
*Formulae precandi sub persecutione contra inimicos et hostes*

## Augustine, Soliloquies, chapter 12

{p.206} O Lord Jesus Christ, my Redeemer and Deliverer, I beseech thee by thyself, help me, that I fall not in the sight of mine adversaries, taken in their snares, which they have prepared for my feet, to bow down my soul. But deliver me, O strength of my salvation, lest mine enemies, who hate thee, make sport of me. Arise, O Lord my God, my strong one, and let thine enemies be scattered, and let them that hate thee flee before thy face; as wax melteth before the fire, so let sinners perish before thy face. And I shall be hidden in the secret of thy presence, and shall rejoice with thy children, filled with all good things. And thou, O Lord God, Father of the fatherless, and thou Father of thine orphans, hear the wailing of thy children, and stretch forth thy wings, that we may flee under them from the face of the enemy. O tower of the strength of Israel, for thou slumberest not, neither wilt thou sleep, keeping Israel; for he sleepeth not, {p.207} neither slumbereth, the enemy who fighteth against Israel.

## Prayer for deliverance from the hand of enemies, gathered out of the Psalms *Precatio pro liberatione de manu inimicorum, ex Psalmis collecta*

In thee, O Lord, do I put my trust; let me never be ashamed: deliver me in thy righteousness.

Bow down thine ear to me; deliver me speedily.

Plead my cause, O LORD, with them that strive with me: fight against them that fight against me.

Take hold of shield and buckler, and stand up for mine help.

Draw out also the spear, and defend me against them that persecute me.

Let them be confounded and put to shame that seek after my soul: let them be turned back and brought to confusion that devise my hurt.

Let them be as dust before the wind: and let the angel of the Lord chase them.

This thou hast seen, O Lord: keep not silence: O Lord, be not far from me.

Stir up thyself, and awake to my cause, my God, unto my judgment.

Judge me, O Lord my GOD, according to thy righteousness; and let them not rejoice over me.

{p.208} Let them be ashamed and brought to confusion together that rejoice at mine hurt: let them be clothed with shame and dishonour that magnify themselves against me.

O Lord my God, in thee do I put my trust: save me from all them that persecute me, and deliver me:

Lest he tear my soul like a lion, rending it in pieces, while there is none to deliver.

Arise, O Lord, in thine anger, lift up thyself against the rage of mine enemies.

Arise, O Lord God, and let thine hand be lifted up: forget not the poor.

Keep me as the apple of the eye, hide me under the shadow of thy wings,

From the face of the wicked that have afflicted me, and of mine enemies, who have compassed my soul about.

Consider mine enemies; for they are many; and they hate me with unjust hatred.

Have mercy upon me, O Lord; consider mine affliction which I suffer of mine enemies, thou that liftest me up from the gates of death.

To thee is the poor left; thou wilt be the helper of the fatherless.

Let them be confounded and consumed that are adversaries to my soul; let them be covered with reproach and dishonour that seek my hurt.

Let the mischief of their own lips cover the head {p.209} of them that compass me about.

Deliver me, O Lord, from the evil man: preserve me from the violent man;

Which imagine mischiefs in their heart; continually are they gathered together for war.

They have sharpened their tongues like serpents; adders' poison is under their lips.

Grant not, O Lord, the desires of the wicked: suffer not his device to go forward; lest they exalt themselves.

Keep me from the hand of the wicked, and preserve me from violent men, who have purposed to overthrow my goings.

O God, be not far from me: O my God, make haste for my help.

For thou art mine expectation, O Lord: O Lord, my hope from my youth.

In thee have I trusted from the womb: thou art he that took me out of my mother's belly.

In God is my salvation and my glory: the rock of my strength, my hope, is in God.

Many there be which say of my soul, There is no help for him in God.

But thou, O Lord, art my protector; my glory, and the lifter up of mine head.

I will not be afraid of many thousands of people, that compass me about.

Salvation belongeth unto the LORD: thy blessing is upon {p.210} thy people.

Thou art not a God that hath pleasure in wickedness: neither shall the evil man dwell with thee.

Neither shall the unjust stand in thy sight: thou hatest all workers of iniquity.

Thou shalt destroy them that speak leasing: the Lord will abhor the bloody and deceitful man.

Depart from me, all ye workers of iniquity; for the Lord hath heard the voice of my weeping.

The Lord hath heard my supplication; the Lord hath received my prayer.

Let all mine enemies be ashamed and sore vexed: let them return and be ashamed very suddenly.

## Thanksgiving for deliverance from the hand of enemies and foes *Gratiarum actio pro liberatione de manu inimicorum et hostium*

I will extol thee, my God, O King; and I will bless thy name for ever and ever.

Every day will I bless thee; and I will praise thy name for ever and ever.

I will extol thee, O LORD; for thou hast lifted me up, and hast not made my foes to rejoice over me.

{p.211} Thou hast turned for me my mourning into joy: thou hast rent my sackcloth, and girded me with gladness.

Thou hast maintained my right and my cause; thou satest in the throne judging right.

I will bless the Lord at all times: his praise shall continually be in my mouth.

O magnify the Lord with me, and let us exalt his name together.

I sought the Lord, and he heard me, and delivered me from all my fears.

Thou rulest the raging of the sea: when the waves thereof arise, thou stillest them.

O love the Lord, all ye his saints: the Lord preserveth the faithful.

Be of good courage, and let your heart be strengthened, all ye that hope in the Lord.

For the needy shall not alway be forgotten: the expectation of the poor shall not perish for ever.

To thee is the poor left; thou wilt be the helper of the fatherless.

Upon the wicked thou shalt rain snares, fire and brimstone, and a stormy wind: this shall be the portion of their cup.

Thou, O God, shalt bring them down into the pit of destruction: bloody and deceitful men shall not live out half their days.

But I will sing of thy power; yea, I will extol thy mercy in the morning: for thou hast {p.212} been my defence and refuge in the day of my trouble.

Unto thee, O my strength, will I sing: for thou, O God, art my defence, the God of my mercy.

# XIII. Forms of Prayer against the Tyranny, Deceits and Snares of Satan
*Formulae precandi contra tyrannidem, fraudes et insidias Satanae*

## Augustine, Soliloquies, chapter 16

That great red dragon, the old serpent, called the Devil and Satan, having seven heads and ten horns, whom thou hast made to play in this great and wide sea, wherein creep living things both small and great, that is, the divers kinds of devils, which work nothing else by day and by night but that they go about, seeking whom they may devour, unless thou deliver. For he is that old dragon, who arose in the Paradise of pleasure, who with his tail draweth the third part of the stars of heaven, and casteth them to the earth. Who shall defend us from his bitings? who shall {p.213} pluck us out of his mouth, save thou, O Lord, who hast broken the heads of the great dragon? Help us; spread over us, O Lord, thy wings, that we may flee under them from the face of this dragon who pursueth us, and with thy shield deliver us from his horns. For this is his one endeavour, this his one desire, to devour the souls which thou hast created. And therefore, my God, we cry unto thee: deliver us from our daily adversary, who, whether we sleep or wake, whether we eat or drink, or whatsoever work we do, presseth upon us in every way day and night with deceits and crafts, now openly, now secretly aiming poisoned arrows against us, that he may slay our souls. And yet, O Lord, such is our most wretched madness, that although we daily see the dragon with open mouth ready to devour us, we nevertheless sleep and wanton in our slothfulness, as though secure before him who desireth nothing else than to destroy us. The enemy, that he may kill, watcheth always without sleep; and we, that we may guard ourselves, will not awake out of sleep. Behold, he hath spread before our feet snares without number, and hath filled all our ways with divers traps to catch our souls. And who shall escape? {p.214} He hath laid snares in riches, he hath laid snares in poverty; he hath spread snares in meat, in drink, in pleasure, in sleep, in waking; he hath laid snares in word and in deed, and in all our life. But do thou, O Lord, deliver us from the snare of the fowlers, and from the sharp word, that we may give thanks unto thee, saying, Blessed be the Lord, who hath not given us as a prey to their teeth. Our soul is escaped as a bird out of the snare of the fowlers: the snare is broken, and we are delivered. And to him that bruised the Serpent's head, our Deliverer and Protector, be praise and glory for ever and ever. Amen.

## Augustine, Soliloquies, chapter 17 *Ibidem, cap. 17*

O Lord Jesus Christ, my light, lighten mine eyes, that I may see the light, and walk in thy light, and not stumble into the snares of Satan. For who shall escape these many snares, except he see them? And who shall see them, save he whom thou hast lightened with thy light? For the father of darkness himself hideth his snares in his darkness from them that are the children of this darkness, who see not thy light, wherein he that walketh shall not fear. For he that walketh in the day stumbleth not; but he that walketh in the night stumbleth, because there is no light in him. Thou art the light, O Lord, thou the light of the children {p.215} of light; thou art the day that knoweth no setting, wherein thy children walk without stumbling. But he that is far removed from thee, the true light, is the more rolled in the darkness of sins; and the more he is in darkness, the less he seeth the snares in his way, and therefore the less he knoweth them, and for that cause is the oftener taken and falleth into them, and, what is more dreadful, knoweth not even that he hath fallen. And he that knoweth not his own fall careth the less to rise, since he thinketh that he yet standeth. But thou, the light of the mind, O Lord my God, lighten mine eyes, that I may see and know, lest I fall in the sight of mine adversaries. For our enemy laboureth for our destruction[^1]; whom we pray thee to make to melt away from before our face, as wax melteth before the fire. For he, O LORD, is the first and the last robber, who took counsel to seize thy glory; for, puffed up and lifted up, he burst asunder and fell. But now, O my life and my GOD, since he fell he ceaseth not to persecute thy children; and out of hatred of thee, O great King, he desireth to destroy this thy creature, which thine almighty goodness created after thine image, that it might possess thy glory, which he himself lost through his pride. But do thou bruise him, O our strong one, before he {p.216} devour us thy lambs; and lighten us, that we may see the snares which he hath prepared, and escape unto thee, O joy of Israel. And all these things thou knowest better, who knowest his contentiousness and his most stiff neck. Neither do I say this to shew it unto thee, for thou seest all things, and no thought is hid from thee; but before the feet of thy Majesty I make my complaint of mine enemy, O eternal Judge, that thou mayest both condemn him, and save us thy children, whose strength thou art. For this enemy is crafty and crooked, neither can the windings of his way be easily discovered, nor the fashion of his countenance be known, except thou give light. For now here, now there, now as a lamb, now as a wolf, now as darkness, now as light he sheweth himself; and to every several condition, place and time, according to the divers changes of things, he presenteth divers temptations. For to deceive the sorrowful, he himself is sorrowful also; to mock them that rejoice, he feigneth that he also rejoiceth; to beguile the spiritual, he transformeth himself into an angel of light; to crush the strong, he appeareth a lamb; to devour the meek, he appeareth a wolf. These things and many others doth he attempt against us, that son of Belial, that Satan, O Lord our God; and now as a lion, now as a dragon, openly and secretly, within and without, day and night, he lieth in wait to snatch away our souls. {p.217} But do thou deliver us, O Lord, who savest them that hope in thee, that he may grieve over us, and thou mayest be praised in us, O Lord our God, blessed for ever. Amen.

## Augustine, Soliloquies, chapter 18 *Ibidem, cap. 18*

But I, the son of thine handmaid, who have now commended myself to thy hand in these my poor confessions, will confess unto thee, my Deliverer, with my whole heart, and will call again to my mind all the good things which thou hast done for me from my youth, in all my life. I will give thee thanks, lest I be unthankful to thee, my Deliverer, for thou hast delivered me. How often had that dragon already swallowed me up, and thou, O Lord, didst draw me out of his mouth? How often did I sin, and he was ready to devour me, but thou, O Lord my God, didst defend me? When I did wickedly against thee, when I brake thy commandments, he stood ready to snatch me away to hell, but thou didst forbid him. I offended thee, and thou didst defend me. I feared thee not, and thou didst keep me. I departed from thee, and offered myself to mine enemy; thou thyself didst frighten him, that he should not take me. These benefits thou didst bestow upon me, O Lord my God, and I, wretched man, knew it not. For thus hast thou many a time {p.218} delivered me from the jaws of the Devil, plucked me out of the mouth of the lion, and many times brought me back from hell, though I knew it not. For I went down even to the gates of hell, and thou didst hold me, that I should not enter therein. I drew near even to the gates of death, and thou didst bring it to pass that they should not take me. Thee therefore, most merciful Redeemer, Deliverer and Protector, I praise and glorify. I will sing unto thy name while I have my being. Thy praise shall never depart out of my mouth. Blessed be thy name for ever. Amen.

## The Church

O Lord Jesus Christ, the only defence of our mortality, our only hope, our salvation, our victory, our glory and triumph, who in the flesh which thou hadst taken for our sake didst suffer thyself to be tempted of Satan, and who alone hast overcome sin, death, the world, Satan and the whole kingdom of hell, and whatsoever thou hast overcome, hast surely overcome for us; neither wouldest thou have thine own to wrestle with these for any other purpose than that thou mightest crown them the more gloriously, and mightest overthrow in thy members also the enemy whom thou hast vanquished in thine own self: give, I beseech thee, O most invincible Lion of the tribe of Judah, strength to thy soldier against the roaring lion, who ever {p.219} goeth about, seeking whom he may devour. Thou, that health-bringing Serpent fastened upon the high tree, give to thy little one wisdom against the wiles of the most crafty serpent. Thou, that snow-white Lamb, conqueror of the tyranny of Satan, give to thy little sheep the strength of thy Spirit, that, frail in itself, strong in thee, it may overcome all the assaults of the Devil, lest mine enemy glory over me, but that, a conqueror through thee, I may give thanks unto thy mercy, which never forsaketh them that trust in thee: who livest and reignest with God the Father and the Holy Ghost, without end. Amen.

They shall bear thee up in their hands, lest thou dash thy foot against a stone.

Because he hath set his love upon me, therefore will I deliver him: I will protect him, because he hath known my name.

He hath called upon me, and I will answer him: I am with him in trouble; I will deliver him, and honour him.

With long life will I satisfy him, and shew him my salvation.

# XIV. Forms of Giving Thanks to God for Benefits, both Bodily and Spiritual
*Formulae gratias agendi Deo pro beneficiis, tam corporalibus quam spiritualibus*

## Augustine, Meditations, chapters 14 and 16

{p.221} We give thanks unto thee, O most merciful and most gracious God, lover of men, who through Jesus Christ thy Son, when we were not, didst mightily make us, and when we had been lost by our own fault, didst marvellously deliver us. We give thanks unto thy lovingkindness, and render thee many thanks from our whole heart, who, for the unspeakable love wherewith thou hast vouchsafed, of thy wonderful goodness, to love us wretched and unworthy, didst send the same thine only-begotten from thy bosom unto us, to save sinners, who were then children of perdition. We give thee thanks for his holy incarnation and nativity. We give thee thanks for his passion and cross, for his death and resurrection, for his ascension into heaven, and for the seat of his Majesty at thy right hand. We give thee thanks for that most sacred shedding of his precious blood, whereby we are redeemed, and likewise for the most holy and life-giving mystery of his body and blood, wherewith we are daily fed and given to drink in thy Church, are washed and sanctified, and are made partakers of the one most high Godhead. {p.222} We give thee thanks for this thy wonderful and unspeakable charity, wherewith thou hast so loved and saved us unworthy, through thine only beloved Son. For thou so lovedst the world, that thou gavest thine only-begotten, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life. O how greatly are we thy debtors, O Lord our God, redeemed at so great a price, saved by so great a gift, and holpen by so glorious a benefit! How greatly art thou to be feared, loved, blessed and glorified by us wretched ones, who hast so loved us, so saved us, so sanctified us, so exalted us! To thee indeed we owe all that we can do, all that we live, all that we understand. But who hath anything that is not thine? Thou, O Lord our God, from whom all good things proceed, for thine own sake and for thy holy name's sake give us of thy good things, that of thy goods and gifts we may serve thee, and please thee in truth, and daily render thee due praises for so great benefits of thy mercy. To thee be honour, praise and glory for ever and ever. Amen.

## Augustine, Meditations, chapter 11

Many and innumerable good things hast thou done {p.223} unto me, O Lord my God; make me to give thanks, that I may praise and love thee for all thy benefits. Thou who, making good use of the evil of my parents, didst create me of their flesh, and didst breathe into me the breath of life, distinguishing me from those who, either cast forth untimely from the womb, or stifled within their mother's bowels, seem to have been conceived for punishment, not for life. I have received, therefore, that I should be; I have received that I should be a man; I have received understanding, to make a difference between me and the beast; I have received the form of a body; I have received in the body the distinction of the senses: eyes to see, ears to hear, nostrils to smell, hands to handle, a palate to taste, feet to walk, and the very health of the body. This also is surely a great thing, that GOD hath created for me sound and whole members. I count it also a great benefit, that God willed me to be born at such a time and among such men, through whom I might come to his faith and Sacraments. I see that this hath been denied to innumerable men, which I rejoice hath been granted to me, though we and they were of one condition. They were left by justice, and I was called by grace. I will go on yet further, considering that it was his gift that I was brought up by my parents, that the flame hath not hurt me, that {p.224} the water hath not swallowed me, that I have not been vexed by a devil, nor smitten by wild beasts, nor killed by a headlong fall, that unto a fitting age I have been nourished in his faith and in good will. These, therefore, are thy good things, O Lord, mercifully bestowed on me by thee. To which I add these also, as benefits no less: that thou hast mercifully delivered me when I was often set in perils, and hast never forsaken me in my sins. When I forgat thee, thou didst put me in mind of thee; when I turned away from thee, thou didst call me back; when I came unto thee, thou didst graciously receive me; when I repented, thou didst pardon me. Not those sins only which I have committed, but those also which, by thy protection, I have not committed—the whole is to be reckoned as forgiven by thee. For as I have fallen into many sins, so I should have fallen into many more, hadst thou not preserved me. To thee therefore be praise, my God, to thee glory, to thee thanksgiving; but to me confusion of face, who have done so many evils, and received so many good things. Let us therefore confess unto him our evils, let us confess also our good things. Let us confess, then, that our evils are of ourselves, that he may mercifully forgive; let us confess that our good things are of him, that he may preserve and increase them. Let us ascribe to him alone glory and honour, from this time forth and for evermore. Amen.

## The Church

{p.225} I give thee thanks, and render praises unto thee, O LORD my God, who hast vouchsafed to create me out of nothing after thine image and likeness, and to redeem me with thy precious blood, and by the washing of holy Baptism to gather me among the children of adoption, and to refresh me with the mystery of thy precious body and blood. To thee therefore I give praise and thanks, who from the time of my tender infancy even unto this hour, while I was slipping away through many vices, hast by the patience of thine immeasurable lovingkindness waited for my amendment. I glorify thee, I praise thee, who hast very often delivered me out of many tribulations and distresses, calamities and miseries; who hast hitherto exempted me from everlasting punishments and from torments of body and soul. I praise thee, I glorify thee, who hast mercifully granted me soundness of limbs, quietness of times, and also the accomplishment of good things, with many other virtues. I beseech thine unspeakable sweetness, O Lord my God, that thou wouldest perfect in me the benefits and gifts which thou hast begun, and drive away whatsoever in me displeaseth thee; that thou wouldest also deliver me from all the tribulations and iniquities and divers evils that yet remain, wherein I am entangled; that thou wouldest {p.226} order my thoughts and my words, and likewise my deeds, according to thy will; keep me always and everywhere, as I walk amid adversity and prosperity; and bring me at the last happily to the longed-for joys of the sight of thee. Who with God the Father and the Holy Ghost livest and reignest, God, world without end. Amen.

# XV. Forms of Prayer in Grievous and Dangerous Sickness, and at the Last also in the Agony of Death
*Formulae precandi in gravi et periculoso morbo, denique etiam in agone mortis*

## Augustine, Meditations, chapter 10

O Lord Jesus Christ, my only hope, grant me so to keep and govern and end my life, that I may sleep in peace, and rest in thee. Grant me at the end that sleep may receive me with rest, rest with security, security in eternity. Give light, O Light, to the blind man who sitteth in darkness and in the shadow of death, and guide my feet into the way of peace, by which I may go {p.231} into the place of the wonderful tabernacle, even unto the house of God, with the voice of joy and praise. Spare me, O Lord, for my days are nothing. I am sick, I cry unto the physician; I am blind, I hasten to the light; I am dead, I sigh for life. Thou art the physician, thou the light, thou the life. Jesus of Nazareth, have mercy on me, thou Son of David; have mercy on me, O fountain of mercy. Hear the sick man that crieth unto thee. O Light that passest by, wait for the blind; reach him thy hand, that he may come unto thee, and in thy light see light. Amen.

## Augustine, Soliloquies, chapter 11

Spare me, O LORD: I am a rotting carcase, food for worms, a stinking vessel. What am I, that I speak with thee? Spare me, O LORD: my life is as a shadow that passeth away; my life is as a flower which groweth on the tree and straightway withereth, now blossometh and straightway is dried up. My life is a frail life, a fleeting life, a life which, the more it increaseth, the more it decreaseth; the further it goeth forward, the nearer it draweth to death. A deceitful and shadowy life, full of the snares of death. And above all these things this is a great misery, that whereas nothing is more certain than death, yet man knoweth not his end, and when he thinketh he standeth, {p.232} he is dashed in pieces, and his hope perisheth. For man knoweth not when, or where, or how he shall die, and yet it is certain that he must die. Behold, O Lord, how great is the misery of man. I will tell therefore, I will tell my misery; I will confess my vileness before thee, and not be ashamed. Help me, O my strength, by which alone I am lifted up; succour me, O power by which I am upheld; come, O light by which I see; appear, O glory by which I rejoice; appear, O life in which I may live, O Lord my God. Come, Lord, and tarry not; come to visit me in peace; come and bring the prisoner out of prison, that I may rejoice before thee with a perfect heart. Come, my Saviour, shew me thy face, and I shall be saved. Come, my light, my Redeemer, bring my soul out of prison, to give thanks unto thy holy name. How long shall I, wretched man, be tossed upon the waves of my mortality? I cry unto thee, O Lord, and thou hearest not; hear me, O Lord, crying out of this great sea, and bring me to the haven of everlasting felicity. O kingdom of everlasting blessedness, where thou, O Lord, the hope of the saints and the crown of glory, art seen face to face by the saints, making them glad on every side in thy peace, which passeth all understanding. That thou wouldest make me also to come thereto, I beseech thee, O Lord Jesus Christ, {p.233} by all thy mercies. Amen.

## Augustine, Soliloquies, chapter 35

O Christ, GOD of God, the hope of mankind, our refuge and strength, whose light from afar, amid the dark clouds above the storms of the sea, shineth upon our eyes like the beam of the star of the sea, that we may be guided unto thee, our haven. Steer our ship, O Lord, with thy right hand, by the helm of thy cross, lest we perish in the waves, lest the tempest of water drown us, lest the deep swallow us up. But by the power of thy cross draw us out of this sea unto thee, our only comfort, whom from afar, as the morning star and the Sun of righteousness, we scarce see with weeping eyes awaiting us on the shore of the heavenly country. Behold, we cry unto thee, thy redeemed, yet even now thine exiles, whom thou hast redeemed with thy precious blood. Hear us, O God of our salvation, the hope of all the ends of the earth; and afar upon the sea, upon a troubled sea, are we tossed; thou, standing upon the shore, beholdest our perils: save us for thy name's sake. Grant us, O Lord, so to hold the middle course between Scylla and Charybdis, that, both dangers being avoided, with ship and wares safe, we may come securely {p.234} to the haven; through the merit of thy most bitter death and passion. Amen.

## Augustine, Meditations, chapter 37

O most sweet and most gracious Jesus Christ, when shall I see thee? when shall I appear before thy face? when shall I be satisfied, O GOD, with thy beauty? when wilt thou bring me out of the dark prison, that I may give thanks unto thy name, so that henceforth I be pierced with sorrow no more? when shall I pass over into that wonderful and most beautiful house of thine, where the voice of joy and gladness soundeth in the tabernacles of the righteous? Blessed are they that dwell in thy house, O LORD: they will praise thee for ever and ever. Blessed, and truly blessed, are they whom thou hast chosen and taken up into that heavenly inheritance. O how wonderful, how fair, how pleasing are the dwellings of thy house, O LORD of hosts! This my sinful soul longeth to enter into them. LORD, I have loved the beauty of thy house, and the place where thy glory dwelleth. One thing have I desired of the LORD, that will I seek after; that I may dwell in the house of the Lord all the days of my life. As the hart panteth after the water brooks, so panteth {p.235} my soul after thee, O GOD. When shall I come and appear before thy face? When shall I see my God, for whom my soul thirsteth? When shall I see him in the land of the living? Here have we no continuing city, but we seek one to come. Nothing is so sweet to me as to be with my GOD; thither I hasten, where the highest peace reigneth. Hold therefore and govern my spirit, and take it up according to thy will, that, with thee for guide, it may ascend into that land of plenty where thou feedest Israel for ever with the food of truth; that there I also may praise thee with all thy saints for ever. Amen.

## Jerome, in the agony of death *Hiero. in agone*

O how great gain it is to me to die, for henceforth to me to live shall be Christ. Behold, the earthly house of this tabernacle is dissolved, that another may succeed, an house not made with hands, eternal in the heavens. Behold, I put off the garment of that which is mortal, that I may be clothed with the eternal. Hitherto have I been a pilgrim; now I return to my own country. Behold, I reach the haven which I have desired with so great desire. Behold, I am carried from darkness to light, from perils to safety, from want to riches, from battle to victory, from sorrow to joy, from temporal {p.236} life to everlasting, from stench to the sweetest savour. Here I am blind, and I receive sight; wounded on every side, and I am healed. Here I have ever been made sorrowful, and behold, I am made glad. Truly, living here I have been dead, and now am I truly made alive. The life of the world is not life, but death: a deceitful life, a life laden with sorrows, weak and shadowy, a lying life. O sweet and pleasant death—surely no death, which bestowest the true life, which puttest to flight fevers and wounds, and quenchest hunger and thirst. O death most just, and kind to the good and harsh to the wicked, thou humblest the proud, the rich and the mighty, and exaltest the humble. Thou givest punishment to the wicked, and to the righteous an everlasting reward. Come, my sister, my spouse, my love, my beloved; tell me of him whom my soul loveth. Shew me where my Lord feedeth, where my Christ lieth down, and suffer me not to wander any longer through the pathless waste of this my sojourning. Arise, my glory, reach me thy hand, draw me after thee. My heart is opened, and I will arise, and run after thee in the savour of thine ointments, until thou bring me into the chambers of the house of my Lord, that I may be glad and rejoice when I shall appear before thy face. Receive, O Lord Jesus Christ, the son that hungereth here in a strange country, and restore {p.237} him to his father. Receive him that is full of sores, and lay him in Abraham's bosom. Make me to enter into thy vineyard, that I stand not here idle. Take me out of the hand of iniquity, and lead me into life everlasting. Amen.

## Jerome, in the same place *Hiero. ibidem*

O merciful Jesus, my strength, my refuge, my upholder and my deliverer, my praise, in whom I have hoped, whom I have believed, and whom I have loved; highest sweetness, tower of strength, and my hope from my youth. Call me, O guide of my life, and I will answer thee. To the work of thine hands, which thou, the Creator of all things, didst form of the dust of the ground, which thou didst knit together with bones and sinews, to which by thy dying thou hast granted life and mercy, stretch forth the right hand of thy mercy. Command, O Lord, tarry not; for it is time that dust return to dust, and that the spirit return to thee, the Saviour, who didst send it hither. Open unto it the gates of life: for when for my sake thou didst hang as a thief upon the tree of the cross, thou didst promise me that thou wouldest receive it. Come, my beloved; let me hold thee, and I will not let thee go; bring me into thy house. Thou art my upholder, my glory, and the lifter up of mine head, my salvation and my blessing. Receive me, O merciful {p.238} God, according to the multitude of thy tender mercies. For dying thou didst receive the thief upon the cross; let me possess thee, O everlasting blessedness. The blind man crying by the way side, Jesus, thou Son of David, have mercy on me—enlighten him with thy light of the eternal vision. O light invisible, O light without which there is no truth, lighten mine eyes, lest I ever sleep in death; lest mine enemy say, I have prevailed against him. My soul is weary of my life; I will speak in the bitterness of my soul. I am sick; my life is weakened in poverty; my bones are parched as in a frying-pan; and therefore I run unto thee, the Lord, the physician. Heal me, O Lord, and I shall be healed; save me, and I shall be saved; and because I trust in thee, I shall not be ashamed. I wait for thee, O Lord; I believe to see the goodness of the Lord in the land of the living. Come therefore, my beloved, turn my mourning into joy for me; bow down thine ear to me; make haste to deliver me out of this vale of tears and miseries. Amen.

## Jerome, in the same place *Hieron. ibidem*

O Lord Jesus Christ, be merciful to me a sinner, that thou mayest be unto me for a help and for a place of refuge, to save me. Behold, O merciful Jesus, I lie dead: {p.239} arise, raise me up, and I will give thanks unto thee. I am sick and weak, and there is no more any soundness in my flesh; thou art the physician, heal me. Bring me out of this snare, my protector, my governor, my strength, my refuge, my guide and my king, into whose hands I commend my spirit, which thou hast redeemed upon the tree of the cross, to which thou hast granted life and mercy. Look, O Lord, upon my low estate, and shut me not up into the hands of the enemy. This day shall I enter with thee into the place of the wonderful tabernacle, that I may dwell in thy house for length of days, for ever and ever. Amen.

## Dionysius

O most sweet Lord Jesus Christ, brightness of the Father's glory and Sun of righteousness, who for me, thine unworthy servant, didst vouchsafe to suffer this most vile form of pain, who on mount Calvary didst give up thy soul for the redemption of the world, and praying didst commend it to the Father: grant me to bear about continually in my breast both the pain and the love of this thy most bitter death, and daily to exercise myself to die with thee by the mortifying of all vices, that when the end of life draweth nigh I may be found worthy {p.240} to breathe again in the light of thy mercies, and happily to enter with thee into the joys of Paradise. Be present with me when I am dying, succour me in mine agony, meet me in my longing, defend me from mine enemies, deliver me out of my miseries, comfort me groaning, strengthen me trembling, revive me fainting, receive me yielding up the ghost. Let thy last word upon the cross be my last word in this light; and when I can speak no more, hear my last desire: Father, into thy hands I commend my spirit; thou hast redeemed me, O Lord God of truth. Amen.

## The Church

O Lord Jesus Christ, Son of the living God, who for the salvation of the world wast given gall and vinegar to drink upon the cross: as thou, when all things were finished, yielding up the ghost upon the cross, didst commend thy spirit into the hands of the Father, so in the hour of my death I commend my soul into the hands of thy lovingkindness, that thou mayest receive it in peace, and command it to be gathered into the choirs of thine elect. Who livest and reignest with God the Father in the unity of the Holy Ghost, God, world without end. Amen.

## The Church

O Lord Jesus, the only salvation of the living, the everlasting life of the dying, to thy most {p.241} holy will I submit and yield myself wholly, whether it please thee that this poor soul abide longer in the dwelling of this poor body, to serve thee, or whether thou wilt have it depart out of this world; being sure that that cannot perish which is committed to thy mercy. This frail and wretched flesh I will lay down with a quiet mind, namely in hope of the resurrection, which shall restore it to me far happier. I beseech thee to strengthen my soul by thy grace against all temptations, and against all the assaults of Satan lift me up with the shield of thy mercy, whereby of old thou didst make thy Martyrs invincible against dreadful torments and most cruel deaths. I see how there is no defence for me in myself; my whole trust is in thine unspeakable goodness. I have nothing of merits or of good works to plead before thee; of evil works, alas, I see too many; but through thy righteousness I trust that I shall be reckoned in the number of the righteous. For me thou wast born, for me thou didst thirst, for me thou didst hunger, for me thou didst teach, for me thou didst pray, for me thou didst fast, for me thou didst accomplish so many good works in this life, for me thou didst suffer things so bitter, for me thou didst give up thy precious soul unto death upon the cross. Let those things now profit me which thou hast freely given, who hast given thy whole self to me. Let thy blood wash away the stains of {p.242} my offences, let thy righteousness cover mine unrighteousness, let thy merits commend me to the supreme Judge. As the sickness groweth heavier, increase thy grace, that faith waver not in me, that hope stagger not, that charity wax not cold, that human weakness be not cast down by the terror of death; but, after death hath seized the eyes of the body, let the eyes of the mind nevertheless not be turned away from thee; and when it hath taken away the use of the tongue, let the heart nevertheless cry earnestly unto thee: Into thy hands, O Lord, I commend my spirit. To whom be honour and glory without end. Amen.

## Prayer in grievous sickness or in the agony of death, gathered out of the Psalms *Precatio in gravi morbo vel agone, ex Psalmis collecta*

O Lord, rebuke me not in thine anger, neither chasten me in thy hot displeasure.

Have mercy upon me, O Lord; for I am weak: O Lord, heal me; for all my bones are vexed.

My soul is also sore vexed: but thou, O Lord, how long?

Return, O Lord, deliver my soul: oh save me for thy mercy's sake.

For in death there is no remembrance of {p.243} thee: in the grave who shall give thee thanks?

I am weary with my groaning; all the night make I my bed to swim; I water my couch with my tears.

My face is consumed because of grief; it waxeth old, while all afflict me.

The sorrows of death compassed me, and the floods of iniquity made me afraid.

The sorrows of hell compassed me about: the snares of death prevented me.

Unto thee, O Lord, do I cry; and unto God I make supplication.

What profit is there in my blood, when I go down to corruption? Shall the dust give thee thanks? shall it declare thy truth?

Hear, O Lord, and have mercy upon me: Lord, be thou my helper.

Make thy face to shine upon thy servant: save me in thy mercy.

Forsake me not, O Lord my God: be not far from me.

Lord, make me to know mine end, and the number of my days, what it is; that I may know that I must cease to be.

Behold, thou hast made my days as an handbreadth; and mine age is as nothing before thee.

{p.244} For every man that liveth secure is nothing but vanity.

Alas, how man passeth away as a shadow, and how vainly are they disquieted! he heapeth up, and knoweth not who shall gather them.

And now, Lord, what wait I for? in thee will I hope.

Deliver me from all my transgressions.

Remove thy strokes away from me: for I am consumed by the chastening of thine hand.

When thou dost correct man for iniquity, thou makest his beauty to consume away like a moth: alas, how every man is vanity!

Hear my prayer, O Lord, and give ear unto my cry; hold not thy peace at my tears: for I am a stranger with thee, and a sojourner, as all my fathers were.

O spare me, that I may recover strength, before I go hence, and be no more.

For innumerable evils have compassed me about: mine iniquities have taken hold upon me, so that I am not able to look up; they are more than the hairs of mine head: and my heart hath failed me.

Be pleased, O Lord, to deliver me: O Lord, make haste to help me.

{p.245} Let them be ashamed and confounded together that seek after my soul to destroy it; let them be driven backward and put to shame that wish me evil.

God will redeem my soul from the power of the grave: for he shall receive me.

Into thine hands, O Lord, I commend my spirit: thou hast redeemed me, O Lord God of truth.

I shall not die, but live, and declare the works of the Lord: I will please the Lord in the land of the living.

## Prayer *Oratio*

O Lord Jesus Christ, I ask for thy Paradise, not for any worth of my merits, seeing I am dust and ashes and a most wretched sinner, but in the virtue and efficacy of thy most holy passion, whereby thou wast pleased to redeem me, wretched man, and didst vouchsafe to purchase Paradise for me with the price of thy blood.

## Thanksgiving for deliverance from sickness *Gratiarum actio pro liberatione ex morbo*

### Psalm 116

I love the Lord, because he hath heard the voice of my prayer; because he hath inclined his ear unto me, therefore will I call upon him in my days.

The cords of death compassed me, and the pains of hell gat hold upon me: I found trouble and sorrow.

Then called I upon the name of the Lord; O Lord, deliver my soul.

Gracious is the Lord, and righteous; yea, our God is merciful.

The Lord preserveth the simple: I was brought low, and he delivered me.

Return unto thy rest, O my soul; for the Lord hath dealt bountifully with thee.

For he hath delivered my soul from death, mine eyes from tears, and my feet from falling.

O Lord, truly I am thy servant; I am thy {p.247} servant, and the son of thine handmaid: thou hast loosed my bonds.

I will offer to thee the sacrifice of thanksgiving, and will call upon the name of the Lord.

I will pay my vows unto the Lord in the presence of all his people.

### Jonah, chapter 2

I cried by reason of mine affliction unto the Lord, and he heard me.

Out of the belly of hell cried I, and thou heardest my voice.

And thou hadst cast me into the deep, in the midst of the sea; and the flood compassed me about.

All thy billows and all thy waves passed over me.

Then I said, I am cast out of thy sight.

Yet I will look again upon thy holy temple.

The waters compassed me about, even to the soul: the depth closed me round about, the sea covered my head.

I went down to the bottoms of the mountains; the bars of the earth shut me in for ever.

Yet thou wilt bring up my life from corruption, O Lord my God.

When my soul fainted within me I remembered {p.248} the Lord: that my prayer might come in unto thee, into thine holy temple.

They that observe vanities forsake their own mercy in vain.

But I will sacrifice unto thee with the voice of thanksgiving; I will pay that that I have vowed, for my salvation, unto the Lord.
