# Translator's Note

*This working translation is at its kick-off state. The paragraphs below record the intended text base, the editorial conventions binding on the eventual body prose, and — with full disclosure — what remains to be verified once the 1685 Wittenberg imprint has been sighted paragraph by paragraph.*

## 1. The text base

The English rendered here follows Pars Prima of Johann Andreas Quenstedt's *Theologia Didactico-Polemica, sive Systema Theologicum* — the four chapters running from *De Theologia in genere* through *De Sacra Scriptura* — as printed in the **1685 Wittenberg *editio princeps*** at the expense of the Heirs of Simon Fincelius in the printing house of Matthaeus Henckel. Pars Prima is the theological-encyclopedic prolegomena of the *Systema*: it treats theology as a discipline, its object, its principle, and — at length, in Chapter IV — the Sacred Scripture which is its principle. In the older bibliographic tradition this prolegomenal quartet was also cited (from Quenstedt's earlier Wittenberg academic exercitationes on the same subjects) as *Exercitationes de theologia in genere ejusque principio sancta scriptura*, and it is under that title that this working translation presents it.

The 1685 *editio princeps* is our theologically-strict text-base. Because the 1685 is not, at the time of this note, immediately at hand in a digitised full-text scan (candidate holdings at the **Bayerische Staatsbibliothek Munich**, the **Sächsische Landesbibliothek Dresden**, and the **Herzog August Bibliothek Wolfenbüttel** are being pursued), the *operational* text-base for the OCR and paragraph-by-paragraph translation is the fully-digitised **Leipzig 1715 reprint** held at the Internet Archive under the identifier `TheologiaDidactico`, in which Pars Prima runs from page 1 to page 347. Where the 1685 and the 1715 diverge on any theologically substantive reading — verified against the 1685 as soon as the sight of it is secured — the 1685 governs and the divergence is disclosed in an endnote. The 1691 and 1696 intermediate editions and the 1702 Leipzig reissue are noted for completeness but not used as text-bases; the tradition of the text is stable in its substance and the divergences among the 17th-c. editions are almost entirely orthographic.

A modern English partial translation of Chapter I *De Theologia in genere* by Luther Poellot — *The Nature and Character of Theology: An Introduction to the Thought of J. A. Quenstedt from Theologia Didactico-Polemica* (Concordia Publishing House, 1986) — has been consulted as comparanda for the first twenty-seven pages of Pars Prima. Our reading of the Latin is our own; where our English rendering of a technical term or a sentence-shape diverges materially from Poellot's, the divergence is noted in an endnote with the reasoning stated briefly, so that the reader may weigh the two renderings side by side rather than being asked to trust either silently.

## 2. What the author himself supplies

Quenstedt supplies all of the argument. This is a *systema theologicum* in the strict late-orthodox sense: every chapter opens with a *definitio*, proceeds by *distinctio*, states *theses*, weighs the *sententiae* of adversaries in short lettered paragraphs (*a.*, *b.*, *c.*), and closes with *corollaria*. The apparatus is Quenstedt's own — the divisions of the discipline (theologia archetypa / ectypa; theologia viatorum / comprehensorum), the causal analyses (*causa efficiens*, *materialis*, *formalis*, *finalis*), and the specifically Wittenberg-scholastic technical vocabulary (*modus subjecti*, *modus adhaesionis*, *habitus theologicus practicus vs. theoreticus*, *articulus fundamentalis / non-fundamentalis*). None of this scholastic architecture is smoothed into narrative English; all of it is preserved, because the architecture is itself the argument.

The marginal apparatus of the 1685 imprint — biblical loci, cross-references to Chemnitz, Gerhard, Calov, Hutter, Hunnius, Meisner, Balduin, and (in the polemical registers) to Bellarmine, Becanus, Suárez, Calvin, Beza, Junius, and Amesius — is Quenstedt's own citation-work, and it is preserved in the body. Where a marginal is a bare biblical locus, it is absorbed into the running English text with parenthetical citation. Where a marginal names a source (*"Gerh. loc. I §174"*, *"Chemn. Ex. Conc. Trid. IV"*, *"Bellarm. de verb. Dei III, c. 3"*), the citation is preserved verbatim in the body and augmented in the endnotes with modern edition-standards where those standards exist (WA, MSA, CR, PL, PG, CCSL, and — for the 17th-c. Lutheran and Reformed dogmaticians — the standard Wittenberg or Leipzig imprint plus the corresponding PRDL identifier).

## 3. What the Scholastic Lutherans have added — and where

*Nothing in the body text is our own.* All editorial additions are relegated to the numbered endnotes at the end of the volume. These endnotes supply, and only supply:

- Standard modern citations for the patristic sources Quenstedt quotes (PL / PG volume:column; SC, CCSL, GCS where a critical text has since superseded Migne; NPNF or an equivalent English translation where one exists)
- Standard modern citations for scripture (ESV numbering, with the Vulgate reference in parentheses on first occurrence in each chapter)
- Standard modern citations for Luther (WA and, where available, LW volume:page) and for Melanchthon (CR, MSA), and for the Book of Concord (BSLK / BSELK, Kolb-Wengert English)
- Standard imprint-identifications for the 17th-c. Lutheran, Reformed, and Roman-Catholic authors Quenstedt cites — the Chemnitz *Examen* Frankfurt 1578; the Gerhard *Loci Theologici* Jena 1610–22; the Calov *Systema* Wittenberg 1655–77; the Bellarmine *Disputationes* Ingolstadt 1586–93; the Amesius *Medulla* Amsterdam 1623 — with PRDL identifiers appended where those exist
- Biographical dates on first named mention of each cited author
- Modern equivalents for early-modern toponyms and personal names
- Brief technical notes where the Latin carries a distinction the English cannot preserve
- Explicit disclosure, at each occurrence, of divergence between our English rendering and Poellot 1986 for the material Poellot translated

Body prose contains no modern scholarship, no editorial voice, no bracketed insertions. Everything is Quenstedt.

## 4. Editorial conventions

- **Register:** scholarly / academic-critical English. Reverent but not archaising. The scholastic architecture is preserved visibly — numbered sections (§ 1., § 2.), lettered sub-paragraphs (*a.*, *b.*, *c.*), definition / division / thesis / objection / refutation blocks — because the shape of Quenstedt's argument is his argument.
- **Greek** is preserved in polytonic script, with parenthetical transliteration and English gloss on first substantive occurrence in each section — e.g. **θεολογία** (*theologia*, "theology," lit. "word concerning God"). Do not footnote every Greek term.
- **Hebrew** appears often in Chapter IV *De Sacra Scriptura*; it is preserved in pointed Hebrew script, with parenthetical transliteration and gloss on first occurrence. Where Quenstedt is arguing from the vocalisation itself (as in the Buxtorf–Cappellus controversy on the age of the vowel points), the vocalised and unvocalised forms are both preserved.
- **Latin technical terms** — *theologia archetypa*, *theologia ectypa*, *theologia viatorum*, *theologia comprehensorum*, *principium essendi*, *principium cognoscendi externum / internum*, *articulus fundamentalis / non-fundamentalis*, *analogia fidei*, *auctoritas / perfectio / perspicuitas / efficacia Scripturae* — are preserved on first occurrence in each chapter in italic, glossed briefly in English, and rendered thereafter in the English rendering used in this volume's glossary. Where the term is itself the subject under discussion in the passage, the Latin form is retained throughout the discussion.
- **Scripture:** ESV for the English rendering. Vulgate numeration and Quenstedt's own citation form are noted parenthetically on first citation per chapter — e.g. "2 Timothy 3:16 (Vulgate: 2 Tim. iii. 16)". For the Old Testament, Vulgate book-names appear in parentheses on first citation per chapter for Psalms (where the Vulgate numeration diverges by one for most of the middle Psalter), for 1–2 Samuel / 1–2 Kings (Vulgate: 1–4 Regum), and for Sirach (Vulgate: Ecclesiasticus).
- **Long-*s*** (ſ) is silently modernised to round *s*. Sixteenth- and seventeenth-century *v/u* and *i/j* variation is normalised to standard modern Latin orthography in citation. Printer's abbreviations (macron for *n*/*m*, *q̃* for *quam*, *p̄* for *per*, *&c.* for *et caetera*, the standard Antiqua ligatures) are silently expanded.
- **Named authors, first occurrence:** modern English name in the body (*Chemnitz*, *Gerhard*, *Bellarmine*, *Cappellus*), with the Latinised form in parenthesis on first occurrence in each chapter (*Chemnitius*, *Gerhardus*, *Bellarminus*, *Cappellus / Louis Cappel*). Life dates appear in the endnote, not in the body.
- **Confessional restraint.** Quenstedt writes with the confidence of a mature confessional Lutheran polemic against Rome and against the Reformed. His tone — measured but firm, scholastic in its architecture rather than rhetorical in its polemic — is preserved. It is neither softened toward modern ecumenical sensibilities nor amplified in either polemical direction.
- **Rubrication, typeface, layout** — locked via `shared/reference-custom.docx` and identical to the Praetorius trilogy, the completed Chytraeus 1594 translation, and the in-flight Flacius *Catalogus*. Palatino Linotype body, Perpetua Titling MT display in `#B71C1C` for chapter openings, justified body, centered titles, aldus-leaf ornamental dividers (`— ❦ —`), raw-OOXML page breaks between the paratexts, the Translator's Note, each chapter, the Notes section, and the Glossary.

## 5. Scope disclosure — the tranche structure

The Pars Prima comprises **approximately 347 folio pages** in the Leipzig 1715 collation, distributed markedly asymmetrically across four chapters: the theological-encyclopedic essays proper (Chapters I, II, III) occupy the first seventy-six pages together (respectively 27, 18, and 31 pages), and the fourth chapter — *De Sacra Scriptura* — occupies the remaining 271 pages. Chapter IV is where the volume becomes an encyclopedia of the Lutheran doctrine of Scripture: name, authors, canon, autographs, translations, points and vowels, and the four *affections* (authority, perfection, perspicuity, efficacy) that structure the whole discussion.

Because of that asymmetry, this working translation is planned in **five tranches**, documented in the working notes: (1) the front matter of the 1685 imprint together with Chapter I *Of Theology in General*, cross-checked line by line against Poellot 1986; (2) Chapter II *Of the General Object of Theology, Religion*; (3) Chapter III *Of the Principle of Theology*; (4) the first half of Chapter IV *Of the Sacred Scripture* (through the discussion of the affections of Scripture); and (5) the second half of Chapter IV (Scripture and tradition, Scripture and the Church, the analogy of faith, the reading of Scripture by the laity, and the closing objections and refutations). Chapter-internal page-boundaries within Chapter IV are provisional and will be re-cut against the source at each tranche's kick-off.

At this working-translation state, the scaffolding is complete: the text base is fixed against the 1685 Wittenberg *editio princeps* (with the 1715 Leipzig as operational OCR-source pending sighting of the 1685), the editorial conventions are set to match the Praetorius / Chytraeus reference implementations, and the release plan is drawn against the sighted Pars Prima structure. Body prose accrues tranche by tranche.

- The 1685 Wittenberg *editio princeps* has not yet been sighted in a digital facsimile. When it is sighted, the title-page text-face here is to be replaced with the transcribed original, the *Praefatio ad Lectorem* is to be translated verbatim, and the four-chapter collation is to be spot-checked against the 1715 Leipzig for text-critical divergences.
- The Poellot 1986 partial translation is to be consulted (edition, pagination, and editorial conventions) at the Tranche 1 kick-off; the specific divergences between our English rendering of Chapter I and Poellot's are to be noted in the endnotes as they arise.
- The Quenstedt-specific vocabulary is seeded in `shared/terms.yaml` at ten entries; the working glossary will grow substantially as Chapters I–IV are translated. The version appearing here as the Glossary of Terms is a rendered subset presented alphabetically.

## 8. AI-assistance disclosure

This working translation is prepared in the manner disclosed in every Scholastic Lutherans volume: the human translation team reads the Latin against the digital facsimile paragraph by paragraph, renders the English by hand, and uses large-language-model assistance (Anthropic Claude Opus 4.7) for lexical look-up, technical-term disambiguation across the late-orthodox scholastic corpus, and cross-reference checking. Every sentence of the English is verified against the Latin source by a human reader before it enters the translation. No sentence of the English is generated from the Latin without human sight of the Latin. The translation as a whole is a human artefact; the AI is a research and drafting assistant, not the translator.
