## Structure (per DigiHadw record description)

1. Title page + imprint (recto side of Blatt 1)
2. Sehling's editorial preface / **Vorrede** (Roman-numeral pages `000_ROEM_*`)
3. Table of contents / **Inhaltsverzeichnis**
4. Introductions to each region (Luther's orders → Ernestine Saxony → Albertine Saxony)
5. Church-order texts themselves (bulk of the volume)
6. Chronological index
7. Personnel + geographic index
8. Subject index (Sachregister)

## Genre-specific translation conventions (Sehling-corpus)

Different from the dogmatic-treatise conventions used for Selnecker / Kirchner / Chytraeus. Nail down early; propagate to remaining Sehling volumes.

- **Preserve Sehling's own editorial voice** intact — his 1902 apparatus (introductions, source-critical notes, dating discussions, textual variants) is itself historically important and should be translated faithfully, not paraphrased.
- **Church-order texts themselves** — the actual 16th-century *Kirchenordnungen* — are period documents of *practical Lutheran ecclesiology*: liturgy, catechesis, ordination, visitation, discipline, marriage, poor-relief, school ordering. Translate in an appropriately formal register that mirrors their public/legal character. They were often issued by prince or magistrate under official seal; treat them as constitutional documents, not devotional ones.
- **Territorial names** — keep German names in English body ("Kurfürstentum Sachsen" not "Electorate of Saxony") on first occurrence with English gloss in parentheses, English thereafter. Exception: universally-Englished names ("Saxony", "Thuringia", "Franconia") use English throughout.
- **Personal names** — use the form Sehling gives (which is typically the historical German spelling). Cross-reference modern-critical apparatus to endnotes.
- **Liturgical Latin** in the orders (Kyrie, Introit, Collect, Postcommunion etc.) — preserve verbatim; gloss in endnote if the specific text is non-obvious.
- **Currency, weights, measures** as given in the source; endnote for modern-equivalent approximation only when the sum is material to the narrative (e.g. "a chaplain shall receive X gulden per annum").
- **Bible quotations** in the orders — preserve the German wording, note the source (Luther Bible edition varies by decade), point to modern chapter+verse in endnote.

## Editorial conventions (Sehling apparatus)

Sehling's own notational system, to be preserved in translation:

- **[Square brackets]** — editorial insertions or reconstructions
- **⟨Angle brackets⟩** — variants from parallel witnesses
- **Italic** — used for cross-references and technical terms
- **Roman numeral chapter/paragraph headings** — preserve; do not renumber
- **Small caps** — used for proper names of legal instruments (*Formula Concordiae* etc.)

## Copyright

Sehling's own 1902 text and the 16th-century Kirchenordnungen themselves are all public domain (Sehling † 1928, well past 70-year German pma). The DigiHadw *digital edition* is marked "In Copyright" — fine for private team-internal use. Any future public release of a translated Band I is unproblematic on source-copyright grounds; the DigiHadw digital-edition claim is a compilation/database right question separate from the underlying text.
