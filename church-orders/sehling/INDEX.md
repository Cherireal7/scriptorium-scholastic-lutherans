# Sehling — *Die evangelischen Kirchenordnungen des XVI. Jahrhunderts*

> **Master index** for the Scholastic Lutherans translation program's Sehling corpus.
> 24 territorial volumes (in 29 Teilbände) + 1 Generalregister = 25 physical artefacts.
> Critical-edition project **1902 → 2020** (118 years, three editorial phases).

---

## Publication history

**Publisher:** Leipzig, O. R. Reisland (Bände I–V, 1902–1913); thereafter Tübingen, J. C. B. Mohr / Mohr Siebeck (Bände VI–XXIV, 1955–2017 + Generalregister 2020).

**Editorial phases:**

1. **Sehling era, Erlangen (1902–1913)** — Emil Sehling (1861–1928), jurist at Erlangen, founded the project. Published Bände I–V (1902, 1904, 1909, 1911, 1913). His death and the disruption of the two World Wars halted the project for over four decades.
2. **Göttingen era, EKD Institute (1955–1980)** — After WWII, the Institute for Evangelical Church Law of the German Protestant Church (EKD) in Göttingen resumed the work under Rudolf Smend, Otto Weber, and Ernst Wolf; J. F. Gerhard Goeters assumed editorship in 1970. Produced Bände VI–XV (1955, 1957, 1961, 1963, 1965, 1966, 1969, 1977, 1980).
3. **Heidelberg era, HAdW (2002–2020)** — Since 2002 the edition has been directed by the Heidelberg Academy of Sciences (HAdW) under Gottfried Seebaß († 2008) and Eike Wolgast. Published Bände XVI–XXIV plus multiple back-fill Teilbände (VII/2/2 in 2016; IX in 2011; X in 2012; XVII/1 in 2007; XVII/2 in 2009; XVIII in 2006; XIX/1–2 in 2008; XX/1 in 2011; XX/2 in 2013; XXI in 2015; XXII in 2017; XXIII in 2017; XXIV in 2012), completing the geographic corpus by 2017 and issuing the **Generalregister in 2020** (prepared by Karin Meese, ed. Wolgast).

## Corpus scale

At ~700–800 pages per Teilband × 29 Teilbände + Generalregister, the total corpus is **~20,000–25,000 pages** of Reformation-era Lutheran ecclesiastical constitutional material plus modern critical apparatus. Language mix is early modern printed German (much easier OCR than Fraktur incunabula) with Latin passages in liturgical formularies. Modern editorial apparatus (1902–2020) is scholarly German.

At Praetorius/Selnecker cadence this is a **multi-year sub-program** running in parallel with the [[scholastic-lutherans-program]] dogmatic-treatise corpus. Sensible near-term commitment: master index + Band I as proof-of-concept, then reassess sequencing based on which territorial orders are highest-value to English-language readers of Lutheran orthodoxy.

## Sequencing note (draft)

If translating selectively rather than sequentially, the doctrinally load-bearing volumes for Lutheran orthodoxy are (in probable order):

1. **Band I** (1902) — Luther's own orders + Saxony. Foundational.
2. **Band XIV** (1969) — Kurpfalz. Melanchthonian / Reformed-borderland; critical for understanding the Second Reformation.
3. **Band XI** (1961) — Franconia, esp. Brenz's Nürnberg + Osiander. Formative early Lutheran urban orders.
4. **Band VIII** (1965) — Common Hessen orders. Philipp of Hesse's territorial church.
5. **Band V** (1913) — Mecklenburg + Lübeck + Hamburg + baltic territories. Chytraeus / Chemnitz territory.
6. **Band VI/1** (1955) — Wolfenbüttel + Braunschweig. Chemnitz's own diocese.

Full-corpus sequencing decision deferred until pilot outcome.
