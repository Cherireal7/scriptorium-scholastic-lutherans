## Checked and confirmed, not changed

- Misprint notes on p.97 (img 105): the print reads "Multi debent ministerium" and "alle-/gant ad dignitatem ministrorum" exactly as endnotes C3-4 and C3-6 say (checked on an enlarged crop). Both emendations (*delent*, *alligant*) kept.
- Endnote C3-3 (p.95, img 103): the print reads "in monte Sion". Kept.
- Source endnotes kept as they stand: C3-1 (Athenian ephebic oath, "both alone and with many"); C3-2 (Deut. 16:10 *missath*); C3-5 (Schwenckfeld / "Stenckfeld"); C3-8 (Juvenal 7.203, "Paenituit multos vanae sterilisque cathedrae"); C3-9 (Horace, *Ars Poetica* 413); C3-10 (Hesiod, *Works and Days* 308); C3-12 (Sulpicius Severus, *Epistle* 3, on the death of Martin).
- Greek: every Greek word on the 24 images compared with the print; no misreading found.

## Doubtful, not changed

- p.95 (img 103) — Deut. 16 "oblationem spontaneam manus tuae": the English has "freewill oblation of thine hand" where KJV has "freewill offering". Left, because the whole section turns on the word *oblatio* ("Another question, concerning oblation").
- p.93 (img 101) — "Misericordiam magis volo, quàm sacrificium": the English "I will have mercy rather than sacrifice" follows Selnecker's *magis … quàm* (KJV "and not sacrifice"). Left as following the Latin.
- p.98 (img 106) — Jer. 17 and Hag. 1; p.108 (img 116) — Rom. 13 "iudicium sibi accersit"; p.109 (img 117) — Exod. 20, Lev. 20, Prov. "Maledicens parentibus extinguetur", Job 14; p.110 (img 118) — "Aufertur iustus, ne malitia pervertatur"; p.111 (img 119) — Isa. 56 [57]; p.116 (img 124) — Deut. 19: Selnecker paraphrases or abridges; the English follows his Latin and was left.
- p.94 (img 102) — "videlicet, quia efficiunt ordinem": the English has "namely, that they bring about order" (*quia* strictly "because"). Defensible; left.
- p.101 (img 109) — virtue 10, "semper respondens verae pietati": the English makes it "a glory always answering to…"; the participle may equally belong to the virtue itself. Left.
- p.97 (img 105) — endnote C3-4: *debent* → *delent* is the translator's conjecture; it suits the sense (the Anabaptists and Schwenckfeldians do away with the outward ministry), but another verb is possible.
- Selnecker's own references that do not match the modern location are kept as printed, without a note: "Matth. decimo" (Suffer the little children: Mark 10), "Philipp. 2" (Phil. 1), "Prouerb. 10" for "Maledicens parentibus" (Prov. 20), "Esaiae quinquagesimosexto" (Isa. 57).
