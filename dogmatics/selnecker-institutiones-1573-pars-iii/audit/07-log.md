## Doubtful, not changed

- p.137 (img 145) — Pindar: the print seems to read "μωχαναὶ" (an omega) where the file has "μαχαναὶ" (Pindar's word). The glyph is not clear enough to put a misprint into the text.
- p.138 (img 146) — "τῷ καὶ οὔτι": changed on the strength of the glyph (a tau with omega and iota subscript); if a later reader sees "τὸ", Homer has "τὸ".
- p.130 (img 138) — "Home. ἰλιάδ. 6." stands in the print between the first Greek line and its Latin; the file puts "Homer, Iliad 6" after the first line, which matches the print. Endnote C4-10 (first line Iliad XVIII, 309; second line Iliad VI, 339) is correct and kept.
- p.122 (img 130) — Ovid, "Et vindex in matre, patris malus vltor Orestes": the English follows the print's comma ("avenger upon his mother, an evil avenger of his father"); Ovid's own sense is "avenger of his father upon his mother, an evil avenger". Defensible, left.
- p.126 (img 134) — Prov. 8 "senatores ius administrant": left as "senators administer justice" (KJV "princes decree justice" differs).
- p.118 (img 126) — "Lex est iniustis posita" left after the Latin (KJV 1 Tim. 1:9 is worded differently). The same for Sirach 22 (p.127), Sirach 10 and Prov. 28 (p.128), Rom. 13 "tibi ad vindictam" (p.131), Ps. 44 second half and the Psalm 127 distich (p.137).
- p.132 (img 140) — "Christus Luc. 3 docet Milites": so the print (it is John the Baptist in Luke 3); left without a note.
