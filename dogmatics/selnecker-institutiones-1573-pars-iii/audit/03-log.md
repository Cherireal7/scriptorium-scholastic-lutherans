## Changes

- p.33 (img 041) — print: "Omne Catechismos latus, & Postilla reponit" — was: "and with a postil" — now: "and with postils" — kind: mistranslation (number; *Postilla* is the plural object beside *Catechismos*)
- p.35 (img 043) — print: "Scripta pater, nostra Vates aetate Lutherus" — was: "our father, the prophet of our age" — now: "the father, the prophet of our age" — kind: addition (*nostra* belongs to *aetate* only)
- p.37 (img 045) — print: "Simpliciter verum diuina mente professus" — was: "with a mind taught of God" — now: "with a divine mind" — kind: addition (gloss in place of the word)
- p.43 (img 051) — print: "Et noua sectantur, rationi congrua stultae" — was: "novelties, foolish things agreeable to reason" — now: "novelties, agreeable to foolish reason" — kind: mistranslation (case; *stultae* agrees with *rationi*)
- endnote B-9 — added the Vulgate wording *currus Israel et auriga eius* (the KJV "horsemen" alone does not show where *auriga* comes from) — kind: endnote
- endnote B-10 (Staphylus) — was: "drew up a table of the sects that he derived from Luther, counting Anabaptists and Sacramentarians among the 'Lutherans'" — now: "set out the sects that he derived from Luther; it is this reckoning of Anabaptists and deniers of the Supper among the 'Lutherans' that the poem rejects" — kind: endnote (softened: the "table" and the exact contents are not checked against the book; name, dates, Wittenberg, conversion and title/year kept)
- endnote B-11 (fold, pp. 39–40) — added *mos* (p.39, the print shows "nos" at the fold) and *pontus* (p.40, the print shows "po.tus") to the list of supplied words — kind: endnote

## Doubtful, not changed

- p.32 (img 040) "nostris proponimus Aulis" — "in our halls". With the capital and the Dresden setting "to our courts" is likely meant; "halls" is not wrong.
- p.36 (img 044) "Ut supra vires humanas dixerit esse … quisquis modo vera fatetur" — "that he declares it to be above the strength of man … whoever will but confess the truth". *dixerit* is potential ("would say"); the sense is kept.
- p.39 (img 047) "seu nomine clari Emineant, seu confictae nunc nomina pacis … iactent" — the translator takes the subject as indefinite ("whether men … do vaunt"). The Latin does not say whose; left.
- p.39 (img 047) "hac vulgus texit sub veste lupinum Vulpina virus" — *vulpina* agrees with *veste* ("under this fox's garment"); the English gives it as an apposition to keep the line. Sense intact.
- p.42 (img 050) "Esse Catechetas tu nos sine" — "men of the Catechism". *Catechetae* is properly "catechists"; in the context (babes and sucklings) the looser word is defensible.
- p.43 (img 051) "palmam merito sibi sumit ab illo … Luthero" — "taketh the palm from that … Luther". Meant is "next after Luther"; the literal English can be misread as "wins it away from".
