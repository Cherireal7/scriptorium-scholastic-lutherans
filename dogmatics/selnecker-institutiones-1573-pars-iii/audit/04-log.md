## Doubtful, not changed
- p.55, p.59 (img 063, 067) — "Non habebis Deos alienos coram me" stands as "Thou shalt not have strange Gods before me" (KJV: "Thou shalt have no other gods before me"). Left, because the Latin says *alienos*, the Antithesis on p.59 expounds "Dii alieni", and part 01 has the same wording; part 10 (line 458) uses the KJV form. The editor may want one form for the whole volume.
- p.48 (img 056) — "Secundum simile est priori" stands as "The second is like unto the former" (KJV "like unto it"); the exposition turns on *priori*. "Qui dicit se diligere Deum, & proximum suum odit" and "Quae uni fecistis ex minimis istis, qui in me credunt" are Selnecker's paraphrases and follow his Latin.
- p.49 (img 057) — "In praeceptis meis ambulate" ("Walk ye in my commandments") and "Nihil addite, & nihil auferte": free citations, left after the Latin.
- p.55 (img 063) — "Eduxi te de Aegypto" left as "I brought thee out of Egypt" (no relative in the Latin).
- p.64 (img 072) — sixth Greek line: the print's letter after β in "βρίθεις" could be read ει ("βείθεις"); the same glyph stands for ρ in "χειρὶ" on that line, so βρίθεις is kept.
- p.66 (img 074) — "πάντῃ": the iota subscript cannot be confirmed or excluded on the image.
- p.68 (img 076) — first Sibylline line: the translation ends it with a comma; the print has no clear stop there.
- p.62 (img 070) — print "in gentibus testimoniis" read as "ingentibus testimoniis" ("mighty testimonies"); right, no note needed.
