### How manifold are good works?

The kinds of good works are to be distributed according to the order of the commandments of the Decalogue, as shall be shown a little hereafter in the table following. But in general good works are threefold, namely: works of the love of God; works of charity toward the neighbour; works of every man's calling.

### What are the works of the love of God?

Those which are contained in the first table of the Decalogue.

### What are the works of charity toward the neighbour?

Those which are commanded in the second table, concerning which these testimonies are to be held fast. **Luke 6**: *"Be ye merciful, as your Father also is merciful."* **Matt. 9**: *"I will have mercy, and not sacrifice."* **Prov. 14**: *"He that hath pity upon the poor lendeth unto the Lord."*[^D2-1]

### Who is called our neighbour?

Every man, whether known or unknown, whether he hath deserved well or ill of us, who standeth in need of our counsel and help; whom we ought to help with heart, mouth, and deed, by thinking well of him, by speaking well of him, and by succouring him either with counsel or with the thing itself.

### For what causes is he called neighbour?

Because, first, all men are bound one to another by a <!-- p.184 -->threefold bond: of creation, of likeness, and of the common life or human society. Secondly, Christians are moreover bound one to another by a fourfold bond, namely:

1. Of the mystical body, whose head is Christ.
2. Of regeneration.
3. Of the common calling unto the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace.
4. Of the glory to come.

### What are the works of the calling?

Those which every man's lawful function, whether ecclesiastical, or political and civil, or domestic (*oeconomica*), requireth, *"with all lowliness and meekness,"* **Ephes. 4**.

### How manifold is the calling?

Twofold: common and personal.

### What is the common calling?

It is the calling whereby we all and every one have been called out of the world by the voice of the Gospel, that we may be Christians, that is, that we may perform kingly and priestly offices, in faith in Christ, the highest King and Priest.

### What are the kingly offices of Christians?

1. To be angry with oneself because of sin.
2. With courageous faith and firm confidence to lay hold on the shield, which is the promise of mercy for the Son's sake.
3. To have dominion over sin, the flesh, and the world.
4. In the victory of Christ patiently and valiantly to bear the cross, to overcome the devil and death.

Being clothed with righteousness, to be a good example unto others. These kingly offices are most elegantly described in the Christian soldier, **Ephes. 6**.

### What are the priestly offices of Christians?

<!-- p.185 -->

To teach, to call upon God, and to offer unto God:

1. A broken spirit.
2. The sacrifice of righteousness, or an humble faith, laying hold on the merit of Christ and applying it to itself.
3. The sacrifice of praise and of confession.
4. The sacrifices of beneficence, which are the offices of charity.
5. The reasonable sacrifice of their own bodies, that is, the new obedience in general.

These priestly offices have for their foundation humility in the blood of Christ, even as the kingly offices have fortitude in the same blood of Christ.

### Why is the new obedience of Christians called a sacrifice in holy Scripture?

For four causes:

1. That we may know that our obedience pleaseth God for the Son's sake.
2. That we may know that obedience is earnestly required by God.
3. That the godly may be discerned from the ungodly.
4. That there may stand forth a testimony of our thankfulness toward God.

### Of what sort are the sacrifices of Christians?

They are all ***εὐχαριστικά***, that is, of praise. For the right of teaching the Word of God (every man's calling being kept), and the right of calling upon God, and the right of offering the sacrifice of praise, proceed from a thankful mind, which acknowledgeth the immeasurable mercy of God. But the sacrifice of Christ alone is ***ἱλαστικόν***, or propitiatory, meritorious, and expiatory.

### What is the personal calling?

It is the lawful and faithful discharge of some office proper to a certain person, commanded and founded in the Word of God, done in faith and charity, patiently bearing adversities, and directed to the glory of God and the profit of the commonwealth, the help of God being implored.

<!-- p.186 -->

### What are the degrees of the personal calling?

There are three chief degrees, of which it is said:

> *Thou, pray as a suppliant; thou, protect; and thou, labour.*  

And Shem betokened the ecclesiastical, Japheth the political, Ham the domestic kind of life; as did also Abel, Seth, and Cain.

### What are the kinds of the political calling?

Every commonwealth (*politia*) is either:

- **Monarchy**
    - lawful, ***βασιλεία*** (kingship);
    - violent, ***τύραννις*** (tyranny).
- **Oligarchy**
    - ***ἀριστοκρατία*** (aristocracy), lawful;
    - ***τιμοκρατία ἢ ὀλιγαρχία*** (timocracy or oligarchy), violent.
- ***Δημοκρατία*** (democracy)
    - ***πολιτεία ἢ δημοκρατία*** (polity or democracy);
    - ***ὀχλοκρατία ἢ ἀναρχία*** (mob-rule or anarchy).

### How do the degrees of dignity differ in the ecclesiastical function?

The power of all ministers is the same, and there is but one head and one High Priest, Jesus Christ; but the orders of dignity are unequal, and these consist in three things:

1. In the excellency of gifts.
2. In the greatness of labours.
3. In the diversity of calling.

This pre-eminence is not of lordship, but of ministry.

### What is the household government (*oeconomia*)?

It is the administration and government of a private house, ***παρὰ τὸ τὸν οἶκον νέμεσθαι*** (so named from "ordering the house"), instituted by God in Paradise, the fountain of the other orders in mankind, the Church and the commonwealth, <!-- p.187 -->consisting of three yokes, as it were: husband and wife; parents and children; servants and masters; which have their authority and their degrees of offices from God, to this end, that it may be a school wherein men may be instructed concerning God and good things, and may become temples of God, and may serve the life and society of men, and be saved for ever.

### II. How can good works be done?

As the Law sheweth what works are to be done, so the Gospel sheweth when works can be done, namely in these ways:

1. By the consideration of the commandment of God.
2. By the knowledge of Christ and by confidence, or faith in Christ.
3. By quickening (*vivificatio*), that is, by the presence and efficacy of the Holy Ghost, who enlighteneth the mind with the knowledge of Christ and with faith, and moveth the will to the beginning of a willing obedience, and kindleth invocation in us.

For this cause it hath been commonly said that there are three causes of good works:

1. The Word of God, which is as it were the body.
2. The Holy Ghost, who is as it were the soul of the Word.
3. The mind and will of man, which the Holy Ghost teacheth, bendeth, moveth, ruleth, leadeth, and in which and through which he putteth forth and bringeth forth his own actions.

Hither belong the sayings: *"Whatsoever is not of faith is sin."* *"Without faith it is impossible to please God."* Likewise: *"Without me ye can do nothing."*

### How were the heathen able to do good works?

The virtues of the heathen are not called in the holy Scriptures good works pleasing to God. For these properly are such as are done of faith in Christ. But the virtues of the heathen, even the most splendid, are comprehended under this rule: *"Whatsoever <!-- p.188 -->is not of faith is sin."* For although the work seemeth to be good, and in respect of the work considered by itself is worthy of praise and admiration, yet, seeing the person is not reconciled, the work also which is performed by a person not yet reconciled is not called by the Holy Ghost a good work which can please God, but is referred only to outward discipline, which is a good of nature, not a good of grace. But grace being taken away, the whole of nature is under sin.

**On the contrary** (*Contra*):

> The things which are of the Law are good.  
> The works which the heathen did are works of the Law, **Rom. 1**.  
> Therefore they are good works.  

**I answer.** I grant the whole, if we understand the good of nature as created by God, which even after the fall doth yet in some manner remain, and is required for the preservation and tranquillity of human society. But that same good doth not prove that nature is good and without sin. And seeing nature is corrupt, it cannot be made good save by grace. If the good of grace be wanting, the good of nature is nothing else before God than that which **Paul** saith: *"Whatsoever is not of faith is sin."* Therefore **Augustine** saith excellently: *"Human righteousness compared with the divine righteousness is unrighteousness; for a lamp also is seen to shine in the darkness, but set in the beam of the sun it is darkened."* And **Ambrose** saith: *"Not they are righteous who hear the Law; but they who believe in Christ, whom the Law promised, these do the Law."* And the rule is most true: Whatsoever is not perfect, pure, willing, perpetual, and in no wise hindered, that is rejected and condemned by the voice of the divine Law, that is, it is sin.

### How do the virtues of the heathen differ from the good works of Christians?

<!-- p.189 -->

They differ in four ways, namely: 1. in the impelling causes; 2. in the efficient causes; 3. in the form; 4. in the end.

The **impelling causes** which bend the heathen and the unregenerate to the pursuit of virtue are four. The first is the dignity of virtue. The second is the reverence due toward superiors and those who have deserved well: magistrates, parents, teachers, the aged. The third is the reward set before honesty, whether inward, as tranquillity of mind and gladness of conscience, or outward, as a good name. The fourth is punishment, which cometh at length with silent feet.[^D2-2] These four causes are not disallowed by Christians, but are adorned and made more illustrious, six other causes being added besides, which are:

1. The commandment of God.
2. The end of creation, redemption, and sanctification.
3. The remembrance of the divine mercy.
4. The glory of God.
5. The nature and safety of faith, of which he that violateth godliness maketh shipwreck.
6. The sure hope of the glorious resurrection, and of the judgment to come, and of the account to be rendered.

The **efficient causes** of virtue in the unregenerate are two: 1. the judgment of right reason; 2. the freedom of the will, which in some manner bridleth the outward members. But in the regenerate they are the Word of God, faith, and the Holy Ghost.

The **form** of the virtues in the unregenerate:

1. Is altogether maimed, and is mutilated and imperfect not only in quality, but also in knowledge, in number, and in relation.
2. Is hypocritical, and defiled with many false opinions concerning God and with depraved lusts.
3. Is very seldom willing, but for the most part wrung out by the fear of punishment.
4. Is destitute of invocation and of confidence in the divine help.
5. Is destitute also of the humility of a mind acknowledging its weakness and imperfection and its own uncleanness.

But the form of the virtues in the regenerate:

1. Is entire, as touching the knowledge of all the virtues, inward and outward, and as touching the number, and the relation to God and to the neighbour and to oneself, although the quality be not perfect; which nevertheless <!-- p.190 -->they have perfect in Christ.
2. Next, the regenerate hate all hypocrisy, and endeavour to purge out the old leaven, that is, depraved lusts and opinions.
3. Afterward, they render a willing obedience, not being constrained, according to that saying: *"Though he slay me, yet will I trust in him."*
4. Fourthly, the form of the virtues in the regenerate is always joined with invocation and with confidence in the divine aid.
5. Fifthly, the regenerate humble themselves, and confess that they are unprofitable servants.

As the **end** of their virtues the unregenerate have principally their own glory; whence also they use the name of virtue (*virtus*), taken from *vir*, a man, even as the Greeks say ***ἀρετήν*** from ***ἀρέσκω***, "I please", and the Germans *Tugend*, that is, that which is becoming, that it may be one's own honour and ornament. But the regenerate have as the end of their good works the glory of God, the hallowing of the divine Name, and also the profit of the neighbour.

### III. How do good works please God?

Good works please God in the converted. For without faith it is impossible to please God, **Heb. 11**. Therefore **Peter**, in his first Epistle, chapter 2, saith: *"Offer up spiritual sacrifices, acceptable to God for Christ's sake,"* who is both the Propitiator and now maketh intercession for us. The person therefore being reconciled and pleasing, the work pleaseth. The person not pleasing, neither doth the work please.

### What rules are to be held, when it is asked whether and how good works please?

Four rules.

The first: Let the person that doeth good works be assured that he pleaseth God freely, for Christ's sake, through faith.

The second: Let him acknowledge and bewail the manifold sins <!-- p.191 -->yet cleaving in us, which hinder us, that we cannot satisfy the Law of God.

The third: Let him be assured that God requireth also the obedience begun, which agreeth with the Law.

The fourth: Let him know that this obedience begun pleaseth God by faith for Christ's sake, not for the worthiness of the work.

### IIII. Why are good works to be done?

The causes are four: 1. The glory of God. 2. Necessity. 3. Dignity. 4. Reward.

Of the glory of God the Son of God saith, **Matt. 5**: *"Let your light shine before men, that they may see your good works, and your Father which is in heaven may be glorified."* And **1 Cor. 10**: *"Do all to the glory of God."*

### How manifold is the necessity?

Fivefold:

1. Of the commandment, as: *"Walk in my statutes."*
2. Of debt, **Rom. 8**: *"We are debtors to God, not to the flesh."*
3. Of not losing faith, **1 Tim. 1**: *"War a good warfare; holding faith, and a good conscience."*
4. Of avoiding punishments present and eternal.
5. Of regeneration, which is the beginning of a new life in true conversion.

### What is the dignity?

It is this, that the converted man is a son of God, and a temple of the Spirit of the living God, in whom Christ dwelleth by faith, and an instrument of salvation to his own soul and to others, and a member of Christ, having the holy Angels as companions and defenders of his life. To be willing to deface this dignity is devilish.

### What is the reward?

The reward is manifold: bodily, temporal, <!-- p.192 -->spiritual, eternal. Therefore it is written, **1 Tim. 4**: *"Godliness hath promise of the life that now is, and of the life eternal."* Life indeed is not given for works' sake, because eternal life is the gift of God; but in the present life and in the eternal there is made a free recompense of the obedience of believers.

### Is it rightly said: Good works are necessary to salvation?

Apart from controversy, many orthodox men have used this phrase, understanding the same thing as **Paul**: *"With the mouth confession is made unto salvation."* For they took the word "salvation" not of the righteousness of man before God, which resteth upon faith in Christ alone, but of the life to come. But at this day, seeing that phrase hath begun, at an unseasonable and unhappy time, to come into controversy,[^D2-3] it is for that cause rightly omitted:

1. Because it is ambiguous.
2. Because merit is understood by it.
3. Because it is used by the adversaries, who will have our works to be meritorious of salvation and eternal life.
4. Because it offendeth and troubleth the simpler and more unlearned.
5. Because it is rather legal than evangelical.
6. Because it breedeth and increaseth dissensions in the Church, even among good and orthodox men.

### Is it rightly said: Good works are hurtful to salvation?

Many have set this phrase over against the former, meaning that, if confidence be placed in good works, they become and are pernicious to salvation, because the self-righteous (*iustitiarii*) and hypocrites are not saved, seeing they do not repent. But since the phrase is absurd (because that which is pernicious cannot be good; and because the good works of believers are fruits of righteousness and of the Holy Ghost; likewise, because when the confidence of obtaining <!-- p.193 -->remission of sins and eternal life is placed in them, they are not good works proceeding from faith and pleasing to God), it is therefore rightly omitted.

### Why doth Scripture vouchsafe unto good works the honour of a reward?

Because God promiseth and rendereth, for duty and labour that is owed, rewards and a recompense that is free, not owed, **Luke 17**; but of free charity and bounty, not of debt or of condign desert.

### V. Whose obedience pleaseth, and whose not?

Sins are to be distinguished. There are in the converted and in the saints many remnants of sins, and vicious affections, which they resist, lest they should rush on against conscience. And although the uncleanness of the saints is no light evil, but such as that of which it is said: *"In thy sight shall no man living be justified,"* yet the Son of God is a covert unto believers, who by faith receive the imputation of righteousness. The sins of these are venial. But other sins, in the unconverted, are done against conscience, and are mortal. And when the converted fall into these, either falling away from the foundation, that is, from some article of the faith, whether they understand it or understand it not, being deceived by the juggleries of others; or rushing into heinous crimes, as David into adultery and murder; then they cast out the Holy Ghost, they cast away faith, and become guilty of the wrath of God, and, unless they repent again, they rush into eternal punishments.

## *De Cultibus Dei* · On the Worship of God

<!-- p.194 -->

### What is the worship of God?

The worship of God (*cultus Dei*) is a work commanded by God, done of faith, that is, in the true knowledge of and confidence in the Mediator, which is referred principally to this end, that honour may be given to God, that is, that we may testify that he is truly God whom we thus worship, and that he willeth to be thus worshipped.

### What things are required unto the true worship of God?

Three: the commandment of God; faith in Christ; the celebration and honour of the divine Name.

### Give the rules concerning the worship of God.

1. No worship pleaseth God, save that which he himself hath instituted by his own Word.
2. All ***ἐθελοθρησκεῖαι*** (will-worships) and superstitions, which are without the Word of God, are condemned by God.
3. The foundation of the true worship of God is the acknowledgment of God and the knowledge of ourselves.
4. The knowledge of God is to be sought from the Word and testimony, which sheweth that God is the fountain of all good, and is most ready to help, and willeth that we flee unto him, and that all glory is due unto him.
5. The knowledge of ourselves is to be sought from the end of creation, or from our duty, which is to bear the image of God in sincere righteousness and godliness; next, from the estimation of our own powers, who are not able to perform the duty that is owed; afterward, from the easiness of falling into sin.
6. Faith is the worker of all true divine worship.
7. Faith maketh the person pleasing to God, and rendereth a willing and voluntary worship, and joineth the worship of the mind with the outward work, that it may become a spiritual worship.
8. <!-- p.195 -->All worship without faith, whether it be commanded by God in respect of the work, or brought in from elsewhere, is to be condemned, even as God condemneth sacrifices, **Isa. 11**;[^D2-4] **Ps. 50**.
9. All worship ought to tend unto the celebration of the glory of God.

### What is it to glorify God?

It is to think rightly of the essence, majesty, and veneration of God, and to ascribe unto him all wisdom, mercy, and righteousness, to obey him, and by a godly example to invite others to acknowledge God.

### What is it to take away his glory from God?

1. Not to think rightly of the essence and majesty of God.
2. Not to acknowledge the virtues and benefits of God.
3. To live wickedly against the commandment of God.
4. To be a stumblingblock unto others.
5. Not to ascribe unto Christ the glory of the Redemption, and the divine Majesty, which he hath bestowed also upon his human nature.
6. In bodily things to claim the success for one's own counsels, labours, and wisdom, and for one's own fortune.

### How manifold is the worship of God?

Twofold. The expiatory, or meritorious, or propitiatory, in Greek ***ἱλαστικός***, that is, the obedience of the Son of God, which meriteth eternal life for all that believe. And the eucharistic, of all the sons of God, which of itself meriteth nothing, but of grace hath the promises of the present life and of that which is to come.

### How manifold is the eucharistic worship?

Twofold: inward and outward. The inward is a motion of the mind and heart, agreeing with the Law of God. The outward is an <!-- p.196 -->outward action, agreeing with the Law and will of God. In **1 Thess. 1** **Paul** ascribeth to the inward worship three affections: faith, charity, and hope. To the outward worship he ascribeth three effects of those affections: ***ἔργον*** (work) to faith, ***κόπον*** (labour) to charity, ***ὑπομονήν*** (patience) to hope. Work, or virtue, and labour, and patience are effects of the inward worship, and constitute the outward worships, so that these also become spiritual.

## *De Peccato* · On Sin

### What is sin?

Whatsoever is against the Law of God, **1 John 3**: ***ἁμαρτία ἐστὶν ἀνομία*** (*"sin is the transgression of the law"*); that is, sin is a defect, or corruption, or inclination, or action against the Law of God, offending God, and deserving the wrath of God and eternal punishments, unless remission be made for the sake of the Son, the Mediator.

### How manifold is sin?

Twofold: original, and actual, or personal, as **Cyprian** calleth it.

### What is original, or hereditary, sin?

It is, in all men born of the seed of man, both the corruption of human nature propagated from Adam (in the mind the loss of light, or the want of the knowledge of God and of firm assent concerning Providence, the promises, and the threatenings; in the will a turning away from God, void of the fear of God, of confidence in him, and of love toward him; in the heart concupiscence, contumacy, and violent motions against the divine order) and also the guilt (*reatus*) whereby they that are born are, because of that corruption of the whole nature, guilty of the wrath of God and of eternal death, unless remission be made for the sake of the Son of God, the Mediator.

<!-- p.197 -->

### Why is it called original sin?

For two causes. First, in respect of original righteousness, to which original sin is opposed. For as original righteousness before the fall was the integrity of the whole human nature, and the approbation whereby God had his ***εὐδοκίαν*** (good pleasure) in man, so the sin of our origin is the corruption of the whole human nature, and guilt. Next, because, being propagated from Adam, it hath infected all men, according to that saying of David: *"In sin did my mother conceive me."*

### What is called the material and the formal of original sin?

The material (*materiale*) is the corruption itself, the deformity and ***ἀταξία*** (disorder). The formal (*formale*) is the guilt, that is, the being ordained unto damnation. Whence it is said that in Baptism the formal of sin is taken away, the material remaining.

### How doth Scripture name original sin?

The chief names are these: concupiscence, the flesh, the old man, the law of sin, the law of the members, indwelling sin, rebellion, sin absolutely so called.

### Is sin the substance of man, seeing it is called the flesh and the old man?

In no wise is it the substance of man. For the substance and nature of man was created by God, and the Son of God assumed it, namely our flesh, without sin. But sin is an adventitious quality, whereby human nature hath been <!-- p.198 -->deformed, and made guilty of wrath and of eternal death. But "the flesh" and "the old man" are used by metonymy: the flesh for the viciousness or rebellion of the flesh; the old man for unrighteousness and sin. For the name of the substance is put for the adjunct, and the cause for the effect; which is to be observed for the confuting of the Manichees, who referred those names to the substance.

### What is the efficient cause of sin?

There are two causes: the tyranny of the devil, and the will of men that obey the devil. But God is not the cause of sin, neither doth he will, nor approve sin, nor move any to sin, but he hateth, abhorreth, and punisheth sin, **Ps. 5**: *"Thou art not a God that hath pleasure in wickedness. Thou hatest all workers of iniquity."*

### What is actual sin?

It is every action, whether inward, as an evil thought, doubting, concupiscence, or outward, that fighteth against the Law of God.

### What are the causes of outward sins?

1. The blindness and darkness of the human mind, or errors.
2. Depraved lusts.
3. The instigation of the devil.

### What is the matter *in which* of sin?

The mind and will of man, the heart, the faculties, and all the outward members.

### What is the form of sin?

Guilt, that is, the obligation unto wrath and eternal punishment.

### What are the effects of sin?

The wrath of God. The tyranny of the devil. All the calamities of mankind. Concupiscence, or the ***ἀταξία*** (disorder) of all the powers, which <!-- p.199 -->is at once sin and the punishment of sin. The death of the body. Eternal damnation.

### Rehearse other divisions of sin.

One sin is mortal, another venial. This division pertaineth not only to the regenerate, but to the unregenerate also. For although all the sins of the unregenerate, original and actual, are mortal or ***φονικά*** (deadly), yet when the unregenerate are converted, their sins become venial. In like manner, although all the sins of the regenerate are venial for Christ's sake, yet when they assent to an error in the foundation, or fall against conscience, they cease to be regenerate, that is, they let go grace, the Holy Ghost, and faith, and become guilty of the wrath of God and of eternal punishments, unless they repent and be converted unto God. Wherefore their sins become mortal, if they return not unto God.

### What is mortal sin, or sin reigning and having dominion?

In the unregenerate it is any sin whatsoever, whether original or actual, according to that saying, **John 3**: *"He that believeth not the Son, the wrath of God abideth on him."* But in the regenerate it is an error in the foundation, and a fall against conscience, for which sin a man casteth out the Holy Ghost, and loseth faith, and becometh guilty of wrath and of eternal death, unless he be converted unto God.

### What is venial sin?

The same as original sin in the regenerate, such as are the affections that sprout forth, and the errors that befall, to which the saints do not assent, but which they resist. Likewise, in all that turn themselves unto God, that also becometh a venial sin which before conversion was mortal, and of its own nature would bring death, did not the Father, being gracious for the Son's sake, grant pardon unto them that believe and by faith wrestle against mortal sin.

<!-- p.200 -->

### In how many ways do men sin mortally?

In two ways. Remissibly, or venially, when a man is converted unto God, repenting, and wholly ceaseth to sin that sin. And irremissibly, namely when a man persevereth in sins against conscience without repentance.

### How manifold is irremissible sin?

Twofold. The first is every sin against conscience in which a man persevereth without repentance, and dieth in it. Next, there is a certain special sin, irremissible and unto death, which is called the sin against the Holy Ghost, of which there are principally three degrees. The first of them is, knowingly and willingly to set oneself against the divine truth acknowledged and evident, and to contradict it, to persecute and to hate it. The second is, knowingly to fall away from the heavenly truth to gain the favour of men, and not to return unto it. The third is, to despair of the grace of God, and to charge him with falsehood in his promises, and in that despair to depart out of life, and finally to resist the Holy Ghost.

### Are there more divisions of sins?

Some sins are of commission, when those things are done which are forbidden in the Law. Others are of omission, when those things are not done which are commanded in the Law.

**Likewise:** Some are of infirmity. Others of ignorance. Others of malice, which are done ***ἐκ προαιρέσεως*** (of deliberate purpose).

**Likewise:** Some are voluntary, others involuntary, which a man resisteth, and wherein he taketh no delight at all.

<!-- p.201 -->

### In how many ways is sin accused by the Holy Ghost?

In eight ways chiefly:

1. In every man's conscience.
2. By the voice of the Law, or by the Word.
3. By the setting forth of the causes why the Son of God was made man.
4. By the divine threatenings in the Word; by signs in heaven, in the air, in the waters, in the earth.
5. By the rod, ***λιμῷ, λοιμῷ, καὶ πολέμῳ*** (by famine, pestilence, and war), and by every man's cross, whether inward or outward: the loss of goods, the losing of friends, diseases, infamy.
6. By the tragical examples of others.
7. By the degeneration of all things.
8. By death.

## *De Promissionibus et Comminationibus* · On the Promises and Threatenings

### Rehearse the rules concerning the divine promises and threatenings.

The first is general. All the divine promises and threatenings, in that form and sense in which God hath delivered and interpreted them, are true, certain, ratified, firm, and unmoved, because God is true.

The second. The interpretation of the legal promises and threatenings is to be taken from the Gospel.

The third. The Gospel sheweth that the legal promises, although they have annexed the condition of entire obedience, which no man can perform in this life, are nevertheless ratified and firm unto them that believe in the Son, **2 Cor. 1**: <!-- p.202 -->*"All the promises of God in Christ are Amen."* **Rom. 3**: *"We establish the law through faith."* For the whole fulfilling of the Law consisteth in the imputation of the righteousness of Christ; and the obedience begun also agreeth with the Law of God, and hath the promises of the present life and of that which is to come.

The fourth. The promises of grace, of reconciliation, and of eternal life for Christ's sake are fulfilled unto believers without any condition and exception, and are to be held fast with most firm faith; and they are universal and free.

The fifth. The promises of bodily things have a fourfold exception:

1. Of the punishment of the ungodly.
2. Of the chastisement and cross of the godly.
3. Of the liberty that is in God, to whom neither the manner nor the time is to be prescribed wherein he ought to bestow the good things promised.
4. Of the divine calling. For bodily goods are not to be snatched by our own counsels without the divine calling, as the sons of Ephraim, invading the promised land without a calling, are slain, **1 Paral. [1 Chron.] 7**.

The sixth. To the threatenings of eternal malediction the Gospel opposeth the universal and free promise of the remission of sins for the sake of the Son of God, the Mediator, who took those threatenings upon himself, sustained them, overcame them, and made perfect satisfaction for them.

The seventh. The threatenings of bodily punishments annexed to the Law have the condition of repentance, or an exception out of the Gospel concerning conversion, whereby always either mitigation of calamities or deliverance from them is obtained for the sake of the Son, who hath made entire satisfaction for all threatenings and punishments; and in whom, as the promises are fulfilled, so the threatenings cease.

### For what causes hath God annexed promises of bodily good things to good works?

<!-- p.203 -->

For five causes.

The first is, that they may be testimonies of the divine providence. For God is the author and preserver[^D2-5] of this bodily life, neither are bodily goods scattered by chance, but they are distributed by the sure counsel of God.

The second cause, that they may be testimonies that God willeth to preserve the Church in this life also, for whose sake he feedeth the rest of men likewise.

The third, that we may be put in mind of faith, invocation, hope, and thanksgiving. For God willeth that bodily goods also be asked and looked for by faith, and that we be exercised in faith, hope, expectation, patience, humility.

The fourth, that they may be admonitions concerning the promise of grace, or of spiritual good things, according to that saying: *"Whatsoever ye shall ask the Father* IN MY NAME *(here faith in Christ shineth before) he will give it you."*

The fifth, that we may know both that the Church is subject to the cross, and yet that amid the very dangers she is wonderfully preserved in this life.

### For what causes hath God annexed bodily threatenings and punishments to evil works?

For six causes. The first is the righteousness of God. The second is, that there may be a difference between virtues and vices, and that in this difference every man may know what manner of God he is, namely, good, righteous, chaste. The third, that there may stand forth admonitions concerning the eternal punishments. The fourth, that repentance may be done and the life amended. The fifth, that the public tranquillity may be preserved. The sixth, that others, being deterred by the examples of horrible punishments, may avoid the offences by which punishments are drawn down.

## *Decalogus* · The Decalogue

<!-- p.204 -->

### Why is the Decalogue to be continually meditated upon?

1. Because it admonisheth us concerning God, who and what manner of God he is.
2. It teacheth us unto what end human nature was created, and what manner of creature man was before the fall.
3. It is the rule of life and the governess of manners.
4. It is the mirror of sin.
5. It driveth us to seek the Mediator, who fulfilleth the Law for us. For the Law is a schoolmaster unto Christ.
6. It kindleth invocation, whereby we ask that conformity with the Law may be restored in us.

### Rehearse the rules concerning the Decalogue.

1. The first Table is always to be preferred before the second Table.
2. The first commandment is to be included in all the rest. For it is for GOD's sake that obedience is to be rendered in all the commandments. And in the violation of every commandment there is contained the contempt of God in the first commandment.
3. In the negatives the affirmative sense is contained withal, and in the affirmatives the negative sense is contained withal, that is, the thesis and the antithesis.

### Rehearse the words of the Decalogue.

**Exod. 20. Deut. 5.**

**I.**

> I am the Lord thy God, which brought thee out of the land of Egypt, from the house of bondage. Thou shalt have none other gods before my face. Thou shalt not make thee any graven image, or any likeness of the things that are in heaven above, or whatsoever are in the earth beneath, or that are in the waters beneath the earth. Thou shalt not <!-- p.205 -->bow down thyself unto them, nor serve them. For I am the LORD thy GOD, a jealous God, visiting the sin of the fathers upon the children, unto the third and fourth generation of them that hate me; and shewing mercy unto thousands of them that love me and keep my commandments.

**II.**

> Thou shalt not take the name of the LORD thy God in vain. For the LORD thy God will not hold him guiltless that taketh his Name in vain.

**III.**

> Keep the sabbath day to sanctify it, as the LORD thy God hath commanded thee. Six days thou shalt labour, and do thy work: but on the seventh day is the sabbath of the LORD thy God. In it thou shalt not do any work, thou, and thy son, and thy daughter, thy manservant, and thy maidservant, thine ox, and thine ass, and any of thy cattle, and the stranger that is within thy gates; that so thy manservant and thy maidservant and thine ass may rest as well as thou, and that thou mayest remember that thou also wast a servant in the land of Egypt, and that thou wast brought out thence by the LORD thy God through a mighty hand and by a stretched out arm. Therefore the LORD thy God hath appointed thee to keep the sabbath day, and to sanctify it.

**IIII.**

> Honour thy father and thy mother, as the LORD thy God hath commanded thee; that it may go well with thee, and that thy days may be prolonged upon the land which the LORD thy God shall give thee.

<!-- p.206 -->

**V.**

> Thou shalt not kill.

**VI.**

> Thou shalt not commit adultery.

**VII.**

> Thou shalt not steal.

**VIII.**

> Thou shalt not bear false witness against thy neighbour.

**IX.**

> Thou shalt not covet thy neighbour's house.

**X.**

> Thou shalt not covet thy neighbour's wife, nor his field, nor his manservant, nor his maidservant, nor his ox, nor his ass, nor his beast, nor any thing that is thy neighbour's.

These words the LORD spake out of the midst of the fire, of the cloud, and of the thick darkness, with a great voice, writing them in two tables of stone, etc.

### Why was the Law written in tables of stone?

1. That the hardness of our hearts might be shewn, in which the Law of God cannot be written until God himself with his own finger move and bend our hearts, and write his Law in them.
2. That God might shew that the obedience which he requireth in the Moral Law is abolished by no length of time.
3. That the rigour and strict right, or burden, of the Law, which presseth us, and the hard voice and preaching of the Law, might be signified.
4. That a manifest difference between the Law and the Gospel might stand forth, according to that saying: *"I will take away from you the stony heart, and I will give you an heart of flesh."*
