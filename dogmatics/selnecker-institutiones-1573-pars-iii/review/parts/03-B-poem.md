# *Carmen in Explicationem Capitum Doctrinae Christianae, quae Catechismum Lutheri nominamus, Dresdae scriptum, Anno 1563* · A Poem upon the Exposition of the Heads of Christian Doctrine, which we call Luther's Catechism, written at Dresden in the Year 1563

*(The heading names neither author nor addressee. The poem, in hexameters, was written at Dresden in 1563, where Selnecker was then court preacher; it speaks in the first person throughout and addresses, by turns, Christ, the reader, and the schoolboys. The print has no headings within the poem; its divisions are the indented verse paragraphs, each of which is given here with its first Latin line.)*

*Christe Deus, da successum, rege Flamine pectus*

> O Christ, O God, grant good success; rule with thy gracious Spirit  
> this breast of ours, who now, professing thy words,  
> lo, again and yet again set forth in our halls  
> those words, in the Catechism's form and wholesome method,  
> even as the great-souled **Luther**, in right order and with art,  
> prescribed them with great success, that noble hero.  
> That thou wilt do this my mind doth promise me, and prayeth for it.  
> But before I enter, as it may be, into our gardens,  
> a question comes to be set forth, in no long verse,  
> and counsel to godly minds (for so it is right to speak),  
> in a time so wretched, foul and full of tumult,  
> in a state discordant and shifting, pitiable, dreadful,  
> (if that can be done in a slender poem) must be given.  
> Let them grant pardon, whom zeal for godliness and love  
> doth lead, and who honestly mark not who he is, but what is said:  
> for the rest we care not, and fear them not at all.  

*Quaero, Catechismis noster cur orbis abundet?*

> I ask: why doth our world abound in catechisms?  
> Perhaps this first answer lies ready, and is true:  
> great is the grace of God, who the seeds of true doctrine  
> thus suffereth to be spread abroad in every place,  
> and mercifully preserveth the oracles of the divine Word,  
> and good writers, lovers of true godliness,  
> bringeth forth now here, now there, as his purpose stands:  
> whether a man seek the godly doctrines in Greek dress,  
> or the grace and sure ornaments of the Latin tongue,  
> or what the arduous strength, ever in our  
> time, of the German speech brings forth with great endeavour.  
> <!-- p.33 -->Of books there is enough; all things are full of Christ.  
> Every side is stored with catechisms, and with a postil.  

*Sed tamen est aliquid, de quo mens iusta moneri*

> Yet there is something of which a right mind ought  
> to be warned, that it may shun the tricks and nets of the world.  
> For many are minded to write, not with a sound breast,  
> not with a mind that seeketh the good, or true salvation,  
> but swollen with mad ambition and with pride,  
> or by the goads of dire envy, by the evil spirit's gadfly,  
> daring a work that deserves long Gyara and a long  
> imprisonment:[^B-1] hence books exist, on many accounts,  
> which it is hurtful to have seen, and profitable not to know.  
> Hence by the title of "Catechism", and by the sheepskin's  
> spoils, which, like a swathing-band, the wolf's great venom  
> conceal, godly simplicity, both young men and old,  
> are deceived, and the country folk are made uncertain.  
> Every heresy doth ever vaunt itself under this title,  
> is sold, and by stealth gathers strength as it goes.[^B-2]  
> The dire troops of the Papists rise up under such a name;  
> so today, on every side, that kind of men (which leaves  
> nothing undared, whether by force, or fraud, or art,  
> that it may change the sacred mysteries of the divine Supper)  
> thrusts itself upon the simple, and seeketh strength.  
> So the Anabaptists, so the remaining crowd of the wicked  
> lay their snares under the name of true godliness.  
> Whoever thou art, ponder these things in silence, and warily  
> prove all things, if thou wilt, with a sound mind: a snake lurks in the grass;  
> dire death befouls the pot with loathsome poison.[^B-3]  
> Flee a thousand catechisms, nor take up any whatsoever  
> with easy daring: by reading much, not many things,[^B-4]  
> thou shalt be learned, and shalt get thee solid and sincere  
> heads of doctrine, and so shalt run upon the true path.  
> Either too uncertain in himself, or changeable in a single  
> <!-- p.34 -->moment, or even a heretic and a hater of virtue,  
> or overwise is he, whoever, taking all distinction away,  
> reconciles all things, approves all writings, for quiet's  
> sake, as they say, and defends persons,  
> and God's many gifts, if any there be, the highest or the lowest,  
> but neglects the real matters, and censures no abuse.  

*Tolle oculos, et iudicium, quod frivola spectat,*

> Away with the eyes, and the judgment that looks upon trifles,  
> and leans upon the outward senses only, deeply  
> sighing, praising arts and gifts of tongue.  
> Consult thou the mind, if thou wouldst be held sincere;  
> consult the words of God; steadfastly consult what is right:  
> art avails nothing, all favour passes, all praise goes astray,  
> if thou know not the truth, nor cleave to the things themselves.  
> All learning, when godliness is taken away, is poison.  
> The candid simplicity of faith deservedly bears the palm;  
> and if it be joined to godly arts, it conquers all,  
> and, God favouring, lays low and wards off all counterfeits.  
> He is a liar who in his vain breast thinketh otherwise.  

*Saepe quidem vidi praegnantes arte, sed illis*

> Often indeed have I seen men big with art, but in them  
> was no godliness; they would needs be censors.  
> They could also discourse of honourable things,  
> which the learned, after their manner, are wont to bend this way and that.  
> Shouldst thou see them, thou wouldst say they live the life of Epicurus,  
> from which constancy is torn away, and all honesty,  
> and which the pleasures (that mould waxen hearts)  
> pursues, nor regards what is the glory of Christ.  
> Shall I then approve these men, though they write a thousand volumes,  
> or burst, in their pride, with the bulk of their learning?  
> Not so: far otherwise hath he committed to us what we must do,  
> Almighty God, Almighty Man, Christ, the Image  
> of the eternal Father, who at the Father's right hand in equal  
> majesty sitteth, knoweth all things, and governeth all things.  

<!-- p.35 -->

*O pia simplicitas, fidei sincerior usus,*

> O godly simplicity, O sincerer practice of faith,  
> how cold art thou today, how art thou nothing but an empty name!  
> How rare is the concord of the teachers, is matter for grief;  
> perhaps constancy also is nothing but a name.  
> All things are in confusion, doctrine and life, and there thrives  
> the work of ambition, deceitful persuasion, pride.  
> Candour is wanting in the ministers; all things are full of rage.  
> The tongue indeed is holy, and would ever seem holy,  
> but the heart is wanting, and all fruit; most things are a mockery.  

*Eheu nos miserè natos in secula tali*

> Alas for us, wretchedly born into ages full of such  
> a state! Yet this, O Christ, is thy righteous will.  

*Possidet ille satis, qui te sibi possidet unum,*

> He possesseth enough, who possesseth thee alone,  
> when otherwise he sees no friends whom he may admire,  
> and is alone, as one shut up in a sad wilderness,  
> while he lives a looker-on at the manifold arts of the world,  
> and in silence marks its falls and its great ruins,  
> and trusts in thee alone, a lover of godliness, and thee alone  
> knoweth for his protector and the Lord of his salvation.  

*Sed redeo, tibi consilium fortasse daturus,*

> But I return, to give thee counsel, it may be,  
> whoever thou art that lovest the peace approved of Christ and of the Father,  
> which the Spirit alone bestoweth from the height of heaven.  

*Mille Catechismos fugias, agnosce priores:*

> Flee a thousand catechisms; acknowledge the earlier ones:  
> whatsoever is first, that, when the trial is made, is true.[^B-5]  
> Let not the frequent alleging of the Word deceive thee.  
> Satan also used the Word; so doth the crowd of sorcerers  
> use it, so do the heretics, and the unspeakable rabble.  
> And as dogs tear rags, so doth every heresy  
> tear the Scriptures, itself stuck fast upon stony rocks.  

*Fortè Catechismos qua dicam lege priores*

> Perhaps by what rule I call some catechisms "the earlier"  
> thou askest: thou hast from me an answer true and friendly.  
> Many writings he gave, truly great by the power of God,  
> our father, the prophet of our age, **Luther**,  
> <!-- p.36 -->than whom, thou being witness, O God, none since the times of the  
> apostolic company hath lived greater; upon whom from heaven every  
> skill so breathed, of seeing all sacred things,  
> and of so uttering with great weight the thoughts of his mind,  
> and with an astonishing pen, when need should be,  
> of drawing them out, that nowhere is it the letter only  
> which he wrote, but sound things live as with a blood of their own,  
> and move men's minds by the Law and by the consolation of Christ.  

*Non loquor hîc Monachis, nihil hîc mea vena Papistas*

> I speak not here to monks; here my vein careth nothing for  
> Papists, and scoffers[^B-6] I heed not here: let every man be gone  
> who standeth upon the earth alone, and to the lofty stars  
> of heaven lifteth not his mind, and how great is the Thunderer's  
> grace never considereth, but despiseth all things,  
> or in silence hunteth after an empty name, an apostate,  
> or is overwise, and a great hypocrite within himself.  
> I speak the truth, I feign nothing, and for witnesses the minds  
> of good men I here allege, that **Luther** alone, with a mind  
> unbroken, and upheld by the power of God, the truth  
> brought forth, and the darkness, when the light was born again,  
> drove far away, constant in virtue, in courage and in faith;  
> and that what a thousand others, however for name and art  
> renowned, it may be, grew weary and left buried,  
> this one man performed with such success and such divine power,  
> that he declares it to be above the strength of man,  
> and beyond comprehension, whoever will but confess the truth.  
> Whoever thou art that haply deniest this, thou liest and errest.  
> Take away the books of so great a prophet, or his translated Bible,  
> take away the Catechisms, and take away his other labours:  
> come now, how trusty a soldier, without force, without strength,  
> and how well furnished wilt thou be, who knowest not, even now,  
> how scant is thy household stuff; worthy to have branded on thee  
> the ungrateful guest's marks by the lictor, or, a long letter,  
> <!-- p.37 -->to feed the ravens.[^B-7] But thou sayest, perhaps: What then  
> was there before, when the shadow of **Luther** had not yet shone?  
> I hide not my mind:[^B-8] many, venerable for godliness,  
> have lived, teachers shining with candour;  
> nor were the cloisters of the monks altogether  
> void of all faith: but, if thou wilt confess the truth,  
> foul was the gloom, in a rude shadow of godliness,  
> the filth of doctrine, squalor, and rotten stenches:  
> if anything there was, it lay wholly dark, hidden without light.  
> In the churches the people read words full of frothy  
> bellowing; the same were handled by the high pulpits  
> of the Schools. The thing is known, and clearer than the sun himself.  

*Numine sed vivo, divino Flamine motus,*

> But by the living Godhead, moved by the divine Spirit,  
> he showed the truth, with favouring winds and sails,  
> the great-souled father, and of our religion the king, teacher and hero,  
> and the charioteer of Israel,[^B-9] **Luther**,  
> after whom we, who keep the doctrines of the sincere faith,  
> with constant mind confess that we are Lutherans.  

*Ille vir è tenebris lucem splendore coruscam*

> That man out of darkness brought back the light, flashing with brightness,  
> flowing from Christ, the brightness of the Father.  
> Showing the errors, he, sure of his ground, the godly doctrines  
> delivered by a method unknown to monks and gymnosophists.  
> Simply professing the truth with a mind taught of God,  
> he would seek nothing else but the name of Christ,  
> and this he found, with a success sprung from heaven.  
> He scattered also the mists, if any strove to corrupt  
> the light, and cast them to the ground, driven off:  
> such as Müntzer, such as he that is known by his surname,  
> Carlstadt, such as Zwingli, and more of the wicked,  
> whose barbarous names these verses refuse;  
> and both them, and the dark company, we shut far out from hence,  
> that admires them, or with dull mind approves them,  
> <!-- p.38 -->as many of us as would, it may be, be reckoned Lutherans.  
> Of this name we are never ashamed, and I myself will pray:  
> O that at length we were worthy of such a name!  
> For not true is the lying blasphemy of Staphylus,[^B-10]  
> that all those are Lutherans whom he himself reckons up,  
> his list now marking all that teach vain things,  
> whom we call Anabaptists, and enemies of the Supper,  
> craftsmen in tropes of many kinds, and men that use a mathematic  
> which climbs the bodily heavens and the wain of Boötes.  
> Not so: these have been driven out of our borders,  
> and rightly, the voice of God so commanding, removed,  
> however great, however learned, or advanced in honours.  
> Not of persons, but of the matter is our contention.  

*Utque scias, quos Discipulos statuisse Lutheri*

> And that thou mayest know whom thou canst rightly hold  
> for disciples of **Luther**, here I speak the truth, though the troop  
> of the ***μωροσοφῶν*** (the foolish-wise) fight against it, and utter thereto their sundry whisperings.  

*Biblia sacra probant, quicunque hoc nomine gaudent,*

> They approve the holy Bible, whoever rejoice in this name,  
> to be Lutherans: the words of the Thunderer alone,  
> which the Scripture holds, delivered by the breath of God,  
> they receive, nor suffer themselves to be torn away by right or art  
> or snares, and they follow true things with a true  
> candour, things approved by a wit that looks into them.  
> Nothing else is welcome to them; no friendly speech persuades them;  
> the power of the world avails nothing here: alone  
> the Word of God avails, and the Scripture, with its nod to be revered.  
> They fear nothing, if only the Scriptures, free from filth,  
> they may possess, though dangers hang over them without end.  
> One thing there is: to be able to have pleased God, and the name of Christ,  
> with a sincere breast, without paint, to be able, were it but by one  
> moment the sooner, to carry forward unto long years,  
> and to serve Christ alone, according as the rule hath been left  
> in the Word, which moveth hearts, which bendeth breasts,  
> <!-- p.39 -->in which we must live, as the unborn child liveth in the womb.[^B-11]  

*Hos ego, non alios unquam, dixisse Luthero*

> These, and never any others, would I have called  
> **Luther**'s own, it may be, whether, famous in name,  
> they stand high, or whether men now the names of a feigned peace,  
> under the pretext of peace, of godliness, of religion,  
> (as this rabble is wont, scattering lies) do vaunt.  
> Look not upon the garment, but upon that which under the garment  
> the reprobate must hide: so frame thy judgment, so all things.  

*At breviter: quisquis Christo, Verboque salubri*

> But briefly: whoever upon Christ and the saving Word  
> doth lean, and by faith to draw near unto the Thunderer  
> doth learn, and abideth constant, ever faithful, in the true  
> knowledge of man, and of Christ, and of the mighty God,  
> he is truly a Lutheran. That this man is more safely  
> a Lutheran in mind, thou mayest affirm even unto the very fires,  
> than if thou knewest many men by face, and thereupon,  
> while there is peace, men great and holy, by ample tokens,  
> it may be, deceived (as is the manner, the courtly custom  
> of the world), thou shouldst pronounce sincere by thy  
> judgment, whose force is beguiled by the cunning of the world.  

*Non manet, et nihil est, si quis pia dogmata Christi*

> It abideth not, and is nothing, if any man the godly doctrines of Christ  
> doth bend, and turn them hither and thither by his own endeavour.  
> Yet ever under this garment hath the rabble covered the wolf's  
> venom, a fox's garment, a poison black with dire gall.  
> How often, alas, doth this befall at last in our clime,  
> where a man may keep watch, and yet not be safe in the city?  

*Ergo Dei quisquis Verbum capit, approbat, ornat,*

> Therefore whoever receives, approves, adorns the Word of God,  
> him thou shalt call a Lutheran: but whoever, by cunning  
> and by snares overcome, and by the art of foolish reason,  
> bends the words of God, fits them to a feverish mouth,  
> and thrusts them into a rotten belly, without mind and without God,  
> and contrives novelties, and forms and phrases fashions anew,  
> well-nigh new ones, which the sincere flock of Christ abhors,  
> <!-- p.40 -->him a heretic, shut far out from our flock,  
> call, profess, report, expose: for by speaking the truth  
> thou shalt sin in nothing, and so shalt be able to render to Christ  
> a true account with sincere voice; whether this  
> the world hate or love, there is no danger thence.  

*Quare Dei nomen, veramque inquire salutem,*

> Wherefore seek out the name of God, and true salvation:  
> in this way thou canst be a faithful Lutheran.  
> May I perish, if I speak not the truth, whatsoever the proud  
> may here report: God is witness, and ever shall remain.  

*Sive ruat coelum, seu terra et pontus et aër,*

> Though heaven should fall, or earth and sea and air,  
> the words of Christ abide; to be buried in the dust the truth  
> knoweth not, and to it all things give way with swift feet.  

*Hoc ita de sacro nobis sermone relatum est,*

> Thus much hath been reported to us of the holy Word;  
> and whoever approves it, being a hearer and reader of **Luther**,  
> desires, craves, burns to be a Lutheran,  
> and seeks nothing else but the name of the great Christ.  

*Caetera turba vale, tibi non est gloria cordi*

> Ye crowd that remain, farewell: thou hast not at heart the great  
> glory of God; thou seekest thine own, and, professing Judas's  
> treachery, after coins and the splendid names of the world  
> thou followest, our Saviour, alas, forsaken.  

*Dic mihi, quid docuit, quid scripsit quaeso Lutherus,*

> Tell me, what did **Luther** teach, what, I pray, did he write,  
> that sounded not together with the living Word of Christ,  
> or that did not celebrate the great name of our God?  
> "I know not" (perhaps thou wilt say): be silent then, thou envious man, until  
> thou be taught; so mayest thou afterwards speak the truth.  
> Or, if thou confess thyself plainly an enemy, thou wilt deny it,  
> and many things, which it were better not to have stirred, with wicked  
> judgment thou wilt say were thrust into the writings of **Luther**,  
> and wilt reject the man, his name, his matter, his gifts, his labours:  
> as Staphylus rages, harrying, out of all, one thing,  
> the rest being profitable, which he boasteth to be a great  
> crime, and one that must be expiated (woe to us!)[^B-12] by a foul death,  
> <!-- p.41 -->whereas he himself, in wicked fashion, forsakes the mark.  
> But this is nothing: we follow a judgment without fraud or guile,  
> a sincere one, and rejoice to be **Luther**'s  
> disciples: O that we were so in doctrine, and in name, and in practice!  

*Sed cesso: fortasse suo dicentur et ista*

> But I cease: perhaps these things also shall be spoken in their own  
> more convenient time; grief hath thus far wrung those words  
> from me, and hath had its fill, for the love of godliness,  
> not with the thought that haply by a rude poem there could now  
> be praised and redeemed that **Luther** who is everywhere traduced:  
> who, unless he be praised as worthy by the name of God,  
> and himself, with Christ for his leader, defend himself at all hours,  
> all is over, whatever the rest may do, whether poets by right,  
> or such as bear the name of versifiers.  

*Sed satis: ad ceptum redeo. Dum sacra Lutherus*

> But enough: I return to what I began. When **Luther** gave  
> the sacred words, and scattered them far through the whole world,  
> and the Catechisms, which in our speech the Smaller  
> (he put forth also forms for rightly teaching the doctrine)  
> and the Larger, by right of its measure, we call,  
> so briefly, so clearly, in so right an order  
> he gave all things, that nothing can be taken away, nor more be added,  
> which use might haply require for the excellent  
> doctrine of true faith and of sure salvation.  

*Ergo Catechismos primò nos ponimus illos,*

> Therefore we set those Catechisms in the first place,  
> directed unto the name of Christ who saveth us,  
> as many of us faithful as would be Lutherans,  
> however much the wise man of this world, after his manner,  
> may here guffaw, or refuse to stand by such a judgment,  
> and in silence burst within himself, an apostate.  

*Christe tibi magnas sincero pectore grates*

> O Christ, to thee great thanks with sincere breast  
> we speak, that thou thyself the light and the seeds of thy Word,  
> scattered by the Catechisms, and by the much labour of **Luther**,  
> against the gates of Hell, and the rages of the world,  
> <!-- p.42 -->among us wretched men, unworthy of the name, dost preserve.  
> Do thou work, do thou hold fast, do thou defend us henceforth:  
> without thee there is no faith, without thee no constancy.  
> Whatever we yet are, we are misery and sorrows.  
> Unless thou us, our boys, and our tender youth  
> dost teach by the Word, and strengthen, and adorn, and help,  
> the Word which thy prophet and servant **Luther** unfolded,  
> all is over with us; of ourselves, O Christ, we now perish.  
> Spare thine own race; spare us, O Christ most good.  
> Make us babes and sucklings, by thy gracious  
> nod; grant that we wretched men aspire not to high things,  
> nor, as fools, enjoy only a foolish reason.  
> Suffer thou us to be men of the Catechism, and the living breasts  
> of thy Word to suck, and to hold true salvation.  
> While I ponder these things, my mind and spirit stand by me,  
> reporting that thou, O Christ, dost hear our groaning prayers.  

*O pueri, sacrata Dei grex, optima proles,*

> O boys, the hallowed flock of God, most excellent offspring,  
> learn righteousness, being warned, and despise not Christ.  
> The Bible read is pleasing; ten times repeated it will please.[^B-13]  
> Much repetition of the Catechism will profit every man;  
> this one reading is more precious than all treasures.  
> Flee ye a thousand catechisms, and beware the black  
> writings that mar the method of your own Catechism.  

*Nunc labor est etiam non praetereundus eorum,*

> Now also the labour of those must not be passed by,  
> who with faithful mind have joined their own pains,  
> and have written books, using godly reasons,  
> and catechisms, of one form and one meaning,  
> and with a mind to adorn the doctrine and the name of Christ,  
> and have joined them as a kind of succour to the earlier prophet.  
> These also the great grace of God, upholding us,  
> hath sent, and raised up as faithful and godly fellow-workers,  
> to do what each, according to his office, was able,  
> <!-- p.43 -->and by their godliness to stay up Moses as he prayed.[^B-14]  

*Ut reliquos taceam (nec enim nunc copia cunctos*

> To say nothing of the rest (for there is not now room to  
> recount them all, and only those who have given learned  
> catechisms, with a simple mind and in seemly form,  
> I am minded to mention): first in virtue **Philip**,  
> lo, cometh, and deservedly taketh the palm from that  
> great-souled companion, father and leader, **Luther**;  
> to whom joining his labours, with a mind consecrated to thee, O Christ,  
> he wrought a great thing, and out of a small body great  
> strength put forth, which the Church now praiseth.  

*Tertius hac acie milesque comesque laborum*

> Third in this battle-line, soldier and companion of the labours,  
> is **Brenz**, whose godly writings the great **Luther**  
> so pleased, as good men know, that he even set them  
> before his own writings, and to every age of posterity  
> declared that by their own vigour they would never die:  
> so godly, so good are they, so grand, and so full of Christ.  
> But among them the Catechism stands out, a most excellent book,  
> which deservedly those who of the Word and of wholesome method  
> are teachers do admire, and oftentimes in their hands  
> (unless they alone be wise, and know all things) do turn it over.  

*Christe seni tanto vivendi tempora, et annos*

> O Christ, to so great an old man grant seasons of life, and long  
> years: for of this man's art and faith  
> we wretched men today, a flock truly afflicted, have need.  

*Decedunt passim doctique, senesque, Patresque,*

> On every side depart the learned, and the aged, and the fathers;  
> on every side succeed men swelling with the heat of youth,  
> who break down the roofs built aforetime, and the covenants,  
> and follow after novelties, foolish things agreeable to reason.  
> So likewise, after its manner, by our own fault, alas, the truth  
> dieth little by little; scarce a small shadow is left.  

*Ergo Catechismos, quorum est authentica virtus,*

> So then the Catechisms whose force is authentic  
> we have named, and deservedly we live content with them.  
> If haply godly men, by better studies, add  
> <!-- p.44 -->more writings that adorn the Catechisms, it is to be approved.  
> These we reject not; we praise an honourable zeal,  
> if haply there be nothing new, and no opinion mingled therewith.  

*Quàm mira est bonitas, quàm mitis gratia Christi,*

> How wonderful is the goodness, how mild the grace of Christ,  
> who by writings the godly seeds of the Word thus to be spread abroad  
> taketh care, and himself doth men for so great an office, lovers  
> of doctrine, and furnished with solid arts, as their author  
> stir up, fashion, preserve, shield, instruct, adorn.  

*Sed, miserum, restant (tristi quod mente fatendum est)*

> But, O misery, there remain (which must be confessed with a sad mind),  
> to perform such great labours, scarce one or two.  
> Zeal also faileth; there is no more a glorious ardour;  
> and black fury is at hand, and a feigned knowledge of things.  

*Finis adest rerum, Christum expectamus ovantes:*

> The end of things is at hand; we look for Christ, rejoicing.  
> Thou, if godliness be at thy heart, if care for salvation  
> move thee, be thou willing to be a simple hearer, and a sheep  
> of Christ the Only-begotten; let the sacred page be approved of thee;  
> and to it the Catechisms, of the doctrine thus enlarged the body,

> A thousand catechisms, under whatsoever name they be given,

> God helping, proveth to be sound, and cherisheth,

*Christe Deus nos in Verbo retinere salutis*

> O Christ, O God, to keep us in the Word of salvation  
> be thou willing, O Christ; quench the wicked, quench the rages,  
> and preserve among us the seeds of the pure Word,  
> and false novelties by thy strength mercifully ward off,  
> lest we be made like unto Sodom in a wretched world,  
> we who already are the dregs of the world, and an offspring  
> all too lame: have mercy, O God, have mercy upon thine own;  
> so shall honour, name and praises abide to thee for ever.
