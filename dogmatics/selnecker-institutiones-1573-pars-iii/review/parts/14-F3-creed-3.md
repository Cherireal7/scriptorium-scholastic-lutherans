<!-- p.275 -->

**Argument.**

Passion and death are properties agreeing not to the Divine, but to the human nature.

Therefore it is not rightly said that the Passion is of the person.

**I answer.** It is one thing to speak of the Passion and Death of Christ in so far as it is a property of the human nature, which alone is torn, smitten, scourged, and dieth: which injury nevertheless falleth to the reproach of the Son of God, because that body is divine, that is, it is the body and flesh of the Only-begotten of God, who receiveth, beareth, and interpreteth that injury with no other mind than if it were done to His own divine nature. But it is another thing, that by that passion and death the wrath of the Father hath been appeased, the head of the serpent bruised, death destroyed, life restored, the captives set free. For these are operations of the divine power, yet not without the flesh. For the divine power of the Son of God, as **Cyprian** saith,[^F3-1] *"was as it were a certain hook, covered with the habit of human flesh,"* that, being devoured by the serpent Leviathan, He might through that infirmity of His passion and death take and overcome that strong man armed.

### How dost thou understand the customary saying of Irenaeus, that Christ was crucified and died, the Word resting, that He might be able to be crucified and to die?

**Irenaeus**[^F3-2] setteth forth to be considered that wonderful obedience of the Son of God resting, that is, that His Divine nature, dwelling bodily in Christ, although it could most easily have repelled all injuries, sufferings, and death, that they should not be able to fall upon that mass of the assumed human nature, <!-- p.276 -->in which all the fulness of the Godhead dwelt, yet did not use its Power, did not put forth its strength to keep off from its own flesh injuries, sufferings, and death, but gave place to the wrath of the eternal Father against the sin of mankind, permitting that that flesh might be crucified and die.

### How manifold is the Meditation of the Passion of Christ?

Threefold: the Pedagogical, or Historical; the Spiritual; and the exemplary, or Paradigmatical.

**To the Pedagogical meditation these things are required:**

- Consideration of the Prophetical Predictions concerning the Passion of Christ.
- Consideration of the Types and Figures, as in Abel, Isaac, Jonah; and of the Sacrifices.
- The Definition of the Passion.
- The Time of the Passion.
- The Causes.
- The Kind and manner of the Passion, according to the History.
- How manifold the Passion is.
- The Difference between the Passion of Christ and that of other Saints.

**To the spiritual meditation these things are required:**

- The fear of God, contrition, acknowledging the heinousness of sin, the greatness of the wrath of God, and the severity of His justice.
- Trust in grace through and on account of the ***λύτρον*** (ransom).
- Invocation.
- Thanksgiving for the love and humility of the Son.
- Godliness of life toward God and our neighbour.
- Holy care in our Vocation.
- A good conscience founded in the Blood of the Son of God.
- Consideration of the tempering of justice and mercy in God.

<!-- p.277 -->

**To the exemplary these things are required:**

- Patience under the cross.
- Humility.
- Consolation.
- Mutual love, Peace, Concord, Forgiveness, Beneficence.
- Consideration of the images which paint the state of the Church, which God willeth to be made conformable to the Image of His Son, **Rom. 8**.

### How is the application of the Passion of Christ made?

It is made by the Word, by Faith, and by the use of the Sacraments.

### Why is mention made of Pilate in the Creed?

1. That we may know at what time and place the Lord suffered.
2. That we may consider the Prophecies concerning Shiloh, and the seventy weeks of Daniel.
3. That we may refute the dotages of the Jews who look for a Messiah.

### Why was Christ crucified?

1. To shew forth the abomination and heinousness of sin.
2. To prove that Christ took upon Himself our guilt, and the punishment of our guilt, being made a curse for us.
3. That the Truth might answer to the figures, to the sacrifices lifted up on high, to the offering of Isaac, to the Brasen serpent, etc.

### Why would Christ die?

1. That He might fulfil the predictions of the Prophets.
2. <!-- p.278 -->That He might represent the figures in very deed. For the victims were slain.
3. That by His passion and death He might bring forth these effects and fruits: 1. That He might render entire Obedience unto death, even the death of the cross. 2. That He might overcome the Devil, and bind him. 3. That He might deliver Man from sin and death. 4. That He might abolish Death by His own death. 5. That He might make Jews and gentiles equal, the enmity being abolished through death. 6. That He might bring it to pass that we should die unto sin, and live unto righteousness for ever.

**Augustine:** *"Through the Death of our Redeemer we have been called from darkness to light, from death to life, from corruption to incorruption, from exile to our Country, from mourning to joy, from the earth to the heavenly Kingdom."*

### Why was Christ buried?

1. That He might shew that He had truly been dead.
2. That He might bury our Sins together with Himself.
3. That He might consecrate our sepulchres by the touch of His most holy Body.
4. That He might afford us a way out of our sepulchres unto life.
5. That we, being buried with Him, might rest from sin, and walk in newness of life.

## *Triumphus* · The Triumph

## *Descendit ad inferos* · He Descended into Hell

**Ephes. 4.** *"He descended into the lower parts of the earth."* **Ps. 16.** *"Thou wilt not leave my soul in hell."* These words pertain not to the passion, or to the burial, much less to torments inflicted in hell, but are a part of the triumph and Victory of Christ, and therefore are joined with the Resurrection. <!-- p.279 -->And this hath ever been the faith of the orthodox Church.

### Why did Christ descend into Hell?

1. That He might testify that He is the Conqueror of Hell.
2. That He might utterly tread down the head of the serpent, and destroy the gates of hell, and bind the Devils, that is, overcome them, and condemn them for ever by a decisive sentence, which He Himself denounced unto His enemies by His own voice and presence.
3. That He might deliver us from the power of Hell.

## *Tertia die resurrexit a mortuis* · The Third Day He Rose Again from the Dead

### Why did Christ rise again?

1. That He might shew that He is the Conqueror of sin, of death, of hell, and of the whole kingdom of Satan.
2. That our righteousness before God might be certain. For the Fruits of the Resurrection of Christ are:

    1. Remission of sins, **1 Corinth. 15**.
    2. Justification, **Romans 4**.
    3. The giving of the Holy Ghost, **Galat. 4**.
    4. The confirmation of the covenant of grace, or the stipulation of a good conscience, **1 Peter 3**.
    5. Our renewing, in newness of life, **Rom. 6**.
    6. Immortality, and the resurrection of bodies, **1 Cor. 15**.
    7. Mutual brotherhood, for that we have one God and Father.
    8. A remedy against all calamities, according to that saying of Paul: *"Remember that the Lord Jesus was raised."*
    9. <!-- p.280 -->A lively hope of glory that fadeth not away, and of eternal salvation, **1 Pet. 1**.

## *Ascendit ad coelos* · He Ascended into Heaven

### What is it to ascend into Heaven?

To ascend into Heaven signifieth in the Creed to pass out of this visible conversation into the life invisible. How this was done the History sheweth, **Acts 1**.

### What doth "to Ascend" signify?

It signifieth visibly to pass out of this visible conversation, and to be exalted by the Right Hand of God above all Heavens unto the Height, and Eminence, and Divine Glory, and to have all things subject unto Himself, and present, and serving.

### Who doth so expound the force of the word "to Ascend"?

The Son of God Himself, saying: *"I ascend unto my Father, and your Father."* And: *"All Power is given unto me in Heaven and in earth."* And the Apostles interpret this Article in these Forms of speech:

> He ascended into the Heavens. He was received up into Heaven.  
> He received Heaven.  
> He ascended up far above all Heavens.  
> He entered into the Glory of His Father.  
> He was made Higher than the Heavens.  
> He received Power over all Powers.  
> All things are put under His feet.  
> He hath passed through the Heavens.  

<!-- p.281 -->

### What doth "Heaven" signify in this Article?

It signifieth not only the Air, the Elements, the Clouds, or the Firmament, the Stars, the Planets, or the Corporeal Heavens, all which Christ ascending passed through with His Body; neither doth it signify a Physical place in some Heaven, where Christ, circumscribed in His Body, is contained: but (as we say, *Our Father, which art in heaven*) it signifieth the heavenly Habitation and Throne of God, who dwelleth in the light which no man can approach unto, above all Heavens, and above all places. For Christ ascended up far above all Heavens, and entered into the Glory of His Father, being gone into Heaven, Angels, and Authorities, and Powers being made subject unto Him, **1 Pet. 3**.

## *Sedet ad dexteram Dei Patris omnipotentis* · He Sitteth on the Right Hand of God the Father Almighty

### Why did Christ ascend with His Body into Heaven?

1. That He might refute the dreams of the Jews concerning a Political Kingdom of the Messiah.
2. That He might testify that He would not converse with us in a visible and comprehensible manner before the Last Day.
3. That He might prepare a place for us, and an Access unto the Heavens and unto the Fellowship of all Angels and men.
4. That He might put all things under His feet, and Himself be the Head of the Church, in Merit, Efficacy, Dignity, and Perfection.
5. That He might bear witness of the Victory against the Kingdom of sin and death.
6. <!-- p.282 -->That He might rule His Church from above, and be present with her even unto the End of the world, and might fill all things, **Ephes. 4**. *"He ascended up far above all Heavens, that He might fill all things,"* giving Gifts unto men, entering into the Holy of Holies, our Propitiator and Advocate.
7. That He might draw our hearts unto Himself, **Phil. 3**. *"Our* ***πολίτευμα*** (conversation, or citizenship) *is in Heaven."* **Coloss. 3.** *"Set your affection on things above, where Christ sitteth on the Right Hand of the Father."*
8. That He might sit on the Right Hand of the Father, and might bestow also in Himself upon His human nature the Glory which from eternity He had as God with the Father, according to that saying, **John 17**: *"Father, glorify thou me with thine own self with the Glory which I had with thee before the World was."* Of which words **Cyril** speaketh most weightily thus: *"His own Glory, which He always hath as God, He asked as man. Nor are these things said by Him because He was ever void of His own Glory, but because He would bring up His own Temple into the Glory which is always present with Him as God."* And: *"He asked not Glory of the Father for Himself, whereof, as being God by nature, He had no need; but by asking, as man, He bringeth over the Father's glory unto the man whom He assumed."* Likewise **Augustine**: *"He ascended into the Heavens, that is, the condition of our nature, which, being born of a human mother, He assumed, He placed above the Heavens on the right hand of God the Father,* ***ἐκάθισεν***, *that is, He made it to sit."* And **Cyprian**: *"He ascended into the Heavens, not where God the Word had not been before, seeing He was always in the Heavens, and abode in the Father, but where the Word made flesh did not sit before."* And: *"To sit on the right hand of the Father is a Mystery of the assumed flesh: for neither are these things fitly added to that incorporeal nature without the assumption of the flesh; neither doth the divine nature seek the perfection of the heavenly seat, but the human."*

<!-- p.283 -->

### Did Christ so ascend into Heaven, that before the last day He can be with His Body nowhere else than in a certain place of Heaven?

By no means. For because He ascended into Heaven that He might carry up His Body to the right hand of the Majesty and power of God, therefore also He can be with His Body wheresoever He will be, without hurt to the truth of His Body, although the manner whereby that may be done square not with Physical rules and properties. For all things are possible with God, *"who doth with His Body whatsoever He will, and yet it remaineth a Body,"* saith **Augustine**.

### Must not Christ be received by Heaven, that is, be contained, as it is written, Acts 3: "Christ must be received by Heaven until the times of the restitution of all things"?

In no wise is Christ contained by Heaven, so that He cannot be elsewhere on earth with His Body and Blood. And the version of the place **Acts 3** is plainly perverse.[^F3-3] For Christ received Heaven, possessed it, occupied it, subjected it unto Himself, that is, as **Peter** expoundeth himself: *"He is on the right hand of God, being gone into Heaven, Angels, Authorities, and Powers being made subject unto Him."* And the Heavens received Christ ascending no otherwise than that with joy and triumph they most humbly subjected themselves unto Him, and acknowledge and salute and proclaim Him Lord.

### Is not Christ by a bodily location in some place of Heaven, ***σώματι περίγραπτος*** (circumscribed in body)?

Most freely can Christ be wheresoever He will be. <!-- p.284 -->By a bodily location indeed, as touching the manner of a true Body, Physically: but without Physical circumscription, Hyperphysically and in a Divine manner, unknown to us, because He hath not only a human Body, as other men have, but hath carried up that very Body to the Right Hand of His Almighty Father, above all heavens, above all places, and above all Orders, He only excepted which did put all things under Him, because He sitteth not above Him, but on His right hand.

### In what place then is Christ with His human Body?

No other place can be named but the Right Hand of God, if this for teaching's sake may be called a place. For to him that asketh, Where Christ is in Heaven, the Scripture answereth: On the Right Hand of God the Father Almighty. For He ascended into the Heavens that He might sit on the Right Hand of the Father.

### What doth the Right Hand of God signify?

**Augustine:**

> *"By the Right Hand understand ye the Power which that man received, who was taken up by God."*

**Cyril:**

> *"He ascended with the flesh into the Glory of the Godhead, and is believed to be Lord of all even with the flesh. Every knee boweth to Him, and that not to the grief and reproach of the Father, but rather to His Glory. For He rejoiceth and is glorified together, while the Son is adored of all, although He was made like unto us."*

The Right Hand of God therefore is not a Corporal place, but is the Majesty, Power, and supreme Dominion of God, the Might and Magnificence of God.

### What doth it signify to Sit on the Right Hand of God?

It signifieth to be partaker of the supreme Dominion and Judgement, and <!-- p.285 -->to reign with equal power with God over all Creatures, and to be adored and worshipped of all Creatures.

### To which nature in Christ was it said by the Father: "Sit thou at my Right Hand"?

To the human nature. For according to His eternal Divinity Christ not only always reigneth with the Father and the Holy Ghost with equal power, but is, and is called, the very Right Hand of God. But according to the manhood He began at a certain time to sit on the Right Hand of God, and reigneth for ever God and man in the Trinity itself over all Creatures, and is invoked of us God and man, as often as we say:

> Our Father, which art in Heaven.

**Chrysostom:**

> *"Let us learn what Nature it is to which He said: Sit thou at my Right Hand, and be partaker of my Seat. That, namely, which had heard: Earth thou art, and unto Earth shalt thou go."*

**Athanasius:**

> *"These words (Sit thou at my Right Hand) were spoken of the Lord's Body. For if 'I fill Heaven and Earth, saith the Lord,' God containeth all things, and is contained of none: on what seat doth He not sit? It is the Body therefore to which He saith: Sit thou at my Right Hand."*

That is, the Divinity of Christ is beforehand everywhere, and filleth all things, and therefore unto it it cannot be said: *Sit thou at my Right Hand.* But these words are directed unto the human nature in Christ, or unto the Son of man, in so far as He is man, the holy Body not being laid aside, but united together in the perfection of the one Godhead unto equality of honour. Likewise **Cyprian**: *"That the invisible God received the visible Son unto His Right Hand is manifest: and that the lowliness of our flesh, honoured with the Throne of Judgement, was well-pleasing to the Father's Highness."*

<!-- p.286 -->

### Doth Christ sit in His humanity on the Right Hand of the Power and Majesty of God in the same manner wherein He sitteth on the Same in His eternal and Divine Nature?

There is not a twofold Session at the Right Hand of God, and a twofold Throne of God, or a twofold Omnipotence and Majesty: but Christ hath at a certain time so given, communicated, and attributed unto His assumed nature also His Glory, Majesty, Dominion, and Power, which He hath from everlasting to everlasting, that that Glory, Might, and Majesty doth wholly shine in the human nature, putteth itself forth through it, and exerciseth its actions with it.

**Cyril:**

> *"The Divine Nature quickeneth of itself, unto whose power the Flesh ascendeth by conjunction. But how that is done we can neither understand with the mind nor speak with the tongue, but we receive it with silence and firm Faith."*

### Dost thou say that the human nature in Christ is equal to God?

No. For God is the Head of Christ the man, **Ephes. 1**,[^F3-4] and the human nature is always under God, although it be carried up above all Creatures. God giveth, it receiveth. God is the Creator: it is created. The Son of God is the very Right Hand of God: but it is carried up to the Right Hand of God. The Son of God is the Lord, the inhabitant: it is the temple and habitation, wherein dwelleth all the fulness of the Godhead ***σωματικῶς*** (bodily). The Son of God is He that assumeth and glorifieth: it is assumed, and glorified in Christ. The Son of God could do all things even without it: but it without Christ and out of Christ could do nothing. The Son of God of Himself and in Himself hath all things: but it, not according to itself, but as given unto it of Christ, receiveth, and in Christ hath and retaineth, all <!-- p.287 -->Glory and Power. And yet it is one and the same Christ, Creator, Father, God, Lord, and our brother, our flesh and blood at the Right Hand of God, to whom be praise, honour, glory, thanks, worship, and celebration for ever, Amen.

## *Inde venturus est iudicare vivos et mortuos* · From Thence He Shall Come to Judge the Quick and the Dead

Christ shall return in the visible form wherein also He ascended into Heaven, **Acts 1**, and in the universal judgement He shall judge the quick, which shall then remain in the World, and the dead, which are already buried, and shall be buried before the judgement, being raised up and called together by the Trump of the Archangel; that is, He shall punish the ungodly, and deliver the godly, and adorn the Church with life eternal, according to the process which Christ Himself describeth, **Matth. 25**.

## *De Tertio Articulo Symboli Apostolici, id est, de Spiritu Sancto, de Ecclesia, et praecipuis Dei beneficiis* · On the Third Article of the Apostles' Creed, that is, of the Holy Ghost, of the Church, and of the Chief Benefits of God

### What are the testimonies proving the Divinity of the Holy Ghost?

1. The testimonies of Scripture, **Acts 5**: *"He hath lied to the Holy Ghost, not unto men, but unto God."*
2. The work of creation, **Job 33**: *"The Spirit of the Lord hath made me."*
3. To be everywhere, **Ps. 139**: *"Whither shall I go from thy Spirit?"*
4. To be ***καρδιογνώστην*** (a knower of hearts), **Wisd. 1**: *"The Spirit of the Lord hath filled the world, containeth all things, and hath knowledge of the voice."*
5. To be the quickener, **Genes. 1**: The Spirit of the Lord did quicken by His moving the things created.
6. <!-- p.288 -->The worship of faith, which is due to God alone.
7. The Confession of the true Church.

### What are the benefits of the Holy Ghost?

1\. To teach. 2. To renew and sanctify. 3. To help by testifying of Christ, by giving, by comforting, by praying. 4. To seal the promises. 5. To be the earnest of our salvation and eternal inheritance. See concerning these the *Paedagogia*.[^F3-5]

### What is the Church?

The Church is the visible assembly of men who embrace the uncorrupt sense of the Gospel, and retain the true use of the Sacraments, and Reverence toward the Ministry, wherein the Son of God is truly effectual through the word, regenerateth and sanctifieth many, that they may be made heirs of eternal life, although in this assembly there be many hypocrites mingled, who nevertheless retain consent concerning Doctrine and concerning the use of the Sacraments.

### What is the Catholic Church?

The Catholic Church is that which throughout the whole world is gathered out of the whole human race by the voice of the Gospel, and embraceth the universal and entire doctrine delivered by the Prophets and Apostles, and retaineth consent and unity in the foundation, although in rites and ceremonies there be some unlikeness, as in the greek and latin Church the Ceremonies are unlike concerning Easter, concerning leaven, concerning fasts.

And the word *Catholic* is opposed to Jewish, Popish, and heretical imaginations; for ***καθολικὸν*** is the same as *universal*: and therefore the Church is so named, because she is not tied to a certain people, to certain persons, to certain places, and to an ordinary succession of Bishops, but is wheresoever the voice of the Gospel soundeth. Next, because in all ages of the world she is gathered out of the human race. Lastly, because she embraceth the whole body of the Prophetical and Apostolical doctrine.

<!-- p.289 -->

### What are the signs that shew the true Church?

They are three. 1. Consent in the uncorrupt doctrine of the Gospel, as touching the foundation. 2. The lawful use of the Sacraments. 3. The Obedience due to the Ministry according to the Gospel. These are the substantials. But Ceremonies are accidentals.

### How is the Church holy, seeing she hath yet much infirmity and filth?

As the justification of man before God consisteth in imputation alone, whereby the righteousness of Christ is imputed to them that believe, which is to be received by faith: so the sanctification of man in this life consisteth in the same imputation, and moreover in an inchoation, whereby spiritual newness is begun in them that believe through the Holy Ghost. The Church therefore in this life is holy in two ways, by Imputation and by inchoation. In the world to come she shall be holy by consummation, where God shall be all in all.

### Why callest thou the Church holy, whereas nevertheless there are many ungodly, evil, and wicked men in the Church?

The article of the holy Church is understood of the living members of the Church, not of the dead members; and she is in this life named holy by *Synecdoche*, because there are many members sanctified by the Holy Ghost and elect. Now the naming is made from the principal part, and the sanctified members are commonly named the living members of the Church. The others, not holy, are called dead members, if so be they consent concerning doctrine.

<!-- p.290 -->

### What is the meaning of these words: "I believe the communion of Saints"?

These words are the definition of the Church, and are added by grammatical apposition. For to him that asketh what the Church is, answer is made briefly and roundly in the words of the Creed: The Church is the communion of Saints, that is, the Society or assembly of men who are called by the Gospel unto the inheritance of eternal life, and are one body, because they partake of one bread, which is the ***κοινωνία*** or communion of the Body of Christ, whereby the Body of Christ is communicated unto Christians, and they have the benefits of Christ in common, namely, Remission of sins, the Holy Ghost, righteousness and life eternal, although in gifts there be unlikeness, and although they live in divers times and places.

### For what causes is the Church of God subject to the cross?

Of all the calamities of the whole human race, of the Church and of the ungodly, the common efficient and impelling causes are four.

1. The common sin of the whole human race, which God willeth to be abolished; therefore He destroyeth the sinful nature by death and other calamities.
2. The errors of the human mind.
3. Evil lusts.
4. The tyranny of the Devil casting men headlong into miseries and calamities.

**And the chief ends are two.**

1. That men may acknowledge that God is wise and just, <!-- p.291 -->and that the wrath of God against sin and unrighteousness is true and dreadful.
2. That by calamities men may be bowed unto the fear of God, unto modesty, and unto the bridling of the lusts whereby afflictions are drawn upon them.

**But of the calamities of the Church fourteen peculiar causes are reckoned, whereof four are impelling.**

1. The Fall of our first Parents, and the sin thence derived unto all.
2. The inward uncleanness of nature, namely doubtings concerning God, carnal security, admiration of self, the ***ἀταξία*** (disorder) of all the affections, etc., which the world neglecteth; this God willeth to be acknowledged and amended by the Church.
3. The hatred of the Devil, who lieth in wait for the Church more furiously than for other men.
4. Certain offences of the Saints in the Church, which are punished with certain punishments.

**The Final Causes are ten.**

1. God layeth not calamities upon us to the end that He may destroy us, but that He may do us good. **Ezech. 33.** *"As I live, I have no pleasure in the death of the sinner; but this I will, that he turn and live."* **Ps. 118 [119].** *"It is good for me, O Lord, that thou hast afflicted me."*
2. That the virtues of the Saints may be proved, exercised, and confirmed, the Fear of God, Faith, invocation, patience, Confession, etc. These highest virtues grow when the Saints are exercised with afflictions; in prosperity they become more languid, and by little and little are utterly quenched.
3. That the Church may acknowledge the wrath of God against the common sin of the whole human race, and against the inward uncleanness, which the world neglecteth.
4. <!-- p.292 -->That afflictions may be testimonies of the immortality of the godly.
5. That they may be testimonies of the certainty of the doctrine, and may be occasions of learning the doctrine more diligently.
6. That the godly may be made like unto the image of the Son of God, **Rom. 8**.
7. That they may amend inward sins, doubtings, pride, security, etc., and that they may beware of future falls.
8. That it may appear that the Church obeyeth, not for any commodities of the body, but for the glory of God alone.
9. That the presence and help of Christ may be more clearly beheld in wonderful deliverances.
10. That they may obtain the most ample rewards promised to the afflicted. **Matth. 5.** *"Of such is the kingdom of Heaven."* **2 Cor. 4.** *"The momentary lightness of afflictions worketh an eternal weight of glory."*

### How are the degrees of afflictions in the Church distinguished?

The kinds are four.

1. ***τιμωρίαι*** (punishments), as the exile of David.
2. ***δοκιμασίαι*** (trials), as the Prison of Joseph.
3. ***μαρτύρια*** (testimonies, or martyrdoms), as the death of the Baptist, of Paul, etc.
4. ***λύτρον*** (ransom), which is the obedience of Christ alone.

Christ hanging on the cross payeth the ***λύτρον***. The Thief on the left hand suffereth a ***τιμωρίαν*** (punishment) corporal and eternal. The Thief converted becometh a ***μάρτυρ***, and witness of Christ's innocency and Merit. Mary and John standing by the cross ***δοκιμάζονται*** (are tried).

### What are the chief and effectual consolations to be set against the cross and calamities?

<!-- p.293 -->

The Heathen or philosophical consolations are seven.

1. The Necessity of bearing evils which cannot be taken away by grieving.
2. Virtue is to be preferred before grief.
3. A good conscience.
4. Examples.
5. The comparison of events.
6. The final Causes, or the profits.
7. The uncertain hope of deliverance.

But of Christian consolation there are twelve fountains, whereof the six former, although in title they agree with the Philosophical, yet contain Greater things; and six others are added, truly effectual, and unknown to the Philosophers, but revealed in the Gospel alone and proper to the Church.

1. The Necessity of obeying God, under whose mighty hand we must be humbled, and in no wise resist His wisdom and justice.
2. The Dignity of virtue, and of godliness toward God, and of confession, which is to be preferred before all things prosperous or adverse.
3. A good conscience, and the healing of the wounded conscience through faith in the Son of God.
4. The examples of the Son of God, of Abel, of the Apostles, of the Martyrs, and of the whole Church, which must be made like unto the image of the Son of God.
5. The comparison of events. For in prosperity all men become more secure and more insolent.
6. The ten final Causes have been before recited, whereunto let testimonies be joined.
7. The acknowledgement of the good will of God certainly promised for Christ's sake, or of the benevolence of God toward us.
8. The Presence of God in afflictions.
9. The Promise of Divine help strengthening hearts in afflictions, that they may be able to bear the burden through.
10. <!-- p.294 -->The Promise of the mitigation of calamities in this life, and of certain deliverance, either in this life or in the life eternal.
11. When unto these promises there cometh faith, which is assured that God is present with us and doth mitigate calamities, invocation, obedience, hope of help and deliverance, patience, etc.: all these joined together become a sacrifice most acceptable unto God.
12. The last and chief place of consolation is the immortality of the Church, the resurrection of bodies, and the haven of eternal life, wherein the Church, delivered out of all miseries, shall most sweetly rest, and shall be filled of God with new Light, Wisdom, Righteousness, glory and gladness.

### Are not calamities mitigated unto them that are truly converted unto God?

That either deliverance or mitigation of temporal punishments doth by God's hand befall them that are converted unto God, the example of the Ninevites sheweth, and the saying of Christ: *"Except ye repent, ye shall all likewise perish."* Now temporal punishments are mitigated altogether for the sake of the Son of God, who is the shadow and covert of the Church. Afterward this also is true, that in the converted the endeavour of well-doing hath its rewards even in this life.

## *Credo Remissionem peccatorum* · I Believe the Remission of Sins

These words deliver the doctrine of the justification of man before God, to which doctrine may fitly be added the doctrine of continual repentance. For we have always need of the free remission of sins for the Son's sake. We shall therefore repeat the true, customary, and simple questions.

### What is justification?

It is the Remission of sins, and the imputation of the righteousness of Christ, <!-- p.295 -->and the free acceptation unto life eternal, not for any virtues or works, but for Christ the Mediator alone, apprehended by faith alone.

### How is remission of sins, or justification, received?

By faith alone in the Son of God Jesus Christ, that is, freely, not for our own works or merits, but only for the one Mediator, Jesus Christ, who was made a sacrifice and propitiator for us, doth man obtain remission of sins, and is made righteous, that is, pleasing and accepted unto God, righteousness being imputed for Christ's sake, and is made an heir of eternal life, when he certainly believeth that these good things are given him of God for the Son's sake. For so it is written, **Acts 10**: *"To him give all the Prophets witness, that through his name all who believe in him receive Remission of sins."* **Rom. 3.** *"We are justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus: whom God hath set forth to be a propitiator, through faith in his blood."*

### What is the difference between the righteousness of the Law and of the Gospel?

The Law promiseth remission of sins, righteousness, and Life eternal, but withal requireth perfect obedience, as the cause.

Therefore the righteousness of the Law is either a perfect obedience of all the powers toward the whole law of God, void of all sin, such as hath been in no man, Christ only excepted; or it is an obedience, such as it is, of the outward members, performing the outward works which the Law of God requireth, and it never satisfieth the law of God.

But the Gospel freely promiseth Remission of <!-- p.296 -->sins, righteousness, and Life eternal, not for our obedience toward the law, but for the Son of God alone, our Lord Jesus Christ, whom we embrace by faith alone.

### What are the weights of the words, "I believe the remission of sins"?

First, it is a testification concerning faith, whereby every man ought firmly to hold that unto himself, believing, remission of sins is certainly given in this life, against the popish doubting.

Next, whereas sins are named in the plural number, it is a confirmation of most sweet consolation: That every sin is remitted, namely unto him that repenteth and by faith embraceth the promise of grace, certainly ratified for the sake of the Son of God.

Thirdly, the word *remitting* teacheth that not only the fault is forgiven, but also the eternal punishment. For to remit is to deliver from the fault and from the eternal punishment: and this relation is chiefly comprehended in the word *remission*, namely the loosing of the obligation unto eternal death and damnation, and the forgiving of the punishment due.

Fourthly. Because the recitation of the Creed is made daily, let the remission of sins be understood of a remission perpetually enduring and promised, even after we are born again; and let the regenerate also know that in this life there always remain the remnants of sin, the remission whereof is to be asked of God.

### What is the cause of Remission?

Unless remission were free, it could not be called remission, but rather compensation. Therefore there is in us no Meritorious cause of remission: But, if thou look unto Christ, there is a full satisfaction for sins.

<!-- p.297 -->

### What doth the word "remission" teach besides?

1\. It destroyeth the dogma of the Monks concerning satisfactions. 2. It overthroweth all human merits. 3. It refuteth the error of the Novatians, who denied to the lapsed a return unto grace. 4. It testifieth that all the regenerate carry sin about with them, so long as they are conversant in this life. 5. It openeth the door of salvation to wretched and afflicted consciences. 6. In the agony of death it is a fountain of consolation. 7. It perpetually testifieth of the price of the sacrifice, by the benefit whereof we are received into grace, earnestly asking pardon.[^F3-6]

### Is not sin abolished, when it is remitted?

There are two things in sin, namely the defect and the guilt. The defect itself is daily diminished in the Saints through the mortification of the old man. But while we carry about this mass, it is not altogether abolished. For there remaineth perpetually in this life the strife of the spirit and the flesh in the Saints, **Galat. 5**. But the guilt is so abolished in the godly, that God saith He will not remember their sins, **Isaiah 45**:[^F3-7] *"I blot out thine iniquities before me, and will not remember thy sins."*

### Is the remission of the fault distinct from the remission of the eternal punishment?

It is not distinct, but the remission of the fault is altogether the same as the Remission of the eternal punishment, according to the saying, **Isa. 45**: *"I daily blot out thine iniquities before me, and will not remember thy sins any more."* And **Isaiah 1**: *"Though your sins be as Scarlet, and as crimson,"* etc.

<!-- p.298 -->

### Is the remission of the fault distinct from the remission of temporal punishment in this life?

It is distinct. As Adam and David receive remission of the fault, and yet withal are subjected to punishments in this life. But these punishments are in no wise compensations or merits in place of the eternal punishment, as the Monks have feigned, but have altogether other causes, which it is necessary for all men to know, for doctrine and consolation.

### Why then are the Saints often punished for their sins?

The causes have been set forth above. Yet the Saints are not so much chastised because they have sinned, as restrained, that they sin no more. For God chasteneth His Saints with a fatherly discipline, that they should not be condemned with this world; but He punisheth not with judicial vengeance, that they should perish.

### What is justifying Faith?

It is a knowledge of the person and benefits of Christ and of the whole doctrine delivered of God: and an assent, whereby we embrace the whole doctrine, and in it the promise of free reconciliation, or of the imputation of righteousness, to be given for Christ's sake: and a trust resting in Christ the Mediator according to the promise, and certainly holding that sins are remitted unto him, that he pleaseth God, that he is received and heard for Christ alone, freely, not for his own worthiness.

### How is this proposition to be understood, "By faith alone we are righteous"?

<!-- p.299 -->

It is to be understood correlatively, namely, that for the Son of God alone, our Lord Jesus Christ, we receive remission of sins, righteousness, and life eternal freely, not for our own merits.

And the Scripture therefore useth this form of words, *By faith we are justified*, that it may teach both things.

1. What is the cause or merit of justification, or what are the benefits of Christ: Namely, that through and for Christ alone there is given unto us remission of sins, righteousness, and life eternal.
2. Next, how they ought to be applied unto us and transferred unto us: Namely, by faith alone embracing the promise and resting in Christ. It is commonly said thus: *"Not for our believing, but for Him in whom we believe, are we justified."* Likewise: *"As a ring is greatly esteemed for the gem's sake: So faith justifieth, not because it is a quality in us, but because it apprehendeth the Mediator."*
