# *De Secunda Parte Catechismi* · On the Second Part of the Catechism

### What is the second part of the Catechism?

The Apostles' Creed (*Symbolum Apostolicum*).

### What is contained in the Creed?

A certain summary doctrine of the Articles of faith, or heads of the Christian Religion, by the true understanding and profession whereof the Church is distinguished from all other sects which are strangers to the Christian Religion.

<!-- p.227 -->

### What is the reason of the name?

*Symbolum*, ***ἀπὸ τοῦ συμβάλλειν***, from bringing together (*a conferendo*), is a soldier's word, and signifieth the token (*tessera*) given unto soldiers, whereby they may be discerned from enemies and spies; in German, *ein losung, oder kennzeichen* (a watchword, or mark of recognition). The Symbol of the Church, therefore, is the token of the soldiers of Christ, that is, of them that believe, by which they testify that they have given in their names to Christ alone, and that they dissent from all other sects.

### Why is this Creed called the Apostles'?

Either because it was written by the Apostles, and delivered as it were from hand to hand unto the Church, as Antiquity hath always held, as doth plainly appear out of **Irenaeus** and **Tertullian**, who name this Creed the Apostolic tradition; or because the Apostolic doctrine, gathered as it were into one bundle and comprehended, is extant in this Creed, that it may be as it were the most firm foundation of the whole Christian doctrine.

### Why is the Creed to be recited daily?

1. Because it putteth us in mind of God, and of the chief works and benefits of God.
2. Because in this recitation the benefits of the Son of God are applied unto him that believeth.
3. Because it is a consolation in the greatest dangers.
4. Because it is a confession against the devils and heretics and other ungodly men, and withal it ministereth weapons whereby Satan is put to flight and overcome.
<!-- p.228 -->
5. Because it accustometh us to think upon the testimonies of the doctrine, namely, the revelations of God and the miracles.

### How many Creeds of the Christian faith are there in the Church?

There are three authentic ones: the Apostles', the Nicene, and the Athanasian.

Of the Apostles' Creed the words of **Irenaeus** are these: *"We ought not to seek among others the truth which it is easy to take from the Church, seeing the Apostles have most fully bestowed upon her, as into a rich storehouse, all things that are of the truth, that every man, whosoever will, may take from her the drink of life. For this is the entrance of life; but all the rest are thieves and robbers: wherefore we ought indeed to shun them; but the things that are of the Church to love with great diligence, and to lay hold of the tradition of the truth,"* etc.[^F1-1]

The Nicene hath its name from Nicaea, a city of Bithynia, where in the year of Christ 328[^F1-2] the Emperor **Constantine the Great**, three hundred and eighteen Bishops being gathered together, appointed an Ecumenical Synod against Arius, the blasphemer of the Son of God, which lasted longer than three years.

The Athanasian Creed is thought to have been written by **Athanasius** against the Arians and the Pneumatomachi, in the Synod of Alexandria, which was assembled in the year of Christ 371.

### Into how many Articles is the Creed commonly distributed?

The Articles in the Creed are distinguished into three degrees, according to the three persons of the Godhead, which are: the Father, the Son, and the Holy Ghost. The first Article is of Creation. The second of Redemption. The third of Sanctification.

<!-- p.229 -->

### What is the subdivision of the three Articles of faith?

The second Article is divided into six Articles, the third into five, so that with the first Article there are twelve Articles of faith, according to the number of the twelve Apostles. For this is a godly division of Antiquity, as appeareth out of **Augustine**.

### Recite the Nicene Creed.

> I believe in one God, the Father Almighty, maker of heaven and earth, of all things visible and invisible. And in one Lord Jesus Christ, the only begotten Son of God, and begotten of the Father before all worlds: God of God (**Basil**: ***θεὸν ἐκ θεοῦ***, God of God. **Epiphanius**: ***τουτέστιν ἐκ τῆς οὐσίας τοῦ πατρός***, that is, of the substance of the Father), Light of Light, very God of very God: begotten, not made, coessential with the Father, by whom all things were made (**Epiphanius**: which are in heaven, and in earth). Who for us men, and for our salvation, came down from heaven, and was incarnate by the Holy Ghost of the Virgin Mary. AND WAS MADE MAN. He was crucified also for us under Pontius Pilate, he suffered and was buried. And he rose again the third day according to the Scriptures. And ascended into heaven, sitteth at the right hand of the Father. And he shall come again with glory to judge the quick and the dead, whose kingdom shall have no end. And in the Holy Ghost, the Lord, the giver of life, who proceedeth from the Father and the Son, who with the Father and the Son together is worshipped and glorified. Who spake by the Prophets. And one holy Catholic and Apostolic Church. I acknowledge one Baptism for the remission of sins, <!-- p.231 --> and I look for the resurrection of the dead, and the life of the world to come. Amen.

This Creed, when the Pneumatomachi, that is, they who blaspheming the Holy Ghost said that he was created, were growing strong, was set forth more clearly in more words, which they that came to Baptism did recite, as **Epiphanius** witnesseth.[^F1-3] For where it is said in the Creed, *"And was incarnate,"* there these words were added besides: *"That is, perfectly born of the holy Mary, ever virgin, by the Holy Ghost. And was made man, that is, he took upon him perfect man, soul and body and mind, and all things, whatsoever man is, without sin: not of the seed of a man, neither in another man, but he formed flesh unto himself, into one holy union: not as he breathed, spake, and wrought in the Prophets, but perfectly made flesh. For the Word was made flesh, not suffering a change, nor altering his Godhead into the human nature, but uniting this into one holy perfection and Godhead. For there is one Lord, Jesus Christ, and not two. The same God, and the same Lord, and the same King: who suffered in the flesh, and rose again, and ascended into heaven in the very body, in glory, who shall judge the quick and the dead, whose kingdom shall have no end.*

*"And we believe in the Holy Ghost, who spake in the Law, and preached in the Prophets, and came down upon Jordan, and spake in the Apostles, and dwelleth in the saints. And thus we believe in him, that he is the Holy Spirit, the Spirit of God, the perfect Spirit, the Spirit the Comforter, uncreated, proceeding from the Father and the Son. And we believe one Catholic and Apostolic Church: and one baptism of repentance, and the resurrection of the dead, and the judgement of righteous souls, and of bodies:[^F1-4] and the kingdom of heaven, and life everlasting. Amen."*

## *De Primo Articulo Symboli Apostolici* · Of the First Article of the Apostles' Creed

### Why sayest thou, I believe in God?

For five causes chiefly. 1. That I may distinguish my faith from intuitive knowledge (*intuitiva noticia*). For in this life faith is the certainty of things not seen. But in the life to come *Fides* shall be written with a V, *vides* (thou seest). 2. That I may testify that I detest the Academic doubting and suspension of judgement, which holdeth no certain assent concerning God. 3. That I may shew that I have not only an historical faith, but that as a Christian I do truly believe, and trust in God, with mind, will, and heart. 4. That I may shew that the benefits of God are applied to me that believe. 5. That I may reject all idols, which are nothing, and may submit myself to the revelation of God, and may believe his word or Promise.

### To which person doth the name of God pertain?

When we say, *I believe in God*, we understand not any one person apart, but the one Essence of God common to the three persons, the Father, the Son, and the Holy Ghost. For there is one God, and one Essence of God, and there are not more Gods, **Deut. 9**.[^F1-5] But there are three persons of the Godhead, neither more nor fewer. For so hath God revealed himself, especially in the Baptism of Christ, **Matt. 3**.

### What is God?

God is a spiritual Essence, understanding, eternal, other than <!-- p.236 --> all creatures, true, good, righteous, chaste, merciful, bountiful, most free, of infinite wisdom and power, angry with sins: the eternal Father, who begat the Son, his own image, from everlasting; and the Son, the coeternal image of the Father; and the Holy Ghost, proceeding from the Father and the Son: even as the Godhead hath revealed itself by a sure word and by divine testimonies, that the eternal Father, with the Son and the Holy Ghost, did make and doth preserve heaven and earth and all creatures, and is present with all creatures as touching their preservation, and gathereth unto himself in mankind a Church, for the Son's sake and through him, that he may be acknowledged, called upon, and praised by it in life everlasting.[^F1-6]

### What is called Essence?

***Οὐσία***, or Essence (*Essentia*), signifieth a nature common to many individuals, which containeth the whole essential property of each of them.

### What is a Person?

***ὑπόστασις*** is commonly defined thus: A Person is an individual substance, understanding, incommunicable, not sustained in another.

### Why are only three persons of the Godhead to be acknowledged and called upon?

Because God hath so revealed himself in the Baptism of Christ, and in the institution of our Baptism. And it is a universal Rule: That we ought not to think and teach otherwise concerning the essence and will of God, than as God hath revealed himself in his word, delivered by the Prophets and Apostles.

### How are the persons of the Godhead distinguished?

The Father is the first person, begetting the Son, his own image. <!-- p.237 --> The Son is the second person, begotten of the eternal Father, and sent to reveal the Gospel, and to take upon him the nature of man, and to deliver it.

The Holy Ghost is the third person of the Godhead, proceeding from the Father and the Son, and sent into the hearts of them that believe, that he may kindle in them such motions, love, and joy, as he himself is.

### How many are the properties, or idioms, of the Godhead?

The properties (*proprietates*) are of two sorts. Some are common to the three persons; others are proper, belonging to the several persons, whereby the persons are distinguished. The common ones also are of two sorts, namely essential, and external. The proper ones, belonging to the several persons, are likewise of two sorts, internal, and external. Let therefore this little table be before the eyes.

- **The properties common to the three persons are either:**
    - *Essential*: as goodness, righteousness, power, majesty, glory divine and eternal. For these are not accidents in God, but are the very essence of God.
    - *External*: as the work of creation, the preservation of things, the redemption of mankind, the order and property of the persons being kept.[^F1-7]
- **The personal properties are either:**
    - *Internal*: as, to beget is proper to the Father; to be begotten is proper to the Son; to proceed is proper to the Holy Ghost.
    - *External*, taken from the benefits toward the Church: as to send and to be sent. The Father sendeth. The Son is sent. The Holy Ghost is sent.

**Argument.**

No accident falleth upon God, because God is eternal and unchangeable.

<!-- p.238 -->

Righteousness, wisdom, power, are accidents.

Therefore these attributes do not belong unto God.

**I answer to the Major.** No absolute accident falleth upon God, as quantity, or changeable quality; but yet there are many relations belonging unto God, as of the Father to the Son, of the Son to the Father, etc.

**To the Minor.** Righteousness, wisdom, power, strength, and the like attributes are accidents in reasonable creatures, but not in the Creator, in whom righteousness is the essence itself, ***ἰδίωμα οὐσιῶδες καὶ συστατικόν*** (an essential and constitutive property). For God is not wise, righteous, mighty, strong, by wisdom, righteousness, power, strength, as by some quality or accident cleaving in the divine essence; but the divine essence itself is wisdom, power, righteousness, strength, etc.

### A Rule concerning the essential properties.

The essential properties (*idiomata*) of the Godhead cannot be essentially communicated to any one without the Trinity, unless the divine Essence itself be communicated, as the Father hath communicated unto the Son his divine essence, and all the properties thereof.

### How is the saying of Augustine to be understood, The works of the Godhead outward are undivided, but the works inward are divided?

Works outward (*opera ad extra*) are the name given to the external actions, as the creation and preservation of things, the redemption of mankind, salvation, sanctification. For these works are undivided, that is, common to the three persons. But works inward (*opera ad intra*) are the internal and eternal actions of the Godhead, whereby the persons are distinguished: as to beget, to be begotten, to proceed.

<!-- p.239 -->

**Argument.**

The works of the Trinity outward are undivided.

The salvation of man is a work outward.

Therefore it is wrought no otherwise by the Son, than by the Father and the Holy Ghost.

**I answer to the Major.** The rule is true, but with the property of each person kept. The Father is not sent. The Son and the Holy Ghost are sent: the Son of God, that he may become the Son of man, redeeming man; wherefore it is said, *The Son worketh by himself, but not of himself*; and the Holy Ghost, that he may sanctify the Church. Of this matter the **Schoolmen** have rightly spoken after this manner: The divine nature hath united unto itself the human nature, not in the three persons in common, but properly in the hypostasis of the Son alone; as if three maidens should put a coat upon one of themselves.

### How do to be begotten and to proceed differ?

To be begotten is from the knowing power, because that which is begotten is the image of him that begetteth. To proceed is from the will, ***τὸ ἐκπορεύεσθαι*** (to go forth), because the Holy Ghost is the mover or stirrer. For ***πόρος*** signifieth also motion, and ***ἐκπόρευσις*** a going out, a setting forth, and efficacy.

### How do these differ: to believe that God is, to believe in God, and to believe God?

To believe that God is (*credere Deum*), is to ascribe unto him essence, that he is God. To believe in God (*credere in Deum*), is with firm trust to rest in God. To believe God (*credere Deo*), is to ascribe truth unto God, and to assent unto his word. Rightly therefore we say: I believe in the Father, I believe in the Son, I believe in the Holy Ghost. For unto God alone is the worship of faith to be given. But we say not, I believe in the Church, but, I believe the Church. For to believe in anything belongeth to the Godhead; to believe anything agreeth to every thing. Faith, however, comprehendeth all: to believe that God is, to believe God, and to believe in God.

<!-- p.240 -->

But whereas in the Nicene and Constantinopolitan Creed the preposition IN is added, this was done in regard and respect of the Holy Ghost, who spake by the Prophets IN one Catholic and Apostolic Church; that is, one and the same Spirit hath wrought all things in the Old and in the New Testament. And **Epiphanius**, when he saith, ***πιστεύομεν εἰς μίαν καθολικὴν καὶ ἀποστολικὴν Ἐκκλησίαν*** (we believe in one Catholic and Apostolic Church), speaketh ***καταχρηστικῶς*** (by an improper use of the word), understanding not worship or trust, but the knowledge of the one Church, which is one body in the one Holy Ghost.

### Of how many sorts is the knowledge of God?

Of two: universal and peculiar. The universal is common to all men, **Rom. 1**, building up out of the beholding and consideration of things some manner of description of God, such as is that of **Plato**, or rather of **Speusippus**: *God is an eternal mind, the cause of good in nature.* The peculiar is proper to the sons of God, or to the Church of Christ.

### By what ways is God known?

There are two ways: the Word, and the Testimonies. For we must lay hold on God as such a one as he hath revealed himself to be by the word and the testimonies. The Word is the Law and the Gospel. The Testimonies either are added for the confirming of the word, as all the miracles, the preserving of the godly in the flood, the passage through the Red sea, the raisings of the dead, and the like; or they confirm the trust conceived out of the word, as the sacraments of the Old and of the New Testament: circumcision, the lamb; Baptism, the Lord's Supper.

### What is Faith?

Christian Faith is a knowledge of the whole doctrine delivered of God, and an assent whereby we embrace the whole doctrine, and in it the promise of free reconciliation, or of the imputation <!-- p.241 --> of righteousness, to be given for Christ's sake; and a trust in Christ the Mediator, resting according to the promise, and certainly holding that a man's sins are forgiven him, that he pleaseth God, that he is received and heard for Christ's sake alone, freely, not for his own worthiness.

### What faith doth the Creed require concerning the first person of the Trinity?

1. That he is God.
2. That he is Father.
3. That he is Almighty.
4. That he is the Creator of heaven and earth.

The name of God pertaineth to the Father, to the Son, and to the Holy Ghost.

**Rules.**

1. Whatsoever is said of one person absolutely, namely of God, and of the one Essence, the same must be said also of the other two; as: The Father is God, Lord, Almighty, Creator. Such also is the Son, such also the Holy Ghost.
2. By the attributes of each person the persons are distinguished one from another, but the one nature of the Godhead is not divided. The Father begetteth, the Son is begotten, the Holy Ghost proceedeth. These are the properties of the persons, and yet the Essence is one.

### Of what things doth the name of God put us in mind?

1. That there is one God. 2. That he is the fountain of all good. 3. That all who call upon him do draw from thence. 4. That he is a hater of evil things. 5. That he is longsuffering.[^F1-8]

<!-- p.242 -->

### Why is he named Father?

In a twofold respect: of the only begotten Son; and in this regard the first person of the Trinity alone is called Father. Then in respect of men, in a fourfold regard: 1. Of Creation. 2. Of Government. 3. Of Adoption. 4. Of the giving of all good things. And so the whole Trinity is called Father. Whence we say, *Our Father which art in heaven.*

### Of what things doth the name of Father put us in mind?

1. That God beareth toward us a fatherly care. 2. That he will not cast us off. 3. That we owe him all things, first of all honour, imitation, that is, love, mercy toward the needy, and obedience and patience.

### What doth the word Almighty shew?

It sheweth first, that God can do all things which he will, that is, which he hath appointed, ordained, and promised. Secondly, that no second or natural causes, and no power, can resist God, or hinder that which he hath promised. Thirdly, that God is the preserver of his Church, even when it is destitute of the means known to human reason. Fourthly, that God will destroy all the enemies of his Word. Fifthly, that our hope of the future deliverance promised, and of the damnation of the ungodly, is sure.

**Rule.**

1. Christ can be wheresoever he willeth to be, not only <!-- p.243 --> in his divine nature, wherewith he always filleth all things, but also in his human nature, or with his Body and Blood, even against the nature of a natural body, because he is almighty.
2. Christ with his Body is there, where he hath promised that he will be, even though this be done against the rule and course of natural properties; as in the Church, and in a peculiar manner, according to the words of institution, in the Supper; because he is true.

### Wherefore is mention added of the Creation of heaven and earth?

That by this argument the Almightiness of God may be shewn, who created all things out of nothing.

### What things are to be thought upon concerning Creation?

Five heads are to be considered.

**I. Wisdom.**

The first, what is the efficient cause, and who is the Master-builder of this wonderful workmanship; namely, that creation is the common work of the eternal Father, of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost, **Ps. 33**.

**II. Almightiness.**

The second, of what matter the world was made; namely, of none: for all created things, visible and invisible, were made of nothing, **Ps. 33**: *"He spake, and it was done."* God speaking and commanding, all things are made.

<!-- p.244 -->

**III. Freedom.**

The third, that God made the world freely and willingly, without Stoic necessity, seeing that God was before the world from everlasting, **Ps. 135**: *"Whatsoever he pleased, that did he in heaven, and in earth."* This Rule therefore is to be held: God is a most free agent, and is not bound to second causes, or to physical or natural rules, but whatsoever he will, and what he hath promised, he is able to do, above all things and exceeding abundantly, **Eph. 3**. It is therefore a foul error to say that, by reason of the circumscription of a natural body, Christ cannot be present with his Body and Blood in the Supper at one and the same time in divers places, wheresoever on earth the Supper is administered according to the Institution of Christ.

**IIII. Righteousness.**

The fourth, that there is truly a providence, because God hath not departed from his work, but is present with his creature by essence, by presence, and by power (*enter, praesenter, et potenter*), sustaining, cherishing, and preserving the nature made by him, according to that saying, **Heb. 1**: *"Upholding all things by the word of his power,"* and **Acts 17**: *"In him we live, and move, and have our being."*

**V. Goodness.**

The fifth is, that God made the world and all creatures to this end, that he might reveal himself, shew forth testimonies of himself, and communicate unto the reasonable nature his wisdom <!-- p.245 --> and goodness, and that he might in turn be acknowledged and praised by it in all eternity.

### What then is Creation?

Creation is an external action of God, whereby God of his own will framed out of nothing, by his own counsel, all other things which are without the divine Essence, visible and invisible, that he might found unto himself an everlasting Church, by which he may be acknowledged and praised, and in which he may dwell for ever.

### Of which person is Creation the work?

Of all the three persons, according to **Ps. 33**: *"By the Word of the Lord were the heavens made; and all the host of them by the Spirit of his mouth."*

### Wherefore is Creation ascribed unto the Father, seeing it is the common work of the Godhead?

First, this is done because each person of the Godhead hath revealed himself chiefly in some one work: the Father in Creation, the Son in Redemption, the Holy Ghost in Sanctification. Secondly, because Creation is the first work, which is deservedly ascribed to the first person, as a sign and mark pointing out the Father. Thirdly, because the Father is the first fountain of the Godhead, and doth also will the Creation, and expresseth this his will by the Word, and is ***προκαταρκτικὴ αἰτία τῶν γενομένων*** (the originating cause of the things that are made), even as the Son is ***δημιουργική*** (the creating), and the Holy Ghost ***τελειωτικὴ αἰτία*** (the perfecting cause).[^F1-9]

**Argument.**

He that calleth upon God the Creator of heaven and earth, calleth upon the true God.

<!-- p.246 -->

The Turks and the heathen call upon God the Creator of heaven and earth.

Therefore they call upon the true God.

**I answer to the Major.** The Major is true according to the divine revelation, as God hath revealed himself in his Word. The Minor I deny. For there is no other Creator of heaven and earth but God the Father, the Son, and the Holy Ghost, as God hath revealed himself. Seeing that the Mahometans and the heathen do not only not call upon this God, but flatly deny him, they call not upon the true God, but feign unto themselves an idol, which is not God.

### What is the difference between God, who is supremely Good, and created things, which also are good, as it is written: God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good?

God alone is good, **Luke 18**, namely by nature, cause, and origin, not having the ground of his goodness from elsewhere. But created things are good, both because they were made of God and ordained to good uses, and because each of them in its own order ought to serve God, and they please God.

### What are the degrees of created things?

Four. The lowest is of things which only are, or exist, that they may serve the three degrees following, as the lower serve the higher; such as are the elements, stones, metals. The next degree is of things which are and live, with the vegetative life, that they may serve the two degrees following; such as are plants, herbs, shrubs. The third is of things which are, live, <!-- p.247 --> and have sense, that they may serve the last degree; such as are the brute beasts. The fourth is of things which not only are, live, and have sense, but also understand; such as are men, who have God above them, whom they themselves ought to serve. For a man not serving God doth against his own nature, and overturneth the order of the nature of things. Rightly therefore is it said: All things were made for man; but man was made for God, that he may acknowledge and praise him, and serve him: which thing the Angels also do, ***οἱ ὑμνοῦντες καὶ διακονοῦντες*** (they that sing praise and minister).

### What is providence?

It is the action of the divine mind, whereby it beholdeth all things, and foreseeth all events to come; and it is the divine government or administration, whereby God sustaineth, governeth, ordereth, and preserveth the whole of nature, made by himself, unto the glory of his Name, and chiefly careth for mankind, guardeth civil society, punisheth heinous crimes with heinous punishments, and at the last adorneth the righteous with rewards.

### How many are the modes of the presence of God?

Four. The first is the universal presence, whereby God filleth all things. The second degree is that whereby God is present and dwelleth, in a manner also visible and inseparable, in the Angels and the blessed, as in his own temples and lodgings. The third degree is that whereby God in this life is present with the regenerate, but separably; likewise, whereby he is present with his whole Ministry, and in his Sacraments, in that manner which he himself hath defined. The fourth degree is proper to the Son of God, who hath taken upon him the human nature by an inseparable and hypostatic union, and hath in himself lifted it up to the right hand of Almighty God, and reigneth with equal power with the Father and the Holy Ghost, God and man.

### Of how many sorts are reasonable creatures?

Of two. Some are incorporeal and invisible, as Angels and the souls of men. Others are corporeal and visible, as men, etc.

### What is an Angel?

A good Angel is a created substance, spiritual, made of God after the image of God, understanding, true, righteous, most free, which is not a part of another kind, and is immortal, confirmed of God, that it may continue in the righteousness given unto it, and ordained to the praising of the glory of God, and to the ministry of the Church and of the godly.

An evil Angel is a spiritual substance, created of God out of nothing after the image of God, which of its own will freely turned itself away from God, out of mere pride and stubbornness, despising the Son of God, and denied and forsook the truth it had known, and chose lying and murder, and therefore was cast down into everlasting punishments, raging with hatred of God, and exercising cruelty against mankind out of hatred of the Son of God, and driving others to other wickednesses, blasphemies, lies, murders, lusts, and the like, not that it may be the better with itself, but out of ***ἐπιχαιρεκακίαν*** (joy at another's hurt), and that it may load God and our Lord Jesus Christ with reproaches.

### What is the reasonable soul?

The soul of man is a spiritual substance, made of God after the image of God, understanding, true, free, and righteous, which is the chief part of man, the mind and the will, neither is it put out when it departeth from the body, but is immortal; and so long as it abideth in the <!-- p.249 --> body, it can be turned unto God, being helped of God, and can fall away from God by its own will.

### What is man?

He is a substance created of God, consisting of a body and a reasonable soul, made after the image of God with a free will, and the ruler of the lower creatures, that he may acknowledge and praise God perpetually.

### Of how many sorts is the state of man?

Of four. The first is the state of innocency and of the wholeness of the entire image of God in man, before the Fall. The second is the state of miseries under sin and under the evil of guilt and of punishment. The third is the state of grace, proper to them that believe and are regenerate in the Church of Christ. The fourth is the state of consummation and of glory in everlasting blessedness; but of the ungodly, in hell.

### What is the Image of God?

The Image of God, after which man was made, was the entire agreement, ***εὐταξία*** (good order), and likeness of all the powers of man with the Archetype, or God himself: in the mind, a true and clear knowledge of God; in the will, true obedience, burning love, and joy in God; in the heart, the conformity of all the affections with the Law planted in the mind, *"righteousness and true holiness,"* **Eph. 4**; and dominion over all things of the lower orders.

### Of how many sorts is the Image of God?

Of two: Natural, and of Grace (*gratuita*). The natural Image of God is in Christ alone, who is both the essential Image of the eternal Father, ***ὁμοούσιος, συναΐδιος, καὶ ὁμότιμος*** (of one substance, coeternal, and of equal honour), the Character (express image) of the Father's substance, ***λόγος καὶ ἀπαύγασμα*** (the Word and the brightness), and is also the Image through the glory of the power shining forth in Christ by the power of the divine works, according to that saying, **John 14**: *"Philip, he that hath seen me <!-- p.250 --> hath seen the Father."* But the Image of grace is that after which man was made.

### What is original righteousness?

It is the wholeness of human nature, in which the Image of God shineth, the obedience and ***εὐταξία*** (good order) of all the powers, of the mind, the will, the heart, the affections, the body, and the actions, toward God, and the approval and acceptance of mankind with God.

### What is Free Will?

It is the mind and the will, and withal the faculty of the mind and reason to discern the honest and the base, good and evil, and of the will to choose which of the two it listeth, and of the powers to perform either of them.

### Give a Rule concerning Free Will.

Free Will (*Liberum Arbitrium*) is of those things in which the judgement is whole, the will not hindered, and the powers sufficient.

### How many are the degrees of actions?

Four. 1. The natural, as to eat, to drink, to govern the faculty of moving from place to place, to go, to stand. 2. The moral, as to bear the office of a Magistrate, to be the father of a household, to serve, to till the fields, to deal in contracts. 3. The outward ecclesiastical, as to teach, to hear the Word, to come together, to use the Sacraments. 4. The spiritual and inward: to believe, to pray aright, to fulfil the Law, etc.

### In which degrees doth man either have, or not have, Free Will?

In the first degree man hath Free Will. In the second and third degree, seeing that the judgement is not so whole, nor the will so cheerful, as in the first degree, Free Will is very often hindered by two things, the tyranny of the Devil, and the weakness of the powers; so that both the ***προαιρετικὸν*** and the ***δεσποτικὸν*** <!-- p.251 --> kind (the power that chooseth, and the power that commandeth) is often hindered, and the ***αὐτεξούσιον*** (the power of self-determination) falleth utterly to the ground. In the fourth degree man, left to himself, hath no freedom of the will at all. For the ***φρόνημα*** of the flesh (the carnal mind), that is, the judgement, the will, and the powers, are a grudge and enmity against God. There is need therefore of the Word of God and of the Holy Ghost, for the informing of the judgement, and the reforming of the will and of the heart, and for a willing obedience pleasing unto God for the Son's sake.
