### What do these particles exclude: By faith alone, Freely, Without the Law, Without the works of the Law?

They do not exclude the merit of Christ, but they exclude the merit and worthiness of all our works, whether these go before, or are present, or follow after; and they transfer the cause of the remission of sins, or of justification, unto Christ the Mediator alone, laid hold of by faith, so that the article of Justification standeth in the sole merit of Christ alone, without any addition of our works. And in the beginning, the middle and the end it is always to be held that we are righteous by faith and pleasing unto God, and heirs of life everlasting, for the sake of Christ the Mediator alone, not for the sake of any works or virtues of ours, as **Paul** disputeth at large, **Gal. 3**. **Rom. 5**: *"Being justified <!-- p.300 --> by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ."* **Hab. 2**: *"The just shall live by his faith."* Neither can faith lean upon two things, Christ and our works. Otherwise it would become altogether uncertain, and would bring no firm consolation, **Rom. 4**. For sins always remain even in the Saints in this life, for which they pray: *"Forgive us our debts."* Therefore Christ is a Priest for ever, making intercession for us and applying his righteousness unto us; *"neither is there salvation in any other."*

### Why is the exclusive particle "Freely" to be retained, and likewise "We are justified by faith alone"?

For five causes.

1. Because there is no other means whereby we can acknowledge and lay hold of the promise of grace and of Christ, save faith alone.

2. That the honour which is due and proper unto Christ may be given unto him: namely, that his obedience alone is the merit of the remission of sins, of righteousness, and of the life everlasting that is to be given unto us, and that our works are not a merit for the sake of which we are received.

3. That consciences may have a sure and firm consolation, the promise of grace leaneth upon the Son of God alone and not upon our merits; therefore it is said, *"By faith alone,"* that the heart may assuredly hold that God is gracious unto us for Christ's sake. This cause **Paul** rehearseth, **Rom. 4**: *"Therefore it is of faith, freely, that the promise might be sure."*

4. That the difference of the Law and the Gospel may be the more clearly seen. For the particles *Freely*, *By faith*, and the express mention of the Mediator do chiefly distinguish the Law and the Gospel.

5. That true invocation may be rendered, which must needs lean upon the Mediator alone, the Son of God, not upon our virtues.

### What is it that is named Grace, and what the Gift?

<!-- p.301 -->

*Grace* (*gratia*) is justification itself, that is, the absolution from sin of the person that believeth in Christ, the imputation of the righteousness of Christ, and the acceptance unto life everlasting, freely, for Christ's sake. But the *Gift* (*donum*) is the giving of the Holy Ghost, whereby the man that is justified by faith alone is regenerated, that is, is mortified and quickened, so that he now becometh a new creature and liveth according to the will of God, so far indeed as this can be done through human weakness, and that after the example of the holy Patriarchs and of other godly men, whose chief endeavour it was to set obedience toward God before all the things of the world. Even as we see in Abraham, who at the commandment of God was ready to slay his only son, than whom he had nothing in the world more precious, more pleasant, more sweet. And therefore **Paul** admonisheth that, *"warring a good warfare, we hold faith and a good conscience."*

### What is repentance?

Repentance (*poenitentia*), or conversion unto God, is to be terrified by the knowledge of the wrath of God against sins, and to grieve that we have offended God, and yet not to sink under the grief, but by faith in Christ to receive remission of sins, grace and the Holy Ghost, and in this consolation to begin a new obedience agreeing with the Law of God, and to be an heir of life everlasting.

### What are the parts of repentance?

They are three: 1. Contrition, which is truly to be terrified by the knowledge of the wrath of God against our sins, and to grieve that we have offended God. 2. Faith, acknowledging that the wrath of God against our sins is appeased for Christ's sake, and assuredly holding that sins are forgiven it, and that it pleaseth God for Christ's sake. 3. New obedience, which is, faith in Christ shining before, <!-- p.302 --> to obey God according to all his commandments, to this end, that God may be honoured and praised.

### Is the preaching of repentance to be taken from the Law, or from the Gospel?

Repentance properly so called, that is, contrition, the knowledge of sin and of the wrath of God, which is grief in the conscience, hath its origin simply from the Law. But that this contrition may become wholesome, and may not degenerate into servile fear and into despair, but may become filial fear, and that the man may be converted unto God, there is need of the doctrine of the Gospel, which showeth the Mediator, and through which the Holy Ghost kindleth in the mind the thought of the promise concerning the free remission of sins for Christ's sake, and enlighteneth and moveth the will, that it may assent and by true confidence apply the merit of Christ unto itself.

### In what articles chiefly is the conflict with the Adversaries?

In three: namely, concerning the Church, concerning the Remission of sins, concerning Repentance. The Church they transform into a political kingdom; they feign that the Roman Pontiff is the monarch and supreme head in the Church; they ascribe unto the Pope, the Bishops and the Councils the power of framing doctrines and worships, and of interpreting the Scriptures with a royal or praetorian authority; they bind the Church to the ordinary succession of the Pope and the Bishops, and to their traditions and interpretation. They contend that the observances of such decrees and traditions are of the necessity of salvation. Afterward they enact laws to establish their own power, as is the law concerning celibacy, and by bodily force they compel men to obey. This hypothesis being laid down, they afterward under the pretext of the Church establish idols, as the invocation <!-- p.303 --> of the dead, the profanation of the Supper in Masses, and the carrying about of the bread in shows.

The doctrine concerning justification, or the remission of sins, they corrupt in four ways. First, they teach that it is enough when a man hath a general faith, whereby he believeth that remission of sins befalleth some, although he distrusteth that it befalleth himself. Secondly, they teach that a man meriteth remission of sins and is righteous, not for the sake of Christ alone by faith alone, but also by good works, or by his own virtues agreeing with the Law of God; likewise by monastic works, Masses, fastings, etc. Thirdly, since no man knoweth whether he hath enough of good works, they teach that we must always doubt whether we have remission of sins, and whether we be in grace. Fourthly, they teach that a man can in this life satisfy the Law of God, and by this fulfilling of the Law is righteous, and meriteth life everlasting.

The parts of repentance they make to be: contrition of the heart, confession of the mouth, satisfaction of the work. They therefore omit faith, which is the chief part of repentance, and unto those very parts which they reckon up they add horrible errors; as, concerning contrition they have two foul errors:

1. That contrition ought to be sufficient; whereas it is certain that no griefs of any creature, how great soever, are equal to the wrath of God, nor can make compensation for sins.

2. That contrition meriteth remission of sins. This detestable error transferreth unto our work the honour that is due unto the Son of God alone, and fighteth with the whole doctrine of justification.

Concerning confession they teach that the enumeration of all offences is necessary, and meriteth remission of sins. Concerning satisfaction they teach that works not due, that is, not commanded by the Law of God, but works of supererogation, merit the remission of the pains of purgatory, or of other pains of this life, etc. Against these lies <!-- p.304 --> it is enough to set this one rule: *"In vain they do worship me with the commandments of men."*

## *Credo carnis resurrectionem, et vitam aeternam* · I Believe in the Resurrection of the Flesh, and the Life Everlasting

**Cyprian:**[^F4-1]

> *"I believe in the resurrection of this flesh,"* ***δεικτικῶς*** (demonstratively, that is, pointing to it).

### What is the Resurrection of the dead?

It is a universal outward action of God, whereby God, according to his immense power, and according to the most free decree of his will, shall deliver the bodies of all men from this alternation of generations and corruptions, and shall join them again with their souls, that unto all eternity they may either pay the penalties of their wickedness together with the devils, or enjoy the communication of the divine light, wisdom, righteousness and life.

### Recite some testimonies concerning the immortality of the soul, the Resurrection of bodies, the Last Judgment, and the Life everlasting.

**On the immortality of the soul.**

**Matt. 10.**

> *"They are not able to kill the soul."*

**Luke 23.**

> *"To day shalt thou be with me in paradise."*

**Matt. 17.**

Christ talketh with Moses and Elias, who had died long before.

**Exod. 3.**

> *"God is the God of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob."*

This saying is cited <!-- p.305 --> **Matt. 22**.

**Luke 16.**

Lazarus and the rich man live.

**2 Pet. 2.**[^F4-2]

> *"Christ preached unto the spirits in prison."*

**On the Resurrection of bodies.**

The seat of the matter concerning the Resurrection of the dead is the fifteenth chapter of the former Epistle to the Corinthians.

**1 Thess. 4.**

> *"Concerning them which are asleep I would not have you to be ignorant, brethren, that ye sorrow not, even as others which have no hope of the Resurrection."*

**John 5.**

> *"The hour shall come, when all that are in the graves shall hear the voice of the Son of God, and shall come forth, some unto the resurrection of life, others unto the resurrection of punishment."*

**John 6.**

> *"This is the will of God, that every one which seeth the Son, and believeth on him, should not perish, but have everlasting life: and I will raise him up at the last day."*

**John 11.**

Lazarus is raised up. *"I am the resurrection, and the life."*

**Job 19.**

> *"I know that at the last day I shall rise again out of the earth, and I shall be clothed again with my skin, and in my flesh I shall see God,"* etc.

**On the Last Judgment.**

**Matt. 25.**

> *"When the Son of man shall come in his glory, and all the holy angels with him, then shall he sit upon the throne of his glory, and shall judge,"* etc.

<!-- p.306 -->

**Acts 17.**

> *"God hath appointed a day, in the which he will judge the world in righteousness."*

**2 Thess. 1.**

> *"In the revelation of the Lord Jesus from heaven they shall be punished with everlasting destruction, that obeyed not the Gospel."*

**Rom. 2.**

> *"Thou treasurest up unto thyself wrath against the day of wrath and revelation of the righteous judgment of God, who will render to every man according to his deeds."*

**On the Life everlasting.**

**John 3.**

> *"God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life."*

**John 6.**

> *"This is the will of God, that every one which believeth on the Son may have everlasting life."*

### How manifold is the condition of them that shall rise again?

Fourfold.

1. They shall rise again with the same bodies.

2. They shall have incorruptible bodies: the good in glory, the evil in punishment.

3. They shall rise again with the entireness of all the parts of the body.

4. They shall never die.

### How manifold shall be the condition of the good that rise again?

Fourfold.

<!-- p.307 -->

1\. Brightness (*claritas*). 2. Impassibility (*impassibilitas*). 3. Agility (*agilitas*). 4. Subtilty (*subtilitas*). For the bodies shall be spiritual, not that they shall be spirits, but in all things subject unto the spirit, so that where the spirit shall will to be, there the bodies shall be, at the beck and thought and will of the blessed man. And seeing this shall be so with our bodies, what is to be thought of the Body of Christ in the unity ***τοῦ λόγου*** (of the Word), lifted up unto the right hand of the Father Almighty?

### How many kinds of Life and of Death are there?

Four.

1. The life of nature, and the death of nature.
2. The life of sin, and the death of sin.
3. The life of grace, and the death of grace.
4. The life everlasting, and the death everlasting.

### What is the Life of Nature?

It is that whereby we naturally live in this life, so long as soul and body are joined together. This is good in itself, because it is the gift of God, and because it is promised unto them that obey God as the reward of obedience, **Exod. 20**. But this life varieth according to the condition of men. For unto Abraham it was good, because he spent it in godliness and in the spreading abroad of the glory of God. But unto Nero it was evil, because he abused it unto the reproach of its Author, and unto his own destruction.

Unto this life of nature is opposed the death of nature, which is the departing of souls from bodies. This is in itself evil, but it varieth according to the condition of men. For as unto Abraham it was good, for it was unto him a passage from the calamities of this world unto everlasting felicity, so unto Nero it was evil, for unto him it was a passage from the pleasures, the power and the tyranny of this world unto perpetual reproach and everlasting contempt.

<!-- p.308 -->

### What is the Life of sin?

It is that whereby sin liveth in a man, and reigneth through its lusts. This is always evil, because it is both hateful unto God, and is the way unto everlasting death and damnation. Unto this is opposed the death of sin, and it is when sin is mortified in a man by the power of Christ. This death is good, as being a passage from deserved damnation unto free blessedness.

### What is the Life of Grace?

It is that whereby Christ liveth in us through faith and his Spirit, **Gal. 2**. This is always good, because it is the power of the Spirit of God in us, and is the way unto glory. Unto this is opposed the death of grace, which is the privation of grace, namely, when we cast away faith and fall back into sins, so that sin reigneth in us anew through its lusts, and we obey it knowingly and willingly. This death is evil, because it is condemned of God, and is the guide unto everlasting torments.

### What is the Life everlasting?

It is both Christ himself, **1 John 5**, and the blessed felicity which the elect shall enjoy in all eternity, their bodies being raised up. This life is the end of good things, and so the chief of all good things, which the Lord had promised to them that love him. Unto this is opposed the death everlasting, which is the separation of a man from Christ, so that he enjoyeth not the sight of him. This death is the uttermost of all evils, never to be ended. Hither belongeth that of **Augustine**: *"As the soul is the life of the body, so God is the life of the soul. As the body expireth when it loseth the soul, so the soul expireth when it loseth God. God lost is the death of the soul; the soul lost is the death of the body."*

<!-- p.309 -->

### To what purpose is it profitable to hold these differences of life and death?

That we may learn rightly to die, and rightly to live: to die indeed unto sin, but to live unto grace, that after this life, passed in true godliness even unto the end, we may come unto the life everlasting through Christ Jesus our Lord.

### What is the Life everlasting?

The Son of God our Lord, **John 17**, delivereth this wonderful definition: *"This is life eternal, that they might know thee to be the true God, and Jesus, whom thou hast sent, to be the Christ"*; namely, life everlasting is the knowledge of the true God and of Christ. And this definition is to be understood not only of the nature of that life, but also of the beginning of the life everlasting in this world.

Of the life to come it is commonly said: Life everlasting is the sight of God, wherein the Church, raised up from death and wholly delivered from all sin, shall behold face to face the Essence and Will of the whole Godhead, of the eternal Father, the Son and the Holy Ghost, and shall be filled with a new and perpetual light, wisdom, righteousness, life and everlasting gladness by God, who shall be all in all, and shall in turn praise God. For when God shall show himself face to face as favourable, and shall pour through us his light and wisdom, then shall there be kindled in us an unspeakable and perpetual gladness and life, when, namely, *"we shall see him as he is,"* **1 John 3**, and shall behold his Essence face to face, when God shall be all in all. The saying of **Augustine**: *"They shall rejoice as much as they shall love; they shall love as much as they shall know."* And they shall know God showing himself in immense mercy, and laying open his wisdom and righteousness, and pouring through us his own wisdom, righteousness and felicity.

Unto this is opposed the death everlasting, which is the perpetual casting away[^F4-3] <!-- p.310 --> of all the damned from God, and the torments of soul and body that are never to be ended.

### How is the life everlasting begun in us in this life?

Life everlasting is begun in us in this life, when out of the revealed Word we know God, and rest in his mercy, promised for the Son's sake, and when our hearts feel the consolation of the Holy Ghost, whereby there is at the same time kindled a new light, righteousness and gladness, which is the taste of the life everlasting. Thus much of the Creed.

# *De Tertia Parte Catechismi* · On the Third Part of the Catechism

### What is the third part of the Catechism?

The Lord's Prayer.

### What is Invocation?

Invocation is, in the name of and in confidence in Christ the Mediator, to ask benefits of God, and to give thanks for good things received, and to praise and proclaim the goodness of God their Author.

### What things doth Invocation comprise?

Two: prayer and thanksgiving.

### What is prayer?

It is, in the name of and in confidence in Christ, to ask something of God, and assuredly to believe that our prayers are heard and received for the sake of Christ, who is our Mediator and High Priest, bearing our groanings unto the Father, and maketh intercession for us.

<!-- p.311 -->

### What is thanksgiving?

It is to acknowledge that God is not idle, but truly careth for us, heareth us, and giveth benefits present and eternal; and to praise the immense power and goodness of God, and to submit ourselves unto him humbly from the heart, and by our obedience and confession to testify our thankfulness toward God, and to invite others to ask and to look for benefits from God.

### What things are required unto true Invocation?

Six heads.

1. Who the God is, whom we address.

2. The commandment of God concerning invocation: *"Ask, and it shall be given you."* *"Men ought always to pray."* **1 Thess. 5**: *"Rejoice evermore, pray without ceasing, and in every thing give thanks."* Likewise: *"Call upon me in the day of trouble."* It is therefore a horrible and enormous sin not to call upon God. And of the ungodly the Psalmist saith: *"They have not called upon the Lord."*

3. Let every man consider whether he be truly converted unto God. For *"God heareth not sinners."*

4. The promise is to be considered, that we may know whether and why we are heard, as it is written: *"The Lord is nigh unto all them that call upon him, to all that call upon him in truth. He will fulfil the desire of them that fear him, and will hear their cry."* And **Isa. 65**: *"While they are yet crying, I will answer."* Likewise: *"Whosoever shall call upon the name of God shall be saved."* And: *"Verily, verily, I say unto you, Whatsoever ye shall ask the Father in my name, he will give it you."*

5. Faith is to be stirred up, that thou mayest believe that thou art assuredly heard: in spiritual and eternal good things without any condition, but in bodily things with the condition of the cross. And <!-- p.312 --> faith comprehendeth both, petition and obedience, and holdeth that our prayers are not void, although the event is deferred, or answereth not unto our thought.

6. Consider what thou wouldest ask of God, lest the prayer be an empty ***βαττολογία*** (vain babbling).

### What is the difference between true Invocation and the false, or heathen?

Two things make the difference, namely, the knowledge of the Essence, and the knowledge of the Will. The heathen and the Turks, although they say, as Nestor in **Homer**, that we must pray, seeing we all have need of God, yet, because they deny that he is God who is the eternal Father, Son and Holy Ghost, they stray from the Essence of God, according to the saying of Christ: *"Ye worship ye know not what."* Therefore they call upon a feigned name, that is, an idol, which is nothing, as she saith: *"Jupiter, I call upon thee, wheresoever thou art, whether in heaven, or in the air, or on the earth, or in the waters."* And since they know not the promises and the Mediator, they stray from the Will of God, and doubt, as that barbarian king in **Virgil** saith: *"And we cherish an empty report."*[^F4-4] But the Church knoweth the Essence of God revealed in the Word, and knoweth his will, and in the name of and in confidence in Christ looketh upon the promise confirmed by the divine oath, and believeth that she is assuredly heard.

### Why did one of the Ancients say that nothing is harder than to pray?

This was truly said. For in prayer that is not an empty murmur, or ***βαττολογία*** (vain babbling), all these things are required:

1. The thought with how great a Majesty thou speakest.

<!-- p.313 -->

2. The consideration of the testimonies concerning God.

3. The revelation, and the precept concerning invocation.

4. Attention unto the several parts of the prayer.

5. Conversion, without which prayer is not only not effectual, but is a mocking and contempt of God.

6. The thought of the divine promises.

7. Faith, without which prayer not only taketh not away sin, but itself also becometh sin.

### What things are to be asked of God?

All things spiritual and bodily that agree with the commandments, which are set out in the best order in the Lord's Prayer. The exposition and phrase of this we have set down above.

### What are the causes for which Invocation is to be continually exercised?

1. The glory [of God].
2. The public necessity and welfare of the Church.
3. Every man's own necessity and welfare.
4. That spiritual learning and wisdom may be increased.
5. Consolation.
6. That the devils may be confounded and put to flight.
7. Confession, distinguishing the Church from other sects.

# *De Quarta Parte Catechismi* · On the Fourth Part of the Catechism

### What is the fourth part of the Catechism?

The doctrine concerning the Sacrament of Baptism.

### What is a Sacrament?

A Sacrament is a sacred action, instituted of God, consisting <!-- p.314 --> of the Word and the entire element, and added unto the promise which is proper unto the Gospel, that it may also seal and confirm faith in the living.

### Why were the Sacraments instituted?

1. That they may be testimonies of the will of God toward us, and ***σφραγῖδες*** (seals) of grace and of imputed righteousness.

2. That in very deed there may through them be given unto us the grace of Christ and the salvation obtained through Christ, not by the work wrought (*ex opere operato*), but unto them that believe, and that by faith apply unto themselves the merit and benefits of the Son of God. For the Sacraments are not only signs and representations of spiritual cleansing and quickening, but in the Sacraments the merit of Christ is really given and applied unto them that believe.

3. That they may be signs of confession, and may distinguish the Church from the other nations.

### How many Sacraments of the New Testament are there?

Two: Baptism and the Lord's Supper.

### What is Baptism?

It is an action, or ceremony, instituted of God, containing the dipping, or washing, and the entire pronouncing of the words: *"I baptize thee in the name of the Father, of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost"*; which testifieth that he who is baptized is received of God, is endued with the Holy Ghost, and is accepted unto the inheritance of life everlasting; and it is the means through which there is given reconciliation, deliverance from sin, the righteousness of Christ, and life and salvation unto all that believe and that abide in faith, according to the promise: *"He that believeth and is baptized shall be saved."*

### What is the principal end and use of Baptism?

<!-- p.315 -->

The principal end is the application of the remission of sins unto him that is baptized. For Baptism is the means through which the merit of Christ is given, and is by faith applied, really and effectually, because it is *"the washing of regeneration and of renewing through the Holy Ghost."*

### Are infants to be baptized?

They are to be baptized, because unto them pertaineth the promise of salvation, as it is said: *"Of such is the kingdom of heaven."* But there is no salvation without the Church, as it is written, **John 3**: *"Except a man be born of water and of the Spirit, he shall not enter into the kingdom of heaven."* Therefore it is necessary that infants be regenerated through Baptism, and ingrafted into the Church. And Christ is effectual in infants through Baptism, and worketh in them faith and other motions, as he saith, **Matt. 18**: *"It is not the will of the Father, that one of these little ones should perish, which believe in me."*

# *De Quinta Parte Catechismi, quae est Coena Dominica* · On the Fifth Part of the Catechism, which is the Lord's Supper

### What is the Lord's Supper?

The definition is to be taken from the words of the Institution: *"Take, eat; this is my body, which is given for you. Drink; this cup is the new testament in my blood."* And from the exposition of **Paul**: *"The bread is the communion of the body of Christ. The cup, or the wine, is the communion of the blood of Christ."*

This therefore is the definition, out of the words of the Son of God himself:

The Lord's Supper in the Church of Christ is the true body and <!-- p.316 --> the true blood of our Lord Jesus Christ, which body is received with the mouth, and which blood is drunk with the mouth, as often as the bread and wine of blessing are received with the mouth; which Supper was instituted by Christ himself, when he was now about to die for our sins, that it might be a remembrance of Christ offered for us upon the Cross as a sacrifice of atonement, and a confirmation of the New Covenant concerning grace and the remission of sins, and might testify that Christ is in us not only spiritually through faith, but also with his body and blood, or by a natural participation, and that we ourselves are ingrafted into him not only according to the soul, but also according to the body, for the assurance of our resurrection, and for consolation in all temptations and distresses.

I ask what the Supper is. The Son of God himself answereth that it is his last Will and Testament, which he made in the night in which he was betrayed.

I ask what is given by Christ in that Supper. Our Lord Jesus Christ himself, taking bread and wine, answereth that there is given by him his body and blood, offered and shed for our sins.

I ask what that bread is. The Son of God answereth: *"This is my body; the cup is my blood."*

I ask what manner of body, whether the true and natural, or a spiritual one, which is received by faith only. The Son of God himself answereth: *"It is my body, which is given for you."* Likewise: *"This cup is my blood, which is shed for your sins."*

I ask how the body of Christ is received. The Son of God himself answereth: *"Take, eat."* And this is done with the mouth, and not by any other means or instrument.

I ask how this is possible. The Son of God himself answereth: *"I say, Take, eat; this is my body."* Let it therefore suffice us that our Lord Jesus Christ, almighty and true, saith it, with whom nothing is impossible. <!-- p.317 --> His saying is doing and being done. *"Whatsoever he saith unto you, do it,"* saith Mary; even as the disciples do not ask how he should or could give his body, and how they should or could receive it with their mouth and eat it, but in silence humbly submit themselves unto the words and commandment of him whom they know to be almighty and true, and, bringing their reason into captivity unto the obedience of Christ, humbly consecrate and offer it wholly unto Christ, assuredly holding that he cannot err who with a simple and suppliant faith and humility believeth the words and the last will of Christ, in whose mouth there is no guile.

I ask what **Paul** meaneth, when he nameth the bread of blessing the communion, ***κοινωνίαν***, of the body of Christ. I answer: he meaneth the same that Christ meaneth. For it is a communion, because the body of Christ is communicated unto us, that is, with the bread the body of the Lord is delivered and received.

### Wherefore, or to what end, was the Lord's Supper instituted?

1. That it may confirm faith.

2. That we may give thanks unto God. For it is the *Eucharist*.

3. That it may be the sinew of the public assembly. For it is the *Synaxis*.

4. That it may be a testimony of confession and of doctrine.

5. That it may be a token of mutual love, and of alms, whence it is called ***ἀγάπη*** (love-feast).

6. That it may be a token of our resurrection, as the Nicene Canon calleth the body and blood of Christ ***σύμβολα τῆς ἡμετέρης ἀναστάσεως*** (tokens of our resurrection); of which matter we have discoursed more fully in the Second Part of the *Paedagogia*, in the doctrine concerning the Resurrection.

### Against what doth the doctrine of the presence of the true body of Christ, and of the bodily eating of it, delivered by Christ himself, strike?

<!-- p.318 -->

1. Against the judgment and reason of man.

2. Against the rules and properties of natural philosophy.

3. Against certain sayings of the Fathers; of whom, nevertheless, all and each, there is none that denieth that the body of Christ is in the Supper ***σωματικῇ παρουσίᾳ*** (by a bodily presence), and is in very deed eaten with the mouth.

### But what is to be judged of these things?

All things are to be set after the Word, the commandment and the institution of God. And the theological rule of all Christians ought to be this: Whatsoever striketh not against the omnipotence and the will, or the promise, of God expressed in the Word, that, if it be founded in the Word of God, is true and unmoved, and is to be received and held with a simple faith, although it be against all natural reason and rule. On the contrary, whatsoever striketh against the divine omnipotence and truth, as doth the opinion of all that deny the bodily presence and eating of Christ in the Supper, that, although it agree in all things with human reason and the rules of natural philosophy, is yet erroneous and false, and altogether to be abhorred in the doctrine of the Christian religion.

### Who use the Lord's Supper worthily?

All that are converted, or that repent.

### Who are named unworthy?

They that repent not, or that examine not themselves. These, whether they be hypocrites, or defiled with other sins against conscience, when they partake of the bread of blessing in the holy Supper, which is the communion of the body of Christ, are made partakers also of the body of Christ, unto judgment and damnation to themselves, because by eating and drinking they become guilty of the body and blood of Christ.

<!-- p.319 -->

### What are the chief abuses of the Supper among the Papist adversaries?

1. The figment of transubstantiation.
2. The shutting up and the carrying about.
3. The adoration of the bread, ***ἀρτολατρεία*** (bread-worship).
4. The sacrifice of the Mass.
5. The work wrought (*opus operatum*).
6. The mutilation, or taking away of the one kind.
7. The figment concerning the deliverance of the dead out of Purgatory, to the furthering of which the Supper is transferred.

### In what ways hath the holy Supper been profaned among the Sacramentarians?

1. They deny that the body of Christ is present, and is communicated unto them that eat of the bread of blessing; so that, although they say that the presence of the body of Christ in the Supper is true, real and substantial, not feigned nor imaginary, and that the delivering also is true, and that the body of Christ is taken with the mouth through the outward tokens (for so Albert Hardenberg spake and wrote), yet they always hide a deceit, and beguile the simple with the words *By faith*, *In the Spirit*, *Sacramentally*, by which they mean this, that the body of Christ is as far removed from the Supper as the highest heavens are distant from the earth.

2. They say that the body of Christ is locally received and contained by heaven, so that on earth, even though Christ should will it, it cannot be.

3. They affirm that the bread and wine of blessing are only signs of the absent body and blood.

4. They teach that the eating is done through faith ascending by its imagination into heaven, in which the body of Christ is locally detained.

5. That the unworthy receive nothing else save bread and wine, <!-- p.320 --> but that the worthy are by faith better than the unworthy; and yet that both receive only bread and wine with their mouth.

6. They contradict Christ, the Evangelists, Paul and all the Orthodox, and set the omnipotence and truth of Christ after the rules of natural philosophy.

7. They teach that Christ is as far and as distantly removed from us in his body, as heaven is distant from the earth.

8. They devise a Communication of Properties (*Communicatio Idiomatum*) that is only verbal and dialectical, without truth in the exaltation of the human nature in Christ; and the right hand of God, unto which Christ as man is lifted up, they feign to be a bodily place, wherein Christ is circumscribed, detained and contained; and that Christ in one place only of heaven, wheresoever that be, is God and man together, but elsewhere is only God.

9. Being convicted by the truth they are provoked, and begin to rage with threats, hatred, biting speech and implacableness, and yet they creep further with their opinions among the secure and the worldly in courts and commonwealths, with minds beyond measure bitter, hard and harsh, according to that saying of **D. D. Luther**: *"Ich habe noch nie gesehen, daß ein Schwermer bekeret sei worden"* (I have never yet seen that a fanatic was converted).

10. Of the war against images, of the ***διαφωνίᾳ καὶ ἀμουσίᾳ*** (discord and want of music) in the churches, of the extenuating of Baptism, of the ***ἀταξίᾳ*** (disorder) in ceremonies and the ecclesiastical confusion, of the conventicles, and of the irreverence in the administration of the Supper in many places, we must speak elsewhere. These things only have we noted, that the boys may learn to flee monstrous opinions and blasphemies against the Son of God, and may pray God that he would sanctify us, and keep us in his truth, that is, in his Word.

### What is private Absolution?

It is the announcing and applying of the remission of sins, <!-- p.321 --> which is done in the name and by the authority of Christ through the voice of a man in the ministry, according to the saying: *"Whose soever sins ye remit, they are remitted unto them,"* **John 20**. **Matt. 16** and **18**: *"Whatsoever ye shall loose on earth shall be loosed in heaven, and whatsoever ye shall bind on earth shall be bound in heaven"*; that is, absolution applied through the Minister is effectual, ratified and firm before the judgment seat of God; and in like manner excommunication also.

### Is the Confession of all, or of each, or of some offences necessary?

Before God that confession is necessary whereby a man owneth himself guilty of all sins, and acknowledgeth them, as **Paul** saith: *"Let a man examine himself."* But in the confession which they call *auricular*, before the Ministers of the Church, enumeration is not necessary, both because it is not commanded, and because the enumeration of every offence is impossible, according to that word: *"Who can understand his errors?"* But where there is need of doctrine, instruction, the trying of faith, counsel and consolation, there both he that confesseth and he that absolveth will do those things which conscience and the nature of the office require.

### Recite the form of Confession.

Reverend Sir, I pray you to hear my confession, and in the name, in the stead and by the command of Christ to absolve me from my sins.

> I, a poor sinner, confess before God and before you that I have many sins; and that, being conceived and born in sins, I have in manifold wise offended, and do offend, against the First and the Second Table, and that I am guilty of the wrath of God and of everlasting punishments. But I grieve with my whole heart that I have ever <!-- p.322 --> offended my God, and do offend him, and I humbly pray that God would have mercy upon me, and forgive me all and each of my sins, and justify me through and for the sake of the Son, the Mediator, and sanctify me by his Holy Spirit, that I may be and may abide a temple of God, and an instrument wholesome unto my own soul and unto the Church, and a vessel of mercy. That I may assure and seal this faith in myself, I will come unto the holy Synaxis, in which, according to the commandment, institution and will of Christ my Lord, I shall receive the true body and the true blood of my Redeemer Jesus Christ, offered and shed for me. I ask therefore of you, that in the name and in the stead of Christ you would absolve me from my sins, and announce unto me the remission of sins. I promise thankfulness unto God and unto you, the Holy Ghost helping and governing me, and amendment of life.

***τῷ θεῷ δόξα*** (To God be the glory.)
