# *Quaestiones Catecheticae* · Catechetical Questions

## On Catechesis

### Whence is Catechesis so called?

From ***κατηχεῖν***, which is, to deliver some doctrine by the living voice, and to require it again of the hearers when it hath been delivered. ***ἠχεῖν*** signifieth to sound with the voice: whence is ***ἠχώ***, a sound, an echo, which giveth back the voice, or a resounding. Now both ***κατηχεῖν*** and ***κατηχίζειν*** are said, although the Apostles use the former only, and the Ecclesiastical writers the latter: whence also ***κατηχήτης*** and ***κατηχίστης***, *Catecheta* and *Catechista* (catechete and catechist), and ***κατήχησις*** and ***κατήχισμος*** (catechesis and catechism) are said.

<!-- p.167 -->

### Where is this word found in the Holy Scriptures?

**Luke 1**: *"I will write the history of the things done by Christ, that thou mightest know the certainty of the doctrine which thou hast received in Catechesis,"* ***περὶ ὧν κατηχήθης λόγων τὴν ἀσφάλειαν***. And **Galat. 6**: ***κοινωνείτω ὁ κατηχούμενος τὸν λόγον τῷ κατηχοῦντι ἐν πᾶσιν ἀγαθοῖς***, that is, let the Catechumens be thankful toward their Teacher, and maintain him who hath instructed them in Catechesis. And **Acts 19**, of Apollos, who had been initiated with the Baptism of John, it is written, ***κατηχημένος τὴν ὁδὸν τοῦ Κυρίου***, *"instructed in the way of the Lord."* And **Rom. 2**: ***κατηχούμενος ἐκ τοῦ νόμου***, *"instructed out of the law."* For they who are instructed are named ***κατηχούμενοι*** and ***κατηχήμενοι*** and ***κατήχητοι***; but they who instruct are named *Catechetae* (catechetes), such as of old the sponsors and godfathers also were, who were for that cause named Catechetes.

### Whence is the word ***κατηχεῖν*** taken?

From the ancient custom of the Patriarchs and of the holy Fathers even before Moses, who did not commit the mysteries of Religion to writings, but delivered them by the living voice to those who were already initiated in the sacred things (in the Old Testament the circumcised, in the New the baptized). Whence also *Cabala* was of old the name, which signifieth tradition, ***παράδοσιν***. And **Irenaeus** and others name the Creed the Apostolic tradition.

Hither pertaineth the excellent saying of **Epiphanius**, book 2, tome 1, against the Apotactics or Apostolics, such as are the Franciscans:[^D1-1] ***πάντα τὰ θεῖα ῥήματα οὐκ ἀλληγορίας δεῖται, ἀλλὰ ὡς ἔχει· θεωρείας δὲ δεῖτε, καὶ αἰσθήσεως, εἰς τὸ εἰδέναι ἑκάστης ὑποθέσεως τὴν δύναμιν, δεῖ δὲ καὶ παραδόσει κεχρῆσθαι.*** That is, the divine words need not allegory (or a foreign and alien sense, such as the Monks have feigned in a fourfold order, the Literal <!-- p.168 -->or Historical, the Tropological, the Allegorical, and the Anagogical). For the Moral law, the promises of grace, the Articles of faith, the Sacraments, and the Creeds or confessions, do not admit Allegories. But there is need of speculation, that is, of attention, reading, study, consideration of the context and of the members, of the definition, the division, the arguments, and of the whole order, according to that of **John 5**: *"Search,"* ***ἐρευνᾶτε***, that is, read, repeat, inquire, turn them over one with another (for this is ***ἐρευνᾶν***, from ***ἐρῶ***, I ask, and ***εὐνή***, a conversing and abiding, ***διατριβή, ὁμιλία καὶ συζήτησις*** (a spending of time, converse and joint inquiry), or ***συνουσία*** (a being together)). Paul nameth this ***θεωρείαν*** (speculation) ***διδασκαλίαν*** and ***ἔλεγχον***, **2 Timoth. 3**, that is, the certainty and confirmation of doctrine, and the judging or refutation of false opinions. Next there is need of sense, of practice, of the motion of the Holy Ghost, or, as Paul nameth it, ***παιδείᾳ*** and ***ἐπανορθώσει***, that is, of godliness and repentance, of the cross, of sorrow, faith, consolation, gladness. All these doth tradition serve, that is, Catechesis, and the testimonies of the Church, which confirm, illustrate, and propagate the doctrine.

### What then is Christian Catechesis?

It is the first instruction of Novices, wherein the sum of Christian doctrine, comprehended in a certain Method, is delivered to the more unlearned by the living voice of him that teacheth. Paul, **2 Timoth. 1**, calleth it ***ὑποτύπωσιν***, that is, a delineation, a form, a pattern of sound words, wherein the chief Articles of Christian doctrine are comprehended, being brought into one body.

### For what causes is Catechesis necessary?

The first cause is the commandment of God, **Psalm 68**: *"Bless ye God in the congregations, even the Lord, from the fountain of Israel."* **Isa. 8**: *"To the law <!-- p.169 -->and to the testimony."* **Matth. 10**: *"Hear ye him."* **1 Tim. 4**: *"Give attendance to reading, to exhortation, and to doctrine."*

The second cause is the worship of God, wherein God is most of all delighted, according to that saying: *"Suffer little children to come unto me."* And **Psal. 8**, **Matth. 21**: *"Out of the mouth of babes and sucklings thou hast perfected praise,"* that is, thou doest great things through the voice of the ministry of thy word.

The third cause is, that the knowledge of the doctrine may be confirmed and illustrated by frequent exercises, according to that of **Luke 19**: *"Unto him that hath shall be given,"* that is, to him that exerciseth the doctrine the light of the doctrine is increased. The little bees that make honey in the garden of the Lord

> *"drive from the hives the drones, a slothful herd."*[^D1-2]

The fourth, the propagation and preservation of the doctrine. **Psalm 78**: *"Our fathers have told us. We will not hide them from their children, and from the posterity, shewing the praises of the Lord, and his strength, and his wonderful works that he hath done: that the generation to come might know them, even the children which should be born, and should arise, and shall declare them to their children: that they might set their hope in God, and not forget the works of God, but keep his commandments."*

The fifth, the profit, which consisteth in these things:

1. In the receiving of the doctrine concerning the essence and Will of God, and concerning ourselves, the fall, death, the restoration, salvation.
2. In ***προασφαλίσει***, a fortifying beforehand and steadfastness against all temptations, heresies, calamities, death, according to that saying: *"Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path."*
3. In godliness of life, according to that saying: *"Wherewithal shall a young man cleanse his way? By keeping thy words."*
4. In true consolation in all adversity, as it is written: *"This is my comfort in my affliction: for thy Word hath quickened me."*
5. In patience and the hope of eternal life.

Of all these Paul speaketh, **2 Tim. 3**: *"From a child thou hast known the holy scriptures, which are able to make thee wise unto salvation."* And **Roman. 15**: <!-- p.170 -->*"Whatsoever things were written were written for our learning, that we through patience and comfort of the scriptures might have hope."* The end of these is eternal life, which we find in Catechesis, or in the Reading of the holy Scriptures which testify of Christ.

Let the younger sort hold fast the saying of David, **Psalm 86**: *"Teach me thy way, O Lord; I will walk in thy truth: UNITE my HEART to fear thy Name."*

### How many, and which, are the chief heads of the Catechism?

Five: the Decalogue or Law, the Creed or Gospel, the Lord's Prayer or invocation, Baptism, and the Lord's Supper. **Psalm 116**: *"I said in my consternation, All men are liars."* (The Decalogue.) *"What shall I render unto the Lord for all his benefits toward me?"* (The Gospel.) *"I will take the cup of salvation, and call upon the name of the Lord."* (The Lord's Prayer.) *"I will pay my vows unto the Lord in the presence of all his people."* (Baptism, ***τὸ ἐπερώτημα***, the answer, of a good conscience.) *"Precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his saints."* (Being founded in the precious Blood of the Son: the death, namely, of those who have been made partakers of the body and Blood of Christ crucified and dead for us.)

### Which are the most general heads of the heavenly doctrine?

The Law and the Gospel. **Isa. 8**: *"To the law and to the testimony."*

### To what places (*loci*) may all things which are read in the Holy Scriptures be most conveniently referred?

To six places.

1. The first is of God.
2. Of the creature.
3. <!-- p.171 -->Of the Church.
4. Of the Decalogue, and of the Gospel.
5. Of the Sacraments of the Old and New Testament.
6. Of the consummation, the judgment, eternal life and eternal death.

## On the Law, the First Part of the Catechism

### Which is the first part of the Catechism?

The doctrine of the divine law, or the Decalogue.

### Whence hath the Decalogue its name, and what doth it contain?

It hath its name from the number of the ten commandments, which in **Deuteron. 10** are named the ten words. And it containeth the sum of the Law, which is commonly called the moral law (*lex moralis*).

### What things are chiefly to be held concerning the law?

These especially.

1. The diverse signification of the word *law* in the Holy Scriptures.
2. What the Decalogue, or moral law, is.
3. Whether any man can satisfy the law, and why no man can satisfy it.
4. Of the use of the law.
5. How far the law is abrogated.
6. How Christ hath fulfilled the law.
7. What is the division of the law.
8. What is the difference of the law and the Gospel.
9. What are the parts of the law, and the explication of them.

### I. What are the significations of the word *law*?

Law in general signifieth doctrine: with the Hebrews *Thora*, from *Iara*, that is, he hath taught; as **Psal. 1**: *"In the law of the Lord is his delight and <!-- p.172 -->meditation day and night."* **Psalm 19**: *"The law of the Lord is undefiled,"* that is, the heavenly doctrine, or the word of God.

Law signifieth the Pentateuch, the five books of Moses, Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, Deuteronomy; as **Rom. 3**: *"The righteousness of faith hath witness in the Law and in the Prophets."* **Luc. 24**: *"All things must be fulfilled, which are written in the law of Moses, and in the Prophets, and in the Psalms, concerning me."*

Law set over against the Gospel signifieth the moral law, as: *"We are justified freely without the deeds of the law."* Set over against grace, it signifieth wrath and the curse, as: *"We are not under the Law, but under grace."* Set over against the Truth, and the time of Christ Incarnate, it signifieth the types and shadows of the law; likewise the Mosaic Polity, the kingdom and the priesthood, as **Gal. 3**: *"Before faith came, we were kept under the law."* Whence **Epiphanius**: ***οἱ τύποι ἐν τῷ νόμῳ, ἡ ἀλήθεια ἐν τῷ Εὐαγγελίῳ*** (the types are in the law, the truth in the Gospel). Likewise: Christ hath the body, but Moses the shadow of the body. *"The law was given by Moses, Grace and Truth came by Christ."*

Law sometimes ***μετωνυμικῶς*** (by metonymy) signifieth some force that impelleth, either to good or to evil, as: the law of the mind, the law of the spirit, the law of sin, the law of the members. The law of faith, that is, faith itself. The law of works, that is, good works.

### II. What is the Decalogue, or Moral law?

It is the eternal and immutable wisdom in God, which in the creation was poured over into rational minds, and afterward on mount Sinai was published by the voice of God in the year of the world 2454, 3081 years ago (reckoning backward from this current year 1572),[^D1-3] with great majesty, and confirmed with miracles; which discerneth things just and unjust, commanding what manner of persons we ought to be, what things we ought to do, what to leave undone; requiring entire obedience <!-- p.173 -->and agreement with the will of God which is expressed in the word itself; and pronouncing that God is horribly angry with sin, and punisheth in this life, and after this life condemneth with eternal punishments, all in whom there is not entire agreement, unless remission be made for Christ's sake; and promising rewards to the obedient.

### Why is it named Law (*Lex*)?

Because it ought to be read (*legi*) and set before the people continually, publicly and privately, that it may be known to all, and observed of all, and be the norm and rule of life and of actions. Therefore in **Deuteronom. 21** it is ordained that it be read every year in the solemn general assembly.

### Why is it called Moral?

Because it is the perpetual rule of living, according to which the manners (*mores*) of every man are to be squared.

### III. Can any man fulfil the law in this life?

No man, save Christ. **Psalm 14**: *"There is none that doeth good, no, not one."*

### How is it shewn that no man can satisfy the law in this life?

This is shewn first by the depravation of the nature of men through sin. *"For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God." "They are all gone out of the way,"* **Rom. 3**. Next, by universal experience, because for sin we all are subject to the wrath of God and to death, **Psal. 90**: *"We are consumed in thine anger; all our days are passed away in thy wrath."* Thirdly, the same is shewn by all the testimonies of the whole Scripture, as: *"The imagination of man is evil from his youth." "They are all become unprofitable." "All men are liars."* <!-- p.174 -->*"In thy sight shall no man living be justified."* Fourthly, by the Sacrifice and Death of Christ. For if we could satisfy the law, Christ had died in vain, **Gala. 2**. Lastly, by the judgment of God, which can never be unjust.

### But what is to be determined concerning outward discipline? Is this possible?

Discipline is called the government of the locomotive power, that is, of the outward gestures, and it is outward obedience according to the commandments of God, in such wise that the outward members commit not the offences forbidden by the law of God. And this discipline is possible to all men, even to the unregenerate; but it is not the fulfilling of the Law, nor righteousness before God, nor doth it merit the remission of sins.

### But for what causes is discipline necessary?

For five causes. The first is the necessity of obeying the commandment of God. The second, the necessity of avoiding the punishments, present and eternal, wherewith God punisheth the violation of discipline. The third, the necessity of not troubling others, but of cherishing and preserving the common tranquillity and society. For foul is the word of Cain: *"Am I my brother's keeper?"* What is it to the Romans, that the Greeks perish? Rightly saith **Euripides**: ***ὁ γὰρ δίκαιος τοῖς πέλας πέφυκε ἀνήρ***. *"The just man is born for his neighbours."*[^D1-4] The fourth cause is, that we may be a good example to others, and invite them to virtue. The fifth cause is, that discipline may be a certain Pedagogy and leading by the hand unto Christ, not by its own virtue, but by reason of that which followeth. For men cannot be taught where there is no discipline, and the benefits of Christ cannot be understood without the acknowledgment of sin, nor be received and retained when a man liveth and persevereth in wickednesses against conscience.

<!-- p.175 -->

### IV. What is the use of the Moral law?

The use is threefold. The first is universal, outward, or political, or pedagogical. The second is Theological, inward, or spiritual. The third is proper to the Gospel in the regenerate.

### What is the Political use of the Law?

That which pertaineth to the outward man, even the unregenerate, or the Heathen, whom the Law governeth outwardly[^D1-5] in discipline, and bendeth to virtue, either by the love of honesty and the judgment of right reason, or by the fear of punishments, according to that saying, **1 Timoth. 1**: *"The law is good to him that useth it lawfully."* **Matth. 26**: *"He that taketh the sword shall perish with the sword."* **Isa. 33**: *"Woe to thee that spoilest: shalt not thou be spoiled?"* **Proverb. 12**: *"Lying lips are abomination to God."* **Hebr. 13**: *"Whoremongers and adulterers the Lord will judge."*

### What is the spiritual use of the law?

It is the use proper to the law, pertaining to the conscience of man, which the Law convicteth as guilty and condemneth for the whole depravation, the inward and the outward sins, laying open the judgment and wrath of God against sin, according to that saying, **Rom. 3**: *"By the law is the knowledge of sin."* **Rom. 4**: *"The law worketh wrath."* And this use of the Law is not in all men, but only in those who wrestle with sin and with the sense of the wrath of God, and either sink under the sorrow, as Cain, Saul, Judas, and rush into despair, or struggle out of it, laying hold of the promise of mercy for Christ's sake, as Peter, Mary Magdalene, the Thief on the cross, and the like.

### What is the third use of the Law, which regardeth the Regenerate?

It is to admonish the believers concerning true works, and to teach what <!-- p.176 -->worships please God, and withal to restrain and reprove the remnants of sin, that repentance may increase, faith in Christ being the guide and mistress; the fruits of which faith flow forth from this third use of the Law, as ministers of faith.

### How do the Regenerate begin the works commanded in the Law? And how doth the obedience begun please God?

The Regenerate first acknowledge by faith the Mediator, for whose sake the person is made acceptable to God. Next, the Holy Ghost being received, there is begun an obedience agreeing with the Law of God; and although it is imperfect, yet it pleaseth God in the person reconciled for the sake of the Son the Mediator, through faith. For when the person pleaseth, the work pleaseth.

### V. Is the Mosaic Law abrogated, and how far?

The whole Law and all its parts are abrogated, as touching the justification of men, and the condemnation of those who are converted. But the parts of the Mosaic Law are to be distinguished.

The Ceremonial Laws are rightly said to be abolished as touching the use, not as touching the signification, because the things figured by those Ceremonies endure for ever.

The Judicial Laws also are said to be abolished, or rather to be free, as touching the use. For the Magistrate may either use them or not use them. But in their signification they are perpetual and immutable. For the Mosaic Polity was a Type of the kingdom of Christ.

The Ceremonial and Forensic Laws are also said to be abolished as touching the species, not as touching the genus. For it is necessary that there be Ceremonies for the sake of good Order and of discipline, and it is necessary that there <!-- p.177 -->be Judgments and certain Laws. And that which in the ceremonial and judicial laws is natural or moral is perpetual and immutable. But the Moral Law hath two parts, namely the Rule of life, or the precept, and the Appendix concerning the promises and threatenings, to wit this:

> *"I am the Lord thy God, visiting the iniquity of the Fathers upon the children, unto the third and fourth generation of them that hate me, and keep not my commandments: and shewing mercy unto thousands of them that love me, and keep my commandments."*

LIKEWISE:

> *"He that doeth these things shall live in them."* And: *"Cursed is he that continueth not in all things which are written in the book of the Law."*

As touching therefore the former part of the Moral Law, that is, the norm of life, it is not abolished, but is perpetual and immutable, both in this and in the life to come. Therefore it is commonly wont to be said: The Moral Law is not abrogated as touching obedience. **1 Timoth. 1**: *"The sum of the Law is Love,"* saith Paul. This love of God and of our neighbour God willeth to be perpetual, **Deuteronom. 6**, **Matth. 22**. But as touching the Appendix of the Law, the promises are made sure to the godly in Christ, but the threatenings are abolished to them that are converted, according to that saying: *"There is now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus."* Likewise: *"We are not under the law, but under grace."* Likewise: *"The law is not made for the righteous,"* that is, it doth not press them. And **Galat. 3**: *"Christ was made a curse for us."*

### V. [VI.] In how many ways doth Christ fulfil the law for us, and in us?[^D1-6]

<!-- p.178 -->

In five ways.

1. By perfect obedience toward the whole law.
2. By the turning of the punishment upon himself. For he fulfilled the law actively and passively, and is ***τέλος τοῦ νόμου***, the perfection of the law, superabundantly.
3. (Namely, imputatively.) By the imputation of his active and passive obedience and satisfaction.
4. (Namely, inchoatively.) By the gift of the Holy Ghost, or by the beginning whereby we begin to do the things which are of the Law.
5. (Namely, consummatively.) By the consummation in eternal life, according to that saying: *"We through the Spirit wait for the hope of righteousness by faith."*

### VII. What is the division of the Law?

1\. One Law is Ceremonial, *Kirchengepreng* (church ceremony); another Judicial or Forensic, *Statut und Recht* (statute and law); another Moral, which prescribeth to men the universal norm of living.

The Ceremonial Laws in Moses were precepts delivered by God concerning the outward rites of the Sacrifices, and of certain exercises in meat and apparel, given that they should endure for a certain time, so long as the Jewish Priesthood was to remain.

The Judicial Laws in Moses were precepts delivered by God concerning outward actions, governing this Civil society in the division of inheritances, in Contracts, in judgments, in punishments, etc., given that they should endure for a certain time, so long as the Mosaic Polity was to remain.

2\. Some Laws regard God immediately, others our neighbour. And this is the sum of the Moral Law, according to that saying:

> *"Thou shalt love the Lord thy God, and thy neighbour as thyself."*

<!-- p.179 -->

### VIII. How do the Law and the Gospel differ?

They differ in six ways. The first difference is taken from the knowledge of them: the Law is in some manner known by nature. But of the Gospel it is written: *"The Son, which is in the bosom of the Father, he hath declared it unto us."*

**To the contrary.**

> Paul saith, **Roman. 7**: *"I had not known lust to be sin, except the law had said, Thou shalt not covet."*
>
> Therefore the Law is not known by nature.

I answer. The Law is sometimes naturally known, as Paul saith: ***τὸ ἔργον τοῦ νόμου γραπτὸν ἐν ταῖς καρδίαις αὐτῶν*** (the work of the law written in their hearts), namely, as touching conscience, or the judgment concerning outward actions whether honest or dishonest. But the entire law, so far as pertaineth to the inward obedience, is not naturally known. Therefore the inward uncleanness or concupiscence, the imperfection, and the weakness, is not accounted to be sin whereby God is offended.

### Why was the Law given, seeing it is known by nature?

1. That the authority of the Law of nature might be confirmed by a new publication.
2. That those things which had begun to be darkened by the custom of sinning and by the multitude of the wicked might be made manifest.
3. That there might stand fast a certain manner of worshipping God.

**Another.**

> The power of God is naturally known, **Rom. 1**.
>
> Christ and the Gospel is the power of God, **Rom. 1**.
>
> <!-- p.180 -->Therefore the Gospel also is known to men by nature.

I answer to the Major: the power, namely of the creation and preservation of things, is naturally known. To the Minor: Christ is the power, namely, of Redemption and Salvation.

The second difference is taken from the matter. The Law principally commandeth, accuseth, condemneth. The Gospel principally promiseth, comforteth, saveth.

The third, from the form of the promises. The Legal promises are conditional, that is, they have joined to them the condition of the law fulfilled. But the condition of our works or merits the Evangelical promises have not, which are free, and require only faith, as the instrument whereby the mercy of God is to be laid hold on.

The fourth, from the form of the threatenings. The legal threatenings are without all exception and condition. The threatenings of the Gospel (which are referred to the Gospel by consequence and antithesis, and yet are made by the ministry of the law) have the exception of conversion and repentance. For this cause the law was graven in Tables of stone.

The fifth, from the objects. The Law is to be set before the secure and impenitent; the Gospel before the penitent.

The sixth, from the effects. The Law justifieth no man, saveth no man, but sheweth, accuseth, increaseth, and condemneth sin. But the Gospel is the power of God unto salvation to every one that believeth, and freely, for Christ's sake, giveth to the believers remission of sins, righteousness, and eternal life.

### IX. What are the parts of the Divine Law?

There are ten commandments, comprehended in two Tables, whereof the former containeth three commandments, which shew what <!-- p.181 -->worships are to be rendered immediately to God. For the first commandment preacheth of the knowledge of God in the mind, and of the motions or affections in the will and heart. The second commandeth concerning the profession or confession of that knowledge and of those affections. The third preacheth of the outward worships, and of the whole ministry whereby the propagation of the Heavenly Doctrine is made.

The latter Table commandeth concerning the duties toward our neighbour, and containeth seven commandments, whereof the order is this. The fourth fenceth the common society of men, and appointeth governors. The fifth fenceth bodies and life. The sixth defendeth marriages. The seventh, houses and goods. The eighth defendeth good name, and ordereth judgments. The ninth and tenth declare the meaning of the whole Decalogue, and shew that not only an outward, but an inward, pure, perfect, perpetual, and nowise hindered obedience is required, and that the inward uncleanness and concupiscence is condemned.

### Recite the Sum of the whole Table.

**Matth. 22**: *"Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thy heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy mind. This is the first and great commandment. And the second is like unto it, Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself. On these two commandments hang all the Law and the Prophets."*

### Why is the second said to be like unto the first commandment?

This is not so by reason of the order, or of the object, or of the degree of love (for the love of God is in order the former, in object the worthier, and in degree higher than the love of man), but it is so by reason of the affection, the obligation, and the consequence. The affection ought to be pure. The obligation is either to obedience or to punishment. The <!-- p.182 -->consequence is, that he who loveth not his neighbour, the same loveth not God either.

### What things are to be considered in each several commandment of the Decalogue?

These five.

1. What virtues or works are commanded.
2. How they can be done, and how they please.
3. What sins are forbidden.
4. What promise obedience hath.
5. What threatening and punishment the violation hath.

Here a short Examination is to be instituted concerning good works, concerning the worships of God, concerning sin, concerning the promises and threatenings.

## On Good Works

### What is to be said concerning good works?

1. What good works are.
2. What is the efficient cause, and how they can be done.
3. What is the formal cause, and how they please God.
4. What are the final causes, and why they are to be done.
5. Of what men the obedience pleaseth, and of what men it pleaseth not, seeing all men have sin.

### I. What works then are good, which are to be done?

The works commanded by God himself, which are done with faith in Christ shining before, to this end, that God may be honoured, and the obedience due may be rendered to him.

### Are no works to be accounted good, which are not commanded by God?[^D1-7]

None. For it is written, **Ezech. 20**: *"Walk ye not in the statutes of your <!-- p.183 -->fathers, neither observe their judgments, nor defile yourselves with their idols: I am the Lord your God; walk in my statutes, and keep my judgments, and do them."* Likewise: *"In vain they do worship me with the commandments of men."*
