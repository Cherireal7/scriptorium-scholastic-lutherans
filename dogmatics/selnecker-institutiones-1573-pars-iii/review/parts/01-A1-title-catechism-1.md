# Luther's Small Catechism

<!-- p.1 -->

> ΚΑΤΗΧΗΣΙΣ ΤΟΥΤΕΣΤΙ, ΤΑ ΠΡΩΤΑ ΚΕΦΑΛΑΙΑ τοῦ Χριστιανισμοῦ, σὺν τῇ τοῦ Λουθήρου ἐξηγήσει.

> *Catechesis, hoc est, praecipua capita Christianae doctrinae cum explicatione D. LUTHERI.*

The Catechism, that is, the chief heads of Christian doctrine, with the explication of **D. Luther.**[^A1-1]

## The Ten Commandments

> καθ᾽ ὃν τρόπον οἱ παιδαγωγοὶ τοὺς αὐτῶν παῖδας, τὰς δέκα ἐντολὰς ἁπλούστατα διδάξαι ὀφείλουσι.

> *Quomodo Paedagogi suos pueros decem praecepta simplicissime docere debeant.*

How schoolmasters ought to teach their boys the Ten Commandments in the simplest manner.

### *I. Praeceptum* · The First Commandment

> ἡ πρώτη ἐντολὴ α.
>
> Οὐκ ἔσονταί σοι θεοὶ ἕτεροι πλὴν ἐμοῦ.

> *I. PRAECEPTUM.*
>
> *Non habebis Deos alienos coram me.*

Thou shalt have no other gods before me.

> τί ἐστι τοῦτο;
>
> ὀφείλομεν τὸν θεὸν πρὸ πάντων φοβεῖσθαι, ἀγαπᾶν, καὶ αὐτῷ πείθεσθαι.

> *Quid hoc est?*
>
> *Debemus Deum supra omnia timere, diligere, et ei confidere.*

**What is this?**

We ought to fear God above all things, to love him, and to trust in him.

### *Secundum* · The Second Commandment

> ἡ δευτέρα β.
>
> οὐ λήψῃ τὸ ὄνομα κυρίου τοῦ θεοῦ σου ἐπὶ ματαίῳ.

<!-- p.2 -->

> *SECUNDUM.*
>
> *Non assumes nomen Domini Dei tui in vanum.*

Thou shalt not take the name of the Lord thy God in vain.

> τί τοῦτό ἐστιν;
>
> ὀφείλομεν τὸν θεὸν φοβεῖσθαι καὶ ἀγαπᾶν, ὥστε μὴ ἐπὶ τῷ αὐτοῦ ὀνόματι ἐπαρᾶσθαι ἡμᾶς, μήτε ἐπιορκεῖν, ἢ γοητεύειν, διαψεύδεσθαι, ἢ ἐξαπατᾶν, ἀλλὰ αὐτὸν ἐν πᾶσι δεινοῖς ἐπικαλεῖν, προσεύχεσθαι, δοξάζειν, καὶ εὐχαριστεῖν.

> *Quid hoc est?*
>
> *Debemus Deum timere et diligere, ne per nomen eius execremur, iuremus, incantemus, mentiamur, aut fallamus, sed eum in omnibus necessitatibus invocemus, adoremus, laudemus, et gratias ei agamus.*

**What is this?**

We ought to fear and love God, that we curse not by his name, nor swear, nor use enchantment, nor lie, nor deceive; but that in all necessities we call upon him, worship him, praise him, and give him thanks.[^A1-2]

### *Tertium* · The Third Commandment

> ἡ τρίτη γ.
>
> μνήσθητι τὴν ἡμέραν τῶν σαββάτων, ἁγιάζειν αὐτήν.

> *TERTIUM.*
>
> *Memento, ut diem Sabbati sanctifices.*

Remember the sabbath day, to keep it holy.

> τί τοῦτο λέγειν ἐστίν;
>
> ὀφείλομεν τὸν θεὸν φοβεῖσθαι καὶ ἀγαπᾶν, ἵνα οὐ μὴ καταφρονῶμεν τῶν κηρυγμάτων καὶ λόγου αὐτοῦ, ἀλλὰ λόγον αὐτοῦ ὅσιον ἔχωμεν, ἀσμένως ἀκούειν καὶ μανθάνειν.

> *Quid hoc sibi vult?*
>
> *Debemus Deum timere et diligere, ne conciones et verbum eius negligamus, sed sancte veneremur, libenter audiamus et discamus.*

**What meaneth this?**

We ought to fear and love God, that we neglect not preachings and his word, but hold it in holy reverence, and gladly hear and learn it.

### *Quartum* · The Fourth Commandment

> ἡ τετάρτη δ.
>
> τίμα τὸν πατέρα σου, καὶ τὴν μητέρα σου, ἵνα εὖ σοι γένηται, <!-- p.3 --> καὶ ἵνα μακροχρόνιος γένῃ ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς, τῆς ἀγαθῆς, ἧς κύριος ὁ θεός σου δίδωσί σοι.

> *QUARTUM.*
>
> *Honora patrem tuum, et matrem tuam, ut bene tibi sit, et ut sis longaevus super terram bonam, quam Dominus Deus tuus dabit tibi.*

Honour thy father and thy mother, that it may be well with thee, and that thou mayest be long-lived upon the good land which the Lord thy God shall give thee.

> τί τοῦτό ἐστιν;
>
> ὀφείλομεν τὸν θεὸν φοβεῖσθαι καὶ ἀγαπᾶν, ἵνα τοὺς γονεῖς καὶ δεσπότας ἡμῶν οὐ μὴ παροξύνωμεν, μήτε παροργίζωμεν. ἀλλὰ δεῖ αὐτοὺς τιμᾶν, καὶ ὑπηρετεῖν αὐτοῖς, καὶ ἐν εὐπειθείᾳ, φιλοστοργίᾳ, καὶ εὐσεβείᾳ αὐτοὺς αἰδεῖσθαι.

> *Quid hoc est?*
>
> *Debemus Deum timere et diligere, ne parentes et dominos nostros contemnamus, aut irritemus: sed oportet eos honore afficere, illis servire, et in obedientia, amore et pietate eos venerari.*

**What is this?**

We ought to fear and love God, that we despise not our parents and masters, nor provoke them; but we must do them honour, serve them, and reverence them in obedience, love, and dutiful affection.[^A1-3]

### *Quintum* · The Fifth Commandment

> ἡ πέμπτη. ε.
>
> οὐ φονεύσεις.

> *QUINTUM.*
>
> *Non occides.*

Thou shalt not kill.

> τοῦτο τί ἐστιν;
>
> ὀφείλομεν τὸν θεὸν φοβεῖσθαι καὶ ἀγαπᾶν, ὥστε οὐ μὴ τοῦ τὸ πλησίον σῶμα βλάπτειν, ἢ κακοποιεῖν, ἀλλὰ βοηθεῖν, καὶ ὠφελεῖν ἐν πᾶσι τοῦ σώματος ἀναγκαίοις.

> *Quid hoc est?*
>
> *Debemus Deum timere et diligere, ne corpus proximi laedamus, aut iniuria afficiamus, sed iuvemus, et promoveamus in omnibus vitae necessariis.*

**What is this?**

We ought to fear and love God, that we hurt not our neighbour's body, nor do it wrong; but help and further him in all the necessities of life.[^A1-4]

<!-- p.4 -->

### *Sextum* · The Sixth Commandment

> ἡ ἕκτη. ϛ.
>
> οὐ μοιχεύσεις.

> *SEXTUM.*
>
> *Non moechaberis.*

Thou shalt not commit adultery.

> τί ἐστι τοῦτο;
>
> ὀφείλομεν τὸν θεὸν φοβεῖσθαι καὶ ἀγαπᾶν, ἵνα ἁγνῶς καὶ καθαρῶς τὸν βίον διάγωμεν, ἐν λόγοις τε καὶ ἔργοις, καὶ ἕκαστος τὴν ἑαυτοῦ γαμετὴν φιλῇ καὶ τιμῇ.

> *Quid hoc est?*
>
> *Debemus Deum timere et diligere, ut caste et pudice vivamus, in verbis et operibus, et unusquisque suam coniugem amet et honoret.*

**What is this?**

We ought to fear and love God, that we live chastely and modestly in words and in deeds, and that each one love and honour his own spouse.

### *Septimum* · The Seventh Commandment

> ἡ ἑβδόμη. ζ.
>
> οὐ κλέψεις.

> *SEPTIMUM.*
>
> *Non furtum facies.*

Thou shalt not steal.

> τί τοῦτο λέγειν ἐστί;
>
> τὸν θεὸν φοβεῖσθαι καὶ ἀγαπᾶν ὀφείλομεν, ὥστε οὐ μὴ τὰ τῶν πλησίον ἡμῶν χρήματα, ἢ κτήματα ἀφελέσθαι, μήτε δόλῳ καὶ ἀπάταις ἀδίκως νοσφίζεσθαι, ἀλλὰ τὴν ὑπάρχουσαν αὐτοῦ οὐσίαν πλείω ποιεῖν καὶ φυλάττειν.

> *Quid hoc est?*
>
> *Debemus Deum timere et diligere, ne proximo suam pecuniam, aut bona auferamus, neque dolo aut imposturis iniuste accipiamus, sed ipsius bona et alimenta augere et custodire studeamus.*

**What is this?**

We ought to fear and love God, that we take not from our neighbour his money or his goods, nor get them unjustly by guile or by impostures; but that we endeavour to increase and to guard his goods and his sustenance.

<!-- p.5 -->

### *Octavum* · The Eighth Commandment

> ἡ ὀγδόη. η.
>
> οὐ ψευδομαρτυρήσεις κατὰ τοῦ πλησίον σου μαρτυρίαν ψευδῆ.

> *OCTAVUM.*
>
> *Non loqueris contra proximum tuum falsum testimonium.*

Thou shalt not bear false witness against thy neighbour.

> τουτέστι.
>
> ὀφείλομεν τὸν θεὸν φοβεῖσθαι καὶ ἀγαπᾶν, ὥστε οὐ μὴ τοῦ πλησίον καταψεύδεσθαι, κατηγορεῖν, καταλαλεῖν, ἢ διαβάλλειν· ἀλλὰ ὑπεραπολογεῖσθαι, καὶ ἀγαθὰ περὶ αὐτοῦ λέγειν, καὶ εἰς τὸ βέλτιον τρέπειν ἅπαντα.

> *Hoc est.*
>
> *Debemus Deum timere et diligere, ne proximum mendaciis involvamus, falso accusemus, traducamus, calumniemur: sed excusemus, et bona de ipso loquamur, et omnia in meliorem partem interpretemur.*

**That is:**

We ought to fear and love God, that we entangle not our neighbour in lies, nor falsely accuse, defame, or slander him; but that we excuse him, speak good of him, and interpret all things in the better part.

### *Nonum* · The Ninth Commandment

> ἡ ἐνάτη. θ.
>
> οὐκ ἐπιθυμήσεις τὴν οἰκίαν τοῦ πλησίον σου.

> *NONUM.*
>
> *Non concupisces domum proximi tui.*

Thou shalt not covet thy neighbour's house.

> τουτέστι;
>
> ὀφείλομεν τὸν θεὸν φοβεῖσθαι καὶ ἀγαπᾶν ὥστε οὐ μὴ τῆς τοῦ πλησίον κληρονομίας καὶ τοῦ οἴκου ἐπιθυμεῖν, μηδὲ κακουργήμασι τῇ οὐσίᾳ αὐτοῦ ἐπιβουλεύειν, μηδὲ τῶν δικαίων προφάσει τὰ αὐτοῦ τοῖς ἡμῶν προσάπτειν, ἀλλὰ σπουδάζειν, καὶ προθύμως ἡμᾶς ἔχειν, ἐν τῷ τὰ αὐτοῦ διαφυλάττειν.

> *Hoc est.*
>
> *Debemus Deum timere et diligere, ne haereditatem* <!-- p.6 --> *et domum proximi appetamus, aut malo dolo bona eius captemus, neque sub specie recti nostris facultatibus adiungamus, sed studeamus et parati simus ad custodiendum illa proximo.*

**That is:**

We ought to fear and love God, that we seek not after our neighbour's inheritance and house, nor grasp at his goods by evil guile, nor join them to our own substance under colour of right; but that we be zealous and ready to keep them safe for our neighbour.

### *Decimum* · The Tenth Commandment

> ἡ δεκάτη.
>
> οὐκ ἐπιθυμήσεις τὴν γυναῖκα τοῦ πλησίον σου, οὐδὲ τὸν ἀγρὸν αὐτοῦ, οὔτε τὸν παῖδα αὐτοῦ, οὐδὲ τὴν παιδίσκην αὐτοῦ, οὔτε τοῦ βοὸς αὐτοῦ, οὔτε τοῦ ὑποζυγίου αὐτοῦ, οὔτε παντὸς κτήνους αὐτοῦ, οὔτε ὅσα τοῦ πλησίον σου ἐστί.

> *DECIMUM.*
>
> *Non desiderabis uxorem proximi tui, neque agrum eius, neque servum eius, neque ancillam eius non bovem, non asinum, nec ullum iumentum eius, nec quidquam eorum, quae proximi tui sunt.*

Thou shalt not desire thy neighbour's wife, nor his field, nor his manservant, nor his maidservant, nor his ox, nor his ass, nor any beast of his, nor any thing of those that are thy neighbour's.

> τουτέστι.
>
> ὀφείλομεν τὸν θεὸν φοβεῖσθαι καὶ ἀγαπᾶν, ὥστε οὐ μὴ ἀπὸ τοῦ πέλας ἡμῶν πρὸς ἡμᾶς ἐφέλκεσθαι, ἀπαγαγεῖν, ἢ ἀποσπᾶν γυναῖκα, ὑπηρέτας, ἢ τὰ βοΐδια αὐτοῦ, ἀλλὰ παρανοεῖν αὐτοὺς πρὸς τὸ παραμένειν, καὶ ποιεῖν τὸ προσῆκόν τε καὶ δέον.

> *Hoc est:*
>
> *Debemus Deum timere et diligere, ne a proximo nostro ad nos attrahamus, abalienemus, aut abstrahamus uxorem, familiam, aut pecudes eius, sed monendo omnes eos in officio retineamus.*

**That is:**

We ought to fear and love God, that we draw not to ourselves from our neighbour, nor estrange, nor entice away his wife, his household, or his cattle; but that by admonishing we keep them all in their duty.[^A1-5]

### What doth God say summarily of all these commandments?

> τί δὲ ὅλως ὁ θεὸς λέγει περὶ τούτων τῶν ἐντολῶν πασῶν;
>
> οὕτω λέγει. Ἐξόδ. κ.
>
> Ἐγώ εἰμι κύριος ὁ θεός σου, θεὸς ζηλωτής, ἀποδιδοὺς ἁμαρτίας τῶν πατέρων ἐπὶ τέκνα, ἐπὶ τρίτην καὶ τετάρτην γενεὰν τοῖς <!-- p.7 --> μισοῦσί με. καὶ ποιῶν ἔλεος εἰς χιλιάδας τοῖς ἀγαπῶσί με, καὶ τοῖς φυλάσσουσι τὰ προστάγματά μου.

> *Quid Deus summatim dicit de his praeceptis omnibus?*
>
> *Sic dicit Exod. 20.*
>
> *Ego sum Dominus Deus tuus, Deus Zelotes, visitans iniquitatem Patrum in filios in tertiam et quartam generationem eorum, qui oderunt me: et faciens misericordiam in millia iis, qui diligunt me, et custodiunt praecepta mea.*

Thus he saith, **Exod. 20:**

*"I am the Lord thy God, a jealous God, visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children unto the third and fourth generation of them that hate me; and showing mercy unto thousands of them that love me, and keep my commandments."*[^A1-6]

> τί τοῦτο λέγειν ἐστί;
>
> ἀπειλεῖ ὁ θεὸς τιμωρίας πᾶσι τοῖς τὰ προστάγματα ταῦτα παραβαίνουσι. διόπερ δεῖ ἡμᾶς τὴν ὀργὴν αὐτοῦ φοβεῖσθαι, καὶ μὴ κατὰ τούτων ποιεῖσθαί τι. ὑπισχνεῖται δὲ χάριν καὶ ἀγαθὰ πάντα πᾶσι τοῖς τὰ προστάγματα ταῦτα σώζουσι. διόπερ ὀφείλομεν αὐτὸν ἀγαπᾶν, εἰς αὐτὸν ἐλπίζειν. καὶ σπουδαίως τὸν βίον διατελεῖν κατὰ τὰς ἐντολὰς αὐτοῦ.

> *Quid hoc est?*
>
> *Deus minatur poenas omnibus, qui praecepta ista transgrediuntur. Quare oportet nos iram eius metuere, et nihil contra illa praecepta facere. Promittit autem gratiam et omnia bona omnibus, qui mandata ista observant. Quocirca debemus ipsum diligere, ei confidere et diligenter vivere iuxta praecepta eius.*

**What is this?**

God threateneth punishments to all who transgress these commandments. Wherefore we ought to dread his wrath, and to do nothing against those commandments. But he promiseth grace and all good things to all who observe these precepts. Wherefore we ought to love him, to trust in him, and diligently to live according to his commandments.

## The Creed

> Κατὰ ὃν τρόπον οἱ παιδαγωγοὶ τοὺς αὐτῶν παῖδας τὸ Σύμβολον τῶν ἁγίων ἀποστόλων διδάξαι ὀφείλουσιν.

> *Quomodo paedagogi suos pueros Symbolum S. Apostolorum docere debeant?*

How schoolmasters ought to teach their boys the Symbol of the holy Apostles.

### *Primus Articulus. De Creatione* · The First Article: Of Creation

> Μέρος πρῶτον περὶ τῆς τῶν πάντων ποιήσεως.

<!-- p.8 -->

> *PRIMUS ARTICULUS.*
>
> *De Creatione.*

The First Article. Of Creation.[^A1-7]

> α.
>
> Πιστεύω εἰς ἕνα θεόν· πατέρα παντοκράτορα, ποιητὴν οὐρανοῦ καὶ γῆς.

> *1.*
>
> *Petro adscribitur.*
>
> *Credo in unum Deum: Patrem Omnipotentem, creatorem coeli et terrae.*

1\. *It is ascribed to Peter.*

I believe in one God, the Father Almighty, Creator of heaven and earth.

> τί τοῦτο λέγειν ἐστί;
>
> Πιστεύω, ὅτι ὁ θεὸς ἐμὲ ἔκτισε σὺν πᾶσι κτίσμασι, καὶ ἐμοὶ σῶμά τε καὶ ψυχήν, ὀφθαλμούς, ὦτα καὶ τὰ ἄλλα μέλη, καὶ τὸν λόγον, καὶ τὰς ψυχικὰς δυνάμεις πάσας ἔδωκε, καὶ νῦν ἔτι τηρεῖ. ἔτι δέ, τὰ ἐνδύματα, καὶ ὑποδήματα, φαγήματα, καὶ τὴν πόσιν, οἰκίαν καὶ οὐσίαν, τὴν γυναῖκα, καὶ τὰ τέκνα, καὶ τοὺς ἀγρούς, καὶ τὰ βοΐδια, καὶ πάντα τὰ ἄλλα ὑπάρχοντα, πλουσίως καὶ καθ᾽ ἡμέραν ἑκάστην δίδωσι καὶ φυλάττει, αὐτοῖς πᾶσιν ἐπιτηδείοις καὶ τῇ διατροφῇ τούτου σώματος καὶ βίου, κατὰ πάντων κινδύνων διασώζει, καὶ πάντων κακῶν με ἀπαλλάττει, καὶ διατηρεῖ. καὶ ταῦτα πάντα διὰ τὴν ἁπλῶς πατρῴαν καὶ θείαν ἀγαθότητα καὶ ἔλεος, ἄνευ τῆς ἐμοῦ ἀξίας, δωρεάν. τούτου ἕνεκα χάριν αὐτῷ ἔχειν, καὶ ἐπαινεῖν καὶ ὑπουργεῖν, καὶ πειθαρχεῖν ὀφείλω. πιστὸς λόγος.

> *Quid hoc sibi vult?*
>
> *Credo, quod Deus creaverit me una cum omnibus creaturis: corpus, animam, oculos, aures, et omnia membra, rationem, et omnes sensus mihi dederit, et adhuc conservet. Ad haec vestes, calceos, cibum, potum, domum, uxorem, liberos, agros, iumenta, et omnia bona, cum omnibus corpori et* <!-- p.9 --> *vitae ad victum necessariis, opulente et quotidie suppeditat, et servat: me contra omnia pericula protegit, et ab omnibus malis liberat et custodit. Et haec omnia ex mera sua paterna et divina bonitate et misericordia absque mea dignitate et merito. Quapropter illi gratias agere, eum laudare, ei servire, et obedire debeo. Hoc verissimum est.*

**What meaneth this?**

I believe that God hath created me together with all creatures; that he hath given me body, soul, eyes, ears, and all my members, reason, and all my senses, and doth still preserve them. Moreover, that he richly and daily supplieth and keepeth raiment, shoes, meat, drink, house, wife, children, fields, cattle, and all goods, with all things needful for the sustenance of body and life; that he protecteth me against all perils, and delivereth and guardeth me from all evils. And all this out of his mere fatherly and divine goodness and mercy, without any worthiness or merit of mine. Wherefore I ought to give him thanks, to praise him, to serve him, and to obey him. This is most true.[^A1-8]

2\. *To James the Greater.*

And in Jesus Christ his only-begotten Son, our Lord.

3\. *To John the Evangelist.*

Who was conceived of the Holy Ghost, born of the Virgin Mary.

4\. *To Andrew.*

Suffered under Pontius Pilate, was crucified, dead, and buried.

5\. *To Philip.*

He descended into hell; the third day he rose again from the dead.

6\. *To Thomas.*

He ascended into the heavens; he sitteth at the right hand of the Father Almighty.

7\. *To Bartholomew.*

From thence he shall come to judge the quick and the dead.[^A1-9]

> τί τοῦτό ἐστι;
>
> πιστεύω, ὅτι Ἰησοῦς χριστὸς ὁ ἀληθινὸς θεός, ἐκ τοῦ πατρὸς πρὸ αἰώνων γεννηθείς, ὅμως τε καὶ τέλειος ἄνθρωπος ἐκ Μαρίας τῆς παρθένου τεχθείς, ἐστὶ κύριος ἐμός, ὃς ἐμὲ ἀπολωλότα καὶ κατακριθέντα βροτὸν ἀπελύτρωσεν, ἐπόνησεν εὑρισκόμενος, καὶ ἐνίκησε, καὶ ἀφ᾽ ἁμαρτιῶν πασῶν, καὶ τοῦ θανάτου καὶ τῆς τοῦ σατανᾶς δυνάμεως ἐλυτρώσατο, οὐ χρυσῷ οὔτε ἀργυρίῳ, ἀλλὰ τῷ ἁγίῳ καὶ τιμίῳ αἵματι αὑτοῦ, καὶ ἀθωοτάτῳ παθήματι καὶ θανάτῳ αὑτοῦ, ἵνα αὐτὸς ἐγὼ γένωμαι αὐτοῦ οἰκεῖς, καὶ ἐν τῇ βασιλείᾳ αὐτοῦ ὑπ᾽ αὐτῷ ὤν τε καὶ ζῶν, ὑπηρετήσω αὐτῷ μετὰ δικαιοσύνης αἰωνίου, ἀβλαβείας, καὶ σωτηρίας, ὥσπερ ἐκ τῶν νεκρῶν αὐτὸς ἀνέστη, ζῇ καὶ βασιλεύει εἰς αἰῶνας. πιστὸς λόγος.

<!-- p.11 -->

> *Quid hoc est?*
>
> *Credo, quod Iesus Christus, verus Deus a Patre ab aeterno genitus, idemque verus homo natus ex Maria Virgine, sit Dominus meus, qui me perditum et damnatum hominem redemit, recuperavit et obtinuit, atque ab omnibus peccatis, a morte, et potestate Diaboli liberavit, non auro et argento, sed suo sancto et precioso Sanguine, et sua innocentissima passione et morte, ut ego essem totus ipsius, et in Regno eius sub ipso viverem, et ipsi servirem in aeterna iustitia, innocentia, et beatitudine, quemadmodum ipse resurrexit a mortuis, vivit et regnat in aeternum. Hoc verissimum est.*

**What is this?**

I believe that Jesus Christ, true God, begotten of the Father from eternity, and the same also true man, born of the Virgin Mary, is my Lord; who hath redeemed me, a lost and condemned man, hath recovered and won me, and hath delivered me from all sins, from death, and from the power of the Devil, not with gold and silver, but with his holy and precious Blood, and with his most innocent passion and death; that I might be wholly his own, and live under him in his Kingdom, and serve him in everlasting righteousness, innocence, and blessedness, even as he himself is risen from the dead, liveth and reigneth for ever. This is most true.[^A1-10]

**What meaneth this?**

This is most true.[^A1-12]

## The Lord's Prayer

> ΚΑΤΑ ΟΝ ΤΡΟΠΟΝ ΟΙ ΠΑΙΔΑΓΩΓΟΙ τοὺς αὐτῶν παῖδας τὴν κυριακὴν προσευχὴν ἁπλούστατα διδάξαι ὀφείλουσι.

> *QUOMODO PAEDAGOGI SUOS PUEROS Orationem Dominicam simplicissime docere debeant.*

How schoolmasters ought to teach their boys the Lord's Prayer in the simplest manner.

### *Pater noster qui es in coelis* · Our Father which art in heaven

> Πάτερ ἡμῶν, ὁ ἐν τοῖς οὐρανοῖς.

> *Pater noster qui es in coelis.*

Our Father which art in heaven.

> τί τοῦτό ἐστι;
>
> τούτοις τοῖς λόγοις προσκαλεῖ ἡμᾶς ὁ θεὸς ὥστε πιστεύειν ἡμᾶς, πατέρα εἶναι αὐτὸν ἡμῶν ἀληθινόν, καὶ ἡμᾶς παῖδας αὐτοῦ γνησίους, ἵνα μετὰ θράσους, καὶ ἀγαθῆς ἐλπίδος καὶ πίστεως προσευχώμεθα αὐτῷ, ὥσπερ οἱ παῖδες ἀγαπητοὶ τῷ πατρὶ αὐτῶν ἀγαπητῷ.

> *Quid hoc est?*
>
> *His verbis Deus nos invitare vult, ut credamus, ipsum verum Patrem nostrum esse, et nos ipsius esse Liberos veros, ut firma fiducia, fide, et spe eum invocemus, quemadmodum chari liberi charum parentem suum.*

**What is this?**

By these words God would invite us to believe that he is our true Father, and that we are his true children, that with firm confidence, faith, and hope we may call upon him, even as dear children call upon their dear father.

### *Petitio I* · The First Petition

> Αἴτησις ἡ πρώτη.
>
> Ἁγιασθήτω τὸ ὄνομά σου.
>
> τί τοῦτό ἐστι;
>
> τὸ τοῦ θεοῦ ὄνομα ἅγιον μὲν κατ᾽ αὐτό ἐστιν, αἰτούμεθα δὲ ἐν τῇ ταύτῃ προσευχῇ, τὸν καὶ παρὰ ἡμῖν αὐτοῦ ἁγιασμόν.
>
> <!-- p.14 -->
>
> πῶς ταῦτα γίνεται;
>
> ὅταν ὁ λόγος τοῦ θεοῦ καθαρῶς καὶ ἀκεραίως κηρύττεται, καὶ ἡμεῖς ἁγίως, ὡς οἱ τοῦ θεοῦ υἱοί, κατ᾽ αὐτόν, ζῶμεν. τοῦτο σὺ δὸς ἡμῖν, ὦ πάτερ οὐράνιε. ὅστις δὲ ἄλλως διδάσκει, καὶ βίον διάγει, ἢ ὁ τοῦ θεοῦ λόγος δείκνυσι, τὸ θεῖον ὄνομα αὐτὸς ἐν ἡμῖν λυμαίνεται· ἀφ᾽ ὧν διάσωζε ἡμᾶς, ὦ πάτερ οὐράνιε.

> *PETITIO I.*
>
> *Sanctificetur nomen tuum.*
>
> *Quid hoc est?*
>
> *Nomen Dei per se quidem sanctum est, verum nos oramus hac petitione, ut apud nos quoque sanctificetur.*
>
> *Quomodo fit istud?*
>
> *Cum verbum Dei pure et syncere docetur, et nos sancte, ut decet filios Dei, secundum illud vivimus: in quo adiuva nos, o coelestis Pater. Qui vero aliter docet, aut vivit, quam Verbum Dei ostendit, is nomen Dei inter nos profanat. Hoc ne fiat, tu, o coelestis Pater, provide.*

Hallowed be thy name.

**What is this?**

The name of God is indeed holy of itself; but we pray in this petition that it may be hallowed among us also.

**How is this done?**

When the word of God is taught purely and sincerely, and we live holily according to it, as becometh the sons of God: wherein do thou help us, O heavenly Father. But he that teacheth or liveth otherwise than the Word of God showeth, he profaneth the name of God among us. That this come not to pass, do thou provide, O heavenly Father.

### *Petitio II* · The Second Petition

> β.
>
> ἐλθέτω ἡ βασιλεία σου.
>
> τοῦτο τί ἐστι;
>
> ἡ τοῦ θεοῦ βασιλεία καὶ χωρὶς τῆς ἡμετέρας προσευχῆς ἀφ᾽ ἑαυτῆς ἔρχεται, ἀλλὰ καὶ ἡμεῖς αἰτοῦμεν ἐν ταύτῃ τῇ προσευχῇ, ἵνα καὶ πρὸς ἡμᾶς ἔρχηται.
>
> πῶς τοῦτο γίνεται;
>
> ὅταν ὁ οὐράνιος πατὴρ ἡμῖν δίδωσι πνεῦμα τὸ ἑαυτοῦ ἅγιον, ἵνα διὰ τὴν αὐτοῦ χάριν τῷ αὐτοῦ λόγῳ πιστεύωμεν, καὶ θεοφιλῶς διαζῶμεν, ἐν τῷ παρόντι, καὶ μετέπειτα εἰς αἰῶνας.

> *II.*
>
> *Adveniat regnum tuum.*
>
> <!-- p.15 -->
>
> *Quid hoc est?*
>
> *Regnum Dei venit etiam per se absque nostra Oratione, sed petimus hac precatione, ut ad nos quoque perveniat.*
>
> *Quomodo fit istud?*
>
> *Cum Pater coelestis nobis dat Spiritum suum Sanctum, ut verbo eius per gratiam eius credamus, et divine vivamus, in hac, et postea in aeterna Vita.*

Thy kingdom come.

**What is this?**

The Kingdom of God cometh indeed of itself, without our prayer; but we ask in this prayer that it may come unto us also.

**How is this done?**

When the heavenly Father giveth us his Holy Spirit, that by his grace we may believe his word, and live godly, in this life and hereafter in the life everlasting.

### *Petitio III* · The Third Petition

> γ.
>
> γενηθήτω τὸ θέλημά σου, ὡς ἐν οὐρανῷ, καὶ ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς.
>
> τί τοῦτο λέγειν ἐστί;
>
> τὸ τοῦ θεοῦ ἀγαθὸν καὶ ἵλεον θέλημα ἀεὶ γίνεται ἄνευ τῆς ἡμῶν προσευχῆς, ἀλλὰ αἰτοῦμεν ἐν ταύτῃ τῇ προσευχῇ, ἵνα καὶ ἐν ἡμῖν γίνεται.
>
> πῶς τοῦτο γίνεται;
>
> ὅταν ὁ θεὸς πᾶν τὸ πονηρὸν βούλευμα καὶ θέλημα κάρφει καὶ ἐμποδίζει, ὅ,τι τὸ τοῦ θεοῦ ὄνομα ἁγιάζειν, καὶ τὴν αὐτοῦ βασιλείαν πρὸς ἡμᾶς ἐλθεῖν, ἡμῖν κωλύει, οἷόν ἐστι τοῦ διαβόλου, τοῦ κόσμου, καὶ τῆς σαρκὸς τῆς ἡμετέρας θέλημα. ἡμᾶς δὲ στηρίζει καὶ διατηρεῖ καρτερῶς ἐν τῷ αὐτοῦ λόγῳ καὶ πίστει, μέχρι τῆς ἡμῶν τελευτῆς· τοῦτό ἐστι τὸ θεοῦ ἵλεον καὶ ἀγαθὸν θέλημα.

> *III.*
>
> *Fiat voluntas tua sicut in coelo, ita etiam in terra.*
>
> *Quid hoc sibi vult?*
>
> *Bona et misericors Dei voluntas fit etiam sine nostra petitione: nos autem precamur hac petitione, ut illa quoque apud nos fiat.*
>
> *Quomodo fit istud?*
>
> *Cum Deus omne malum consilium et voluntatem frangit,* <!-- p.16 --> *et impedit, quae nobis Nomen Dei sanctificare, et Regnum eius ad nos pervenire non sinunt, ut est Diaboli, mundi et carnis nostrae voluntas: verum corroborat et conservat nos firmiter in suo verbo et fide usque ad vitae nostrae finem. Haec est misericors et bona eius voluntas.*

Thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven.

**What meaneth this?**

The good and merciful will of God is done indeed without our asking; but we pray in this petition that it may be done among us also.

**How is this done?**

When God breaketh and hindereth every evil counsel and will which suffer us not to hallow the Name of God, nor his Kingdom to come unto us, such as is the will of the Devil, of the world, and of our own flesh; but strengtheneth and keepeth us steadfast in his word and in faith unto the end of our life. This is his merciful and good will.[^A1-13]

[^A1-1]: The Greek title reads "the first heads of Christianity, with the exposition of Luther". Throughout this Catechism the Greek is transcribed as it stands in the print, with its ligatures and abbreviations resolved; evident slips of the compositor are left standing (so ***τοῦ τὸ πλησίον σῶμα*** in the Fifth Commandment for ***τὸ τοῦ πλησίον σῶμα***; ***τῆς τοῦ σατανᾶς δυνάμεως*** and ***οἰκεῖς***, for ***οἰκεῖος***, in the Second Article). The English is made from the Latin.

[^A1-2]: Where the Latin has simply *iuremus*, "swear", the Greek has ***ἐπιορκεῖν***, "to swear falsely"; and for *in omnibus necessitatibus* it has ***ἐν πᾶσι δεινοῖς***, "in all dangers".

[^A1-3]: In the commandment the Greek has the present ***δίδωσι***, "giveth", where the Latin has *dabit*. In the answer the Greek has two verbs of provoking, ***παροξύνωμεν*** and ***παροργίζωμεν*** ("that we neither exasperate nor anger"), and nothing answering to the Latin *contemnamus*, "despise".

[^A1-4]: The Greek says "in all the necessities of the body" (***ἐν πᾶσι τοῦ σώματος ἀναγκαίοις***), the Latin "of life".

[^A1-5]: The Greek ends otherwise: "but exhort them to remain, and to do what is fitting and due". The print has ***παρανοεῖν***, which gives no sense here; ***παραινεῖν***, "to exhort", answering to the Latin *monendo*, is evidently meant.

[^A1-6]: The Greek follows the wording of the Septuagint: "repaying the sins of the fathers upon the children, unto the third and fourth generation, to them that hate me".

[^A1-7]: The Greek headings call the three Articles "parts": "The first part, of the making of all things"; "The second part, of redemption"; "The third part, of sanctification". The Greek numbers the members of the Creed with the letters α to λ and has nothing answering to the Latin headings that ascribe each member to one of the twelve Apostles.

[^A1-8]: The Greek differs in several small points: "eyes, ears and the other members, and reason, and all the powers of the soul"; "house and substance" (***οἰκίαν καὶ οὐσίαν***), where the Latin has only *domum*; "without my worthiness, freely" (***δωρεάν***), where the Latin has *absque mea dignitate et merito*. Here and at the end of the other two Articles the Greek closes with ***πιστὸς λόγος***, "faithful is the saying", for the Latin *Hoc verissimum est*.

[^A1-9]: The Greek print marks three alternative readings with asterisks, which the Latin does not have: for "into hell" either ***εἰς ᾅδου*** or ***εἰς ᾅδην***; for "at the right hand" either ***ἐκ δεξιῶν*** or ***ἐν δεξιᾷ***; for "he shall come" either ***ἐλεύσεται*** or ***μέλλει ἔρχεσθαι***.

[^A1-10]: The Greek has "begotten of the Father before the ages" (***πρὸ αἰώνων***) for *ab aeterno*, "perfect man" (***τέλειος ἄνθρωπος***) for *verus homo*, and "a lost and condemned mortal" (***βροτόν***) for *hominem*. For *recuperavit et obtinuit* it has ***ἐπόνησεν εὑρισκόμενος, καὶ ἐνίκησε***, "laboured to gain me, and prevailed"; and for *beatitudine* it has ***σωτηρίας***, "salvation".

[^A1-11]: For the last member the Greek print gives an alternative: "Otherwise: and after this life, life everlasting".

[^A1-12]: The Greek keeps two things apart which the Latin joins in one phrase: "not of my own reasoning, nor of my own power". It also has "in true faith" for *in recta fide*, and says that the Holy Ghost forgiveth sins "richly" (***πλουσίως***), where the Latin has *benigne*.

[^A1-13]: The Greek says that the will of God "is always done" (***ἀεὶ γίνεται***) without our prayer, where the Latin has *etiam*; and for *frangit* it has ***κάρφει***, "withereth".
