# Translator's Note

## 1. The text

The English of this volume renders the third and last part of Nikolaus Selnecker's *Institutio Christianae Religionis* in the edition of 1579: *Institutionis Christianae Religionis Pars Tertia, continens Doctrinam Catecheticam* — the part "containing the Catechetical Doctrine: the Catechism of Dr. Luther in Greek and Latin, an Exposition of the Decalogue, and Catechetical Questions fitted to the use of Schools". Parts One and Two, the dogmatic *loci*, appear as companion volumes in this series. Part Three is a different kind of book: a schoolbook, made for the boys of the Latin schools and their masters.

The text-base is the copy of the Bayerische Staatsbibliothek, Munich (shelfmark Dogm. 924-3), in the library's digital facsimile bsb10175492. The title page gives the year 1579 and a privilege for ten years, and names neither place nor printer. The translation was made from the page images of that copy, page by page, and every page was afterwards read a second time against the images by a reader who had not translated it. The library's machine-read text served as an aid only. The printer's abbreviations are expanded silently.

## 2. What the volume contains

- **Luther's Small Catechism**, which the print gives in Greek and in Latin: the Ten Commandments, the Creed, the Lord's Prayer, Baptism, the Lord's Supper, Confession, and prayers for morning, table and evening.
- **A poem** of 409 hexameters upon the Catechism, "written at Dresden in the year 1563".
- **The Exposition of the Decalogue**, the longest part of the book: four introductory points, then each commandment in turn — the works it requires, the virtues with their definitions, the *antithesis* or sins against it, and its promises and threatenings — with many questions and objections answered by the way.
- **Catechetical Questions** on the first part of the Catechism: catechesis, the Law and its uses, good works, the worship of God, sin, the promises and threatenings.
- **A Table of the names of the virtues and of their extremes**, set out by the commandments, in Greek and Latin.
- **Catechetical Questions on the remaining parts of the Catechism**: the Creed (the longest block, with the articles on God, creation, the person of Christ, his passion, resurrection and session at the right hand, the Holy Ghost, the Church, the remission of sins and justification, the resurrection and eternal life), prayer, Baptism, and the Lord's Supper with absolution and confession.
- **Three indexes** of the print: passages of Scripture, sentences of the Fathers, and things and words.

The print has no dedication and no preface; after the title page it begins at once with the Catechism.

## 3. The Greek and the Latin

In the print every passage of the Small Catechism stands twice, first in Greek and then in Latin, and the last prayers stand in Greek alone. This edition prints the English only, made from the Latin (and from the Greek where there is no Latin); a note marks the places where the Greek says something the Latin does not. The Table of the virtues is different: there the Greek and Latin names are themselves the matter, so each item gives the Greek term, the Latin of the print, and the English.

Elsewhere Selnecker writes Latin with Greek words and phrases set in. These are kept in Greek, as the print has them, with the English beside them. Hebrew words, which the print gives in Latin letters, are kept as printed.

## 4. A note on Scripture quotations

Scripture quotations are conformed to the King James Version (1611), which is in the public domain, wherever Selnecker's Latin agrees with it in sense. He often cites from memory, joins two places together, or follows the Latin Bible of his day; where his Latin plainly says something other than the King James Version, the English follows his Latin, and nowhere is another English version used. His references are kept as he prints them, including those that are slips; a note gives the right place where it is certain. Where his numbering of a Psalm differs from that of the King James Version, the latter follows in square brackets.

## 5. Editorial conventions

**Headings.** The headings are those of the print, given in Latin and in English. Where the print has none, a heading has been supplied so that the reader can find his way: the title "Catechetical Questions" over the first block of questions, which in the print begin without a heading in the middle of a page, and three group headings within that block. In the Exposition of the Decalogue the print's bare "On the First Point", "On the Second Point" have their subject added from Selnecker's own list of points.

**Page numbers.** The indexes refer to the pages of the 1579 print, and their numbers are kept as printed. The printer's numbering runs to 322 but skips four numbers, so that the text has 318 pages.

**Misprints.** Where the print is plainly wrong and the right reading is not in doubt, the right reading is translated and the printed one is given in a note.

**Endnotes.** The notes stand together at the end of the volume. They record readings, identify a number of the authors Selnecker cites or echoes, and explain a few terms. They are sparing: a citation that could not be identified with confidence has no note.

**Dates in the text.** The 1579 print still speaks in places of "this year 1571" or "1572", and the poem is dated 1563. The matter of the book is therefore older than its title page, gathered from Selnecker's teaching over many years. These dates are kept as printed.

**Ornament.** The aldus leaf — ❦ — marks the divisions between the major parts, as in the other volumes of this series.
