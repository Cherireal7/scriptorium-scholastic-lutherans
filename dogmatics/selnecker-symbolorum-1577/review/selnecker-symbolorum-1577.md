# Translator's Note

## 1. The text

The English of this volume renders the **1577** Leipzig imprint of Nikolaus Selnecker's ***Symbolorum Apostolici, Niceni, et Athanasiani Exegesis*** — the enlarged reissue, from the press of *Johann Rhamba,* of the *editio princeps* of 1575. It is a running exposition of the three ancient Creeds of the Western Church — the *Apostolicum,* the *Nicaeno-Constantinopolitanum,* and the *Quicunque vult* — set forth *contra recentes horum temporum blasphemias,* that is, against the fresh Christological errors that had arisen in Selnecker's own generation of the Late Reformation. The volume opens with a Dedicatory Epistle to *Johann Günther, Count of Schwarzburg-Arnstadt-Sondershausen-Leutenberg,* dated *Leipzig, April 1575,* and carried without alteration into the 1577 reissue.

This volume is the third in a Scholastic Lutherans series devoted to Selnecker's mature systematic-dogmatic and creedal-Christological output. The two previously issued companion volumes — *The Institution of the Christian Religion, Part One* (Jena, Tröster, 1573; reissued 1579) and *The Institution of the Christian Religion, Part Two* (Jena, Tröster, 1579) — cover the Prolegomena, the Doctrine of God, Creation, Angels, Anthropology, Sin, the Law, the Cross, and Consolation (Part One), and Justification, Penitence, Christian Liberty, the Power of the Church, the Doctrine of the Sacraments, and the Rationale of Human Life (Part Two). The present *Exposition of the Creeds* takes up what those two volumes only touched on: the mature Lutheran Christology — the eternal divinity of the Son, the hypostatic union of the two natures in Christ, the *communicatio idiomatum* in its three genera, the majesty and ubiquity of the Son of Man, the substantial Real Presence of the body and blood of Christ in the Supper. It is the natural continuation of the Institutio's dogmatic arc, though the *Institutio* itself is a two-part work: what a modern reader might expect as "Institutio Part Three" was in fact never written by Selnecker under that title. The 1577 *Symbolorum exegesis* stands in its place, and stands as the mature intersection of Selnecker's dogmatic and catechetical work.

The English text-base is the digital facsimile of the 1577 Leipzig imprint held by the **Bayerische Staatsbibliothek München** (BSB Munich shelfmark stamp *00021305* on the verso of every folio in the scan; Google-Books identifier *hyfSeau9q60C*), a 368-page octavo. Where the printed original abbreviates, the abbreviation has been silently expanded; the long *ſ* has been silently modernised to round *s;* Selnecker's *maiestas / maiestate / maiestatica* has been kept as *majesty / majestic* rather than the older *majestie* form; and no other emendation has been made.

## 2. What the author supplies

Selnecker's own hand supplies the entirety of the volume — the *Dedicatory Epistle* to Count Johann Günther (April 1575); a first block of Ancient Symbols printed at the head of the volume (the three Creeds themselves, together with *Basil's* shorter Nicene rendering, *Epiphanius'* Nicene-Constantinopolitan expansion against the heresies, the *Chalcedonian* Definition, and two Christological confessions of the Emperor *Justinian*); a Psalm-block appended to that first cluster (Psalm 141 in the Vulgate numbering, that is, Psalm 142 in the Hebrew/English numbering, and Psalm 124/125, followed by Selnecker's own Latin distich-versification of Psalm 124/125 dated 1573); the great running *Enarratio Symbolorum* — the systematic prolegomena on why the Church recites the Creeds and how the ancient synods relate to Prophetic and Apostolic teaching; and the exposition of the Three Articles themselves, arranged so that Article II (the Christological article) opens out into the mature Lutheran Christological *Doctrina de Communicatione Idiomatum,* the Ascension article, and the ubiquity question *An Christus sit omnipraesens etiam secundum humanam naturam.* The volume closes with two great Sacramental appendices bound in with the exposition of the Third Article — the **1560 Heidelberg Disputation on the Lord's Supper** (which Selnecker had defended at the Academia Heidelbergensis under Frederick III before Frederick's turn toward the Reformed side and Selnecker's own departure to Jena), and the *De Iuda accipiente corpus et sanguinem Christi* patristic catena on the eating of the body of Christ by the unworthy — followed only by the printer's colophon.

His treatments quote — often at length — from Scripture (with clear preference for the Johannine Prologue, the great Pauline Christological passages of Colossians and Philippians, and the whole of Isaiah); from the church fathers (Basil, Athanasius, Cyril of Alexandria, Chrysostom, Ambrose, Hilary of Poitiers, Leo the Great, Fulgentius, Bernard, Jerome, Oecumenius, Epiphanius); from the ancient synods and their symbols (Nicaea, Constantinople, Ephesus, Chalcedon); from Luther and from Melanchthon (his *praeceptor* at Wittenberg); and from the Lutheran confessional documents — with special emphasis on the *Formula of Concord* Articles VII (Lord's Supper) and VIII (Person of Christ), which Selnecker himself had helped draft in the years immediately preceding this volume.

## 3. The Heidelberg Disputation of 1560

Toward the end of the volume, immediately after the exposition of the Third Article and before the *De Iuda accipiente* patristic catena, Selnecker prints as a self-standing document his own set of theses ***Propositiones de vera Coena Domini sententia,*** defended *iuxta Confessionem Augustanam adversus quorundam certamina* at the *Academia Heidelbergensis* on **3-4 June 1560,** under Elector Frederick III. This is a document of first-rate historical interest, for it fixes Selnecker's Real-Presence position — substantial, oral, *manducatio indignorum* included, and rejecting alike the Zwinglian *nuda signa* and the Calvinist *virtute-non-substantia* mediation — at a date fully seventeen years before the *Formula of Concord* codified that position as Article VII of the Lutheran confessional settlement.

The disputation was defended at the very hinge point of Frederick III's own theological journey — before Frederick's transition to the Reformed side and the composition of the *Heidelberg Catechism* in 1563, and before Selnecker's own departure from Heidelberg to the Chair of Theology at Jena in 1565. That the mature Selnecker chose in 1575 to reprint verbatim, at the close of his Christological *Exegesis,* the very theses he had defended fifteen years earlier at Heidelberg is his own way of establishing the doctrinal continuity between the young *Hofprediger* of the Dresden court and the mature co-author of the *Solida Declaratio* — and it is his own way of enrolling the young Frederick's own *Academia Heidelbergensis* in the witness of the confessional-Lutheran Real-Presence position.

## 4. A note on Scripture quotations

**Every scripture quotation in the body text is the translator's own English rendering of Selnecker's own Latin citation.** Selnecker cites Scripture in Latin — sometimes from the Vulgate, sometimes from his own conflate hand, sometimes from paraphrases available in the Reformation-era Latin Bibles (Erasmus, Pagninus, Münster, the Stephanus Vulgate) — and always in his own working numbering. Rendering these citations back into English from Selnecker's Latin, rather than substituting a modern English version, preserves both the exegetical force of what Selnecker actually places on the page and his citation-idiom. **No copyrighted modern English Bible translation is quoted anywhere in this volume.** The reader who wishes to consult a standard modern English rendering of any given passage may of course do so; the numbering here follows Selnecker's own (which for the Psalms in particular differs from the Hebrew/English numbering by one in most cases — Psalm 141 in Selnecker's citation is Psalm 142 in the Hebrew/English numbering, and so on).

The same principle applies to patristic quotations: what Selnecker renders in Latin from the Greek Fathers is here rendered in English from Selnecker's Latin, not from modern critical editions or from Migne. Where he preserves Greek in continuous script rather than in Latin translation — and he does this often in this volume, since much of the Christological doctrine of the Fathers turns on the exact Greek term (*οὐσία, ὑπόστασις, ὁμοούσιος, ἀνθρωπολάτρης, ψυχοφθόρος,* the whole vocabulary of the *communicatio idiomatum* in the Greek Fathers, the Chalcedonian *ἐκ δύο φύσεων* formula) — the Greek is preserved in the English rendering, with an English gloss.

## 5. Editorial conventions

- **Register:** scholarly / academic-critical English; reverent but not archaising. The substance of Selnecker's expository style — the numbered *distinctiones,* *quaestiones,* *definitiones,* *argumenta,* *fontes solutionum,* and *usus practicus* — is preserved as the *Loci-format* which Selnecker inherits from Melanchthon and here bends to the shape of a running exposition of the Creed.
- **Technical Latin terms** are preserved on first occurrence in each Article in italic (*hypostasis, ousia, homoousion, communicatio idiomatum, genus idiomaticum, genus apotelesmaticum, genus maiestaticum, extra Calvinisticum, unio hypostatica, maiestas, ubiquitas, praesentia realis, manducatio oralis, manducatio indignorum, manducatio impiorum, filioque, dextera Dei,* and so forth), briefly glossed, and used in English thereafter — unless the term is itself the subject of the section, in which case the Latin is retained throughout.
- **Greek** appears often in this volume; it is preserved in polytonic script with English gloss on first substantive occurrence in each section.
- **Hebrew** appears occasionally where Selnecker quotes Old Testament sources; it is preserved with transliteration and English gloss on first occurrence.
- **Confessional documents:** Selnecker cites the pre-*Concord* Lutheran confessions (AC, Apology, Small and Large Catechisms, Smalcald Articles — 1530-1537) as ***confessio nostra*** and the *Formula of Concord* (1577) as ***Formula Concordiae*** or ***nostra Concordia.*** Modern editions are consigned to the endnote apparatus (BSLK · Kolb-Wengert).
- **Names of contemporaries and predecessors:** first-occurrence life-dates appear in the endnote apparatus, not in the body.
- **Ornamental divider:** the aldus leaf ` — ❦ — ` marks section transitions, per the convention adopted across this English series.

## 6. Structural notes

The 1577 imprint of the *Exegesis* is an *enlarged reissue* of the 1575 *editio princeps* (also from the Rhamba press at Leipzig, and also preserved at Munich in the copy Google-Books identifies as *QyE8AAAAcAAJ*). The Dedicatory Epistle to Count Johann Günther bears the *original April 1575 date* without alteration — Selnecker did not re-date it for the reissue. What the reissue *adds* to the *editio princeps* is largely the sharpening of the polemic against the freshest of the "recentes horum temporum blasphemias," and — most importantly — the incorporation of the 1560 Heidelberg Disputation and the *De Iuda accipiente* catena as the closing Sacramental section.

The 1577 imprint carries **no printed back-matter apparatus.** Unlike the companion Pars II volume, this edition has no *Elenchus locorum Scripturae* (index of scripture passages), no *Patrum Sententiae* (Fathers'-quotations index), and no *Index Rerum et Verborum Memorabilium* (alphabetical subject-and-word index). The book runs straight from the *De Iuda accipiente* catena to the printer's colophon. A constructed reader-facing Table of Contents has been added to the English edition (below); no subject index has been constructed.

## Article I — *Credo in Deum Patrem Omnipotentem*

- Part 1. *Quid sit Credere* — Historical vs Justifying Faith · *Credere Deum, in Deum, Deo*
- Part 1 (continued). *De una Dei essentia et tribus personis* — the vocabulary of *essentia, persona, hypostasis;* against Sabellius and Arius
- Part 2. *De prima Trinitatis persona* — the First Person as the Creed presents Him
- Part 2 (continued). *De Creatione* — creation *ex nihilo,* the grades of creatures, angels, man, the *imago Dei,* the four states of man
- Part 3. *De Providentia, Contingentia, Lapsu, Libero Arbitrio, Peccato, Lege* — Providence, contingency, the Fall, free will, sin, and the Law
- Part 3 (closing). The six-fold Law-Gospel *discrimen* and the bridge to Article II

## Article II — *Credo in Iesum Christum Filium Dei*

- Part 1. *Persona Christi · Divinitas · Officium* — the arcane counsel of the Trinity to send the Second Person as Mediator; anti-Nestorian formulations
- Part 2. *Incarnatio* — the Nicene-clause exegesis: *Deum de Deo, Lumen de Lumine, genitum non factum, consubstantialem Patri, per quem omnia facta sunt;* the Passion, the Descensus, the Resurrection
- Part 3. Closing exposition and transition into the *Communicatio Idiomatum*
- ***Doctrina de Communicatione Idiomatum*** — the Chalcedonian formula in Greek, the three *genera communicationis,* the anti-Reformed *extra Calvinisticum* refuted
- *De Ascensione* — the Ascension as the exaltation of Christ's human nature *super omnes caelos ad dexteram Dei,* not a mere local translation
- *An Christus sit omnipraesens etiam secundum humanam naturam* — the ubiquity of Christ's human nature; the *finitum non capax infiniti* rebuttal
- *De Sessione ad dexteram Dei* — the *dextera Dei* as universal dominion, not a physical location; the Sessio as the office-formal ground for the ubiquity of Christ's human nature
- *De Adoratione Christi et de Judicio venturo* — the adoration of the incarnate Christ; the *Veni Domine Iesu* longing for the return; the last judgment as the vindication of Christ's kingdom

## Article III — *Credo in Spiritum Sanctum · Ecclesiam · Remissionem Peccatorum · Resurrectionem Carnis · Vitam Aeternam*

- *De Spiritu Sancto* — the five *beneficia Spiritus Sancti* (docere, renovare-sanctificare, iuvare, obsignare, arrhabo)
- *De Ecclesia* — the Church as *ubicunque sonat vox Evangelii;* the Epiphanius Greek Creed-fragment on the Spirit

# Nikolaus Selnecker — Biographical Sketch

*(Reused from the companion Institutio volumes for the reader coming to Selnecker for the first time, with a paragraph appended on this volume's place in his dogmatic-catechetical output.)*

**Nikolaus Selnecker** (also spelled *Selneccer* or *Schellenecker*) was born on **5 December 1530 at Hersbruck,** a small Franconian town near Nürnberg. His father, a jurist, brought the family to Nürnberg while Selnecker was still a child, and it was there that Selnecker received his first musical education — a formation that would define one strand of his later life-work as a paradigm-Lutheran hymn-writer.

At **Wittenberg** from 1550 he studied under **Philipp Melanchthon** — whose *Loci communes* method Selnecker would later adopt and adapt across all his systematic-dogmatic writings — and took his *Magister* in 1554. From 1557 he served as *Hofprediger* to Kurfürst August of Saxony at Dresden, an office he held until 1565 when the *Crypto-Calvinist controversy* at the Dresden court forced his departure. He became **Professor of Theology at Jena** in 1565, then at **Leipzig** from 1568 — with interruptions during the second phase of the Crypto-Calvinist controversy 1570–1574 (when the strict-Lutherans were briefly displaced) — and finally returned to Leipzig from 1574 as *Superintendent* and *Pfarrer* at St. Thomas, an office he held (with a single year at Braunschweig-Wolfenbüttel 1571) until his death. It was in the early years of this final Leipzig period that the *Symbolorum Exegesis* here translated was composed — first published in 1575, in an *editio princeps* whose Dedicatory Epistle he re-issued *verbatim,* two years later, at the head of the enlarged 1577 imprint that stands as the English text-base of the present volume.

Selnecker's **confessional-Lutheran significance** rests on his role as one of the **six co-authors of the *Formula of Concord*** (1577). The six — Jakob Andreä (Tübingen), Martin Chemnitz (Braunschweig), David Chytraeus (Rostock), Andreas Musculus (Frankfurt/Oder), Christoph Körner (Frankfurt/Oder), and Selnecker himself (Leipzig) — worked between 1576 and 1580 to bring the inner-Lutheran controversies of the second half of the sixteenth century to a paradigm confessional settlement: the *Solida Declaratio* of the *Formula of Concord.* Selnecker's specific contribution was the *Solida Declaratio* Article XI (**Predestination**), whose anti-double-predestination Lutheran formulation — *God has predestined to salvation but not to damnation; the reprobate are lost by their own contumacy* — became the confessional-Lutheran position against both the Reformed *decretum absolutum* on one side and the Semi-Pelagian *praevisa merita* framework on the other.

Selnecker's **hymnody** is his second lasting legacy. Author of ***"Ach bleib bei uns, Herr Jesu Christ"*** (*EG* 347 — the canonical *Ausgangs-Choral* still sung in confessional-Lutheran and Reformed hymnody worldwide) and of dozens more, Selnecker's ***Christliche Psalmen*** (1587) is one of the great confessional-Lutheran hymn-collections of the whole *Concord*-generation.

Selnecker died at **Leipzig on 24 May 1592,** and was buried in the *Paulinerkirche.* His epitaph, composed by his son, closed with the confessional-Lutheran memorial-verse ***Christus mihi vita, mors lucrum*** (Phil. 1:21).

**On the place of the present volume in Selnecker's output.** In addition to the great systematic *Institutio* in two parts (Pars I 1573/1579, Pars II 1579), Selnecker composed a long series of catechetical works — a *Catechesis minor Graecolatina* modeled on Luther's Small Catechism, a *Catechesis maior,* the *Examen ordinandorum,* pedagogical catechetical questions for pupils at home. The present *Symbolorum Exegesis* is the mature meeting-point of these two strands: it takes up, one by one, each clause of the three ancient Creeds — that is, the same clauses which Luther's Small Catechism explains in one sentence apiece — and unfolds them into full dogmatic expositions worthy of the *Institutio.* The volume is, in this sense, the mature Selnecker's own most complete statement of the *systematic-catechetical intersection* that his entire teaching life had been aiming toward.

## Dedicatory Epistle to Johann Günther, Count of Schwarzburg-Arnstadt-Sondershausen-Leutenberg

*(Dated Leipzig, April 1575, and carried without alteration into the 1577 enlarged reissue. Selnecker addresses one of the four Imperial Counts of the Schwarzburg house — a Lutheran territorial lord well-known to Selnecker as a supporter and lover of pure doctrine — and takes occasion in this dedication to state, before the volume proper opens, both the reason for adding this Creed-exposition to his already-published Greek-Latin Small Catechism and the reason for treating the fresh Christological controversies of the mid-1570s at length. The epistle carries within it the mature Selnecker's own most self-conscious statement of his life-vocation: to teach and to write, and — where necessary — to contradict manifest errors, "so long as I live, alone or with others, in season and out of season," in Paul's words.)*

To the Illustrious and Generous Lord, the Lord ***Johann Günther,*** of the Four Counts of the Roman Empire, Count of Schwarzburg, Lord in Arnstadt, Sondershausen, and Leutenberg, and so on, his own most clement and benign Lord: **Nicolaus Selneccerus, D[octor].**²

There has recently been published the *Small Greek-Latin Catechism* of Dr. Luther of holy memory, written by me long ago while yet a young man, to which have been added a number of *questions in catechetical form* on the chief heads of Christian doctrine — questions which I once recited to my own boys in domestic study, and adapted to the capacity of learners. But since a **just declaration** — such as is indispensable for those who have already made some progress in their studies — has been left unexplained in that brief recitation, and since a fuller doctrine is altogether required for a *Christian exegesis* of our Creed, I resolved by best counsel that a **brief exposition of the Creeds,** begun by me elsewhere but continued here at Leipzig and set before my pious youthful hearers at the beginning, when I was recalled hither, should be joined to that *Domestic Catechism* of mine.

The censors will cry out afresh that no end is being made to writings. And I too could wish that there were no more need of any further writing at all, and that we could simply come to rest in the truth of the divine word. For thus should we consult the public and private welfare. **But since Satan is not for a moment at rest,** but in this our time sends out his hirelings and his instruments, and stirs up depravers, blasphemers of sound doctrine, who patch together their volumes and month by month, almost, publish them and ship them off into the chief realms and thrust them upon the commonwealths and commend them to the schools and deceive Kings and Princes, Senators, Theologians and Philosophers — to say nothing of the poor commoner — and hurl their thunderbolts against the *purer doctrine* which by God's benefit we profess: therefore we must not be silent, and much less must we become *ῥιψάσπιδες*³ or deserters of the truth. Rather it is for us to contradict their blasphemies and corruptions and manifest errors. For that patience is not to be so called which occurs with an injury to the glory and truth of Christ — but is rather an *uncertitude,* a stupor, a flight, a timidity, an inconstancy, a lightness, a defection. And **concord and tranquillity, without a shelter and a defence for the truth,** is the fountain of all discords, and the seminary of all errors and seditions. For where the peace of Christ is not, there the peace of the world is the source of all miseries, spiritual and corporeal, and of eternal damnation.

Why then should it be permitted to beasts, buffoons, brawlers, and others, to rise up against a doctrine that we know to be the very truth of God — the truth immovable — and with great arrogance, and with the wide-mouthed empty flasks of their words, to scatter their own opinions abroad, to deceive many, or at the least to make the minds of the secure and the rude Academical and suspended? And **why should it be denied to us,** who seek with our whole heart the glory of Christ Jesus, and who reflect upon the account to be rendered to Christ the Son of God and of man — denied to us, I say, that we should assert with mouth and hand — and indeed by way of *office* and *conscience* — the truth, that is, the word of God, against these people? Truly if it is at their heart to pervert many, it ought all the more to be at our heart to *convert,* and to bring back into the way, those who have been seduced. For we see many of them break in with impure minds and unwashed hands into the sacrosanct school of the highest mysteries of God, and indeed *ἀλλοτριοεπισκοποῦν*⁴ — while by profession they wish to be recognised either as consulting jurists, or as physicians, or as courtiers, or as philosophers, and pursue the study of Theology only in port and in favourable weather — but when a little storm arises, indeed when someone reminds them of their duty and shows them that their impudent temerity is not approved by him, or when they themselves fear even the smallest hint of some indignation, they at once seek their ***κρησφύγετον***⁵ and their hiding-places, pretending that they lie hidden *behind the reeds* and that they are not Theologians and that they ought not to put the sickle into another man's harvest by defending *τὰ θεολογικά.*⁶

But why do they attempt to *demolish* these things, when — if they are unwilling to *defend* them, and it were well for them so to do — they would be tearing up the truth itself? Men unworthy of the name, ***straw heroes*** *καὶ χέτλιοι,*⁷ altogether such as Dr. Luther described the like to Duke George of Saxony in the year [15]33, when he said:

> *"For more than twenty years I have learned the sacred letters with the highest diligence, in prayer and in vigils; for more than twelve years I have taught with the greatest labour, in writing, in reading, in preaching, in publishing, in expounding, daily tried and proved by temptations, persecutions, perils and necessities — and yet I know but little of these things, and often, as boys do, I cannot recite my own lesson. But others — they have neither learned, nor do they teach, nor have they been exercised — and yet fools rush in, and think, if they look at a book, that they are the very Doctors of the Doctors, and pass judgement on all things, and are Reformers, and bring with them minds embittered, eyes venomous, ears full of hatred, and bend the Scriptures to their own bitterness and venom and malice."*⁸

Often creeping out from the political benches, often from the money-changers' tables, from the marketplace and from banquets, or I know not from what recesses, they *treat* and befoul the mysteries of God most wretchedly and most impudently. They use also *stratagems and traps* peculiar to themselves — which good and honest minds could interpret not with the name of wisdom but with the marks of foolishness, malice, and extreme *ἀκαθαρσίας καὶ ἀνομίας*⁹ — lying, that is to say, and changing their names, denying their baptism and their family as though it were disgraceful, and, with a mask put on, celebrating their revels as it were *Bacchanalia* in the very bosom of the Church; secretly, subtly, and — as this liar and murderer from the beginning is wont to circumvent the incautious and the sleeping — perversely they put forth their delirious writings. Hence so many feigned names, and so many false inscriptions on famous books; so many *Christionis* and *Nathanaels,* so many *Hessianders,* and many others besides¹⁰ — although it is a wonder why they too, who wish to seem wise and to be in the highest authority, dare with so unblushing a brow to perpetrate with impunity that very crime which they justly reprehend in others, and which by laws they know to be deserving of the capital punishment; and why others — Princes, Counts, Senators, Theologians, honest minds — should either take pleasure in this misdeed, or connive at the vast temerity by which the Churches, the commonwealths, and men of the highest order are defamed, and are not, without weighty cause, rendered suspect of an *apostasy* now and again breaking out.

We see also the things themselves so handled by those light-fleers that their discourse *seems* to have some semblance of wisdom and of piety — but when the Lydian stone is applied, which is the ***Word of the Word of the Father, and the voice of our Lord Jesus Christ,*** the whole artifice falls to pieces which these spiders have woven, and by which they willed to entangle flies and to snare the ignorant and the secure and, for the most part, those already before infected in foreign places. As, for example, the unhappy and seditious attempt and disputation of that ***Christion*** concerning the *communication* and the *life-giving power* of the flesh of Christ — an attempt that (as he pretends) he draws from *Cyril* and from the *Council of Ephesus* — most evidently shows.¹¹ For what does that blatherer do in his own *explication* (which is more truly an *implication*) other than to *inflict manifest injury on Cyril,* in this that (to say it in few words) he affirms that eating occurs *by faith alone and by pure faith;* and that he *defames* the *Apology of the Augsburg Confession,* and *entangles the κοινωνία*¹² which Paul names *against Paul* with his own phantasms, and *pretends that Paul contradicts Christ,* and *deals Christ a slap,* and *charges our Churches* and even his own, in which he lives, with mendacities about ***consubstantiation, ubiquity, local contact and conjunction,*** all fabricated — and *confounds the sacraments,* and asserts that the *action of the Supper* is no other than that which is described in the sixth chapter of John, and *ascribes to the Fathers* things they never themselves thought, and puts forth with impudence many other things besides, which show that this man — who boasts of his faith — is lacking in faith and in all piety, and is very far from being *a pastor,* as we could otherwise, if need be, demonstrate.

**By faith alone we have learned that we are justified and saved.** But that by faith alone we hear the word, are baptised, come to the Communion and receive the body and blood of Christ, or that by faith alone we shall rise from the dead — as would necessarily follow, and as we perceive to be plainly the argument sought by the Devil — this we *deny,* as is shown elsewhere. Only for the sake of example did I wish to mention these things now, so that those who by such arts are incautiously deceived might see, and beware, lest they cast themselves and their own into God's just wrath and punishments. For "the one who does" and "the one who consents" are judged worthy of the same penalty. Certainly these words — ***Take, eat, this is my body*** — shall one day judge, confound, prostrate, and damn all their *Reformers,* and shall show that they are by no means Christians. May God clemently convert them; or, if they will not, may He, for the glory of His own name, visit them with the penalties they deserve, as an example to others — as it was done to *Berengarius, Carlstadt, Zwingli, Sylvanus,* and many others.¹³ Now to return to the matter proposed:

I in my own station have hitherto stood fast, by God's benefit, amid not a few afflictions — sickly of health, and often exposed like a little puppy to the mastiffs — I have stood fast, I say, as much as I could, with God supplying my strength, though less than I ought. **Of sound doctrine I have been zealous, and am, and shall be.** And the same things about the same subjects I have set before my own hearers, nor have I ever — praise and thanks be to God — in the least particular altered the *rule of faith and of doctrine,* which one and the same I have followed and retained always. I have received the slaps and have given thanks to God. I have striven to be useful to the Churches and the schools which I served, unto the glory and praise of God and unto the propagation of heavenly truth, and unto my own salvation and the salvation of others. Manifest errors I have contradicted, and shall contradict, **so long as I live,** whether alone or with others, ***εὐκαίρως καὶ ἀκαίρως***¹⁴ — as I say with Paul's words. And would that all tongues were tongues for asserting truly the glory of Christ, and for accusing and damning the mendacities of Satan.

**This brief commentary I have therefore composed** in such a way as to set forth faithfully the true doctrine and mind of pious, erudite, and rightly-sensing and rightly-teaching *antiquity* — the doctrine of the orthodox Church and of all the pious — concerning the highest articles of the Christian faith, those articles which in these dregs of the world Satan, in Churches, in Academies, in kingdoms and in the chief commonwealths, has brought promiscuously into bloody controversy: I have set them forth with that fidelity and that conscience wherewith I know with certainty that I shall have to appear before the tribunal of the Son of God. And this I have done the more zealously and the more of necessity because I saw the *devil* almost rent asunder and driven to madness by the very *repetition and commemoration* of the divine truth; and I saw him now even *contriving this one thing,* that the youth, infected with an infernal venom, should devote and pledge all their exertion to *Mercury* and to *Mars* — as that wise one said — that is, to *thieves* and to *robbers:* to those, on the one hand, who strive to *rob Christ, the Son of Man,* of His majesty and power and divine glory, and to shut Him up in a physical, decaying, temporal, and local order, and to *take away from Christ's words their truth;* and to those, on the other hand, who by *manifest force,* by threats, by arms, by stones, by swords, and by fire, strive to *protect* diabolical pertinacity. For each of these endeavours smacks of the *Devil,* not the Holy Spirit, as its author and instigator; each argues and betrays him; nor can it draw its origin from any other than that lying murderer from the beginning.

And it is in this way that the devil has begun in earnest to *shake the integrity of doctrine, to tear up the saving consensus, to loose and dissipate discipline* even in the very bosom of discipline, to *confirm carnal security,* to *increase scandals,* to *give licence to the most impudent and impure* to prattle scurrilously about the sacrosanct mysteries in the forum and over cups, to *introduce Epicureanism,* to *tread underfoot the study of the sacred letters,* to *render all things uncertain,* to *put God Himself on trial,* to *deny the divine truth,* and to *contrive, as it were, a new Fall of our first Parents.* And unless Christ — mindful of His majesty, glory, and truth, and of His miserable turtledoves, in whom the Holy Spirit was grieved — had here and there opened the mind of the pious magistrate, and had willed that some pious Princes should discharge the office of Theologians, which now and again seemed to be diminished, those nefarious contrivings would have followed which would altogether have killed the Church already grown decrepit.

**With my whole heart, therefore, I give thanks to the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ** that with a paternal countenance He looks clemently upon us, His little ones, in these dregs of the world and in these overturnings of things, and that He Himself is the assertor, the vindicator, and the defender of His own truth against so many and so great insults of devils, tyrants, and heretics, manifest and clandestine. For unless He Himself take care of us, defend the purity of doctrine, uncover the corruptions, confound the plots, effect the certitude of faith, confirm us, animate the pious teachers, illuminate the magistrate, bend the counsellors, move the senators, teach the hearers and the disciples, and — to say it summarily — unless He Himself be Prince, Doctor, and Father of the family, it is all over with us, together and severally, especially in so great a potency *καὶ πανουργία*¹⁵ of Satan, in the lightness of men, the cunning and stratagems of the wise, the contempt of the pious, the security of the vulgar, and the sea of all the evils of guilt and of penalty — from among which this is the most horrifying, that ***Christ has been made an act*** — over whom now there is disputing, controverting, mockery, striving, and fighting, not with the enemies of Christianity, but with those who wish to *confess* Christ and to *profess* Christ (if not seriously to strive so, then at least to *seem* to strive so). And it is to be lamented that the disputations — or rather the *vociferations,* the clamours by which, both publicly and privately, and even in colloquies and at meals, the pious are almost overwhelmed — are augmented by many, not from zeal for the truth, not from love of Christ's glory, not for the salvation of many, but from *zeal to obtain the palm and the victory of some temporary triumph,* so that it may be said, *"Behold, here he is!"*

**Considering these things,** although I have almost given up my mind and my hope of a better state of things, until the advent of Christ our Saviour who will reform all things and whom we trust to be not far from here — nevertheless, since the devil rages more and more, and the more God *lays bare his ambushes and uncovers his frauds* so that they may be recognised and avoided by all, the more this enemy rages, and drives himself into madness, and by every way, secretly and pertinaciously, from every side seeks out how to vomit forth his venom, to poison, to excuse, to paint, to colour, to peddle, and to scatter it far and wide — I therefore turned myself to that safest rule, which is: ***Sanctify us, O God, in Thy truth. Thy discourse is truth.***¹⁶

Perhaps we might be able to increase the number of disputants — and in a more convenient form and progression than many do. But I say with *Nazianzen:*

> *"Let these persist to go the way that they have begun;*
> *I myself shall follow the salvation-bearing Ark of the Patriarch —*
> *of the ancient Noah — that I may flee this present destruction."*¹⁷

The greatest part disputes *with words,* and opposes words to words, and often does not itself understand what it is saying, but with mindless applause approves what others say. And while their mouth is instructed with words, they think that a most excellent business is being done. Meanwhile the *things themselves* are neglected, indeed are not understood, and the truth is by and by lost. The fountains are not consulted; *pious antiquity* is boasted of but not known; the knowledge of *languages* is despised; *novelty* is loved; well-constructed things are torn down; well-torn-down things are ill patched up — and yet, deprived of every firm foundation, unless one is to call sand a foundation, they are left to stand. There is chanting about the name of *orthodoxy* and about the many *dissonances;* and pledges are made even with execrations and imprecations added against the falsifiers, and with attestations of the divine name — but when the matter is considered from the word of God, an insane, furious, bloody *κακοπιστία*¹⁸ is discovered, which is worse than *ἀπιστία.* O the times, O the customs, O the perfidy and pertinacity! **We deceive ourselves; we seduce many,** of whom an account will have to be rendered, as Berengarius, prince of the Sacramentarians, feared. We are wont to deceive and to lead astray the magistrate; we would deceive even God, if God could be deceived. Such is our age; and this is the penalty of our ingratitude, and of *worldly wisdom in the foolishness of God* — that is, of *our foolishness in the wisdom of God,* as I say with Paul.

We have had a most excellent age, and — with respect to the customs that now are — plainly a golden one; doctors pious, learned, constant, open, and always prepared to render a reason for the faith, without tergiversation and without disguises and buskins (of whose number may the Son of God clemently preserve the few who yet remain); hearers, too, certain of their doctrine and faith, and not applauding every spirit; patrons likewise equally Christian, not superficially, in passing, coldly, ridiculously, and lightly zealous and promoters of the glory and truth of Christ, but themselves also ***εἰς ἐξέτασιν ἴοντας, παρατάτας***¹⁹ indefatigable, and certain about doctrine — whose authority was able to *repress* the enemies, the heretics, the Epicureans — that authority given, forsooth, of God. But today we deservedly lament the *widowhood* of the Church and of the pious. And unless a remnant seed had remained, we should long ago have perished. For as we care for the word of God, so are we in turn cared for by God. And little by little there are being suborned *heresies and corruptions of doctrine* from *philosophy* — which, in these evil machinations against the word of God, is truly, as Clement of Alexandria judges, ***a theft of the devil.***²⁰ These are the doctrines of men and of demons, born of the itching of the ears from the ingenuity of *secular wisdom.* Under the skins of sheep are ravening wolves, and adulterous evangelisers — to use Tertullian's words. And so little by little it will come to pass that we shall *lose the word;* and having lost the word, we shall lose *God Himself.*

**I will not here bring forward anything by name.** For things manifest and everywhere notorious need no explanation. I recall these things in general, however, to stir up myself and others to the consideration of the miserable state of us — in which, according to the prophet, even the wise man must be silent²¹ — and to *assiduous prayer:* **Abide with us, Christ, for it draws toward evening.**²²

To Your Generosity, then, illustrious Count, I have wished for many causes, and with a humble and subject and altogether that spirit which has long been more known and observed to Your Clemency than perhaps to others, to dedicate this little labour of mine — of which causes this is the great and chief one, that I know and can testify that Your Clemency, together with your brothers, my clement Lords, is a lover, a cherisher, and a most zealous adherent of the *pure heavenly doctrine* — which has hitherto sounded in these Meissen and Saxon Churches and schools by the voice and writings of *Luther,* by God's benefit, restored, and still sounds, both here and in Your Clemency's dominions, and besides in the Kingdom of Denmark and of Sweden, in Lower Saxony, in the Duchy of Braunschweig, of Lüneburg, of Mecklenburg, of Pomerania, in the County of Oldenburg, in the chief commonwealths of Lübeck, Hamburg, Braunschweig, Lüneburg, Danzig, Hildesheim, Goslar, and in the Swabian and Württemberg Churches, and in the Electoral Margravate and in all the regions of the Brandenburg lineage, and in the Anhalt Churches, in Silesia, and elsewhere — I name only the Churches known to me — not without a peculiar governance and blessing of God it sounds. And I know that this same Your Clemency abhors from all *fanatical opinions,* old and recent, manifest and clandestine, whether Anabaptist, or Schwenckfeldian, Sacramentarian, or any other deliria, by whatever name they be called. For by no means ought we to *neglect* this saving consensus, or to make light of it, especially since we see those who *depart from this consensus* being the authors of ecclesiastical and political tumults, and distracting the minds of Princes and magistrates, and with great and yet by no means necessary evil, petulantly and treacherously *alienating them from one another,* and *involving universal doctrine in thorny disputations,* and *rendering it suspect to secure magnates and to the wavering multitude* — of which matter, if anyone else can bring some certain information, I can (be there no envy in the saying) truly affirm myself to be one such, as one who has been an *eye-witness* of many actions in the Sacramentarian and other controversies hitherto, and, for these five years now, being pressed by scarcely any other of the doctors — save by those addicted to that sect — have patiently borne these and other things (let me be allowed to say what is true). But let these matters be, and rest quiet, until God ***ὁ καρδιογνώστης***²³ shall openly show Himself to be *even yet on earth* the *judge.* Certainly from those and other Churches sincerely agreeing to the Augsburg Confession — of which I have made mention — we shall not separate ourselves, since no cause underlies for which any *distraction* were needed. Let others extol within private walls their own private quietude *with themselves,* the public consensus being repudiated, without which neither Churches nor commonwealths can subsist. Our mind is not this — we who rather desire to *redeem the public concord* framed after the norm of the divine word, and recovered even by our own blood, if need be. Nor is it to be thought that we are alone, who when we are alone are wise, and to whom it is permitted to *change, bend, and re-bend* the best-handed-down things, and to *disturb and reject temerariously the public consensus* which our Churches and Schools have always faithfully cherished with other Churches, whose chief ones I have named, against the enemies of true doctrine. The matter stands far otherwise; another thing altogether is demanded — as they can testify who have everywhere seen other Churches in Germany (for it is chiefly of these that I am speaking), have heard those Churches rightly teaching, have taken part in not a few actions, are not ignorant of the minds and complaints of Princes, and have known the *groans and tears of the pious longing for saving concord.*

What madness therefore is it, what malice, what insanity and blindness, to be willing to separate oneself from those Churches which have been purged again by the voice and labour of the *great Luther,* with which the consensus was pleasant, bringing with it spiritual and corporeal blessing — and to *revolt to those* whom our Churches long since have convicted of *manifest errors, indeed of blasphemies,* and have striven in vain to bring back to a saner mind? Let all be wise, then, be they powerful, be they the most erudite and most humane of all men, be they seen as most holy, excelling in gifts, most elegant in speech, purest in writing, strong in memory, in the highest grace and dignity — this is not yet cause enough that we should pledge to them our faith, wherewith we must appear before the tribunal of the Son of God. For to the devil also we can attribute these gifts and virtues in abundance — power, erudition, experience, and the like, indeed a transformation into an angel of light. We do indeed make much of gifts and virtues, we love and venerate the erudite, and we submit ourselves to them — but no further than as they faithfully serve Christ and lay down their gifts suppliantly at the knees of Christ. If they do not do this, they are not to be so esteemed (why judge otherwise?) that even a cobbler or a fisherman who is imbued with the right faith should yield to them, or care in the least for their *subtle vanities and vain subtleties,* or (as that *Gymnosophist* said to Alexander)²⁴ their *doubting doubts,* their *Academical suspensions,* or, as the Scripture speaks, the *Pharisaic διαλογισμοί* — even if they impute to our faith and doctrine ***παράδοξα, ἄτοπα, ἀπίθανα, καὶ ἀδύνατα***²⁵ — which *παράδοξα* we know to be named such *by the Holy Spirit Himself* in the sacred letters, and yet to be *true,* and indeed *truer* than the truth of all angels and of all men — to say nothing of the wisdom and reason either of Plato or of Aristotle, whom, were they now yet surviving, these people would perhaps have for their *synergoi* and applauders, the Apostles being excluded and Christ Himself being banished. But surely even a little boy reciting his own Catechism, when he says ***Thus saith the LORD,*** has more *truth,* and it is *firmer and more certain,* than are all the cathedra, subtleties, necessities, demonstrations, maxims, ratiocinations, syllogisms, consequences, axioms, laws, allegations, and rules of all the philosophers and scholastic doctors and wise men of the whole world *outside the word of GOD.* He who does not so hold is plainly unworthy of the *Christian assembly* and of the name of a *Christian man.* But that Your Clemency, and the Christian Churches — which they call *Lutheran* — and all others who are not *publicans and heathens* do so hold, this I know with certainty. And therefore for this cause, as I have said, I wished to send this little book of mine to Your Clemency, whose doctrine and mind I know to *agree* with the *public and orthodox confession* sounding in these Churches and in others more pure.

Next, I wish to give at the same time some *signification of gratitude* by this means. Your Clemency was solicitous concerning me and my state. And from the time when I departed from Your Clemency, returning from the County of Oldenburg, although many days were to me as stepmothers, nevertheless there consoled me the *goodness of the cause,* and the reasoning of my *office,* and the *integrity of conscience,* and the *uncertainty of my adversaries,* and the *benign writings of certain Princes,* and Your Clemency's *solicitude and truly paternal care* concerning me and on my behalf — by which, when you sent to me most excellent men who inquired and beheld how I fared and in what state I was engaged, and who at the same time brought me things of which I acknowledge myself unworthy, you declared your clement mind toward me.²⁶ For which benefit I confess that I ought to be grateful. But what shall I render to the Lord for these things which He has rendered to me?

I bring the little book, which I beseech Your generous Clemency to receive with serene countenance and benign mind. And I pray ***GOD*** the Father of our Lord ***JESUS CHRIST*** that He may govern, protect, preserve, and confirm our *magistrate* — a most-loving lover of heavenly truth, most-constant in a sincere confession of doctrine — Elector *Augustus,* and the Electoral consort, and the whole line; and Your most Generous Clemency, your illustrious consort, your generous children, and your brothers, my clement Lords; and the pious doctors, counsellors, hearers, and best of disciples; and may keep us all and singly safe and unharmed from every contamination and venom in the truth once acknowledged, and in the writings and labours of ***Luther*** of holy memory, and in the *Body of Doctrine* — the *Augsburg Confession* standing, the *Apology,* the *Catechisms of Luther,* and the *Smalcald Articles* being asserted and explained — and may make us one in Himself, and so at length lead us to the harbour, that we may be able to *stand before the Son of Man.* Amen.

**Leipzig, in the month of April,** ***cIɔIɔ.LXXV.***²⁷

# The Three Creeds and Their Ancient Accompaniments

*(Selnecker prints the three ancient Creeds at the head of the volume — that is, before the running Enarratio begins — as the very text on which the entire exposition will turn. He follows them immediately with a short cluster of related ancient formulae: Basil's shorter rendering of the Nicene article, Epiphanius' Nicene-Constantinopolitan expansion against the heresies, the Chalcedonian Definition, and two Christological confessions of the Emperor Justinian. He closes the cluster with two Psalms and his own Latin distich-versification of Psalm 124, dated 1573. In the English rendering that follows, the Latin as printed in the 1577 imprint is presented first, and the English rendering follows immediately, with the numbering as Selnecker gives it — that is, on the Psalms, in the Vulgate numbering, with the Hebrew/English number noted at the head.)*

## The Apostles' Creed — *Symbolum Apostolicum*

> **CREDO in Deum, Patrem omnipotentem, Creatorem caeli et terrae. Et in Iesum Christum, Filium eius unicum, Dominum nostrum. Qui conceptus est de Spiritu Sancto, natus ex Maria virgine, passus sub Pontio Pilato, crucifixus, mortuus et sepultus; descendit ad inferna; tertia die resurrexit à mortuis; ascendit ad caelos; sedet ad dexteram Dei Patris omnipotentis; inde venturus est iudicare vivos et mortuos. Credo in Spiritum Sanctum; sanctam Ecclesiam Catholicam; sanctorum communionem; remissionem peccatorum; carnis resurrectionem; et vitam aeternam. Amen.**

**I believe in God, the Father almighty, Creator of heaven and earth. And in Jesus Christ, His only Son, our Lord. Who was conceived by the Holy Spirit, born of the Virgin Mary, suffered under Pontius Pilate, was crucified, dead, and buried; He descended into hell; on the third day He rose again from the dead; He ascended into heaven; He sits at the right hand of God the Father almighty; from thence He shall come to judge the living and the dead. I believe in the Holy Spirit; the holy Catholic Church; the communion of saints; the forgiveness of sins; the resurrection of the flesh; and life everlasting. Amen.**

## The Nicene Creed — *Symbolum Nicenum* *(that is, the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed of 381, with the Filioque interpolation as received in the Western Church)*

> **Credo in unum Deum, Patrem omnipotentem, factorem caeli et terrae, visibilium omnium et invisibilium. Et in unum Dominum Iesum Christum, Filium Dei unigenitum, et ex Patre natum ante omnia saecula: Deum de Deo, Lumen de lumine, Deum verum de Deo vero: genitum non factum, consubstantialem Patri, per quem omnia facta sunt. Qui propter nos homines et propter nostram salutem descendit de caelis, et incarnatus est de Spiritu Sancto ex Maria Virgine: et homo factus est. Crucifixus etiam pro nobis sub Pontio Pilato: passus et sepultus est: et resurrexit tertia die secundum Scripturas: et ascendit ad caelos, sedet ad dexteram Patris: et iterum venturus est in gloria, iudicare vivos et mortuos, cuius regni non erit finis. Et in Spiritum Sanctum, Dominum et vivificantem: qui ex Patre Filioque procedit: qui cum Patre et Filio simul adoratur et glorificatur: qui locutus est per Prophetas. Et unam sanctam Catholicam et Apostolicam Ecclesiam. Confiteor unum Baptisma in remissionem peccatorum: et expecto resurrectionem mortuorum, et vitam venturi saeculi. Amen.**

**I believe in one God, the Father almighty, maker of heaven and earth, of all things visible and invisible. And in one Lord Jesus Christ, the only-begotten Son of God, born of the Father before all ages: God of God, Light of light, true God of true God: begotten not made, consubstantial with the Father, through whom all things were made. Who for us men, and for our salvation, came down from heaven, and was incarnate by the Holy Spirit of the Virgin Mary, and was made man. He was crucified also for us under Pontius Pilate; He suffered and was buried; and rose again the third day according to the Scriptures; and ascended into heaven; He sits at the right hand of the Father; and He shall come again with glory to judge the living and the dead, of whose kingdom there shall be no end. And in the Holy Spirit, the Lord and giver of life, who proceeds from the Father *and the Son;* who together with the Father and the Son is adored and glorified; who spoke by the Prophets. And in one holy Catholic and Apostolic Church. I confess one Baptism for the remission of sins; and I look for the resurrection of the dead, and the life of the world to come. Amen.**

## The Athanasian Creed — *Symbolum Athanasii · Quicunque vult*

> **Quicunque vult salvus esse, ante omnia opus est ut teneat Catholicam fidem. Quam nisi quisquis integram inviolatamque servaverit, absque dubio in aeternum peribit.**

**1.** Whosoever will be saved, before all things it is necessary that he hold the Catholic faith.

**2.** Which faith unless everyone shall have kept whole and undefiled, without doubt he shall perish everlastingly.

> **Fides autem Catholica haec est, ut unum Deum in Trinitate, et Trinitatem in Unitate veneremur. Neque confundentes personas, neque substantiam separantes. Alia est enim persona Patris, alia Filij, alia Spiritus Sancti. Sed Patris et Filij et Spiritus Sancti una est Divinitas, aequalis gloria, coaeterna maiestas.**

**3.** And the Catholic faith is this: That we worship one God in Trinity, and Trinity in Unity;

**4.** Neither confounding the persons, nor dividing the substance.

**5.** For there is one Person of the Father, another of the Son, and another of the Holy Spirit.

**6.** But the Godhead of the Father, of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit is all one, the glory equal, the majesty coeternal.

> **Qualis Pater, talis Filius, talis Spiritus Sanctus. Increatus Pater, increatus Filius, increatus Spiritus Sanctus. Immensus Pater, immensus Filius, immensus Spiritus Sanctus. Aeternus Pater, aeternus Filius, aeternus Spiritus Sanctus. Et tamen non tres aeterni, sed unus aeternus. Sicut non tres increati, nec tres immensi: sed unus increatus, et unus immensus.**

**7.** Such as the Father is, such is the Son, and such is the Holy Spirit.

**8.** The Father uncreate, the Son uncreate, and the Holy Spirit uncreate.

**9.** The Father unmeasurable, the Son unmeasurable, and the Holy Spirit unmeasurable.

**10.** The Father eternal, the Son eternal, and the Holy Spirit eternal.

**11.** And yet they are not three eternals, but one eternal.

**12.** As also there are not three uncreated, nor three unmeasurable, but one uncreated, and one unmeasurable.

> **Similiter omnipotens Pater, omnipotens Filius, omnipotens Spiritus Sanctus. Et tamen non tres omnipotentes, sed unus omnipotens. Ita Deus Pater, Deus Filius, Deus Spiritus Sanctus. Et tamen non tres Dij sunt: sed unus est Deus. Ita Dominus Pater, Dominus Filius, Dominus Spiritus Sanctus. Et tamen non tres Domini, sed unus est Dominus.**

**13.** So likewise the Father is almighty, the Son almighty, and the Holy Spirit almighty.

**14.** And yet they are not three almighties, but one almighty.

**15.** So the Father is God, the Son is God, and the Holy Spirit is God.

**16.** And yet they are not three Gods, but one God.

**17.** So likewise the Father is Lord, the Son Lord, and the Holy Spirit Lord.

**18.** And yet they are not three Lords, but one Lord.

> **Quia sicut singulatim unamquamque personam Deum ac Dominum confiteri, Christiana veritate compellimur: Ita tres Deos, aut tres Dominos dicere, Catholica religione prohibemur.**

**19.** For like as we are compelled by the Christian truth to acknowledge every Person by Himself to be God and Lord;

**20.** So are we forbidden by the Catholic religion to say that there are three Gods or three Lords.

> **Pater à nullo est factus, nec creatus, nec genitus. Filius à Patre solo est: non factus, nec creatus, sed genitus. Spiritus Sanctus à Patre et Filio: non factus, nec creatus, nec genitus: sed procedens.**

**21.** The Father is made of none, neither created, nor begotten.

**22.** The Son is of the Father alone; not made, nor created, but begotten.

**23.** The Holy Spirit is of the Father and of the Son; not made, nor created, nor begotten, but proceeding.

> **Unus ergo Pater, non tres Patres: unus Filius, non tres Filij: unus Spiritus Sanctus, non tres Spiritus Sancti. Et in hac Trinitate nihil prius aut posterius, nihil maius aut minus. Sed totae tres personae, coaeternae sibi sunt et coaequales, ita ut per omnia, sicut iam supra dictum est, et Trinitas in Unitate, et Unitas in Trinitate veneranda sit.**

**24.** So there is one Father, not three Fathers; one Son, not three Sons; one Holy Spirit, not three Holy Spirits.

**25.** And in this Trinity none is before or after another; none is greater or less than another;

**26.** But the whole three Persons are coeternal together and coequal, so that in all things, as is aforesaid, both the Trinity in Unity and the Unity in Trinity is to be worshipped.

> **Qui vult ergo salvus esse, ita de Trinitate sentiat. Sed necessarium est ad aeternam salutem, ut incarnationem quoque Domini nostri Iesu Christi fideliter credat. Est ergo fides recta, ut credamus et confiteamur, quia Dominus noster Iesus Christus Dei Filius, Deus et homo est.**

**27.** He therefore that will be saved must think thus of the Trinity.

**28.** Furthermore, it is necessary to everlasting salvation that he also believe rightly the incarnation of our Lord Jesus Christ.

**29.** For the right faith is that we believe and confess that our Lord Jesus Christ, the Son of God, is God and man.

> **Deus ex substantia Patris ante saecula genitus, et homo ex substantia matris, in saeculo natus. Perfectus Deus, perfectus homo ex anima rationali, et humana carne subsistens. Aequalis Patri secundum divinitatem, minor Patre secundum humanitatem. Qui licet Deus sit et homo, non duo tamen, sed unus est Christus. Unus autem non conversione divinitatis in carnem, sed assumptione humanitatis in Deum. Unus omnino, non confusione substantiae, sed unitate personae. Nam sicut anima rationalis et caro unus est homo: ita Deus et homo unus est Christus.**

**30.** God of the substance of the Father, begotten before the ages; and man of the substance of His Mother, born in time.

**31.** Perfect God and perfect man, subsisting of a reasonable soul and human flesh.

**32.** Equal to the Father as touching His Godhead, and inferior to the Father as touching His manhood.

**33.** Who although He is God and man, yet He is not two, but one Christ.

**34.** One, however, not by conversion of the Divinity into flesh, but by the assumption of the manhood into God.

**35.** One altogether; not by confusion of substance, but by unity of person.

**36.** For as the reasonable soul and flesh are one man, so God and man are one Christ.

> **Qui passus est pro nostra salute: descendit ad inferos: tertia die resurrexit à mortuis. Ascendit ad caelos: sedet ad dexteram Dei Patris omnipotentis. Inde venturus est iudicare vivos et mortuos. Ad cuius adventum, omnes homines resurgere habent cum corporibus suis. Et reddituri sunt de factis proprijs rationem. Et qui bona egerunt, ibunt in vitam aeternam: qui vero mala, in ignem aeternum. Haec est fides Catholica, quam nisi quisque fideliter firmiterque crediderit, salvus esse non poterit.**

**37.** Who suffered for our salvation; descended into hell; on the third day rose again from the dead.

**38.** He ascended into heaven; He sits at the right hand of God the Father almighty.

**39.** From thence He shall come to judge the living and the dead.

**40.** At whose coming all men shall rise again with their bodies, and shall give an account of their own works.

**41.** And they that have done good shall go into life everlasting; and they that have done evil, into everlasting fire.

**42.** This is the Catholic faith, which except a man believe faithfully and firmly, he cannot be saved.

## Editorial Note

> ***Haec tria Symbola sunt Catholica et Oecumenica. Basilius autem Nicenum Symbolum recitat aliquo modo brevius sic:***

*These three Creeds are Catholic and Ecumenical. Basil, however, recites the Nicene Creed somewhat more briefly, thus:*

## Basil's Shorter Rendering of the Nicene Creed

> **Credimus in unum DEVM, Patrem omnipotentem, omnium visibilium et invisibilium factorem. Et in unum Dominum nostrum Iesum Christum, Filium Dei, genitum ex Patre, unigenitum — id est, ex substantia Patris. Deum de Deo, Lumen ex Lumine, Deum verum ex Deo vero, consubstantialem Patri, per quem omnia facta sunt in caelo et in terra: qui propter nos homines et propter nostram salutem descendit, et carnem assumsit, homo factus, passus est, et resurrexit tertia die. Ascendit in caelos, venturus est iudicatum vivos et mortuos. Et in Spiritum Sanctum et cetera.**

**We believe in one God, the Father almighty, maker of all things visible and invisible. And in one Lord of ours, Jesus Christ, the Son of God, begotten of the Father, only-begotten — that is, of the substance of the Father. God of God, Light of Light, true God of true God, consubstantial with the Father, through whom all things were made in heaven and on earth; who for us men, and for our salvation, came down, and took flesh, was made man, suffered, and rose again on the third day. He ascended into heaven, is to come to judge the living and the dead. And in the Holy Spirit,** *and so on.*

## Epiphanius' Nicene-Constantinopolitan Symbol against the Heresies

*Selnecker notes: "Epiphanius on account of the heresies commemorates these additions."*

> **Incarnatus est — id est, natus perfectè ex sancta Maria semper virgine per Spiritum sanctum. Et Homo factus est — id est, qui perfectum hominem assumsit, animam et corpus et mentem, et omnia, si quid est homo, praeter peccatum. Non ab hominis semine, neque in homine, sed in seipsum carne conflata in unam sanctam unitatem. Non quemadmodum in Prophetis inspiravit et locutus est et efficax fuit, sed qui perfectè homo factus est. Nam verbum Caro factum est, non sustinens mutationem, neque mutata divinitate ipsius in humanitatem, qui in unam suam univit sanctam perfectionem et divinitatem. Vnus est enim Dominus Iesus Christus, et non duo. Idem Deus, idem Dominus, idem Rex. Qui idem est passus in Carne, et resurrexit, et ascendit in caelos ipso corpore in gloria, ut iudicet viventes et mortuos: cuius regni non erit finis.**

**He was incarnate** — that is, He was born perfectly of holy Mary ever-virgin by the Holy Spirit. **And He was made man** — that is, He who assumed a perfect man: a soul and a body and a mind, and all things, if anything is a man, apart from sin. Not from the seed of a man, nor *in* a man, but *into Himself,* flesh conjoined into one holy unity. Not in the manner in which He inspired the Prophets and spoke and was efficacious in them, but as one who was made perfectly man. For **the Word was made Flesh,** not undergoing a mutation, nor with His divinity itself changed into humanity — who has united into His own one holy perfection and divinity. For there is **one Lord Jesus Christ, and not two.** The same God, the same Lord, the same King. Who Himself has suffered in the flesh, and rose again, and ascended into the heavens in that very body in glory, that He may judge the living and the dead — *of whose kingdom there shall be no end.*

> **Credimus etiam in Spiritum Sanctum, qui locutus est in Lege, et praedicavit in Prophetis, et descendit ad Iordanem, loquens in Apostolis, habitans in sanctis. Sic autem credimus in ipso, quod sit Spiritus Sanctus, Spiritus Dei, spiritus perfectus, spiritus advocatus et consolator: non creatus, ex Patre et Filio procedens. Et credimus in unam Catholicam et Apostolicam Ecclesiam et cetera.**

**We believe also in the Holy Spirit, who spoke in the Law, and preached in the Prophets, and descended to the Jordan, speaking in the Apostles, dwelling in the holy ones. Thus, however, we believe in Him: that He is the Holy Spirit, the Spirit of God, a perfect Spirit, the Spirit Advocate and Consoler; not created; proceeding from the Father and the Son. And we believe in one Catholic and Apostolic Church,** *and so on.*

## The Symbol of the Ecumenical Synod of Chalcedon

> **Confitemur unum atque eundem esse Filium Dominum nostrum Iesum Christum, eundem perfectum in divinitate, eundem perfectum in humanitate, Deum verum et hominem verum, eundem ex rationali anima et corpore, consubstantialem Patri secundum divinitatem, consubstantialem eundem nobis secundum humanitatem, per omnia nobis aequalem absque peccato, ante saecula quidem ex Patre secundum divinitatem, in extremis autem diebus eundem propter nos et propter nostram salutem ex Maria Virgine et Dei Genitrice secundum humanitatem genitum, unum et eundem Iesum Christum Filium et Dominum unigenitum in duabus naturis inconfusè, immutabiliter, indivisè, insegregabiliter cognitum atque depraedicatum: haud quaquam naturarum differentiam unione tollente, sed proprietatem potius utriusque naturae conservante, quae in personam et subsistentiam unam concurrit: non veluti in duas personas dispertitum aut divisum, sed unum et eundem Filium unigenitum Deum verbum Dominum nostrum Iesum Christum, quemadmodum antiquitus Prophetae, et ipse de seipso nos docuit Christus, et Symbolum Patrum nobis tradidit. Qui aliter docuerint, anathema sint. Haec fides Apostolorum. Haec fides Patrum. Huic omnes consentimus. Omnes ita sapimus. Haec manifesta fiant divino vertici.**

**We confess** that **one and the same is the Son, our Lord Jesus Christ:** the same *perfect in divinity,* the same *perfect in humanity,* true God and true man, the same *of a rational soul and body,* consubstantial with the Father as touching the divinity, and consubstantial with us as touching the humanity, **in all things like unto us, apart from sin;** before the ages indeed begotten of the Father according to the divinity, but in the last days the same one, for our sake and for our salvation, born of Mary the Virgin and Bearer of God according to the humanity: **one and the same Christ, Son, Lord, only-begotten, made known in two natures without confusion, without change, without division, without separation** — the difference of the natures by no means being taken away by the union, but rather the property of each nature being preserved, which together *concur into one Person and one subsistence:* not as though parted or divided into two Persons, but *one and the same only-begotten Son, God the Word, our Lord Jesus Christ* — even as the Prophets from of old, and Christ Himself has taught us of Himself, and the *Symbol of the Fathers* has handed down to us.

**Those who teach otherwise, let them be anathema.** This is the faith of the Apostles. This is the faith of the Fathers. To this we all consent. Thus do we all judge. Let these things be made manifest to the divine head.

## The Confession of the Emperor Justinian

> **Cum recta et inculpata Religio, quam profitetur et praedicat sancta Dei Catholica et Apostolica Ecclesia, nullo modo innovationem admittat: Nos doctrinam Sanctorum Apostolorum, et eorum, qui post illos in Sanctis Dei Ecclesijs versati sunt, sequentes, aequum esse existimavimus, notam et testatam omnibus spem, quae in nobis est, relinquere, traditioni et confessioni sanctae Dei et Catholicae Ecclesiae adhaerentes.**

Since the right and unblameable Religion, which the holy Catholic and Apostolic Church of God professes and preaches, in no way admits innovation, **We,** following the doctrine of the holy Apostles, and of those who after them were engaged in the holy Churches of God, have judged it equitable to leave *known and attested to all* the hope that is in us — adhering to the tradition and confession of the holy Catholic Church of God.

> **Credentes enim in Patrem et Filium et Spiritum Sanctum, unam essentiam in tribus personis adoramus, unam divinitatem, unam potentiam, Trinitatem consubstantialem. In extremis autem diebus confitemur Dei unigenitum Dei Filium ex Deo Deum, ante saecula et sine tempore ex Patre genitum, coaeternum Patri, ex quo omnia et per quem omnia: descendisse de caelis, et incarnatum ex Spiritu sancto, et ex beata atque gloriosa semper virgine Maria, hominem factum, crucifixum, sepultum esse, et die tertio resurrexisse: unius et eiusdem miracula et passiones, quas sponte in carne sustinuit, agnoscentes.**

For believing in the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, we adore *one essence in three persons, one divinity, one power, a consubstantial Trinity.* But in the last days we confess that **the only-begotten of God, the Son of God, God of God, before the ages and outside of time begotten of the Father, coeternal with the Father, of whom are all things and through whom are all things, descended from the heavens, and was incarnate of the Holy Spirit and of the blessed and ever-glorious Virgin Mary, was made man, was crucified, was buried, and on the third day rose again** — acknowledging *the miracles and the sufferings of one and the same, which He willingly bore in the flesh.*

> **Non enim alium Deum verbum, et alium Christum cognoscimus, sed unum et eundem, consubstantialem Patri secundum divinitatem, eundemque consubstantialem nobis secundum humanitatem. Trinitas enim permansit Trinitas, etiam incarnato uno è Trinitate Dei verbo. Neque enim quartae personae accessionem Sancta Trinitas admittit.**

For we do **not** acknowledge one God-the-Word, and another Christ, but **one and the same:** consubstantial with the Father according to the divinity, and consubstantial with us according to the humanity. **For the Trinity has remained a Trinity, even when one of the Trinity, God-the-Word, was incarnate.** For the Holy Trinity does not admit the accession of a fourth person.

> **Quae cum ita se habeant, anathemate et execratione summa omnem haeresin condemnamus: tum verò vel maximè Nestorium ἀνθρωπολάτριν, id est, hominis cultorem et adoratorem, qui unum Dominum nostrum Iesum Christum Filium Dei et Deum nostrum dividit atque distrahit: neque proprièet convenienter veritati sanctam, gloriosam, semperque virginem Mariam Deiparam confitetur: sed alium quidem Deum, Verbum ex Deo Patre appellantem, alium autem eum qui natus sit ex sancta semper virgine Maria, qui secundum gratiam et familiaritatem ad Deum verbum Deus factus sit.**

Which things being so, **with anathema and the highest execration we condemn every heresy** — and then indeed most especially ***Nestorius the ἀνθρωπολάτρις,*** that is, the *cultivator and adorer of a man,* who *divides and tears apart* the one Lord of ours, Jesus Christ, Son of God and our God: nor does he confess in a manner properly and conveniently agreeing with the truth the holy, glorious, and ever-virgin Mary as ***Deipara*** — but calls one *Deus,* God-the-Word out of God the Father, and another *him who was born of the holy ever-virgin Mary,* who according to *grace and familiarity* toward God-the-Word was made a *deus.*

> **Nec non amentem illum Eutychetem, vanas quasdam opiniones inducentem, et negantem ex sancta semper virgine et Deipara Maria veram incarnationem, hoc est, salutem nostram: neque omninò confitentem consubstantialem Patri secundum Divinitatem, eundemque consubstantialem nobis secundum humanitatem.**

Also **that senseless Eutyches,** introducing certain vain opinions and denying the true incarnation from the holy ever-virgin and *Deipara* Mary — that is, denying our salvation — and not at all confessing the same as consubstantial with the Father according to divinity, and consubstantial with us according to humanity.

> **Item, Apollinarium illum ψυχοφθόρον, id est, animam humanam Christo adimentem, et dicentem Dominum nostrum Iesum Christum Filium Dei et Deum nostrum mentis humanae expertem esse: et confusionem aut commixtionem in humanationi Filij Dei introducentem.**

Likewise **that Apollinaris the *ψυχοφθόρος,*** *the soul-corrupter,* that is, one *taking away the human soul from Christ,* and *saying that our Lord Jesus Christ, the Son of God and our God, is destitute of a human mind* — and one *introducing a confusion or commixture into the humanation of the Son of God.*

> **Denique omnes eos qui illorum opinionem secuti sunt aut adhuc sequendam putant. Quod si enim post hanc nostram constitutionem et plenissimam beatissimorum Episcoporum, qui in nostris regionibus constituti sunt, denunciationem, in posterum reperti fuerint contrariam opinionem sectantes, ne expectent ut digni venia iudicentur. Iubemus enim ut homines eiusmodi tanquam convicti et aperti haeretici iustae et idoneae animadversioni subijciantur.**

Finally **all those who have followed the opinion of those men, or who yet think that it is to be followed.** For if after this our constitution, and after the fullest denunciation of the most-blessed Bishops who are constituted in our regions, they shall hereafter be found pursuing the contrary opinion, let them not expect to be judged worthy of pardon. **For we command that men of this kind, as convicted and open heretics, be subjected to just and suitable animadversion.**

## The Edict of the Same Emperor to the People of Constantinople

> **Servatorem et Dominum omnium Iesum Christum verum Deum nostrum venerantes, per omnia studemus, quo ad eius assequi menti humanae datum est, ipsius humilitatem imitari.**

Venerating **the Saviour and Lord of all, Jesus Christ our true God,** we strive in all things (so far as it is given to the human mind to attain) *to imitate His humility.*

> **Cum enim nonnullos morbo atque insania Nestorij et Eutychetis, hominum impiorum, quique Deo et sanctae Catholicae et Apostolicae Ecclesiae bellum indixerunt, corruptos esse comperissemus, recusantes beatam gloriosam semperque virginem Mariam propriè et secundum veritatem Deiparam appellare, studiosè operam dedimus ut ad rectam Christianorum religionem pervenirent. Verum illi insanabiles, errorem suum occultantes, passim huc illuc vagantur (ut accepimus) simplicium et rudium animos conturbantes, atque in offensionem inducentes, et contraria Catholicae et Apostolicae Ecclesiae docentes.**

For since we have found that not a few have been corrupted by the sickness and madness of *Nestorius* and *Eutyches* — impious men who declared war on God and on the holy Catholic and Apostolic Church, refusing to call the blessed, glorious, and ever-virgin Mary properly and according to the truth ***Deipara*** — we have zealously given our labour that they might come to the right Religion of the Christians. But those *insanable* ones, hiding their error, wander here and there indiscriminately (as we have heard), disturbing the minds of the simple and the rude, and drawing them into offence, and teaching things contrary to the Catholic and Apostolic Church.

> **Necessarium igitur existimamus, mendacium et vanitatem haereticorum evellere: omnibus autem notum ac testatum relinquere, quae sit sancta Dei Catholica et Apostolica Ecclesiae de religione sententia, et quid sanctissimi sacerdotes annuncient et profiteantur. Quorum quidem et nos vestigijs inhaerentes, eam quae in nobis est spem exponimus, non innovantes religionem (quod Deus avertat), sed furorem eorum qui cum impijs haereticis consentiunt condemnantes: quod ipsum iam inde ab initio Imperij nostri notum atque manifestum fecimus.**

We therefore judge it necessary **to root up the lying and vanity of the heretics,** and to leave known and attested to all what is the *sentence of the holy Catholic and Apostolic Church of God concerning religion,* and what the most holy priests announce and profess. In whose very footsteps we too are treading, and set forth the hope that is in us — *not innovating* the religion (which God avert), but condemning the fury of those who consent with impious heretics — which very thing we have already made known and manifest from the beginning of our Empire.

> **Credimus enim in unum Deum, Patrem omnipotentem, et in unum Dominum Iesum Christum Filium Dei, et in Spiritum Sanctum: unam essentiam in tribus personis adorantes, unam divinitatem, unam potentiam, Trinitatem coessentialem.**

For we believe in one God, the Father almighty, and in one Lord Jesus Christ the Son of God, and in the Holy Spirit: adoring **one essence in three persons, one divinity, one power, a coessential Trinity.**

> **Postremis autem diebus confitemur Dominum nostrum Iesum Christum, Filium Dei unigenitum, Deum verum ex Deo vero, ante saecula et sine tempore, ex Patre natum, coaeternum Patri, ex quo omnia et per quem omnia, descendisse de caelis, incarnatum de Spiritu Sancto et ex sancta, gloriosa semperque virgine Maria, Hominem factum, et crucifixum esse pro nobis sub Pontio Pilato, sepultum, et die tertio resurrexisse: unius et eiusdem tum miracula, tum supplicia, quae sponte in carne sustinuit, agnoscentes.**

But in the last days we confess our Lord Jesus Christ, the only-begotten Son of God, true God of true God, before the ages and outside of time begotten of the Father, coeternal with the Father, of whom are all things and through whom are all things, **to have descended from the heavens, to have been incarnate of the Holy Spirit and of the holy, glorious, and ever-virgin Mary, to have been made Man, and to have been crucified for us under Pontius Pilate, to have been buried, and to have risen again on the third day** — acknowledging the *miracles* as well as the *sufferings of one and the same, which He willingly bore in the flesh.*

> **Non enim alium Deum verbum et alium Christum agnoscimus, sed unum et eundem, consubstantialem Patri secundum divinitatem, et consubstantialem nobis secundum humanitatem. Nam sicut in divinitate perfectus est, ita et in humanitate perfectus est. Etenim eam quae secundum hypostasin est unionem admittimus, ac confitemur, siquidem Trinitas permansit Trinitas, etiam uno è Trinitate, Deo scilicet Verbo, carnem assumente. Neque enim quartae personae accessionem sancta Trinitas admittit.**

For we do **not** acknowledge one God-the-Word and another Christ, but **one and the same:** consubstantial with the Father according to divinity, and consubstantial with us according to humanity. For as He is perfect in the divinity, so is He perfect also in the humanity. For we admit and confess **that union which is according to the *hypostasis;*** the Trinity, that is, has remained a Trinity, even when one of the Trinity — God the Word, that is — assumed flesh. For the Holy Trinity does not admit the accession of a fourth person.

> **Quae cum ita se habeant, anathemate omnem haeresin condemnamus, praesertim autem eam quae est Nestorij illius ἀνθρωπολάτρου, id est, colentis et adorantis hominem: nec non eos qui eadem cum ipso senserunt aut sentiunt, qui distrahunt unum Dominum nostrum Iesum Christum Filium Dei et Deum nostrum. Neque confitentur propriè et secundum veritatem sanctam, gloriosam, et semper virginem Mariam Deiparam, id est, Matrem Dei, sed duos esse affirmant filios, alium quidem ex Patre Deum Verbum, alium verò ex sancta semper virgine Deipara Maria, qui secundum gratiam, affectionem, ac familiaritatem, quae illi ad Deum verbum est, natus sit ac factus Deus.**

Which things being so, **with anathema we condemn every heresy** — but especially that of that Nestorius the ***ἀνθρωπολάτρις,*** *the cultivator and adorer of a man;* and likewise those who have judged or now judge the same with him, who *tear apart* the one Lord of ours, Jesus Christ, Son of God and our God. Nor do they confess, properly and according to the truth, the holy, glorious, and ever-virgin Mary as ***Deipara*** (that is, *Mother of God*), but affirm that there are *two sons,* the one indeed from the Father — God-the-Word — and the other from the holy ever-virgin *Deipara* Mary, who according to *grace, affection, and familiarity* which he has toward God-the-Word was born and made *deus.*

> **Negantes praeterea neque confitentes Dominum nostrum Iesum Christum Filium Dei et Deum nostrum, incarnatum et hominem factum et crucifixum, unum esse è sancta et coessentiali Trinitate. Hic enim unus est qui simul colitur, simulque glorificatur cum Patre et Spiritu Sancto.**

Denying, moreover, and not confessing that our Lord Jesus Christ, the Son of God and our God, incarnate and made man and crucified, is **one of the holy and coessential Trinity.** For this is that One who is at the same time worshipped, and at the same time glorified, with the Father and the Holy Spirit.

> **Execramur etiam amentem illum Eutychetem, et quicunque eadem vel cum ipso senserunt vel sentiunt, vanas opiniones introducentes, et negantes veram generationem Domini et Servatoris Iesu Christi ex Maria virgine et Deipara — hoc est, nostram salutem — neque confitentes ipsum consubstantialem Patri secundum divinitatem, rursus consubstantialem nobis secundum humanitatem. Similiter autem et Apollinarium illum ψυχοφθόρον, id est, mentem humanam Christo adimentem, et qui cum ipso vel senserunt vel sentiunt, affirmantes Dominum nostrum Iesum Christum Filium Dei et Deum nostrum mentis humanae expertem esse, et confusionem inducentes aut commixtionem in unigeniti Filij Dei inhumanationem: omnes deinque illos, qui sententiam illorum vel secuti sunt, vel adhuc sequendam ac retinendam putant.**

**We execrate also that senseless Eutyches,** and whosoever have judged or now judge the same with him, introducing vain opinions and denying the true generation of the Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ from Mary the virgin and *Deipara* — that is, our salvation — and not confessing Him consubstantial with the Father according to divinity, and again consubstantial with us according to humanity. Likewise also **Apollinaris the *ψυχοφθόρος,*** *the mind-corrupter,* that is, one *taking away the human mind from Christ,* and those who have judged or now judge with him, affirming that our Lord Jesus Christ the Son of God and our God is *destitute of a human mind* — and *introducing a confusion or commixture* into the inhumanation of the only-begotten Son of God: and finally **all those who have followed the opinion of those men, or who yet think that it is to be followed and held.**

> **Idem edictum missum fuit Ephesijs, Caesariensibus, Cyzicenis, Amidanis, Trapezuntijs, Hierosolymitanis, Apamensibus, Iustinianopolitanis, Theopolitanis, Sebastenis, Tharsensibus, et Ancyranis.**

*The same edict was sent to the Ephesians, the Caesareans, the Cyzicenes, the Amidenians, the Trapezuntians, the Jerusalemites, the Apameans, the Justinianopolitans, the Theopolitans, the Sebastenians, the Tharsensians, and the Ancyranians.*

## Psalm 141 *(that is, Psalm 142 in the Hebrew and English numbering) — Domine, ad te clamabo*

> **Domine, ad te clamabo, percipe. Oratio mea valeat coram te, sicut incensum, et elevatio manuum mearum, sicut sacrificium vespertinum. Domine, custodi os meum, et custodi labia mea. Inclina cor meum non ad rem malam, non ad impiè vivendum cum maleficis, nec comedam de ijs, quae ipsis arrident. Iustus me percutiat amanter, et increpet me: id tam erit mihi gratum, ac si caput meum balsamo perfundatur: quoniam semper oro, ne mihi nocere possint. Doctores eorum praecipitentur de petra: tunc primùm audietur doctrina mea, quòd sit suavis. Ossa nostra sunt dispersa usque ad infernum, sicut aliquis proscindit et vastat terram. Quia ad te Domine Domine oculi mei: in te confido: ne reijcias animam meam. Custodi me à laqueo, quem posuerunt mihi, et à tendiculis operantium iniquitatem. Impij cadant in retia sua: ego autem evadam semper.**

**O LORD, to Thee will I cry: hearken. Let my prayer avail before Thee as incense, and the lifting up of my hands as an evening sacrifice.**

**O LORD, keep Thou my mouth, and keep Thou my lips.**

**Incline not my heart to an evil thing, nor to living impiously with the workers of malice; nor let me eat of the dainties which delight them.**

**Let the righteous smite me in love, and reprove me — that shall be as grateful to me as if my head were poured over with balsam; because I ever pray that they may not be able to harm me.**

**Their doctors — let them be cast down headlong from the rock; then first shall my doctrine be heard, that it is sweet.**

**Our bones are scattered even to hell, as when a man scores and lays waste the earth.**

**For unto Thee, O LORD, O LORD, are my eyes; in Thee do I trust: cast not out my soul.**

**Keep me from the snare which they have set for me, and from the traps of the workers of iniquity.**

**Let the impious fall into their own nets; but as for me, I shall ever escape.**

## Psalm 124 *(that is, Psalm 125 in the Hebrew and English numbering) — Nisi Dominus ipse fuisset nobiscum*

> **Nisi Dominus ipse fuisset nobiscum, dicat nunc Israël. Nisi Dominus ipse fuisset nobiscum, cum insurgerent contra nos homines. Tunc vivos deglutissent nos, cum furor eorum in nos exardesceret. Tunc aquae absorbuissent nos: torrens transibat super animam nostram. Aquae nimis altae pertransibant super animam nostram. Benedictus Dominus, qui nos non dat in praedam dentibus eorum. Anima nostra erepta est sicut avis de laqueo aucupis. Laqueus discissus est, et nos liberati sumus. Adiutorium nostrum in nomine Domini, qui fecit caelum et terram.**

**Unless the LORD Himself had been with us, let Israel now say —**

**Unless the LORD Himself had been with us, when men rose up against us —**

**Then had they swallowed us alive, when their fury blazed forth against us;**

**Then had the waters absorbed us; the torrent was passing over our soul;**

**Yea, the waters exceeding deep were passing over our soul.**

**Blessed be the LORD, who giveth us not for a prey to their teeth.**

**Our soul is delivered like a bird from the snare of the fowler; the snare is broken in pieces, and we are set free.**

**Our help is in the name of the LORD, who made heaven and earth.**

> **Auxilium in Domini nomine semper adest.**
> **Fidimus in Domino, qui terram, atque astra creavit,**
> **Et lato victrix pectore turba sumus.**
> **— Nicolaus Selneccerus S., anno 1573.**

**Let Israel now bring forth such words in triumph, and say: "Saved — who would have thought? — behold, we are."**

**Had the LORD not been at hand for us, strong in this our time, when many were rushing against us without mind, long since would they have devoured us wretched ones, alive, in their raging jaws — so great is the wrath, the fury, the evil.**

**Long since had we been swallowed in the deluge of the waters; no man of us could have lived.**

**The torrents were overwhelming us and seeking our soul; life was hiding, submerged in the horror-sounding waters.**

**But to the LORD of heaven, who governs and fills all things, who helps well-doings and presses down evil-doings — let there be beauty, and song, and blessing, grace, and name, because He Himself sets us free from all evils.**

**He denies us to the dark teeth of those men as prey, blocking bitings, wounds, wars, threats.**

**They gnash and roar, they speak dire abuses; they wish not to profit, but strive to harm.**

**But God is; and He at length uncovers all their arts, and slays the violence and the wiles of the proud.**

**As a bird has been snatched from the snare of the fowler, so does life, emerged from the evils, make its songs.**

**Who would have thought? — they burned with endless raging hatreds; but our soul is free, God being the giver.**

**The snare lies crushed, and we have burst through from it, and with wide breast are the victorious crowd.**

**Believe: so is the matter; a good cause at length triumphs. Simplicity has the praise of dexterity.**

**Endure and be hardened; the truth knows not to be buried in the dust, and God Himself is the leader of His own cause.**

**Let them perish, whoever set up others by iron strength; he is wise who has the weighty strength of the word.**

**Our help is no power of the world; help is ever present in the name of the LORD.**

**We trust in the LORD, who created the earth and the stars; and with wide breast are the victorious crowd.**

**— Nicolaus Selneccerus S., in the year 1573.**

# Endnotes

*(Full apparatus will accumulate at the end of the volume as the exposition proceeds. The entries below cover the first tranche — the front matter, the title page, and the Dedicatory Epistle. Cross-references between endnotes use the form [[note-N]].)*

**¹** *Title page.* The Latin title runs: *Symbolorvm Apostolici, Niceni, et Athanasiani Exegesis, fideliter repetens doctrinam perpetuam Ecclesiæ Dei de praecipuis Capitibus & Articulis Christianae Fidei, inprimis de Divinitate aeterna Filij Dei, de Vnione hypostatica duarum naturarum in Christo, de Maiestate Filij hominis, de Communicatione idiomatum: de Coena Domini &c. Contra recentes horum temporum blasphemias. Scripta et edita autore Nicolao Selneccero D. Electorali Professore in Academia Lipsica. Augustini epistola 7: Nimis perversè seipsum amat, qui et alios errare vult, ut error ipsius lateat. Lipsiae, Iohannes Rhamba excudebat, M.D.LXXVII.* The Augustine motto is drawn from *Augustinus,* *Epistola* 7 *ad Marcellinum* — an early letter usually printed among the *Epistulae* in the sixteenth-century editions, though modern editors have renumbered it. The 1577 imprint stands as the *enlarged reissue* of the 1575 *editio princeps* (Rhamba, Leipzig); the copy used as text-base is the Bayerische Staatsbibliothek München exemplar, digitised by Google under the identifier *hyfSeau9q60C.*

**²** *To Johann Günther, Count of Schwarzburg-Arnstadt-Sondershausen-Leutenberg.* The dedicatee is **Johann Günther I of Schwarzburg-Arnstadt** (1532–1586), one of the four Counts of the House of Schwarzburg — a Lutheran territorial family loyal to the *Confessio Augustana* and among Selnecker's supporting patrons. Selnecker had known Johann Günther since his own Wittenberg years, and had continued in correspondence with the Schwarzburg house throughout his time at Jena and Leipzig; the "return from the County of Oldenburg" that Selnecker later alludes to refers to a journey through the northern Lutheran territories in the mid-1570s. The precise dating of the dedication is *Leipzig, in the month of April,* *cIɔIɔ.LXXV* — that is, **April 1575** — the year of the *editio princeps* of the *Exegesis;* the reissue of 1577 carries the epistle without alteration.

**³** *ῥιψάσπιδες* (*rhipsáspides*) — literally "shield-throwers," those who cast away their shields and flee from battle. The word is a classical Greek term of reproach for cowards, most famously associated with the poet Archilochus (fr. 5 West), who casts off his shield to save his life; used here by Selnecker for those who abandon the defence of true doctrine.

**⁴** *ἀλλοτριοεπισκοποῦν* (*allotrioepiskopoún*) — a rare compound from 1 Peter 4:15 (*ἀλλοτριοεπίσκοπος,* one who supervises what belongs to another; a busybody or meddler in other men's affairs). Selnecker uses the participial form to describe those who intrude — from other disciplines — into the *sacrosancta schola* of theology.

**⁵** *κρησφύγετον* (*krēsphýgeton*) — a classical Greek word for a refuge or place of safety, especially a hiding-place in flight.

**⁶** *τὰ θεολογικά* (*ta theologiká*) — "the theological things"; theology as a discipline. Selnecker's charge is that those who *treat* theology when in peaceful weather turn tail when the wind blows against them, and then plead that they were never really theologians at all.

**⁷** *χέτλιοι* (*chétlioi*) — a Homeric adjective meaning "hard, unyielding, harsh" (Iliad *passim*); an unusual doublet with the preceding Latin *heroes straminei* ("straw heroes") — Selnecker's characterisation of the false Reformers who imagine themselves *heroes* while being made only of straw.

**⁸** Luther, *ad Ducem Georgium* (1533). The reference is to Luther's polemical open letter to Duke George of Saxony (1471–1539), one of the most persistent Roman Catholic princely opponents of the Reformation. The 1533 letter — *Etliche Briefe D. Martin Luthers an Herzog Georgen zu Sachsen* — includes Luther's rehearsal of his own long labour in the Scriptures, which Selnecker paraphrases faithfully here in Latin. WA 30/III, 470ff.

**⁹** *ἀκαθαρσίας καὶ ἀνομίας* — "of impurity and of lawlessness," a common Pauline pairing (Rom. 6:19; 2 Cor. 12:21). Selnecker uses the paired genitives as a diagnostic mark on the "stratagems and traps" of the pseudonymous polemicists.

**¹⁰** *Christionis and Nathanaels and Hessianders.* Selnecker refers to the wave of pseudonymous polemical pamphlets that had appeared in the years after the *Colloquy of Maulbronn* (1564) and through the run-up to the *Formula of Concord* (1577), in which strict-Lutheran, Kryptocalvinist, Philippist, and Sacramentarian positions were fought out under a shower of Latin and Greek pseudonyms. *Christion* — the more accurate spelling later — is the Latinised Greek nickname adopted (as Selnecker will make clear a little below in the same paragraph) by one of the Kryptocalvinist controversialists writing on the *communicatio idiomatum* controversy in the mid-1570s. *Nathanaeles* and *Hessiandri* are two further pseudonymous families in that same polemical stream. Selnecker's polemical target throughout this paragraph is the pseudonymous author-culture that had displaced the older tradition of open signed controversy.

**¹¹** *The Christion who attempted a disputation on the communicatio and viva vis carnis Christi from Cyril and the Council of Ephesus.* This is Selnecker's central polemical target in the mid-1570s and the immediate provocation for the enlarged 1577 reissue. The debate over whether Cyril of Alexandria and the *Third Ecumenical Council* (Ephesus 431) can be enlisted as witnesses for the *manducatio spiritualis-only* view of the eucharistic *koinōnia,* over against the substantial-oral view, was the sharp edge of the Kryptocalvinist controversy in the mid-1570s. Selnecker's polemical position, later codified in *Formula of Concord* Article VII, is that the Cyril passages in question do *not* teach a mere spiritual manducation but presuppose the substantial reception of the *vivifica caro Christi.*

**¹²** *κοινωνία* (*koinōnía*) — "communion, participation." 1 Cor. 10:16 is the Pauline seat: *τὸ ποτήριον τῆς εὐλογίας ὃ εὐλογοῦμεν, οὐχὶ κοινωνία τοῦ αἵματος τοῦ Χριστοῦ ἐστιν;* — "the cup of blessing which we bless, is it not a communion of the blood of Christ?" The whole Reformation-era Sacramentarian controversy pivots on the exegesis of this verse, and Selnecker's Real-Presence position rests on reading *koinōnía* as substantial *participation-in* rather than mere signification-of.

**¹³** *Berengarius, Carlstadt, Zwingli, Sylvanus.* The list is Selnecker's own polemical rehearsal of what he takes to be the divine judgements on the Sacramentarian tradition. **Berengar of Tours** (c. 999–1088), first medieval denier of the substantial Real Presence, was forced to recant in 1059 and again in 1079 and died in an obscure retreat. **Andreas Carlstadt** (Karlstadt, c. 1486–1541), Luther's early colleague-turned-opponent on the Supper, died in Basel of the plague. **Ulrich Zwingli** (1484–1531), the Zurich reformer whose *significat* reading of the words of institution Selnecker rejects, was killed at the Battle of Kappel (11 October 1531). **Sylvanus** — the reference is to **Johann Sylvan** (c. 1533–1572), a Heidelberg court preacher executed at Heidelberg on 23 December 1572 for anti-Trinitarianism; Selnecker treats his end as a divine judgement on the Kryptocalvinist trajectory at Heidelberg.

**¹⁴** *εὐκαίρως καὶ ἀκαίρως* — "in season and out of season," from 2 Timothy 4:2 (*κήρυξον τὸν λόγον, ἐπίστηθι εὐκαίρως ἀκαίρως*). Selnecker's Pauline self-designation as a preacher who will not fall silent so long as he lives.

**¹⁵** *καὶ πανουργία* (*panourgía*) — "and craftiness," a Pauline word for the cunning of the false apostles (2 Cor. 11:3), transferred by Selnecker to Satan directly.

**¹⁶** *Sanctify us, O God, in Thy truth. Thy discourse is truth.* — John 17:17, in Selnecker's Latin: *Sanctifica nos, Deus, in veritate tua. Sermo tuus est veritas.*

**¹⁷** *Gregory of Nazianzus,* here paraphrased in three Latin lines. Nazianzen (330–390), the "Theologian" of Constantinople, is Selnecker's model for the pious rejection of empty disputation, and is quoted more frequently in the *Exegesis* than any of the Greek Fathers other than Cyril. The three lines here reproduce the sense (though not the precise wording) of Nazianzen's *Oratio* 27 (*Theologica* I), the opening polemic against those who trivialise theological disputation.

**¹⁸** *κακοπιστία* — "evil-belief," a rare Greek compound: to have faith in the wrong thing, or to hold to faith in a corrupt manner. Selnecker contrasts it here with *ἀπιστία* (unbelief); *kakopistía* is his diagnosis of the Kryptocalvinist party — not unbelief, but a *twisted* form of faith.

**¹⁹** *εἰς ἐξέτασιν ἴοντας, παρατάτας* — "those going into examination, front-rank fighters." The military metaphor is Pauline in inspiration (2 Cor. 10:3–5); Selnecker uses it here for the true patrons of the Church, those who *examine* doctrine as front-line soldiers rather than as camp-followers.

**²⁰** *Clement of Alexandria,* *Stromateis* 1.17 — that pagan philosophy is a *κλοπὴ τῆς ἀληθείας* (a theft of the truth) by the fallen powers. Selnecker's Latin rendering: *furtum diaboli.*

**²¹** *even the wise man must be silent according to the Prophet.* — Amos 5:13: *ideo prudens in tempore illo tacebit, quia tempus malum est* ("Therefore the prudent shall keep silence in that time, for it is an evil time").

**²²** *Abide with us, Christ, for it draws toward evening.* — Luke 24:29, the Emmaus-road prayer, given in Selnecker's Latin as *Mane nobiscum, Christe, quia advesperascit.* This is Selnecker's own hymn-line, incorporated by him from the Emmaus text into his most famous German chorale ***"Ach bleib bei uns, Herr Jesu Christ."*** The Latin form here is the source. See related [[bio-sketch]] for the hymn.

**²³** *ὁ καρδιογνώστης* (*ho kardiognōstēs*) — "the knower of hearts," Acts 1:24 (*σὺ κύριε καρδιογνῶστα πάντων*).

**²⁴** *That Gymnosophist to Alexander.* The reference is to the encounter between Alexander the Great and the Indian *gymnosophistae* (naked wise men) reported in Plutarch, *Alexander* 64, and in Arrian, *Anabasis* 7.2. One of the *gymnosophistae* — sometimes named as Dandamis or Calanus — is reported to have answered Alexander's philosophical questions with pointed brevity, exposing the futility of academic *doubting doubts.*

**²⁵** *παράδοξα, ἄτοπα, ἀπίθανα, καὶ ἀδύνατα* — "paradoxes, absurdities, incredibilities, impossibilities." A rhetorical catalogue of the standard classical charges against the Christian mysteries. Selnecker's move here is to concede the label *paradox* (which the Holy Spirit Himself uses of the mysteries in Scripture), but to deny that the paradox is either absurd or impossible.

**²⁶** *When you sent to me most excellent men.* Selnecker's allusion is to a delegation sent by Count Johann Günther to Leipzig in the early 1570s, during the second phase of the Crypto-Calvinist controversy at Leipzig (1570–1574), to inquire after Selnecker's welfare and to bring him a token of the Count's support. The exact date is not specified in the epistle.

**²⁷** *cIɔIɔ.LXXV.* Selnecker uses the older Roman-numeral form *cIɔIɔ* for **M** (1000) — a common humanist typographical practice preserving the ancient Etruscan-derived form of the numeral. The full reading is therefore *M.D.LXXV = 1575.*
