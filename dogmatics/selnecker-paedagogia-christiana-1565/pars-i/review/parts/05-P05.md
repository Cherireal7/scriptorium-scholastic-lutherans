<!-- p.101 -->

**IIII.** There fighteth against the whole second Commandment the hypocrisy of those who say, *"Lord, Lord,"* and yet do not the will of the heavenly Father; that is, who neither have true faith, nor show forth their faith by love or good works. These take the name of God in vain, **Rom. 2**: *"Thou gloriest in the Law, and by transgression of the Law thou blasphemest God."* **2 Cor. 6**.

These sin horribly in these three things: **I.** They make the Word of God a cover for their own sins. **II.** They bring it to pass that by the adversaries, and by others, the true doctrine is blasphemed, and blamed as false, seeing there is not present a life agreeing with the doctrine. **III.** They are an evil example to others with whom they live.

All these there awaiteth the horrible threatening added to the second Commandment: God *will not hold them guiltless*, that is, He will certainly punish them, with bodily and eternal punishments. For He not only doth not hold them guiltless in this life, but also *will not* hold them guiltless: that we may know that the abuse of the divine name is punished with eternal punishments, unless remission be made, for the Mediator's sake, to those who are converted unto God.

The sum of the second Commandment is: **I.** To speak rightly and godly of God, and of all divine things, in teaching, in disputing, and in the whole life. **II.** To apply the Name of God to its true uses in life, etc.

# *Tertium Praeceptum* · The Third Commandment

The order of the Commandments is to be considered. The first Commandment teacheth the inward worships. The second preacheth of the outward profession, namely, invocation. Now the third, of ceremonies and the use of ceremonies.

But before we speak of the worships of the third Commandment, the word *Sabbath* is to be considered, which signifieth a ceasing, or rest; and in the Holy Scriptures there is set forth <!-- p.102 --> a fourfold Sabbath: the Sabbath of creation; the ceremonial or pedagogical Sabbath; the Sabbath of Redemption; the Sabbath of eternal life.

## *De Sabbatho Creationis* · Of the Sabbath of Creation

**Gen. 2**: *"God rested on the seventh day from all his work,"* that is, He created no new kind (*species*) in nature whose matter or likeness had not gone before. And He rested for this cause, that He might show that man, whom He had created last, after all the other creatures, is the uttermost and highest work, wherein He delighteth, and wherein He willeth to rest, and unto which the deliberation and desire of the whole workmanship was directed. For all things were created for the sake of men. And the end is more excellent than the things ordained unto it. For there were four chief causes for which God sanctified the Sabbath: The **first**, because He rested from the Creation of new kinds. The **second**, on account of the excellency of man. The **third**, for the instituting of a certain worship, in which the human race, even in its innocency — that is, even if Adam had not fallen — would have proclaimed the goodness of God, and would have rendered unto God acceptable worships, whereas on the other days other things would have had to be cared for. The **fourth**, for a testimony of immortality, and of a life eternal and better, into which man, even before the fall, would have been translated after a certain time had passed.

Yet God did not so rest as though He had departed from His work, as a Builder departeth from a house made ready and built up, and as though He had ceased to act — as the Epicureans think that there is no Providence; but He rested, as hath now been said, from the Creation of new kinds, not from preservation and sustaining. God is perpetually present with His creature, and most freely sustaineth and preserveth it, maketh the earth fruitful year by year, giveth life to living things, and the power of procreation, and in the Church doth from time to time work wonderful deliverances; **John 5**: *"My Father worketh hitherto, and I work."* **Acts 17**: *"In him we live, and move, and have our being,"* in God the Father, the Son, and the Holy <!-- p.103 --> Spirit. **Col. 1**: *"By him all things consist."* And there is this verse of **Vida**:

> God, filling all things, hath poured Himself into the whole mass.  

**Heb. 1**: *"Upholding all things by the word of his power."* And here the degrees of the divine presence are to be considered, of which the **first** is the general action of God, or universal presence, which is the presence of preservation, whereby God is present with all creatures in such wise that He preserveth them for so long as He willeth to preserve them; **Jer. 23**: *"Do not I fill heaven and earth? saith the Lord."* And of this degree there are many verses, as:

> By essence, by presence, God is, and everywhere by power.  

And our own:

> ***ἔστι θεὸς πάντῃ, ἐν καλοῖς τε κακοῖς τε,***  
> ***πάντα ἔχει, μοῦνος πάντα βλέπει τε θεός.***  

(God is everywhere, both among the good and among the evil; God holdeth all things, and alone seeth all things.)

**Lucan**:

> Jupiter is whatsoever thou seest, whithersoever thou movest.  

Likewise:

> And even a light sod is that which proveth that God is.  

And:

> And even a single herb showeth God to be present.  

And thus God is present even with the wicked. And it is certain that God is thus present with things *separably*, because He destroyeth the natures which He willeth to destroy, as Pharaoh, and the greatest part of the human race in the flood.

The **second** degree is that whereby God is present with regenerate and Holy men in this life, as with David, in such wise that He not only preserveth their life, but also worketh in them motions pleasing to Himself. And yet He is not beheld visibly, as He shall be beheld in the life eternal, and He is present separably.

The **third** degree is that whereby He is present with the blessed Angels and men in heaven, and preserveth not only their life, but also quickeneth them with life eternal, and filleth them with His own light and righteousness, and is beheld by them visibly and immediately; and although He departeth not from them, yet is He present with them by fellowship only. So shall God be all in all in the life eternal.

<!-- p.104 -->

The **fourth** degree is far other, whereby the divine nature in the Son of God, ***τῷ λόγῳ*** (the Word), is present with the human nature in such wise that there is a personal union, not only inseparably, but also after such a manner that there is one ***ὑφιστάμενον*** (subsisting thing) for ever, without end, as **Damascene** saith: *"The human nature, which the **λόγος** (Word) once assumed, He never forsaketh."* And so David prayeth, **1 Paral. 17 [1 Chron.]**: *"Thou hast looked upon me, O God, in the form of a man, who in the highest is the Lord God."*

## *De Sabbatho Ceremoniali* · Of the Ceremonial Sabbath

The Ceremonial, or pedagogical, or Mosaic Sabbath is that which is taught in the third Commandment, instituted for the sake of teaching, and that it may admonish us of the Sabbath to come. God willeth that on the seventh day there be a ceasing from the outward works of the body which hinder the ministry and the holy works; such as are, that others be taught concerning God, that the Sacraments be administered, etc.

Now seeing that the Ceremonial Laws delivered by Moses are abolished, it is here asked, whether the commandment of the Sabbath may be left out, and whether it be abrogated? I answer: The third commandment is mixed; it is partly Moral, as pertaineth to the *genus*; partly it is Ceremonial, as pertaineth to the *species*. The *genus* is the name given to the principal meaning of the commandment, or the final cause of the species instituted, that is, the work itself whereby the ceremonies which God commandeth are administered, and the ministry is preserved, and whereby every man contributeth his own office to the preservation of the public ministry. But the *species* is the name given to the certain day, and that the seventh. Thus therefore we say: I. That the Jews had a divine Commandment concerning the genus and concerning the species, or concerning the work and concerning the day. But seeing that we have a divine Commandment only concerning the work, and have not a Commandment concerning a certain day, therefore we say: That the genus which is contained in the third Commandment is in no wise abrogated, and, seeing it is moral, is perpetual and immutable; but that the species is abrogated, which is ceremonial. And for this cause the seventh <!-- p.105 --> day, which was the day of the Mosaic Sabbath, hath been transferred — the Apostles themselves being the authors, **Rev. 1** — by Christians unto the eighth day, which we name the Lord's day (*Dominicus*), because on that day the Son of God rose again from the dead: for which cause also the beginning of the day, which according to Moses the Jews begin from the evening time, we take from the morning time, calling to remembrance the glorious Resurrection of our Saviour, which came to pass in the morning.

Whence **Ignatius** saith excellently, **Epist. to the Magnesians 3**: ***μετὰ τὸ σαββατίσαι*** (that is, after that the Jewish Sabbath hath now been abrogated), ***ἑορταζέτω πᾶς φιλόχριστος τὴν κυριακὴν, τὴν ἀναστάσιμον, τὴν βασιλίδα, τὴν ὕπατον πασῶν τῶν ἡμερῶν. ἣν περιμένων ὁ προφήτης, ἔλεγεν· εἰς τὸ τέλος, ὑπὲρ τῆς ὀγδόης. ἐν ᾗ καὶ ἡ ζωὴ ἡμῶν ἀνέτειλε, καὶ τοῦ θανάτου γέγονε νίκη ἐν τῷ χριστῷ.*** (After the keeping of the Sabbath, let every lover of Christ keep as a feast the Lord's day, the day of the resurrection, the queen, the chief of all the days; in expectation whereof the prophet said: "Unto the end, for the eighth"; on which also our life arose, and the victory over death was wrought in Christ.)

Now it is to be noted by the younger sort, that of the commandments of God some are Catholic, namely, those which bind all men and every man, either to obey or to suffer punishment; but that others are particular, that is, given to one nation only or to one man only; concerning which three Rules are to be observed. The **first**: No Commandment is in all respects so particular, but that it also in some measure bindeth all men, to wit, as pertaineth to the genus. The **second**: Whatsoever of outward circumstances — that is, of time, person, place, number, order, and other like things — is annexed to particular commandments, all that is to be left to those unto whom those things were commanded particularly and by name, or in the species. The **third**: Whatsoever, the outward circumstances being now set aside, remaineth of the genus, that is, of true faith and charity (for these two are the theses and the mark of the whole Christian doctrine, profession and life), that is plainly to be transferred unto all men and every man, and to be fitted to them with a firm application. Now from these things it followeth, that the ceremonial observance of the Sabbath is left to the Jews, but that the Moral, which requireth faith and charity, pertaineth unto us. And of the ceremony Paul speaketh, saying, **Col. 2**: *"Let no man judge you in respect of an holyday,"* to wit, a <!-- p.106 --> Ceremonial one. For Paul speaketh not of the genus, or the principal meaning of the commandment, and the final cause, which is natural and immutable.

## *De Sabbatho Redemptionis* · Of the Sabbath of Redemption

This is the Sabbath of the Son of God resting after an unspeakable manner in the sepulchre, the whole seventh day, as though He had been forsaken of God. In this manner God hath restored unto us the Feast of the Sabbath, which was lost in the fall. And as the Son of God, before the honour of the Sabbath to come, first rested in death, so the whole Church beginneth the honour of the Sabbath from a like rest or ceasing, that is, from death; wherein although we seem to be forsaken, yet shall we certainly be raised again unto the honour of the eternal Sabbath.

## *De Sabbatho Vitae Aeternae* · Of the Sabbath of Eternal Life

**Heb. 3 and 4**: *"There remaineth a Sabbath to the people."* **Isa. 66**: *"There shall be Sabbath after Sabbath"*; in which, namely, all the offices of the animal life ceasing, without darkness and sin, without any labour and pain, we shall do only the works of the Sabbath, we shall behold God face to face and celebrate Him, and He Himself shall impart unto us the fellowship of His own light and of eternal life. This future and eternal rest the type in the Law of the Sabbath shadoweth forth, as we shall say more fully below.

## *De Numero Septenario* · Of the Number Seven

That the number seven is Sacred, and is so named, cometh to pass for this cause: that God also rested on the seventh day, and that the Holy Scriptures very often use that number, and that experience testifieth that this number is altogether peculiar in the whole of life, in the changes and vicissitudes of kingdoms, of diseases, of the state of life, etc. Hence the critical days, hence the climacteric years, <!-- p.107 --> hence certain fixed periods of some Commonwealths and kingdoms, and other like things well-nigh infinite, many of which **Macrobius** reciteth in the *Dream of Scipio*. For the Heathen also observed this, as **Pythagoras** called the number seven the vehicle and complement of human life. For it comprehendeth body and soul. The body consisteth of four elements. The soul hath a threefold power, ***λογικὴν, θυμικὴν, καὶ ἐπιθυμητικήν*** (the rational, the irascible, and the concupiscible). In Civil matters seven witnesses make credit sure, even as at this day also in the Empire there are seven Electors. And the verses of the Poet **Linus** are these:

> When the seventh Light came, the Almighty Father began  
> To finish all things: and the seventh is among the good, and the selfsame  
> Seventh is also the beginning of all things.  
> The seventh is the same as the first, and the seventh is perfect in sevens:  
> Whence also the high heaven with its wandering stars  
> Is turned, and is borne round about on every side in as many circles.  

Thus therefore they have numbered seven Planets; seven liberal arts; seven distinctions of notes in Music; ***καὶ ἑπτάχορδον*** (and the seven-stringed) Lyre of Mercury; seven ***φάσεις*** (phases) of the Moon in one whole month. So also the Moon herself accomplisheth the Zodiac in 28 days, which consist of four times seven days. For in seven days she cometh from the Northern half to the midpoint of latitude, that is, to the ecliptic place. From which in the seven following she glideth down to the lowest Southern point; again in another seven she climbeth slantwise to the middle; in the last seven she is restored to the Northern summit. But who could number all? **Macrobius**, **Gellius**, and others are to be consulted, who discourse learnedly of these things.

The Holy Scriptures use this number as a certain number for an uncertain, indifferently, and that in the place of multitude and fulness. For **שבע**, *Sebagh*, signifieth fulness and sufficiency: as, *"The just man falleth seven times in a day,"* **Prov. 24**. *"Until the barren bare seven,"* **1 Sam. 2**, that is, many. *"Seven times a day do I praise thee,"* **Ps. 119**. *"The sluggard is wiser in his own conceit than seven men that can render a reason,"* that is, <!-- p.108 --> than many others, **Prov. 26**. *"She that hath borne seven languisheth,"* **Jer. 15**. Elias sendeth his servant seven times to look for the rain, **3 Kings 17 [1 Kings]**. Elisha biddeth Naaman the Syrian wash seven times in Jordan, **4 Kings 5 [2 Kings]**. Hezekiah sacrificeth seven bullocks, seven rams, seven lambs, seven he goats, **2 Paral. 27 [2 Chron.]**. The Church in the time of Hezekiah, after the cleansing of the temple, slew seventy oxen. Asa, seven hundred oxen, and of sheep seven thousand, **2 Paral. 15 [2 Chron.]**. A fault is to be forgiven a neighbour, saith Christ, not seven times, that is, not often, but seventy times seven, that is, always and everywhere. And in Daniel, after the Babylonish captivity, which lasted 70 years, there are numbered seventy weeks unto Christ. The seventh day from the creation was the Sabbath, or rest of God, **Gen. 1**. The seventh day is our ceremonial Sabbath. On the seventh day, or Sabbath, Christ rested in the sepulchre. The seventh thousand of the world shall bring forth the eternal Sabbath, as the saying of Elias testifieth; whereunto pertaineth the oracle of **Orpheus**, which **Plato** allegeth:

> ***Ἕκτῃ ἐν γενεῇ κόσμος καταπαύσετ' ἀοιδῆς.***  

(In the sixth generation the world shall cease from its song.)

And **Irenaeus** recordeth the same concerning the six days of Creation, applied to the six thousands of the world. But thus much of these things. Let the studious, however, themselves gather examples out of **Clement** and other authors, of which there is assuredly a great number in nature. Seven spans make up the length of the human body. The life of man is of seven days, as touching his beginning, and his extinction which cometh by fasting, saith **Hippocrates**. The diameter of a circle is compared to the circumference by the number seven. The days of the week, as touching the names of the Planets which have been given to them, consist of an odd number, to wit, of seven; the seven Planets being numbered thrice every day and fitted to the hours, as though they were thrice seven, and three being added besides, which, when seven days are finished, make three sevens, and four being reserved for the numbering of the hours of the day following, which, when seven days are ended, make up four sevens. <!-- p.109 --> Now it pleaseth me to add the Elegy of **Solon**, which **Clement of Alexandria**, ***στρωμάτ.*** (*Stromata*) book 6, and **Philo** in the Oration *On the Workmanship of the World*, do recite:

> ***Παῖς μὲν ἄνηβος ἐὼν ἔτι νήπιος ἕρκος ὀδόντων***  
> ***Φύσας ἐκβάλλει πρῶτον ἐν ἕπτ' ἔτεσιν.***  
> ***Τοὺς δ' ἑτέρους ὅτε δὴ τελέσει θεώς ἕπτ' ἐνιαυτοὺς,***  
> ***Ἥβης ἐκφαίνει σπέρματα γινομένης.***  
> ***Τῇ τριτάτῃ δὲ γένειον ἀεξομένων ἐπὶ γυίων***  
> ***Λαχνοῦται χροίης ἄνθος ἀμειβομένης.***  
> ***Τῇ δὲ τετάρτῃ πᾶς τὶς ἐν ἑβδομάδι μέγ' ἄριστος***  
> ***Ἰσχὺν, ἥν τ' ἄνδρες σήματ' ἔχουσ' ἀρετῆς.***  
> ***Πέμπτῃ δ' ὥριον ἄνδρα γάμου μεμνημένον εἶναι,***  
> ***Καὶ παίδων ζητεῖν εἰς ὀπίσω γενεήν.***  
> ***Τῇ δ' ἕκτῃ περὶ πάντα καταρτύεται νόος ἀνδρὸς***  
> ***Οὐδ' ἐτιδεῖν ἔθ' ὅμως ἔργα μάταια θέλει.***[^P05-4]  
> ***Ἑπτὰ δὲ νοῦν καὶ γλῶσσαν ἐν ἑβδομάσι μέγ' ἄριστος,***  
> ***Ὀκτώ δ' ἀμφοτέρων τέσσαρα καὶ δέκ' ἔτη.***  
> ***Τῇ δ' ἐνάτῃ ἔτι μὲν δύναται μετριώτερα δ' αὐτοῦ***  
> ***Πρὸς μεγάλην ἀρετὴν σῶμά τε καὶ δύναμις.***  
> ***Τῇ δεκάτῃ δ' ὅτε δὴ τελέσει θεὸς ἕπτ' ἐνιαυτοὺς,***  
> ***Οὐκ ἂν ἄωρος ἐὼν μοῖραν ἔχει θανάτου.***  

That is, as a certain man hath rendered it:

> After the infant hath fulfilled seven years,  
> The teeth are brought forth, to be as a wall of the mouth:  
> After God hath granted him another seven years,  
> He cometh to ripeness, and the seed of generation is given him.

> <!-- p.110 --> When the seventh seven hath been added, and

Would that the fiftieth might now become the jubilee, by the coming of the Son of God. Amen." Thus far he. And certainly what befell again when the seventh year had passed, that is, 1574, is but too well known, the inconstancy of many having been discovered, who were contriving a universal change and defection from the heavenly truth, had not God wonderfully guided the most excellent magistrate, Augustus both in name and in deed, and through him confounded all their devices.

But let these things now suffice. For frequent and studious reading supplieth very many examples in profane letters also.

## *De Libertate Christiana* · Of Christian Liberty

At the outset we must speak of the abuse of the word, which is twofold, in defect and in excess. In defect is the abuse of the word, when the mighty and the wise of this world laugh at the glorying in Christian liberty, whereas nevertheless Christians are subject to divers afflictions and calamities, to death, to diseases, to persecutions; as Pharaoh slayeth the infants of the Israelites, many thousands with Daniel are carried away into Babylon, Herod killeth the infants. And most true is that which is said in the verses:

> With blood the Church was cleansed, with blood she began,  
> With blood she grew up, with blood shall be her end.  

In excess the abuse is committed by ungodly, secure and wanton men, who abuse the name of Christian Liberty to the confirming of their own security and carnal liberty; even as at the beginning of the cleansing of the doctrine many Princes joined themselves to the confession of the Protestants, as <!-- p.112 --> they call them, for these causes only, that they might be able to seize the goods of the Church unto themselves, and that they might live freely after their carnal lusts. Of these it is said:

> Love of Liberty, why dost thou deceive the whole world?  

And at this day well-nigh all among the common people abuse the name and the doctrine of faith, and care neither for God, nor for themselves or their own salvation; they cry out that they are free, baptized, redeemed, wholly Christians, and yet they live not as Christians.

These are the abuses of the glorying in Christian liberty; whereof the former is in a manner political, and belongeth to human reason, which defineth liberty to be that which is not subject to others in this common life, and which aboundeth in power, riches, health, and other gifts. The latter is spiritual, and more grievous and more perilous than the former. For the former sort can oftentimes be instructed. The latter think that they are already instructed, and abuse their instruction, and will not be instructed any further.

The Philosophers also have disputed many things concerning liberty, and have said that it is the faculty or power of living as thou wilt. And **Seneca** saith thus: *"Dost thou ask what liberty is? To be in bondage to no thing, to no necessity; by no chances of fortune to depart from that which is right."* Many also have found fault with the bonds of the Laws, and would have torn them asunder, because these seemed to take liberty away from men; and they have named the Laws a tyranny, even as the Anabaptists also would not be subject to the Laws of the magistrates, making a show of Christian liberty. But **Plato** saith more rightly: *"A moderate liberty, and a moderate servitude, is a just Law."*

It is asked therefore, what Christian liberty is, and how far this name extendeth itself? To this question it is to be answered, that at the outset every man ought to consider that liberty which is to come, and is perfect or consummate, of them that believe in the Son of God, when man shall be in eternal blessedness, and God shall be all in all the blessed, and these shall live without misery, and without death. Of this liberty the Son of God speaketh: *"If the Son shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed."* But that liberty is begun <!-- p.113 --> in this life also, through the Son of God, by the voice of the Gospel, in the soul and heart, although the body is as yet subject to death, and to divers adversities.

Now the whole doctrine of Christian liberty is wont to be delivered in certain degrees. **Bernard** maketh three degrees of liberty: from sin, and from misery, and from necessity. From sin in this life; from misery in the life eternal; from necessity in both lives, namely, that we do nothing by constraint, but be governed by the free Spirit, and serve God from the heart, willingly, not constrained, not driven by force. This distinction is not amiss, and begetteth godly thoughts. But that is clearer which is delivered in the theological loci. The **first degree** is of *grace*, namely, the deliverance from sin, from the wrath of God, and from eternal punishments, or from the curse of the Law against our sins; **Gal. 3**: *"Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the Law, being made a curse for us,"* according to that saying: *"Cursed is he that hangeth on a tree."* Good God, how great a thing is this, that the Son of God is made a curse! And **2 Cor. 5**: *"He hath made him to be sin, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God through him."*

The **second degree** is of the *gift*, and is simply to be joined to the first, namely, the giving of the Holy Spirit, whereby there are wrought in the heart new motions pleasing to God, there is wrought a new life, obedience is rendered to God, there is wrought gladness in the heart, the mind resteth, rejoiceth and liveth in God; as **Stephen**, **Laurence**, **Agnes**, **Vincent**, **Babylas of Antioch**, **Apollonia**, etc.

All these feel a lively gladness in the heart, they are courageous and strong in God, knowing this:

> If this world with devils  
> Swarmed, as it were with worms,  
> We should fear nothing anxiously;  
> We shall overcome at the last valiantly.  
> Let the prince of the world be proud,  
> Let him snarl and rage,  
> The knave knoweth not how to hurt,  
> Seeing he is broken even by one little word.[^P05-2]  

<!-- p.114 -->

Saul, **Cato**, **Brutus** feel none of these things, and slay themselves; but the three Israelites, as a brazen wall and a Marpesian rock, as the saying is, stand fast in the burning furnace of Babylon, and the Son of God is visibly in the midst of them. Of this degree it is said, **John 14**: *"If a man love me, he will keep my word: and my Father will love him, and we will come unto him, and make our abode with him."*

These two are the first degrees of Christian liberty, and they comprehend faith and the fruits of faith, and, as **Ps. 51** saith, they show forth a right Spirit, the holy Spirit, and a free Spirit.

The **third degree** is of the outward and civil life only: namely, that Christians are not bound to the polity of Moses, or to any certain polity of one nation, but in every several place obey the present Laws which are agreeable to the law of nature, and command nothing against the commandments of God. Of this degree the more unlearned are therefore to be taught, because the unskilful often contend that Christians ought to be governed by the Mosaic laws. But of the abrogation of the Mosaic Law it hath been spoken above. And these Questions are to be considered:

I. Whether liberty from the Law be only liberty from the ceremonies and from the civil Laws of Moses, and not from the moral Law.

II. In what respect liberty from the moral Law is to be understood.

III. Why the liberty from the moral Law is not the same as that which is from the other Laws.

IIII. How Adam could be received into grace, whereas nevertheless the moral Law is the eternal and immovable sentence of God.

V. How, or in what ways, the Son of God fulfilleth the Law.

To the first question we have answered above, namely, that all the parts of the Law are abrogated as touching justification. But as pertaineth to the second question, the moral Law (which is so named by a slight word) is abrogated as touching justification, and as touching condemnation, not as touching <!-- p.115 --> obedience. To the third question also we answer briefly: The Moral Law speaketh of the heart and of perpetual righteousness, that is, of conformity with God. But the Ceremonies and the Civil customs are only outward gestures, which shall not be in the life eternal, wherein oxen shall not be sacrificed, nor thieves hanged, etc. And these ceremonial and civil Laws were enacted only for a certain time, even as the whole polity was to endure only unto a determined time, according to the saying: *"The sceptre shall not depart from Judah, until Shiloh come."* From these things the unlikeness can now easily be perceived. To the fourth question the Son of God answereth, otherwise no man. For the Son of God is the Mediator. And the will and righteousness of God is immovable and invariable, in such wise that either the creature obey its Creator, or suffer punishment. For every Law bindeth either to obedience, or to voluntary punishment. The Son of God paid the punishment. Thus is the Law satisfied. And unto us is imputed the righteousness of the Son of God. Thus is the mercy of God laid open. And in this manner Adam, with the whole human race, is delivered.

To the fifth: First, Christ fulfilleth the Law by His own proper obedience. II. By paying the punishment for us. III. By His own efficacy in us, whereby, through the voice of the Gospel and His Holy Spirit, He worketh in us new light, faith, conformity with God, and giveth life eternal. IIII. By teaching and declaring the Decalogue, showing what sin is, and not permitting a licence of offending against the Decalogue. And above we have comprehended the fulfilling of the Law performed by Christ in five words, which are: *actively, passively, imputatively, inchoatively,* and *consummatively*.

The **fourth degree** of Christian liberty is from human traditions in the Church; **Col. 2**: *"Let no man judge you in meat, drink, apparel."* For human traditions do not merit the Remission of sins, neither are they righteousness before God; and it is no sin to omit them, without offence.

## *De Traditionibus Humanis in Ecclesia* · Of Human Traditions in the Church

At the outset let the difference of degrees in outward customs be held fast. For some customs are commanded of God, as those which are comprehended in the divine Law. Others are instituted by the civil magistrate for the preservation of peace and discipline, as, not to bear arms, to betake oneself home at a certain hour of the night. Others have been ordained in the Church by the Bishops for good order, as the distinctions of times, the public assemblies on certain days, the reciting of certain histories, etc. And these human ceremonies are the lowest degree in outward customs. And of this degree we now speak, not of the two former.

Now how much is to be ascribed to this degree must be diligently considered. For human reason for the most part maketh more of ceremonies of this sort than of the doctrine itself, so that often there ariseth thence a peculiar idolatry and superstition, as the histories both Heathen and Ecclesiastical do testify: as, when the Heathen saw that the first parents had sacrificed a lamb at a certain time, they fell straightway into the error, namely, that sacrifice is the greatest worship of God, whereby God is appeased, and that the more costly the sacrifices are, the more acceptable by that very thing they are unto God. Hence many slew an hundred oxen, which kind of sacrifice they named ***ἑκατόμβην*** (a hecatomb), until afterwards they slew even men themselves, that the sacrifice might be the more precious. And the ***ἑκατόμβη*** was known and renowned among the learned Heathen. Whence **Athenaeus**, book 10, speaking of **Pythagoras**, saith: ***Ἀπολλόδωρος ὁ ἀριτμητικὸς καὶ θῦσαι αὐτὸν φήσιν ἑκατόμβην ἐπὶ τῷ εὑρηκέναι, ὅτι τριγώνου ὀρθογωνίου ἡ τὴν ὀρθὴν γωνίαν ὑποτείνουσα, ἴσον δύναται ταῖς περιεχούσαις.*** (Apollodorus the arithmetician saith that he even sacrificed a hecatomb upon having found that in a right-angled triangle the side subtending the right angle is equal in power to the sides containing it.) Hence the Epigram which **Laertius** reciteth, book 8:

> ***ἤνυκε πυθαγόρης τὸ περικλεὲς ἕωρετο γράμμα,***[^P05-5]  
> ***κεῖν' ἐφ' ὅτῳ κλεινὴν ἤγαγε βουθυσίην.***  

(When Pythagoras found that far-famed figure, that for which he brought a renowned sacrifice of oxen.)

This was that last but one of the first book of the *Elements* of **Euclid**. And many other examples are often recited, and our own age <!-- p.117 --> exhibiteth many great men who cleave more to ceremonies than to the truth of the doctrine.

Now although Politic men cry out that the authority of the Laws is shaken, and that the abuse of the doctrine of liberty is confirmed, yet before the complaints of the wise there is to be preferred the glory and truth of Christ, and the salvation of the Church. And both things are to be done: the truth is to be defended, and the abuses are to be reproved. In the midst between these we must walk; and that this may be rightly done, three rules are to be held by all the godly, which are plainly delivered in the loci.

**I. Rule:** It is sin to obey the edicts of any men whatsoever against the commandment of God. For *"we ought to obey God rather than men,"* **Acts 5**. So the three Israelites obeyed not the King commanding the adoration of the image, **Dan. 3**. And the Apostles complied not with the Pharisees commanding that they should not teach concerning Jesus Christ crucified and raised again. So at this day we must in no wise obey the Pontifical and royal Laws commanding anything against the truth of God: as, that the people hear not and read not the holy Bible; that the Mass, as they call it, be received; that the Saints be invoked; that the Priests be and remain unmarried, etc. To these there is to be opposed the most severe commandment of God: *"Flee Idols."*

**II. Rule:** Even Laws concerning *adiaphora* are to be withstood, when errors are sewn on to them. Now those things are named *adiaphora* which are middle and indifferent, neither commanded nor forbidden in the Law of God; as, not to eat flesh, to use common apparel, and the like. When the adversaries mingle errors with these, the adiaphora are not to be approved. Now the errors which are sewn on are these:

I. That by these works the Remission of sins is obtained.

II. Or, if Remission of sins be not obtained by them, yet that they are worships pleasing to God, even as the ceremony of the Nazarites, as, not to drink wine, was a worship of God, acceptable unto God.

The first error is altogether blasphemous against the Son of God, and against the grace of God, and needeth no answer.

<!-- p.118 -->

The second error is more subtle. But when the worships of God are defined, it falleth straightway of itself. For the worship of God is a work commanded by God, or instituted by a sure testimony of God, whereby God pronounceth that He is honoured. But other works, which are commanded only by the institution of men, are not worships of God. *"In vain they do worship me with the commandments of men,"* **Matt. 15**. And **Col. 2**: *"Let no man judge you in meat and drink, or in respect of an holyday."*

III. That those works are perfections, that is, that they are better and more pleasing to God than those which the Decalogue commandeth. Therefore they name them works of supererogation.

IIII. That they are altogether necessary, even outside the case of offence, so that they are to be condemned who observe them not; even as of old certain were condemned who kept not the day of the Passover with the rest. And in the year of Christ 159 there was a vehement contention concerning the observance of the Passover between **Anicetus**, the Roman Bishop, and **Polycarp**. And it was then determined that each should use his own custom in the observance of the Passover, yet so that the concord of the Church should not be broken on account of the unlikeness of time in that ceremony. And there was added this notable Rule: *It is not meet, on account of a dissonance of ceremonies, to break the consonance of faith.*

V. That those works are an imitation of the Mosaic Law, so that, as in the Law of Moses ceremonies were necessary, so now they are necessary. For so speak the Pontifical Decrees, and they transform the kingdom of Christ into a polity like unto the Jewish. This ***κακοζηλία*** (perverse imitation) is refuted in **Gal. 5** and **Heb. 7**.

VI. That the Bishops have power of framing traditions; which, nevertheless, the Gospel doth not permit unto them.

VII. Many distractions and grievous discords arise thence in the Church, and there often cometh to pass a monstrous cruelty of the Bishops against the godly, who, that they may escape those errors, obey not the edicts.

**The Third Rule** is this: Adiaphorous rites are to be kept in the reformed Churches for the sake of order; **1 Cor. 14**: *"Let all things be done decently and in order."* Thus, when the opinion <!-- p.119 --> of righteousness and of necessity is cast away, something may rightly be said of the adiaphora instituted for the sake of order in the reformed Churches, such as is the series of the feast days.

This also is to be added, that those traditions may be omitted, outside the case of offence, without sin, as the eating of flesh on the sixth day. And the rule is to be held: An offence given is never committed in adiaphora among those who know the cause of Christian liberty; but it is committed among those who do not yet know it. For these violate the traditions with a doubting of conscience, as there is an example in **Gal. 2** and **1 Cor. 9**. Therefore Paul saith: *"Him that is weak in the faith receive ye, but not to doubtful disputations."* But when the doctrine of true and false worships, and of liberty, is understood, then at length the unprofitable rites are to be cast away, lest the enemies of the true doctrine be confirmed, and lest the weaker be brought into doubt concerning the doctrine of liberty, and lest the Holy Spirit be grieved in the weaker, who are pained that they are accused of lightness. And the histories of **Spyridon** and **Attalus** are known and renowned. And thus much of human traditions and ceremonies. Now we shall add the usual division of ceremonies.

Of Ceremonies, or ***ἱερομηνίαι*** (sacred seasons), some are divine, others are human. Divine are those which have been commanded by God, either in the old Testament, as the sacrifices and the Levitical worships, or in the new Testament, as the Sacraments, which they call ***μυστήρια*** (mysteries), of which it shall be spoken below in its own place. Human ceremonies are those which they name ***ἀδιάφορα***, undefined, middle, indifferent things, neither commanded nor forbidden in the Law of God. Concerning these the Rules are to be held: **I.** They do not merit the Remission of sins. **II.** They are not worships of God. **III.** They do not please God more than the works of the Decalogue. **IIII.** They are not necessary to salvation, seeing that no works, whether commanded in the decalogue or not commanded, are necessary to salvation as a merit, but salvation standeth in the merit of the Son of God alone.

Concerning Sacrifices let it be observed, that six kinds are described in Leviticus, whereof three are ***ἱλαστικὰ*** or propitiatory, to wit, <!-- p.120 --> ***τυπικῶς*** (typically), but three are ***εὐχαριστικά*** (eucharistic). The first is the ***ὁλόκαυτον ἢ ὁλοκαύτωμα*** (whole burnt offering, or holocaust), *Olach*, *Calil*, **Ps. 51**, wholly burnt, *Brandopffer* (burnt offering), for a sign that the whole human nature is to be burnt up and abolished. This was ***διαλλακτικὸν*** (reconciling), for the appeasing of God; and the ***τελετὴ*** (rite of initiation) of the Greeks was instituted in imitation of it. The second is the ***θυσία περὶ ἁμαρτίας***, the offering for sin, *hasath*,[^P05-3] namely, for sin ***τῷ ἀκουσίῳ***, not voluntary, but committed either through error or through ignorance, *Sündopffer* (sin offering). This was ***ἀπολυτρωτικὸν***, redeeming the punishment of a certain offence, the original, or of some other sin of omission and ignorance. The third is the ***θυσία περὶ πλημμελείας*** (sacrifice for trespass), *Asam*, for sin ***τῷ ἑκουσίῳ***, voluntary, actual, *Schuldopffer* (trespass offering). Away from hence with the adversaries who feign that Christ suffered only for the original sin of men, that is, that He was made a victim ***περὶ ἁμαρτίας*** only, and not also ***περὶ πλημμελείας***; whereas nevertheless Isaiah manifestly saith: *"He shall lay down his soul for sin, Asam."* And Paul: *"Him who knew no sin he hath made sin,"* which is *Asam*. This same sacrifice also is ***ἀπολυτρωτικὸν*** (redeeming), for the redeeming of the punishments of all actual sins.

Of the ***εὐχαριστικοῖς*** (eucharistic sacrifices) the first is the ***δῶρον θυσία*** (gift-sacrifice), *Minha*, oblation, *Speißopffer* (meat offering), a giving of thanks for life and sustenance, immolation, libation. The second is the ***θυσία σωτηρίου***, *Zeba Salomim*, the sacrifice of peace offerings, *Danckopffer* (thank offering), a giving of thanks for certain benefits received. The third is the ***θυσία αἰνέσεως***, the sacrifice of praise and confession, *Thoda*, *Lobopffer* (praise offering), a Confession concerning the true and living God, distinguished from the rest of the feigned deities.

These sacrifices were types of Christ, and of His merit. But the thing itself having been exhibited, there is no more need of types; but now, as **St. Peter** admonisheth, there are to be offered unto God spiritual sacrifices, acceptable to God through and for the sake of Jesus Christ. For when Shiloh cometh, the Sceptre and the Lawgiver cease, **Gen. 49**. Therefore it is written, **Ps. 40**: *"Sacrifice thou didst not desire; therefore said I, Lo, I <!-- p.121 --> come."* And **Dan. 9**: *"He shall cause the sacrifice and the oblation to cease."* And **Jer. 31**: *"I will make a new covenant."* **Heb. 8**: *"In that he saith, A new, he hath made the first old,"* namely, at the time ***διορθώσεως*** (of reformation), **Heb. 9**. For where the body is, there is no more need of the shadow.
