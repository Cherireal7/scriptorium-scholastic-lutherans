# Translator brief — Selnecker, Paedagogia Christiana, Pars Prima (Frankfurt 1577)

Several translators work on this volume at the same time, each on a fixed range of page images. Follow this brief exactly so the parts join without seams.

## The book
Nikolaus Selnecker, *Paedagogiae Christianae, denuo et ultimo auctae, Pars Prima* (Frankfurt am Main, 1577): a preface, a sermon on the Word of God, the distinction of Law and Gospel, and then a long exposition of the Ten Commandments for teachers and learners, with questions, objections and answers, closing with free will, the use of philosophy and the end of man. Public domain.

## What to produce
An English translation of everything printed: headings, questions and answers, objections and responses, lists, tables, verse, marginal notes, Scripture references. Skip only running heads, page numbers, signature marks, catchwords, stamps and handwriting. No summarising and no skipping; a long answer stays long. **English only: do not transcribe the Latin.**

### Register
Early modern theological English, close to the Latin ("doth", "hath"; "thou" in quoted Scripture and prayer). Keep Selnecker's numbered divisions and his question-and-answer form.

### Scripture
Quotations are conformed to the **King James Version (1611)** wherever Selnecker's Latin agrees in sense; where his Latin plainly says something different (he cites from memory, paraphrases, or follows the Latin Bible), translate his Latin. Use no other English Bible. Keep his reference exactly as printed, in bold (`Psal. 51.` → **Ps. 51**), even where it is a slip; where his Psalm number differs from the KJV number, add the KJV number once in square brackets: **Ps. 50 [51]**. The commandments themselves in KJV wording.

### Markdown conventions
- Do NOT use `# ` headings (the editor sets the top-level divisions) unless your task tells you to. Use `## ` for each section title that the print sets in large capitals or as a centred title; `### ` for lesser headings and for each question printed as a heading. Latin title in italics, `·`, then English: `## *De Sabbatho Creationis* · Of the Sabbath of Creation`.
- Short questions inside the run of the text: a bold paragraph. "Obiectio" / "Respondeo" / "Respondeo ad maiorem": bold lead-ins (**Objection.** / **I answer.** / **I answer to the major.**). A syllogism: block-quote lines, one premise per line.
- Names of authorities in bold on mention (**Augustine**, **D. Luther**, **Philip Melanchthon**, **Cicero**).
- Quotations: italic inside double quotation marks, `*"..."*`; a displayed quotation: a block-quote paragraph beginning `> `.
- Verse: block-quote lines, one per printed line, each ending with two spaces. English only, line for line, no rhyme.
- Numbered lists as printed.
- Greek: keep each Greek word or phrase as the print has it (ligatures resolved; do not correct its spelling or accents), bold-italic, followed by an English gloss in parentheses unless the print itself translates it. Hebrew likewise, with transliteration.
- German words or sentences: keep in italics, with the English after.
- Latin technical terms: English, with the Latin in italics in parentheses on first use in a section.
- Marginal notes: `*[Margin: …]*` at the point they stand beside.
- Do not add ornaments (`— ❦ —`).
- Page marks: where each printed page begins put, exactly, `<!-- p.N -->` (N = printed page number), on its own line between paragraphs or inline between words. They are invisible in the book and are used for checking. Every page of your range must have its mark, except your first page if it begins inside the previous translator's paragraph.
- Endnotes, sparingly: `[^KEY-1]` in the text and `[^KEY-1]: text` at the end of your file (KEY is given in your task). Use them for (a) a reading where you translate against an evident misprint — say what the print reads; (b) a term a reader needs. Identify a source (a classical author, a Father, Luther) ONLY if you are certain of it; do not give book and line numbers from memory. When unsure, no note.
- A word you cannot read: best reading followed by `[?]`, listed in your report.
- No raw HTML other than the page marks. No front matter.
