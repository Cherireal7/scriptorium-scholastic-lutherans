## Doubtful, not changed

- p.145 (part 07, Orpheus verse 4) — `[?]` kept on ***ἐν μενίδεοσί***. The image shows a first ligature most likely ἐν, then μεν-ί-δε- and a final group that may be -οσί or the σσ ligature (-σσί). The word meant is probably Homeric μενέεσσι ("in his wrath"), which agrees with the translation, but the print cannot be read letter by letter with confidence.
- p.157 (part 07) — `[?]` kept on **1 Tim. 2**. The printed numeral after "1. Timoth." has been overwritten in ink by a later reader (the stroke turns it into a crossed 2); the printed figure underneath cannot be made out. The passage is 1 Tim. 2:1–2, so 2 is right in sense, but whether the print itself has 2 cannot be confirmed.
- p.142 (part 07) — Anteros verse, line 5, "Haec ut scilicet Anterotis una est": the translation's "the one force of Anteros" takes "vis" from the next line; this is a defensible rendering of an elliptical line and was left alone.
- p.145 (part 07) — Euripides line 7: the translation has ἡδίον; the print seems to show ἥδιον (breathing and accent unclear at this size). Not changed.
- p.149 (part 07) — "Gen. 19" for the name Abimael (actually Gen. 10:28) is as printed; reference kept, no note added.
- p.125 (part 06) — the print spells the Greek word in Latin letters here ("ethelotrescia"); the translator's *ethelothreskia* was left as it stands.
- p.132–134 (part 06) — the end of the Scheurl quotation is a judgement: "Cum autem ad ventres ... desino" could also be read as Scheurl's own close. The evidence ("Noribergenses mei", the pre-Reformation language about priests) shows the quotation goes at least as far as Ambrose; it was ended there.
