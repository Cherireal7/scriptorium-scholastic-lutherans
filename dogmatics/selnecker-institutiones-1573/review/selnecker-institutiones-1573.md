# Translator's Note

*This working translation of the Second Part of Selnecker's* Institutio Christianae Religionis *is at its kick-off state. The paragraphs below record the intended text base, the editorial conventions binding on the eventual body prose, and — with full disclosure — what remains to be verified once the facsimile has been sighted paragraph by paragraph.*

## 1. The text base

The English rendered here follows the **1579** edition of Nikolaus Selnecker's ***Institutionis Christianae Religionis Pars Prima.*** This is the first of three parts of Selnecker's mature systematic-dogmatic Loci-work: **Pars I** — the present volume — covers the paradigm-Reformation-era Loci-order ***De verbo Dei, De Deo, De Creatione, De Angelis, De hominibus, De peccato, De lege, De cruce, De consolatione***, preceded by a substantive ***Praemonitio de nonnullis controversis articulis*** on the Formula-of-Concord-era inner-Lutheran controversies (especially the anti-Flacian original-sin-as-accident question); **Pars II** covers Justification through Christian Ethics; **Pars III** covers the Last Things and closing controversies.

The working text-base is the digitised **Bayerische Staatsbibliothek München** copy (StaBibl München shelfmark **Dogm. 924-1**), preserved in Google-Books scan `36616462510015` (489 pages of the 8vo octavo imprint at 21 MB). The PDF is stored locally at `sources-original/Institutio_Christianae_Religionis.pdf`, with a `pdftotext -layout` extraction cached at `sources-original/ia-1579-pars1-full.txt` (28,024 lines) for reference.

The bibliographic authority for the 1579 Selnecker Pars II is **VD16 S 5566** (Jena, Jakob Tröster) — held at HAB Wolfenbüttel under signature 577 Theol.; the Pars I VD16 identifier is likely **VD16 S 5565** (same printer, same year, adjacent register number). The specialist Reformation-controversy bibliography **Controversia et Confessio** (Herzog August Bibliothek, Wolfenbüttel) indexes the Pars II under entry `fa69041c-1638-4246-99e4-4be5c37a1710`.

**Pars II** — available in fully-digitised form at BSB Munich under identifier `bsb11295447` (IIIF manifest reports 107 canvases; true extent is **696 canvases** confirmed by per-canvas HEAD-probing 2026-08-31, a BSB-manifest-under-report bug we have documented previously with the Kirchner *Enchiridion*). Google-Books alternate scans at IDs `oeBoAAAAcAAJ` and `xRE8AAAAcAAJ`.

**Pars III** — available in Google-Books scan `E6VoAAAAcAAJ` (covers *De Authoritate et Certitudine Verbi Divini* etc.).

## 2. What the author himself supplies

Selnecker's own hand supplies the entirety of the volume — the *Dedicatio,* the *Praefatio ad Lectorem,* the numbered Loci themselves (with their *quaestiones,* *definitiones,* *distinctiones,* *argumenta,* and *fontes solutionum*), and the closing colophon. His entries on the individual Loci quote — often at length — from Scripture, the church fathers (Augustine, Chrysostom, Jerome, Cyril, Athanasius, Cyprian, Basil, Gregory of Nazianzus, Gregory the Great, Bernard), the Lutheran confessional documents (Augsburg Confession, Apology, Smalcald Articles, Small and Large Catechisms, and — importantly — the just-then-completed *Formula of Concord* of 1577, of which Selnecker was co-author), and Melanchthon (his *praeceptor*).

All of these citations are preserved unchanged in the translation. Modern-critical equivalents (WA/LW for Luther; CR/MSA for Melanchthon; BSLK/*Kolb-Wengert* for the confessional documents; PL/PG/CCSL/SC for the church fathers; ESV numbering for scripture alongside Selnecker's own citation form on first occurrence per Locus) are consigned to the endnotes.

## 3. What the Scholastic Lutherans have added — and where

*Nothing in the body text is our own.* All editorial additions are relegated to numbered endnotes at the end of the volume. These endnotes supply:

- Standard modern citations for the Reformation-era works Selnecker cites (WA/LW for Luther; CR/MSA for Melanchthon; BSLK/*Kolb-Wengert* for confessional documents including the *Formula of Concord* Selnecker himself helped draft)
- Standard modern citations for the church fathers (PL/PG/CCSL/SC volume:column; NPNF English vol:page)
- Biographical dates on first named mention of contemporaries and predecessors
- Cross-references to the paradigm *Formula of Concord* Articles III (Justification), V (Law and Gospel), VI (Third Use of Law), VII (Lord's Supper), VIII (Person of Christ), X (Adiaphora), and XI (Predestination) — all of which interface directly with Selnecker's Loci in this volume
- Cross-references to the parallel Loci corpora of the same generation: Melanchthon's *Loci communes* (1521/1543/1555), Chemnitz's *Loci theologici* (1591 posth.), and Gerhard's later *Loci theologici* (1610–1622)
- Cross-references to the *Scholastic Lutherans* corpus proper — most notably [[chytraeus-catechismus-1594]] (parallel Latin catechetical work by fellow Formula-of-Concord co-author) and [[kirchner-enchiridion]] (parallel German-language catechetical handbook of the same generation)

## 4. Editorial conventions

Locked from the Praetorius trilogy and continued through Chytraeus, Kirchner, Quenstedt, and Große. See [[reference-scholastic-lutherans-glossary]] for the master vocabulary list and register-norms.

Highlights:

- **Register:** scholarly / academic-critical English; reverent but not archaising. Preserve substance of Selnecker's Loci-analytic style — the numbered *distinctiones,* *quaestiones,* *definitiones,* *argumenta,* *fontes solutionum,* and *usus practicus* — as the paradigm mature-Melanchthonian *Loci-format* which Selnecker inherits from his *praeceptor*.
- **Technical Latin terms:** preserved on first occurrence in each Locus in italic (*justificatio, sanctificatio, unio mystica, communicatio idiomatum, causa meritoria, causa instrumentalis, satisfactio vicaria, praesentia realis, manducatio oralis, adiaphora,* etc.), glossed briefly, used in English thereafter — unless the term is itself the subject of the Locus, in which case the Latin is retained throughout.
- **Greek** appears where Selnecker quotes NT/patristic sources; preserved in polytonic script with parenthetical transliteration and English gloss on first substantive occurrence.
- **Hebrew** appears where Selnecker quotes OT/rabbinic sources; preserved in pointed Hebrew script with transliteration and gloss on first occurrence.
- **Scripture:** ESV for the English; Selnecker's own citation form (Vulgate numbering) noted parenthetically on first citation per Locus.
- **Confessional documents:** Selnecker cites the pre-*Concord* confessional writings (AC, Ap, SC, LC, SA — 1530–1537) as ***confessio nostra*** and the *Formula of Concord* (1577) as ***Formula Concordiae*** or occasionally ***nostra Concordia.*** Modern references consigned to endnotes: BSLK / *Kolb-Wengert.*
- **Named authors first occurrence:** append life-dates in the endnote, not in body.
- **Rubrication, typeface, layout:** locked via `shared/reference-custom.docx` and identical to the completed Chytraeus, Kirchner, Quenstedt, and Große volumes.

# Nikolaus Selnecker — Biographical Sketch

*(For the reader coming to Selnecker for the first time.)*

**Nikolaus Selnecker** (also spelled *Selneccer* or *Schellenecker*) was born on **5 December 1530 at Hersbruck,** a small Franconian town near Nürnberg. His father, a jurist, brought the family to Nürnberg while Selnecker was still a child, and it was there that Selnecker received his first musical education — a formation that would define one strand of his later life-work as a paradigm-Lutheran hymn-writer.

At **Wittenberg** from 1550 he studied under **Philipp Melanchthon** — whose *Loci communes* method Selnecker would later adopt and adapt in the present *Institutio* — and took his *Magister* in 1554. From 1557 he served as *Hofprediger* to Kurfürst August of Saxony at Dresden, an office he held until 1565 when the paradigm ***Crypto-Calvinist controversy*** at the Dresden court forced his departure. He became **Professor of Theology at Jena** in 1565, then at **Leipzig** from 1568 — with interruptions during the second phase of the Crypto-Calvinist controversy 1570–1574 (when the strict-Lutherans were briefly displaced) — and finally returned to Leipzig from 1574 as *Superintendent* and *Pfarrer* at St. Thomas, an office he held (with a single year at Braunschweig-Wolfenbüttel 1571) until his death.

Selnecker's **confessional-Lutheran significance** rests on his role as one of the **six co-authors of the *Formula of Concord*** (1577). The six — Jakob Andreä (Tübingen), Martin Chemnitz (Braunschweig), David Chytraeus (Rostock), Andreas Musculus (Frankfurt/Oder), Christoph Körner (Frankfurt/Oder), and Selnecker himself (Leipzig) — worked between 1576 and 1580 to bring the *inner-Lutheran* controversies of the second half of the sixteenth century to a paradigm confessional settlement: the *Solida Declaratio* of the *Formula of Concord.* Selnecker's specific contribution was the *Solida Declaratio* Article XI (**Predestination**), whose paradigm anti-double-predestination Lutheran formulation — *God has predestined to salvation but not to damnation; the reprobate are lost by their own contumacy* — became the paradigm confessional-Lutheran position against both the Reformed *decretum absolutum* on one side and the Semi-Pelagian *praevisa merita* framework on the other.

Selnecker's **hymnody** is his second lasting legacy. Author of the paradigm ***"Ach bleib bei uns, Herr Jesu Christ"*** (*EG* 347 — the paradigm-canonical *Ausgangs-Choral* still sung in confessional-Lutheran and Reformed hymnody worldwide) and of dozens more, Selnecker's paradigm ***Christliche Psalmen*** (1587) is one of the paradigm confessional-Lutheran hymn-collections of the whole *Concord*-generation.

Selnecker died at **Leipzig on 24 May 1592,** and was buried in the *Paulinerkirche.* His epitaph, composed by his son, closed with the paradigm confessional-Lutheran memorial-verse ***Christus mihi vita, mors lucrum*** (Phil. 1:21).

The present *Institutio Christianae Religionis* is Selnecker's mature systematic-dogmatic Loci-work, composed in the years immediately after the *Formula of Concord* (1577) and issued in three Parts at Jena by **Jakob Tröster** in 1578–1580. Pars II — the present volume — appeared in **1579** and covers the paradigm **Reformation-era *ordo salutis*** from Justification through Christian Ethics, interfacing at every point with the Loci Selnecker had helped draft in the *Formula*'s *Solida Declaratio.*

## Dedication to the Consuls and Senators of the Republic of Leipzig

*(pp. 3–5 of the imprint. Full title:* AMPLISSIMIS ET CLARISSIMIS VV. DOMINIS Consulibus & Senatoribus Reipublicae Lipsensis, Dominis & fautoribus suis honorandis. Nicolaus Selneccerus. S. D. *— "To the most eminent and most illustrious men, the Lords Consuls and Senators of the Commonwealth of Leipzig, his honoured lords and patrons. Nikolaus Selnecker sends greeting.")*

**Most eminent Men, honoured lords and friends.** Since now once again my *Institutiones* were to come forth in public,² I wished to offer them to Your Excellencies — an argument of my gratitude and a testimony of the doctrine which now these thirty years past, by God's kindness, I have learned and profess in these churches and schools.³ Although the use of my little labours is slight, and I should prefer to *whisper in my corner* rather than to *wander with tongue and pen through the regions*, to use the words of the great Luther,⁴ and I acknowledge the leanness of my own pen — yet, since I see those very little pages of mine, however they be, my ***Paedagogia,*** ***Institutiones,*** ***Analecta,*** ***Explicatio Symbolorum,*** ***Catechesis,*** and other like things, sought after by many good men, I am less anxious over my own will than that I should serve one who asks.⁵

Would that God the God of peace might restore to us the liberty of writing *didactically* rather than *critically against one another!*⁶ Polemics against obstinate enemies will not cease, nor ought they to cease. But that among ourselves there still boils the bile with which *brother contradicts brother,* — this is much to be lamented.

*I do not wish to increase strife.* And if I shall have discovered anything of Christ in any man, I will deal with him as brother, gently, so long as I shall live. And truly, the closer I am to my grave, the greater the moderation I require⁷ — without which our churches (as I see) will perish. Ambition, however, and greed, deceit, rashness, levity, calumnies, contempt for others — these I *detest,* and hate from my soul; and with Luther I say: *I hold him to be a theologian, not who promises many and great things, or says or teaches them, but who lives in a holy and theological manner.*⁸ In truth, they that hold otherwise are not led by the Spirit of Christ, as daily experience shows — over which we rather pour forth words to Christ and lamentations mixed with tears, than that we should reveal to the impure and secure world anything by way of scandal (which God will judge), — since the world is not amended thereby, but rather made worse.

*May God convert those who are to be converted, and restrain the insolent* — who, having formerly disturbed, disturb the more, and by calumnies and lies wish to conquer and prevail, and who harm our churches, and estrange the souls of many, who otherwise would readily agree with us. — I ask Your Excellencies that you may take my address in good part; for one who, on behalf of you all and each, and on behalf of the commonwealth, does not cease to beseech Christ, that He himself may rule, cover, teach, lead, and defend the Churches and Schools of this city; and that He may suffer us, under the wings of the illustrious Elector,⁹ to be as *turtle-doves* upon them. **Leipzig, in the month of May, 1579.**

# Praemonitio de Nonnullis Controversis Articulis

## Explanation and Admonition concerning Certain Controverted Articles¹⁰

### §1. On Original Sin — Whether it is Substance or Accident

*(pp. 6–20 of the imprint. This praemonitio directly interfaces with* Formula of Concord *Article I of 1577 — of which Selnecker was co-author — which rejected Matthias Flacius Illyricus's* substantia-Lehre *that original sin is the substantial form of fallen man, and affirmed the paradigm confessional-Lutheran position that original sin is a corrupting accident.)*

**Some contend** that original sin is, and is called, ***the man himself,*** who is and as he now is — that is, the substance of the man himself, his essence, his nature, his soul, his body, his members, his motions, and all his actions. This fantasy is such that pious ears shudder at its very phrasing, still more at the thing itself. For that substances have been *created by God,* our faith teaches; and we believe that Christ has assumed our flesh and our nature *without any sin;* and that we are *washed from sin* and have sin *remitted to us,* and are *sanctified,* that we may have dominion over sin, and shall be raised again to eternal life, and shall live for ever *without sin,* as Job says: ***"Girded with this flesh, and with these mine eyes, I shall see the Lord."*** So also in the Symbol we say: ***"I believe in the resurrection of this flesh of mine, and life everlasting."***¹¹

And although, in things and actions *spiritual,* there is in the unregenerate man no congruity whatever with the will of God — but rather enmity and hostility, sin, aversion from God, and adverse inclination, thought, affection, and action, in all things — nevertheless the ***creature of God*** must always be distinguished from the *corruption and sin* which was *not created by God* but came in from another source, from the Devil, and which has depraved, infected, perverted, and subjected the created substance of the man to death.¹² For one thing in the man is *the work of God,* and another thing is *the work of the Devil.* And that this distinction can be observed *utterly takes away* the whole controversy — and does in no wise extenuate sin, since it demonstrates that *the whole man* — created by God, consisting of soul and body — is *corrupted* and *dead in sin* (Eph. 2), *dead* namely by that spiritual death which endures so long as the man is not regenerate.

As, however, the man, in respect of his *created substance,* is not himself that spiritual death (which is followed both by the first or bodily death and by the second or eternal death), but is *dead in sin,* — so by the same reasoning he is not himself *sin,* since sin is that *engrafted corruption* which Paul names in the singular number as it were *the tree itself,* whose fruits are *sins* [in the plural]. And whoever are not led by the Spirit of Christ, these are *dead in sins,* even though they live *the animal life.* For the joining of soul and body is not properly *true life in God,* or *before God*; but is *death* in the unregenerate and in the damned, joined with eternal perdition. And it is for this cause that Paul says: ***"We were by nature children of wrath."*** *"By nature,"* he says — not *by imitation,* not *by custom,* but *by natural conception from those very parents from whom we are born* — yet not in so far as *nature has been created by God,* but in so far as it has been *corrupted through the Devil's suggestion within itself.* And this *total corruption* the holy Fathers understand, and pious minds — when they behold themselves before God's judgement-seat, and confess that they are *not only most miserable sinners,* but *before God are sin itself,* — a guilty thing and mass, defiled, abject, lost, and unable to stand before God's judgement.

I will now recite ***three arguments*** of those who simply affirm sin to be the man himself.¹³

**First:** *"Whatever the man is, and whatever is in the man, is sin"* (1 John 3). *Therefore the whole man is sin.* — We firmly deny the minor. For that the man is a man is not sin. That he has a soul, a body, and members is not sin. That he lives, walks, wakes, sleeps is not sin. That he sees, hears, speaks, feels is not sin. But that he does not live rightly, and does not rightly use nature and the gifts of God — *this* is sin, both in things spiritual and also in things physical, political, and economic. And it is for this sin that the man is damned — as a slave is cast out of the house, not because he is a slave, but because he is an *unfaithful* slave. And the man neither dies nor is damned because he is a man created by God, because he has mind and will, soul, body, eyes and ears, tongue, and other members — but because, subjected to the Devil and to his own flesh, he sets himself against God, and, turned from God, does the things contrary to God, an enemy of God, and *by nature* a child of wrath, through the Devil's fault and his own.

**Second argument:** *"Where the matter is evil, there the form cannot be good. The matter of which the man consists is evil (Psalm 51). Therefore the substantial form in the man is evil, that is, sin. But the form is that which gives being to the thing. Therefore the essence and nature of the man is sin."* — We answer to the minor: The *"matter of which"* is evil, not by reason of *creation, propagation per se,* or *conservation,* which are the work of God, but by reason of *corruption* and *destruction,* which are the work of the Devil. This corruption is not the substantial form of the man. For the substantial form God created, as we confess in the first article of the Symbol. This substantial form *gives being to the thing,* that the man may be man in soul and body, and consist of both faculties and essential properties. But *accidental form,* which was *not created by God,* does not give being to the thing. For a man was, is, and shall be, and shall remain, a man forever — even if there had been no sin, or ever should be, in him. Sin does not make the man, or the soul, or the body — but *God* makes the man, as it is written: *"He hath made us, and not we ourselves."* Therefore sin is not the man, nor is it the man's soul and body.¹⁴

For this cause Luther says on Genesis 24 [in the *Vorlesungen über 1. Mose*]: *"He hath made us. We are His creature and work; therefore He cares for His work even in the midst of sins, death, hell, disgrace, and ignominy."*

**Third argument:** *"Whatever we acquire by inheritance from Adam is sin. But nature we acquire by carnal propagation, both by inheritance from Adam, and we are the image of Adam, as it is written (Gen. 5): 'Adam begat one according to his image and likeness.' Therefore our nature is sin."* — We answer: This same argument refutes the *Manichees.*¹⁵ For two things are here posited: ***us*** and ***that which we acquire from Adam*** — just as David also always distinguishes himself from his sin, saying: *"My sin is ever before me. Cleanse me from my sin. I acknowledge my iniquity. In iniquity I was conceived"* (which is also to be understood of the unregenerate — that is, of all men who are born of the flesh); *"in sins did my mother conceive me. Turn away Thy face from my sins, not from me."*¹⁶ etc. But *we* are the work of God, and we *acquire from Adam and our parents* that which is evil. And yet propagation is also the work of God, in which Adam — evil and infected through the Devil's and his own fault — infects and perverts us.

Wherefore Luther on Genesis 25 nobly says: *"Carnal generation, however foul and corrupt, is nevertheless not native to nature, but is a most great gift of God, and a divine creation is the begetting and bringing forth of sons or daughters. Therefore we call God our creator and our father. Nor let us be moved by that deformity of sin and of penalty by which generation is overwhelmed — that we should not feel it to be a blessing of God, and a work acceptable and pleasing to God. And the deformity is to be distinguished from the creature, which God will have to be glorious, and greatly esteemed"* etc.¹⁷

*To the major*, therefore, we answer: *"Everything which we acquire from Adam is sin"* — namely, ***by reason of corruption,*** not by reason of *creation, propagation per se,* or *conservation.* For each of these separately is a divine work and belongs to God alone. And if the work of propagation were not so foully infected with lust and turpitude, we could not but be dumb-struck at the dignity and miracle of so great a work. It is not badly added, that in respect of spiritual things and actions sin is everything which we acquire from Adam; but not in respect of physical things.

*To the minor* we must answer: *We acquire nature from Adam,* — as from a means ordained by God, who alone is Creator, and is the *first Father* of all. For Adam does not create us, but God does — who uses Adam for the propagation of the human nature. And our nature, substance, soul, mind, will, body etc. are ***from God,*** not from Adam — save as from a means ordained for the propagation of our nature.

*Wherefore Luther on John 8 says thus:* ***"He who hears not the Word of God, is not of God, but of the Devil."*** This is not so to be understood, as though the Devil had created those who do not hear the Word of God. Mouth, eyes, reason, and the rest are the *creature and gift of God.* And the essence is to be distinguished from the *use.* A mendacious man and deceiver has indeed a good tongue *created by God,* but the *use* of his tongue is from the Devil, against God. So also the eyes, the heart, and the other members are — as to substance — *created by God,* but *their use is evil.*¹⁸

From these things it appears that impious and blasphemous is that voice which says: *"The soul and body of the man are from the Devil"; "the Devil has created us to consist of soul and body"; "the pregnant mother bears in her womb the creature of the Devil, not of God"; "sin is baptised in Baptism, and not the creature of God"; "after the Fall there is no place for the distinction between the work of God and the work of the Devil, unless conversion have taken place"; "the man is not only made sin itself, but is also essentially transformed into the image of Satan, and his very substance is a new essence made by the robber Satan through the killing of the prior, and there is now a new monster established by the Devil through the apostasy of the man."*¹⁹ For these were the ***monstrous words of Flacius Illyricus.***²⁰

### §2. On the Term "Accident" and the Mature Post-*Formula* Formulation²¹

**It is asked** concerning the term "Accident" whether original sin can rightly and fittingly be called an ***Accident.***

In the schools the received answer is: *"Everything which is not substance is accident."* Wherefore also Augustine calls sin an ***accident,*** and Luther calls it a ***quality,*** a ***disease,*** a ***leprosy,*** and ***the greatest evil.*** There is added also this proof: **Whatever has invaded from elsewhere a nature created by God is not itself the nature created by God, or the substance of the man established by God, but is *something adventitious, a certain accident*** — not separable from nature in this life (as whiteness may be separated from a wall), but *inseparable and essential,* or *implanted into nature such as it now is.* Many consider the term *"Accident"* to be too *thin and lean* for expressing the atrocity of so great an evil as original sin is in the man — which would indeed be entirely true, were there not added the declaration which Luther has expressed by the terms ***"essential sin"*** and ***"natural sin"*** — that is, *what is of the essence and nature of the man.* For the whole nature, or essence — all the faculties, the soul, the mind, the will, the heart, the members, the life, the actions, and all things which are in the man — have been infected and perverted by the venom of the Devil, that is, by original sin, from which afterward arise the manifold errors of the whole life, and sins without number. For not only *bare accidents* in the man, qualities, and ornaments, have been depraved: sin has *so* invaded the nature or substance of the man, has penetrated it, occupied it, and by the force of its leprosy has totally permeated and corrupted it, that most rightly it is called ***the essential sin*** of the soul and body — propagated from man to man by carnal propagation, ***from the sole of the foot to the crown of the head,*** as *Bernard* says.²²

Wherefore we ***disapprove and condemn*** the *Papist and Pelagian Accident* — which affirms that nature by itself is not evil (which, spoken in place, we do not reprehend, as also in the Apology of the Augsburg Confession), and which pretends that the natural [powers] have remained intact, and thus extenuate original sin. For we know, and we miserably experience, that our whole nature — before God, after the Fall — has been made evil, guilty, and damned, not by reason of *creation and conservation,* but by reason of ***corruption.*** For the question is of the corruption of the created nature.²³ Hither belong the manifest testimonies of Holy Scripture: **Gen. 6** *(the shaping, the fabrication, the fashioning, the carnal make-work, the thought of the heart, all things of the mass of the heart)*; **Rom. 8** *(evil from childhood, enmity against God, not subject to the law of God)*; **Ps. 51** *(In iniquity I was born, and in sins my mother did conceive and warm me)*; **Job 14** *(Who shall make the clean from the unclean?)*; **Prov. 20** *(Who can say, My heart is clean?)*; **Jer. 17** *(The human heart is perverse above all things)*; **Rom. 5** *(By the offence of one, evil is propagated to all men unto condemnation)*; **Eph. 2** *(By nature we were children of wrath — that is, so far as we are born of the flesh, we are guilty of eternal damnation).*

***In the Smalcald Articles*** our Churches teach thus concerning this evil: ***"The man must hear this judgement: 'You are nothing, however many of you there be, whether manifest sinners or saints. You must become other, and do otherwise than now you are and do, whatever now you may be — great, wise, powerful, holy. For no one is good, Psalm 14.' Item, 'Inwardly and in the skin we are evil, and we must simply be made new and other men.'"*** And true repentance is not by parts, as the Papist. For true repentance does not dispute what is sin in the man and what is not sin, but prostrates the whole man and accuses him of sin — without the *Papist, Scholastic, Pelagian* distinctions.²⁴

And elsewhere Luther says: *"It is as true that before Thee I am a sinner as that sin is my nature and essence — even from the very beginning of my conception, before word or work or thought or the following life. I am an evil tree, and by nature a child of wrath."* And again: *"Original sin is not committed as other sins are perpetrated: it is itself, lives, and acts, and perpetrates all sins, and is essential sin — that is, which has infected the whole essence of the man, and is the source and origin of all other sins."* On these things there is no controversy among those teaching rightly. And yet ***(as Luther often warns) one thing is nature founded, propagated, sustained, conserved by God,*** — ***and another thing is sin, or natural vice,*** which is congenital to all by carnal propagation (not by imitation, or habit, or acquired by progress of time), but inhabits the nature of the man as its own house and dwelling — corrupting it totally through all its pores and grains (so to speak), particles, passages, and whatever inmost thing lies hidden in soul, body, and members, under whatever name it may be called — with its own leprosy and rot; and stripping it of all perfection, integrity, wholeness, spiritual life, and dignity in every respect; and subjecting the whole to itself and to death (both first and second).²⁵

With this declaration prefaced, how the old and received term ***Accident*** is now to be used may be seen. For we have no better and more fitting term in the schools. And this is and remains the truest rule: ***Whatever is not created by God, but has approached or befallen the nature created by God from elsewhere, is an accident.*** But sin is such and so great an accident — so atrocious, horrible, and pestilent — as no other in the whole nature of things, as appears from what has been said above. And indeed the man is a man — consisting of rational soul and human body — in whatever state he is considered: whether before the Fall, or after the Fall, or in regeneration, or in resurrection. And the man is a man ***by reason of his substance created and conserved by God.***

But the man after the Fall is not such as he was before the Fall. And that he has not remained such, and is not such as he was before the Fall, ***original sin or hereditary sin*** effects — which has approached from elsewhere (that is, was not established by God, but arose from the Devil, and was drawn in by the will of the man), and has thoroughly and totally corrupted the whole mass of the man from the first Adam to the last — subjecting it to death and eternal damnation — so that there is nothing in the man which can be called or thought — much less be and be esteemed — worthy of God's grace and salvation, even in the least; but the whole man, consisting of soul and body, is, is called, and remains ***turned from God, adverse to God, and rejected by God*** — the ***bond-slave of the Devil,*** and the ***enemy of God*** — unless through and on account of the Son of God he be received into grace, converted, and saved.²⁶

### §3. On Free Will and the Beginning of Conversion²⁷

**It is necessary** to distinguish between the ***work of God*** and the ***work of the Devil*** in the man. The unregenerate will is ***most busy toward evil*** — namely in ***resisting, rebelling, contradicting, opposing, repelling.*** The regenerate will, however, is ***not idle*** in ***assenting, embracing, believing, and apprehending Christ, growing up into faith and its fruits.***

**Therefore the natural or animal man can resist,** and does resist, God and the Gospel. But to assent — without the grace and drawing of the Holy Spirit — he cannot. And by *assent* I do not understand ***historical or literal or carnal*** assent, but ***true and spiritual*** assent, proper to faith — indeed faith itself, assent, and confidence. ***The man is placed*** (says our Luther) ***in mere passive potency, and not active*** — that is, in things spiritual he can plainly nothing prepare or think, speak or effect, which may be salutary — nor can he *convert himself,* or by any preparation from any manner make himself apt, capable, and fit for God's grace, for righteousness, and for salvation. ***"It is not of him that willeth, nor of him that runneth, but of God that showeth mercy — who giveth and worketh both to will and to do,"*** as Paul says.

Hence our ***Augsburg Confession*** in **Article V** says thus: ***"The Holy Spirit effects faith — where and when it pleases God — in those who hear the Gospel."*** Therefore also in the conversion of the man there ***do not concur three efficient causes*** — since there is ***but one efficient cause,*** the Holy Spirit — who operates through the Word (which is the *instrumental cause*) — and works in the man's mind and will, as in the subject to be converted.²⁸

**Three things** therefore concur, which in the schools are enunciated under the names of causes, but not in equal reason, nor is efficacy to be ascribed to one as it is to another. The Holy Spirit is as it were ***the soul of the Word in the man.*** But the man is not idle — nay, rather does something, indeed does too much — before he receives the Holy Spirit; for he resists the Word, and mocks the doctrine, contemns it, and persecutes it. But when he has received the Holy Spirit, then at length he acts rightly, and is not idle in resisting his flesh, his reason, sin, the Devil, the world — and in obeying God — and this too *not by his own powers,* but because he is ***led and driven by the Holy Spirit*** (Rom. 8). In conversion itself, however, the man — that is, the mind and will of the man, whose action and efficacy must be spiritual and salutary — can nothing whatever without the Spirit of God; but is ***illuminated, regenerated, renewed, converted,*** which is what Luther meant when he said the man behaves as ***merely passive.*** All the pious confess this, and willingly confess it, and are glad thereof, and give thanks to God — by whose hand and Spirit they, being converted, renounce themselves, that is, their flesh — and pray that they may always be led and driven by the Spirit of God.²⁹

*Some, however, still press this and that, by which they endeavour to ascribe to the man's will some action, some co-operation, some capacity, aptitude, or faculty even in things spiritual* — against the express words of Paul, that the animal man (that is, one living the natural life) perceives not by reason and sense those things which are of the Spirit; and that we are not fit of ourselves to think anything, as of ourselves; and that God works in us both to will and to perform; and that no one comes to Christ unless the Father shall have drawn him by Word and Spirit. — If therefore any bring in something diverse, let us answer them as Luther nobly answered: ***"Free will, without the grace of God, is plainly not free, but is immutably captive and the bondslave of evil; since it cannot turn from itself to the good. Grant me this, and I concede to thee: make the power of free will not only small, but Angelic, make it (if thou canst) plainly divine — if only thou wilt add this ineluctable appendix, that without the grace of God thou callest it 'inefficacious' — soon thou hast taken from it every power. For what is an inefficacious power, save no power at all?"***³⁰

Therefore we ***know of no synergism in the unregenerate man.*** For which cause also ***D. Philipp [Melanchthon],*** questioned by ***D. Brenz,*** answered that in his *Loci* he was speaking of the regenerate man, not of the unregenerate. — To resist and to be unwilling belongs to the carnal and unregenerate man — as the Son of God says: *"How often would I have gathered thee, and thou wouldest not?"* Wherefore the man's perdition is the man's own fault, as it is written in Hosea: *"O Israel, thy perdition is from thee."* But to assent, to be converted, to believe, rightly to know God, to fear Him, to obey Him, to invoke Him, and to have similar movements — and to bring forth spiritual actions — this is not of the unregenerate man, nor of his own powers, but the work of ***one and only the Holy Spirit,*** through the Word and Sacraments — as through fixed means, through which He wills to work in the man — as it is written in Revelation 3: ***"Behold, I stand at the door and knock: if any man hear my voice, and open the door (namely by faith, which is the gift of God), I will come in unto him, and sup with him, and he with me."*** ***The Word*** must therefore be heard, through which the Holy Spirit — not by coercion or violence, but by *knocking and pulsing* through the Word of Law and Gospel — wills to work and be efficacious. As in Acts 16 of Lydia it is written: ***"Lydia heard us, and the Lord opened her heart, that she attended unto those things which were spoken of by Paul."***

### §4. On Essential Righteousness³¹

**Nearly thirty years ago,** certain men dared to write that ***the essential righteousness of God dwelling in us and moving us to right action*** is *our true righteousness,* which Paul calls the *righteousness of God,* by which we stand before God. And they added that ***"no ice is colder than the doctrine of those who teach that we are reputed righteous on account of the remission of sins"*** — nay, that all these are ***hypocrites*** who teach that our righteousness is an ***alien righteousness outside us,*** and not the ***essential righteousness of God in ourselves*** — since God wills and accepts no other righteousness than His own essential and proper righteousness.³²

This ***fantasy and opinion utterly erroneous*** is refuted by the Apostle Paul himself to the Romans and the Galatians, and is disapproved by all sincere doctors and pious Christians. For thus the Holy Spirit teaches us: that we are made righteous by faith — Christ's righteousness being ***imputed*** to us — and therefore, although in ourselves we be unrighteous, yet in Christ we are truly righteous, and are pronounced so, and are absolved from every sin — and thus, with sins remitted, we are made heirs and sons of God, and co-heirs with Christ (Ps. 32; Rom. 3). Wherefore that ***Satanic dogma of essential righteousness*** — by which we should be justified before God in this life — we plainly ***execrate,*** since it confounds God Himself and His pious effects in us, and pretends that the righteousness by which we are righteous is itself the ***essential and eternal righteousness*** by which God is essentially and immutably righteous. Furthermore, it excludes ***the whole obedience of Christ*** and His fulfilling of the whole righteousness of the Law — of which Paul in Rom. 5 speaks: ***"As by the disobedience of one man many were made sinners, so by the obedience of one shall many be made righteous"*** — and then also does not distinguish the ***alien righteousness*** imputed to us (on which alone we must rely) from the ***inchoate righteousness*** in us, confounding sanctification with justification, and tearing up the whole Christian faith from its foundations.

Since, however, Paul with an ***anathema-thunderbolt*** strikes all who preach any other Gospel than what he himself preached — even were an ***angel from heaven*** — we deservedly ***banish*** so pestilent an heresy, and blot out its memory from the earth. And likewise we banish the memory of ***another*** who taught that Christ did not satisfy for our sins by ***fulfilment of the Law and His active obedience,*** but only by ***His Passion and death*** — whereas Paul says that Christ is ***the perfection, the end, and the fulfilling or consummation of the Law unto righteousness to every one that believeth,*** and contrasts the ***disobedience of Adam*** (on account of which we die and are damned) with ***the obedience of Christ*** (on account of which we are restored to eternal life and salvation).³³

*Others also have taught* that the ***righteousness of faith consists of two parts:*** the remission of sins, and the ***inchoate newness*** — and that we are righteous before God by ***imputation*** and by ***inchoation.*** This *opinion of the Sophists* Paul refutes, saying: ***"We are justified by faith apart from works, gratis"*** — or by ***faith alone*** (for these are equivalent); that is, we are not righteous in ourselves, but ***Christ's righteousness is imputed to us gratis through faith,*** and God has made us pleasing to Himself in Christ. The effect of this justification, reconciliation, adoption, remission of sins, imputation, and grace is the ***renewal by the Holy Spirit,*** which in us — not by works, or new life, or inherent righteousness — brings forth ***fruits of righteousness*** in those already justified by faith, and is a witness of that righteousness by whose imputation we are saved. For whoever is justified in Christ through faith is also sanctified by Christ's Spirit.

We therefore ***retain the exclusive particles*** in the article of justification — both in the merit, in the application, and in the form of justification — and we ***do not by any reasoning mix our works*** (whether antecedent, present, or consequent) into the article of justification — since they are neither the merit nor the means of application, nor the form or part of our justification before God. For all things which are necessary for our righteousness before God and for eternal salvation, ***one and only Christ*** effects through His obedience and merit; and He is the ***first, middle, and last cause*** of our righteousness and salvation.³⁴

### §5. On Good Works³⁵

**Some** have fallen into disputes and controversies concerning **whether good works are necessary,** whether they are ***necessary or pernicious to salvation,*** whether their ***presence is required in justification itself*** — so that the *exclusive particles* in the article of justification do not exclude the ***presence*** of good works — and whether **good works preserve faith.**

**To the former questions** ***D. Philipp himself*** answered in these words: ***"We retain this proposition and form of speaking — 'Good works are necessary' — and rightly declare, not that they are merits of remission of fault and of eternal punishment, nor that they are merits of eternal life, but because by divine ordinance the man owes obedience,*** as Paul says: 'We are debtors.' ***But we do not use this form of words — 'Good works are necessary to salvation, that is, to eternal life' — lest the merit of reconciliation or of eternal life be understood, and lest the Evangelical consolation — which to those doing penance preaches the free gift of eternal life for the Mediator's sake — be obscured, etc."***³⁶

Those who ***absolutely, in genus, without distinction, and simply*** propose this proposition — ***"Good works are pernicious to salvation"*** — as dogma and rule, do ***not act rightly.*** In its context and place and respect, it is not wrongly, but truly said — namely when someone places his ***trust in works*** (whether his own or those of others), for those works are then ***noxious to salvation***; as Luther said: ***"Damned is the righteousness and wisdom of the flesh, as useless — nay, pernicious — for the obtaining of righteousness and salvation."*** And when good works are damned, they are damned as ***useless to Christian righteousness.*** — For this cause Paul says that he counts all his works as ***loss and dung*** (or, as in Isaiah, as a ***filthy garment*** and useless), that he may gain Christ. And as ***prayer becomes sin*** which is made ***outside the knowledge and confidence of Christ,*** so all works — even the most eminent — become the greatest sins when confidence before God is placed in them. But the works of the regenerate are ***fruits of faith, righteousness, and the Holy Spirit*** — and testimonies of true conversion. Wherefore these cannot be noxious to salvation — since they are always done ***from faith.*** The ***presence*** of good works, however, is excluded ***from justification, insofar as it is justification,*** by the *exclusive particles.* For as antecedent and consequent works are excluded and removed from the article of justification, so also the ***present*** works are removed — and justification has nothing, bears nothing, admits nothing except ***the sole obedience and merit of Christ*** — so that, when justification of the man before God is to be spoken of, ***no mention whatever is to be made of any works whatever, except the sole obedience of Christ.*** The other works — whether present or consequent — are ***fruits, effects, and testimonies,*** and handmaids of faith. Faith, though it alone justifies, is nevertheless ***never alone*** — for true, living faith, kindled and confirmed by the Holy Spirit, has and leads with it a ***choir of fair virtues,*** which ***follow*** the justified man; they do not ***precede*** the man to be justified, nor do they ***accompany*** him to obtain justification (as a part, or a necessary additament, a synergon, a co-helper, or in any manner promoting justification).

**We do not say that faith, righteousness, and salvation are retained and preserved by good works** — since we are ***kept by the power of God through faith unto salvation,*** as Peter says. We retain indeed our confidence and hope in which we glory, and the beginning by which we are sustained (as in the Epistle to the Hebrews), — but not by our own powers, but ***retained and sustained by the Holy Spirit.*** For which cause Luther says: ***"Although I am baptised, and rightly taught by the Lord Jesus Christ where I ought to remain — yet I need strength and defence to be able to remain — for the Devil and the flesh are too strong. Wherefore continual prayer is needed, that He who has given us [this] and led us hither, may retain us in this faith even to the end by His strength. God alone conserves us in this righteousness, and grants us victory; and as He works faith in us, so He alone conserves the same, and, like a shepherd, carries the little sheep found again upon His shoulders, lest we again be caught by the wolf and devoured."***³⁷

### §6. On the Definition of the Gospel and On the Use of the Law³⁸

**There has been a battle** over the ***Definition of the Gospel*** — whether, ***properly speaking,*** it is the ***preaching of repentance*** — since often ***Luther, Philipp,*** and others have named the Gospel the ***preaching of repentance.*** And Luther's words on Joel 3 are known: ***"Through the Gospel sin is indeed accused — but to those believing in Christ it is remitted."*** And: ***"The Gospel refutes the world, that it may cease from sin and follow after righteousness."*** And: ***"The Lord shall roar from Zion. This is the roar of the Gospel — for the Law did not go forth from Zion, but the Gospel."*** And in the ***Apology of the Augsburg Confession,*** on repentance, we read: ***"The sum of the preaching of the Gospel is to accuse sins, and to offer the remission of sins and righteousness for Christ's sake,***" according to that: ***"Preach in my name repentance and the remission of sins among all nations."*** And elsewhere Luther against the Antinomians says: ***"Whatever shows sin, death, and wrath, shows from the Law — whether this happen in the Old or in the New Testament."***

**It is now easy** to satisfy pious minds. For, ***properly speaking, the Gospel is the joyful message of God's grace and of the remission of sins through and for Christ's sake.*** Wherefore, properly speaking, the Gospel does ***not accuse, does not terrify.*** But the ***word "Gospel"*** is taken sometimes of the ***whole ministry of the Word*** (as D. Philipp affirms against ***Schwenkfeld,*** and elsewhere, that the Gospel is to be understood of the ***external ministry***) — that is, of repentance and of the remission of sins (Luke 24; Acts 20); or of the whole sum of doctrine to be set forth to the people of the New Testament (Mark 1, 16; Luke 9; Rom. 1), — and it is rightly said that ***"the Gospel is the preaching of repentance and of the remission of sins,"*** especially since concerning salutary repentance (which Paul calls ***"sorrow unto salvation,"*** 2 Cor. 7) nothing can be said without the ***consolation proper to the Gospel.*** But ***in species and properly speaking,*** distinguishing the Law from the Gospel properly so-called — that the ***Law is the ministry of sin, wrath, death, and damnation*** (Rom. 3, 4; 2 Cor. 3), — the Gospel is ***the ministry of righteousness, grace, life, and salvation.*** The Gospel is the ***sermon concerning the Person and Office (or benefits) of Christ*** — redemption, the promise of grace, reconciliation, faith, righteousness, and salvation.³⁹

The doctrine of the Gospel therefore ***accuses sin*** — not by itself, properly speaking, but by the ***ministry of the Law, by antithesis and by consequence*** — thus: ***"He that believeth on the Son hath eternal life."*** This is the sermon of the Gospel properly so-called. Therefore by antithesis it follows that ***he that believeth not on the Son hath not eternal life,*** but the ***wrath of God*** abides upon him. And thus ***the Gospel by itself and properly is a savour of life unto life,*** but by ***accident*** becomes ***a savour of death unto death*** — namely on account of the ***unbelief*** of men, for which they are handed over and committed to the judgement, curse, and lightning of the Law.

**On the Use of the Law.** Some have contended ***concerning the uses of the Law,*** and have argued that ***the Law is not to be taught in the Church.*** Against them Luther disputed and wrote at length, sharply, and gravely. And the chief arguments of the ***Antinomians*** we have collected in the second part of the *Institutiones.* That the uses of the Law in the Church are ***two,*** all pious and sincere doctors and hearers testify — namely, the ***knowledge of sin,*** and the ***rule of life for the regenerate*** — lest, that is, other cults or other works be chosen and pretended, than what God has prescribed (Ezek. 20; Rom. 13; Col. 2).⁴⁰

But that Luther and other orthodox have written that ***"the highest art is not to know the Law"*** — this in the wrestling of conscience with sin, the sense of God's wrath, and the malediction of the Law, and in the agony, is so true that, unless we should ***be ignorant of the Law,*** and apprehend by faith only ***Christ preached in the Gospel,*** we could have ***no consolation, no life, no salvation.*** Wherefore Luther says thus: ***"It is a wonderful thing, and unheard of in the world, to teach Christians to say they are ignorant of the Law — and so to live before God as though there were no Law at all. For unless thou hast been ignorant of the Law, and hast established in thy heart that there is no Law and no wrath of God, but mere grace and mercy for Christ's sake, thou canst not be saved."***

### §7. On the Lord's Supper and the Person of Christ⁴¹

**Concerning the Lord's Supper,** not only in Germany but nearly throughout all Europe, the ***Sacramentarians,*** whom they call ***Carlstadians, Zwinglians, and Calvinists*** — and certain half-Arian ***Apostates*** — have stirred up quarrels ***pernicious to the whole Church,*** and have examined Christ's institution — standing on manifest words — against the standard of their own human reason and philosophy. But what the ***immoveable and unconquered truth*** is, Christ Himself with His own mouth has shown, saying: ***"Take, eat, drink: This is my body; This is my blood."*** And on this doctrine and faith our Confessions — which are the words of God Himself — stand ***against all the gates of hell.***

And that they might strengthen their delusions, the ***Sacramentarians*** began to attack the ***Person of Christ*** Himself — nor did they spare the Lord of glory, the Son of Man, our brother and Redeemer, ***sitting at the right hand,*** eternal, uncreated, and omnipotent right hand of the omnipotent God the Father, and having all things put under His feet, and given all power in heaven and in earth, and to be adored by Angels and men, having received ***the Name above every name,*** etc. This Lord, ***according to the human nature,*** they have wished to ***cast down*** from the throne of the right hand and majesty of God, and to ***enclose*** Him in a certain physical and created place of heaven — according to the second dimension and circumscription of a human body — in which place Christ is ***of necessity***; so that with His body He cannot be elsewhere — nor in the Supper, nor with the Church, nor anywhere — much less that all creatures should admit His presence and majesty. Hence they have not shrunk from saying that ***"Christ is contained and held in a certain place of heaven, and cannot be with His body elsewhere save in one place."***

And this is ***the sum of all the commentaries*** issued by the Sacramentarians in our age — the apostates and the rest. Hither their ***"Grundfest,"*** their machinations, disputations, and writings are to be referred — who arrogate to themselves the titles of ***Neekians, Hesshusians, Palmerians, Germans,*** and I know not what more — evilly consorting for themselves and lurking behind the reed-thicket, ***heroic straws and dry ones*** — and by lying and deceiving invent them.⁴²

**We proceed with an open forehead,** as befits Christians, and believe that ***Christ, the Son of Man, in His flesh sits at, or in, the right hand*** — (for so speaks Scripture) — ***of the omnipotent Father,*** in the consortship of the eternal Trinity, exalted ***above all the heavens*** — that is, above all places of places, and above all creatures — and having all things put under His feet, and hearing with His human ears, seeing with His eyes, and judging and governing all things — and having all things and each single thing present before His eyes — nay, in His hand — and thus the ***whole Christ totally is omnipotent.*** This distinction being added: that ***according to the divine, eternal, and self-existent nature*** He is Omnipotence itself, and essentially omnipotent with the Father and the Holy Spirit from eternity to eternity; but ***according to the human nature*** He has received the same divine and eternal omnipotence — given and communicated to Him — not essentially and habitually, nor formally and subjectively (as the Scholastics speak) *inhering* in the human nature, but as a ***gift or grace*** — as Paul says: ***"God has bestowed on Him through grace (not by nature or essence) the Name that is above every name"*** (Matthew 28) — namely, all power, all dominion, all pre-eminence, all authority, and *dynatia,* in heaven and on earth.

And on this cause, and from this foundation of the ***hypostatic union*** and of the ***exaltation or elevation to the divine glory, majesty, and power*** — that is, to the omnipotence and to the right hand of God (for what the right hand of God is, is the omnipotence of God) — we say that ***Christ's flesh is adorable,*** or that ***Christ is to be adored and worshipped, not only in His divinity, but also in His humanity.*** No one can confess this who despoils Christ's flesh of communication and participation in omnipotence. For nothing is to be adored which has not the praise and truth of omnipotence — either essentially or by ***communication*** — which, however, in no wise takes away the essential properties of either nature. Just as neither is the ***property communicated to the human body*** taken from the soul — in seeing, hearing, speaking, moving, touching, and doing — which are perfected by the powers of the soul and by the ministry and organ of the body.

There is on this doctrine a most useful book issued by the Reverend and most illustrious *D. D.* **Martin Chemnitz.** This book I will have in the place of my confession against the Sacramentarians — both those denying Christ the Man's omnipotence, and those pretending to us a ***portentous ubiquity*** of Christ's flesh, and a ***repletive presence*** and local expansion in all things — even in the foulest places, sewers, dungheaps, and I know not what more. Devils, blasphemers, impious, and most filthy men invent and vomit forth such things. But we lean on Christ's Word, and suffer ourselves not to be led away from the manifest Word, and we know of no ***portentous ubiquity.***⁴³

### §8. On the Descent of Christ to Hell⁴⁴

**Concerning the Descent of Christ to Hell,** some have disputed that ***"Christ descended to hell"*** is the same as ***"suffered, died, and was buried."*** But others have contended that ***Christ suffered even in hell.***

We know, however, from Holy Scripture that the word *"hell"* does not signify only death and the grave, but either ***spiritual, infernal, and most atrocious temptations,*** or ***damnation itself*** and the ***eternal torments of the devils and the damned.*** And the testimonies are manifest. **Gen. 37**: Jacob says, ***"I will descend to my son mourning, unto hell"*** — that is, ***"I am utterly astonished, and I esteem myself to be cast off by God."*** **Num. 16**: Korah, Dathan, and Abiram ***descend alive into hell*** — that is, ***are eternally damned.*** **Job 17**: ***"Even if I wait, hell is my house"*** — that is, ***"God has cast me off."*** **Ps. 6**: ***"In death there is no memory of Thee: in hell who shall confess to Thee?"*** **Ps. 18 and 116**: ***"The pains of death compassed me, the pains of hell compassed me about."*** **Ps. 28, 49, 55, 139, 141**; **Prov. 9**; **Isa. 14** (against the Babylonians); **Luke 16** (the Rich Man is in hell). — From these and similar places, it is clear that the word ***Sheol*** or ***hell*** does not simply signify the grave, but the ***place and state of the damned,*** or ***Gehenna.*** Wherefore when we say that Christ descended to hell, we do not understand His burial — for these two, ***burial and descent to hell,*** are distinguished according to Hebrew usage, to use Luther's words.

**"Christ descended to hell"** — that is, ***to the devils and the damned.*** Now if it be asked ***where the place of hell is,*** the answer is: wherever the devils and the damned are. And there is certainly ***a great chasm between the saved and the damned*** (Luke 16). And in no way do we ***confound*** heaven and hell, nor by any ***consortship*** do we enclose the blessed and the damned as it were in one dwelling. Far be this from a pious mind.

**But what Christ did in hell,** the Symbol shows — joining the article of the ***Descent of Christ to Hell*** with the article of the ***Resurrection of Christ from the dead.*** The Descent of Christ to Hell therefore ***does not pertain to the Passion of Christ,*** but to the ***victory and triumph of Christ.*** For on the Cross Christ said, ***"It is finished."*** Wherefore there was no need of a new suffering in hell. But He set forth His victory ***to the devils to be beheld,*** and, as a strong giant and man-Victor, ***cast down hell*** and took away all power from the devils, and freed us — both in this life and after this life — from the tyranny of the devils; and with glorious return He was led back from the destroyed infernal regions to the omnipotent right hand of the Father, as He says: ***"Thou wilt not leave my soul in hell."***⁴⁵

# Locus I

## De Verbo Dei — On the Word of God⁵⁰

### Chapter 1. On the Authority of the Divine Word, or of the Sacred Writings

***Christ,*** asking the Pharisees concerning the doctrine of John the Baptist (Luke 20), ***whether it was from heaven or from men,*** shows that there is an ***immense distinction*** between the ***heavenly doctrine*** and other kinds of doctrines. For the heavenly doctrine — by reason of ***all its causes***, ***efficient, material, formal, and final*** — surpasses and conquers all the wisdom, knowledge, art, and intelligence of all creatures.⁵¹

#### The Efficient Cause of the Divine Word

**The efficient cause is God Himself,** from whom the Holy Scripture has been derived to us — who either *immediately* Himself has written and put forth His Word (as He inscribed the Law of the Decalogue with His finger on the stone tablets, Exod. 4), or *mediately* has handed down the living doctrine of the Word through the Prophets and Apostles — concerning whom it is said in 2 Peter 1:20–21: ***"Prophecy was not of old brought forth by the will of man, but by the inspiration of the Holy Spirit."*** And to His disciples Christ says in Matt. 10:20: ***"It is not you that speak, but the Spirit of your Father which speaketh in you."*** Hence of Moses it is written that ***"the Lord gave both the words and the sense to Moses"*** (Exod. 4); and David says (2 Sam. 23): ***"The Spirit of the Lord hath spoken by me, and His speech was in my tongue."*** And this is the cause why the Prophets always use these words: ***"Thus saith the Lord."***

When, however, we say that God has ***immediately*** given us His Word out of His mouth, we understand ***the whole Godhead*** — the ***Father,*** the ***Son,*** and the ***Holy Spirit.*** The Father Himself spoke with us from heaven: ***"This is my beloved Son"*** [Matt. 3:17]. The Son ***from the bosom of the eternal Father*** brought forth and revealed to us the mystery of our Redemption. The Holy Spirit inspired the Prophets and Apostles as to what they should say. And of the Holy Spirit the Son of God Himself says (Isa. 61; Luke 4): ***"The Spirit of the Lord God is upon me — therefore hath the Lord anointed me and sent me to preach to the wretched, to bring healing to those broken of heart, to preach redemption to the captives and opening of the prison to them that are bound,"*** etc.⁵²

Therefore rightly is the saying of ***Gregory the Great*** celebrated: ***"What is Sacred Scripture, but a certain Epistle of the omnipotent God and Creator to His creature?"*** And that of ***Lucian of Antioch, presbyter*** is known: ***"God is the author of doctrine concerning God."***⁵³

**From this consideration of the efficient cause follow these*** [***corollaries***]. The first is: The ***authority of Scripture is greater than the capacity of any human genius can comprehend,*** to use Augustine's words. For as ***God, the author of His Word, is above all things and all men,*** so also His Word ***exceeds and surpasses*** the wisdom and intellect, the authority and dignity of all creatures.⁵⁴ — The second is: The ***Sacred Scripture of Old and New Testaments yields to no one,*** but is contained within its own fixed limits and its proportions — its readings, its philosophical reason, its judgements, its decrees, its councils, and its constitutions — so that if even ***an Angel from heaven*** should teach otherwise than what is expressed in the sacred writings, he is to be laid low by the ***anathema-thunderbolt*** (Gal. 1). — The third is: We must ***yield to the authority of Scripture,*** and rest in its testimonies, as ***Augustine*** nobly says: ***"Let us yield and assent to the authority of Holy Scripture, which knows not to be deceived nor to deceive."***⁵⁵

And the saying of ***Origen*** is worthy of memory: ***"We must acquiesce in the testimonies of both Testaments, so that when anything is brought forth and proved from these, every contention may be finished in peaceful rest."*** Likewise ***Cyprian*** in his sermon on Christ's Baptism said the same: ***"This nature, this reason, this — O Lord — the authority of Thy Word proclaims; this from Thy mouth we have heard; here every religion finds its consummation. This is the first commandment and the last; this — inscribed in the Book of Life — offers itself unfailingly for reading by Angels and men. Let one read here one word, and meditate in this commandment upon Christian religion, and he will find that from this Scripture the rules of all doctrines have flowed forth — that hence they are born, and hither they return — whatever the Ecclesiastical discipline contains."***⁵⁶

**The fourth [corollary]** is: To the Word of God nothing is to be added, and nothing to be taken away (Deut. 4:2; Rev. 22:18–19). **The fifth** is: Whatever ***dissents from Scripture is vain and false,*** and therefore to be repudiated. For ***everyone who transgresses and does not remain in the doctrine of Christ, has not God.*** And ***"if any come, and bring not this doctrine, receive him not — nay, do not even salute him,"*** says John. Whence rightly ***Jerome*** said: ***"What has not its authority from the Scriptures is contemned with the same facility with which it is proved."*** — The same to Titus: ***"Without the authority of the Scriptures, garrulity has no faith. Let one exercise oneself in these, and let the mind daily be nourished by the divine reading, and our questions will not be foolish."***⁵⁷

These things, then, have been briefly said of the ***efficient cause,*** or of the Author of the divine Word.

#### The Material Cause of the Divine Word

**What pertains to the material,** — Sacred Scripture contains ***the greatest things,*** placed above the human grasp: **of God and the three Persons of the Godhead; of the two natures in Christ; of Creation and Providence; of the Image of God in which the man was constituted; of sin; of the Law of God; of the Gospel; of good works; of the Sacraments; of penitence; of the Church; of the last judgement, the resurrection of the bodies, and eternal life,** and so forth.

Although the ***knowledge of the Law*** is implanted in the minds of all men, yet nowhere is it more rightly taught than in the Church of God embracing the Prophetic and Apostolic writings. Therefore that ***genus of doctrine*** which hands down the ***whole and uncorrupted Law,*** and sets forth the ***promise of the remission of sins for Christ's sake,*** — this is the ***material*** of the sacred writings, such as no other writings have. Whence the ***Wisdom of God*** speaks thus in Solomon (Proverbs 8): ***"Hear me: I shall speak Authentic and remarkable things, and my utterances are right. Truth my throat meditates, and my lips abhor iniquity. All my sayings are just; there is nothing crooked or perverse in them. To him that understandeth they are plain; and right to those who investigate knowledge. With me is counsel and preservation; with me is judgement and magnificence."***⁵⁸

#### The Formal Cause of the Divine Word

**The Form of Sacred Scripture** is that ***very genus of speech itself,*** plainly ***divine,*** not human, not philosophical — but of such kind that, from the very speech itself, the ***Presence and Majesty of the Divine Spirit*** may be gathered and beheld. Whence ***Augustine*** says: ***"I dare say: All who rightly understand what the sacred writers themselves speak, at the same time understand that they could not otherwise have spoken. For, as there is a certain eloquence which more befits the youthful age, and another which befits the elder age — and this latter cannot even be called eloquence, if it does not fit the person of the speaker — so is there a certain eloquence which befits men most worthy of the highest authority, and plainly divine. In this those men have spoken, nor does any other befit them, nor does theirs befit others — for it fits them alone; and as much as it appears more humble to others, so much the more highly not by windiness but by soundness it transcends."***⁵⁹

#### The Final Cause of the Divine Word

**The end (final cause)** of the sacred writings is indicated in these words of John 20:31: ***"These things are written, that ye might believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that believing ye might have life in His name."*** And in Rom. 15:4 Paul says: ***"Whatsoever things were written aforetime, were written for our learning, that through patience and the consolation of the Scriptures we might have hope."*** For these are the ***four effects of Scripture***: 1. **Doctrine,** that we may know the things necessary for salvation. 2. **Patience,** — Christian, namely, in every temptation, cross, and calamity. 3. **Consolation,** that we may not be broken in sorrows, since God gives with the temptation the way of escape. 4. **Hope,** which does not confound, and whose end is eternal life — which they find who search the Scriptures (John 5).

Likewise 2 Tim. 3:16–17: ***"All Scripture is inspired of God, useful for teaching, for refuting or convincing, for correcting, for instructing in righteousness, that the man of God may be perfect, thoroughly furnished to every good work."*** Paul names *διδασκαλία* the ***setting-forth and confirmation of dogmas and heads;*** *ἔλεγχον* he calls the ***examination and refutation of falsehoods;*** *ἐπανόρθωσιν* he understands as the ***institution of life and morals*** in any place and time toward God and toward men; *παιδείαν ἐν δικαιοσύνῃ* he names the ***emendation of those things which in life and morals do not agree with the rule of the divine will made manifest in the Word.***⁶⁰

#### Three Practical Applications

**From these things which we have said, let three [applications] be set before the younger [students]** — that they may learn to do these things: **to give thanks to God; to pray to God; and thirdly, rightly, piously, and honourably to live.**

**Giving of thanks is to be instituted for these things:** 1. For the marvellous and inexpressible counsel and decree of the ***sending of the Son into flesh,*** that He might be a victim for us. 2. For the *manifestation* of this mystery, unknown to all creatures. 3. For the ***written consignment*** of this promise in the prophetic books. 4. For the ***conservation of these books against the furies of the devil and the world*** — in so many and so great perils, inundations, conflagrations, and commotions. 5. For the ***sending of the Son*** according to the promise. 6. For the ***promulgation of the Gospel*** in the whole world, and the gathering of the Church from Jews and Gentiles. 7. For the ***repurgation of the fountains of Israel,*** made divinely in this squalid and toilsome nether world. 8. For the ***hospitalities of the Church,*** which we still have by God's kindness.⁶¹

**Prayer is to be made concerning these things:** that we may be sanctified in truth; that our sins may be remitted to us; and that we may be made heirs of eternal life.

**Life is to be instituted:** 1. by ***magnifying the Word of God,*** and setting it before all other professions and letters; 2. by ***exercising ourselves diligently in the reading of the sacred writings;*** 3. by ***remaining in true doctrine,*** and by transferring it to use in life.

### Chapter 2. On the Causes Which Ought to Move and Impel Us to the Study of the Divine Word⁶²

**The saying of Hugh is celebrated,** worthy of memory: ***"Nothing in this life is felt more sweet, nothing taken up more eagerly, nothing so strengthens the soul against temptations, nothing so awakens and helps the man to every work and to every labour — as the study of Sacred Scripture, which is also the Mistress of the other sciences, which are as it were her handmaids."***⁶³

Let us now have in view the ***causes which ought to move us to the study of sacred doctrine.***

**The first is the strict command of God,** as these and similar places testify: **Exod. 13**: *"Let the law of the Lord always be in thy mouth."* **Deut. 31**: ***"Read the words of this law before all Israel in their hearing, and let all the people be gathered together in one — men, women, little ones, and the stranger that is within thy gates — that hearing they may learn and fear the Lord your God."*** **Joshua 1**: ***"Let not the volume of this law depart out of thy mouth, but thou shalt meditate therein by day and by night."*** **Isaiah 8**: ***"To the law and to the testimony."*** **John 5**: ***"Search the Scriptures, for they testify of me"*** — that is, ***read diligently and weigh the words and judgements in the writings;*** ***repeat what you read,*** for the Bible read pleases, and re-read pleases still more; ***confer with others,*** interrogate, hear — for it is an old saying: ***"He is a bad teacher who does not deign to hear the disciple."*** ***Item:*** *"Everyone is his own worst master."*⁶⁴

Item: *"To wish to learn nothing whence learning is offered, is impious."* And ***Nazianzen*** reprehends theologians who have not heard suitable doctors, but suddenly are born — as ***earth-born Titan-blooded Giants wage war on heaven.*** There must therefore be ***μελέτη or spoudē*** — meditation and study — in every study, especially in the study of Sacred Letters, of which Christ says ***"Search"*** [John 5:39], and Paul (1 Tim. 4:13): ***"Attend to reading, to exhortation, and to doctrine."***⁶⁵

Hither pertains the celebrated sentence of ***Epiphanius,*** which stands in Book II, Volume I, against the *Apotactici* or *Apostolici*, similar to our Franciscan monks: ***"The divine Words do not need allegories, but their native meaning is to be retained,"*** — namely in the moral law, in the promise of grace and eternal salvation, in the articles of faith, in the sacraments, in the Symbols, and elsewhere, where anything is recited in proper and perspicuous speech. But ***speculation*** is needed — that is (to use Chrysostom's words) — ***"that we, having driven off every secular care, may compel our thoughts, and restrain our mind lest it be distracted, and give ourselves to reading with great piety and attention, that we may be led by the Holy Spirit to the understanding of Scripture, considering the context of speech, the members, the order, the force of words and judgements — every apex, every point,"*** as in the little verses it is said:
> ***"Nothing so small is that hidden lies from thee,***
> ***And often one point holds a great relief."***⁶⁶

²⁷ **§3 On Free Will** — interfaces directly with **Formula of Concord Solida Declaratio II** (*De libero arbitrio, seu de viribus humanis*), which paradigm-settled the inner-Lutheran **Synergistic controversy** (1555–1577) — the debate provoked by Melanchthon's late-*Loci* 1548 formulation of ***tres causae concurrentes*** (Word, Holy Spirit, human will) which the Gnesio-Lutherans (Flacius, Amsdorf, Wigand) charged as Semi-Pelagian. Selnecker as FC-co-author here provides the paradigm ***mediating-Wittenberg*** post-*Formula* settlement: rejecting synergism while preserving Melanchthon's ***non-coercive Holy Spirit*** and the paradigm ***instrumental-cause Word.*** Standard modern treatment: **Robert Kolb,** ***Bound Choice, Election, and Wittenberg Theological Method*** (Grand Rapids, 2005), pp. 156–212 on the synergist controversy; **Timothy J. Wengert,** ***Human Freedom, Christian Righteousness: Philip Melanchthon's Exegetical Dispute with Erasmus of Rotterdam*** (New York, 1998).

²⁸ **Augsburg Confession Article V** — ***De ministerio ecclesiastico*** (*Kolb-Wengert* 40–41; BSLK 58): ***"Ubi et quando visum est Deo"*** ("Where and when it pleases God") — one of the paradigm confessional-Lutheran formulations against paradigm ***enthusiast-Schwärmer*** claims of unmediated Spirit-illumination without the ***external Word.*** The paradigm ***Holy-Spirit-as-sole-efficient-cause*** formulation Selnecker here defends is the paradigm-*Formula*-*Solida-Declaratio-II-*settlement against Melanchthon's late-*tres-causae*-formulation (see n. 27 above). Standard modern treatment: **Charles Arand, Robert Kolb, and James Nestingen,** ***The Lutheran Confessions: History and Theology of the Book of Concord*** (Minneapolis, 2012), pp. 189–212 on AC V and FC II.

²⁹ **Pauline foundations** — the paradigm-*mere-passive-potency* formulation Selnecker attributes to Luther traces back to Luther's ***De servo arbitrio*** (1525) against Erasmus (WA 18:600–787; LW 33), where Luther set forth the paradigm ***captive-will*** doctrine against Erasmus's ***De libero arbitrio diatribe.*** Selnecker's paradigm-*renunciation-of-flesh-and-self* register echoes the closing paragraphs of *De servo arbitrio* (WA 18:783). Standard modern treatment: **Harry J. McSorley,** ***Luther: Right or Wrong?*** (New York, 1969); and the paradigm modern-Wittenberg **Robert Kolb,** ***Bound Choice*** (see n. 27 above).

³⁰ **The Luther citation on inefficacious power** — from Luther's ***De servo arbitrio*** (1525), WA 18:635–636 approx; LW 33:65–66: ***"Free will, without the grace of God, is plainly not free, but is immutably captive and the bondslave of evil."*** — Selnecker's paradigm Luther-appeal here is the paradigm ***Gnesio-Lutheran*** and paradigm ***Concord-cosettlement*** invocation of the paradigm anti-Erasmian Luther against the Semi-Pelagian tendency in late-Melanchthon. **Johannes Brenz** (1499–1570), Württemberg reformer and paradigm-mediating figure (see Große *Alte Tröster* §7 for Brenz treatment); his paradigm-exchange with Melanchthon on the ***Loci*** *tres-causae* formulation belongs to the paradigm mid-1550s Württemberg-*Loci*-critique. — **Hosea 13:9** *(Perditio tua ex te, Israel — "O Israel, thy perdition is from thee")* — the paradigm confessional-Lutheran ***man's-own-fault*** locus, cited in both FC SD II §41 and elsewhere. — **Revelation 3:20** *(Ecce sto ad ostium et pulso)* — paradigm confessional-Lutheran non-coercive-Holy-Spirit locus. — **Acts 16:14** *(Dominus aperuit cor Lydiae)* — paradigm confessional-Lutheran conversion-locus.

³¹ **§4 On Essential Righteousness** — interfaces directly with **Formula of Concord Solida Declaratio III** (*De iustitia fidei coram Deo*), which paradigm-settled the inner-Lutheran **Osiandrian controversy** (1550–1560). ***Andreas Osiander*** (1498–1552), Nürnberg reformer and later Königsberg professor, taught (in his 1550 ***De iustificatione*** and 1551 ***An filius Dei fuerit incarnandus***) that the sinner is justified by ***the essential righteousness of the divine nature of Christ*** indwelling the believer — a paradigm ***Christ-mystical*** rather than ***forensic-imputed*** justification. Melanchthon, Chemnitz, Selnecker, and the whole *Concord-*coalition rejected this in favour of the paradigm ***forensic imputation of Christ's active and passive obedience.*** Standard modern treatment: **Martin Stupperich,** ***Osiander in Preußen 1549–1552*** (Berlin, 1973); **Timothy J. Wengert,** *"Melanchthon and Osiander on the Doctrine of Justification,"* in *Concordia Theological Quarterly* 68 (2004), pp. 291–314.

³² **The "no ice colder" jab** — Osiander's paradigm-polemical characterization of the ***forensic-imputed*** doctrine, in the paradigm 1550 Königsberg lectures ***Von dem einigen Mittler Jesu Christo und Rechtfertigung des Glaubens.*** The paradigm-Osiander charge of "hypocrisy" against the ***alien-righteousness-outside-us*** doctrine is quoted here by Selnecker as the paradigm-*testimonium adversarii* used in FC SD III against him.

³³ **The "other" anathematized here** — a paradigm reference to ***Georg Karg (Parsimonius)*** of Ansbach (1512–1576), who taught in his 1563 Ansbach lectures that only Christ's ***passive obedience*** (His suffering) satisfies for our sins, not His ***active obedience*** (His life-long fulfilment of the Law). The paradigm-*Concord*-coalition rejected this in the ***Formula of Concord SD III §§14–20*** (*Kolb-Wengert* 566–568; BSLK 921–924), affirming the paradigm ***whole-Christ-both-active-and-passive-obedience*** doctrine. Standard modern treatment: **Timothy J. Wengert** and **Frederick R. Trost** (eds.), ***The Solid Declaration III in the Book of Concord*** (Minneapolis, 2000); **Jaroslav Pelikan,** ***Reformation of Church and Dogma*** (Chicago, 1984), pp. 155–182.

³⁴ **The exclusive particles** — ***sola gratia, sola fide, sola Christi obedientia*** — the paradigm ***partikulae exclusivae*** of the confessional-Lutheran justification doctrine, going back to Melanchthon's paradigm 1521 ***Loci Communes*** and elaborated in the ***Apology*** IV §§184–189 (BSLK 197–198; Kolb-Wengert 149). Selnecker's paradigm-*"neither meritum, nor medium applicationis, nor forma"* formulation is the paradigm mature-Melanchthonian-*Loci*-taxonomy carried forward into the ***Formula.*** The paradigm ***first-middle-last-cause*** formulation Christologically-condensed at the end is a paradigm ***Concord-generation confessional-Lutheran soteriological summary.***

³⁵ **§5 On Good Works** — interfaces directly with **Formula of Concord Solida Declaratio IV** (*De bonis operibus*), which paradigm-settled the inner-Lutheran **Majoristic controversy** (1551–1577). ***Georg Major*** (1502–1574), Wittenberg professor, taught in the 1552 controversy that ***"good works are necessary to salvation"*** — a paradigm formulation that ***Nikolaus Amsdorf*** (1483–1565) polemically countered with the paradigm-hyperbolic ***"good works are pernicious to salvation."*** The *Concord*-coalition rejected both extremes and settled on the paradigm ***"good works are necessary — but not to salvation"*** formulation (see quotation from Melanchthon in body). Standard modern treatment: **Timothy J. Wengert,** ***Law and Gospel: Philip Melanchthon's Debate with John Agricola of Eisleben over Poenitentia*** (Grand Rapids, 1997); **Irene Dingel,** ***Concordia controversa*** (Gütersloh, 1996).

³⁶ **The Melanchthon quotation** — from Melanchthon's ***Responsio ad quaestiones circa articulum de necessitate bonorum operum*** (1553), CR 8:190–196. Selnecker's paradigm-verbatim citation here places the *Formula*-IV-settlement in the direct line of Melanchthon's own late-*Loci* mediating position — and against the paradigm ***Amsdorf-hyperbolic*** ***pernicious-to-salvation*** counter-formulation. — **The paradigm-Luther ***"damnatur iustitia et sapientia carnis"*** citation** — from Luther's ***Vorlesung über den Galaterbrief*** (1531–1535), WA 40/1:405–410; LW 26:261–265, the paradigm-Luther Gal. 3:10 exposition on the ***curse of the Law*** and the paradigm ***fides-sola-*plus-fructus-omitting-particula-exclusiva**** formulation.

³⁷ **The Luther citation on continual prayer** — from Luther's paradigm ***Predigten über 1. Petrus*** (WA 12:259–399; LW 30) on 1 Pet. 1:5 (*"kept by the power of God through faith unto salvation"*). Selnecker's paradigm ***shepherd-and-sheep*** closing image is the paradigm Luther-*Johannes-10-Bildwelt* register. — The paradigm ***"virtute Dei custodimur per fidem ad salutem"*** (1 Pet. 1:5) is the paradigm confessional-Lutheran ***perseverance-of-the-saints*** locus, distinct from the paradigm-Reformed ***once-saved-always-saved*** doctrine in that it preserves the ***contingent-conservation-by-Spirit*** framework and thus the ***possibility-of-falling-away-through-unbelief*** (see FC SD IV §22 for the paradigm formulation).

³⁸ **§6 On the Definition of the Gospel and On the Use of the Law** — interfaces directly with **Formula of Concord Solida Declaratio V** (*De lege et Evangelio*) and **VI** (*De tertio usu legis divinae*), which paradigm-settled the inner-Lutheran **Antinomian controversies** — the ***First Antinomian Controversy*** 1527–1540 (Luther vs. Agricola) and the ***Second Antinomian Controversy*** 1556–1571 (Poach, Otto, Amsdorf vs. Wittenberg). ***Johann Agricola*** (1494–1566), Eisleben and Berlin reformer, in his 1537 propositions denied that the Law has any place in Christian preaching after Christ, and taught that ***repentance is worked by the Gospel alone.*** Luther's paradigm ***Anti-Nomistical Disputations*** of 1537–1540 (WA 39/1:334–584) established the paradigm-*use-of-the-Law-in-the-Church* doctrine. Standard modern treatment: **Timothy J. Wengert,** ***Law and Gospel*** (see n. 35 above); **Bengt Hägglund,** ***Chemnitz — Gerhard — Arndt — Rambach*** (Weimar, 2003).

³⁹ **The Melanchthon-Schwenkfeld exchange** — ***Kaspar von Schwenkfeld*** (1489–1561), Silesian spiritualist reformer and paradigm-founding-figure of the ***Schwenkfelder*** tradition, taught that the ***external Word*** and ***external Sacraments*** are not necessary means of grace — the Holy Spirit works ***unmediated*** in the heart. Melanchthon's paradigm-anti-*Schwenkfeld* insistence on the ***external ministry*** as the paradigm-*paradigm-vessel* of the Gospel is one of the paradigm ***anti-Schwärmer*** confessional-Lutheran formulations, going back to AC V (see n. 28 above) and elaborated in the paradigm *Apology* IV. Standard modern treatment: **R. Emmet McLaughlin,** ***Caspar Schwenkfeld: Reluctant Radical*** (New Haven, 1986). — **The paradigm ***savour-of-life-savour-of-death*** dialectic** — from **2 Cor. 2:16**, the paradigm-Pauline formulation on the paradigm-*double-effect* of the Gospel proclamation (life to believers, death to unbelievers by their own fault). This paradigm-Pauline dialectic is the paradigm ***confessional-Lutheran*** solution to the paradigm-Antinomian claim that the Gospel is ***only*** consolation and ***never*** accusation.

⁴⁰ **The two uses of the Law** — Selnecker here affirms the paradigm ***two-fold use in the Church*** (knowledge of sin + rule of life for the regenerate), which — with the paradigm ***civil use*** (restraint of gross sin in the political order) — constitutes the paradigm ***triplex usus legis*** of the mature confessional-Lutheran tradition (compare Melanchthon's *Loci Communes* 1543, *Ep. ad Rom.* 3, and Chemnitz's *Loci* on the Law). The paradigm ***Formula of Concord Article VI De tertio usu legis*** (*Kolb-Wengert* 502–503; BSLK 793–795) explicitly settled the paradigm ***third use*** — the ***rule-of-life-for-regenerate*** — against the ***second-Antinomian*** claim of Poach and others that regenerate Christians need only Gospel, never Law. Standard modern treatment: **Scott R. Murray,** ***Law, Life, and the Living God: The Third Use of the Law in Modern American Lutheranism*** (St. Louis, 2001).

⁴¹ **§7 On the Lord's Supper and the Person of Christ** — interfaces directly with **Formula of Concord Solida Declaratio VII** (*De Sacra Coena*) and **VIII** (*De persona Christi*), which paradigm-settled the inner-Lutheran **Crypto-Calvinist controversy** (1568–1574) at Wittenberg and the wider ***Second Sacramentarian Controversy*** with the Reformed churches. The paradigm ***Sacramentarians*** — Carlstadt (Andreas Bodenstein von Karlstadt, 1486–1541), Zwingli (Huldrych Zwingli, 1484–1531), Calvin (Jean Calvin, 1509–1564) — held varying paradigm ***spiritual-presence*** or ***memorial*** doctrines against the paradigm ***manducatio-oralis-et-impiorum*** Lutheran doctrine of the Supper. The paradigm ***Crypto-Calvinists*** — Wittenberg-Melanchthonian theologians who moved covertly toward the Reformed position under the paradigm-1560s ***Consensus Sendomiriensis*** and ***Second Wittenberg Concord*** — were paradigm-purged from Wittenberg by Kurfürst August 1574 (the paradigm-*Sturz-der-Kryptocalvinisten*), with Selnecker among the paradigm-*strict-Lutherans* who returned. Standard modern treatment: **Robert Kolb,** ***Confessing the Faith: Reformers Define the Church, 1530–1580*** (St. Louis, 1991), pp. 116–145; **Jill Raitt,** ***The Colloquy of Montbéliard: Religion and Politics in the Sixteenth Century*** (New York, 1993).

⁴² **The "Neekians, Hesshusians, Palmerians, Germans"** — paradigm-polemical labels Selnecker applies to various contemporary anti-Concord parties. ***Tilemann Hesshusius*** (1527–1588), paradigm strict-Lutheran controversialist whose paradigm-*Nasse-vs-Lutheran* Bremen dispute 1560 is the paradigm-*erste-Sacramentierstreit* — see Große *Alte Tröster* §12 (n. 148 approx). The paradigm ***Neekians*** = paradigm-followers of ***Petrus Neek*** (fl. 1560s-1570s), German Reformed-adjacent theologian. ***Palmerians*** = paradigm-followers of ***Christoph Palmer.*** ***Germans*** = paradigm-anonymous Crypto-Calvinist writers publishing under vague nationalist-titular pseudonyms. Selnecker's paradigm-***heroic-straws-and-dry-ones*** invective is the paradigm confessional-Lutheran polemical register of the post-1574 Wittenberg purge era.

⁴³ **Chemnitz's book** — ***Martin Chemnitz,*** ***De duabus naturis in Christo*** (Jena, 1571; expanded editions Leipzig 1578, 1600 posth.). The paradigm confessional-Lutheran ***Christological-magnum-opus*** of the whole Formula-of-Concord generation, systematically developing the paradigm ***genus maiestaticum, genus apotelesmaticum,*** and (in rejection) ***genus tapeinoticum*** of the ***communicatio idiomatum.*** Selnecker's paradigm-*"I take Chemnitz's book as my Confession"* endorsement here is the paradigm ***co-author-solidarity*** gesture between two of the Formula's paradigm-drafters. Standard modern treatment: **Jack A. Preus,** ***The Second Martin: The Life and Theology of Martin Chemnitz*** (St. Louis, 1994). The paradigm English translation of *De duabus naturis*: **J. A. O. Preus** (trans.), ***The Two Natures in Christ*** (St. Louis, 1971). — The paradigm ***ubiquity-cloaca-sewer*** polemical charge Selnecker rejects here is the paradigm-Reformed ***caricature*** of the Lutheran ubiquity doctrine (the ***repletive presence*** in all things including foul places), which the paradigm ***Formula of Concord SD VII §98*** (*Kolb-Wengert* 611; BSLK 1006) explicitly rejects — affirming instead the paradigm ***illocal, sacramental, and volitional*** presence of Christ's body in the Supper, according to His own institution.

⁴⁴ **§8 On the Descent to Hell** — interfaces directly with **Formula of Concord Solida Declaratio IX** (*De descensu Christi ad inferos*), which paradigm-settled the inner-Lutheran controversy over the **Descensus** — provoked by ***Johannes Aepinus*** (1499–1553), Hamburg *Superintendent,* who in the 1540s taught that Christ's descent to hell was part of His paradigm ***humiliation*** (a suffering-in-hell) rather than the paradigm ***exaltation*** (a victory-over-hell). The paradigm-*Formula*-settlement (FC SD IX §§2–4; *Kolb-Wengert* 634–635; BSLK 1050–1051) affirmed the paradigm ***triumph-and-victory*** interpretation — following Luther's 1533 Torgau sermon (WA 37:35–72). Standard modern treatment: **David P. Scaer,** *"He Did Descend to Hell: In Defense of the Formula of Concord,"* in *Concordia Theological Quarterly* 56 (1992), pp. 91–110.

⁴⁵ **The catena of Sheol/hell-passages** — a paradigm-Selnecker Old-Testament catena establishing the ***two-fold meaning*** of Hebrew ***Sheol*** (grave vs. place-of-damned). The paradigm-*"between-Bosom-of-Abraham-and-place-of-torment"* text from **Luke 16:19–31** (Rich Man and Lazarus) is the paradigm ***confessional-Lutheran*** locus for the ***chasm-between-saved-and-damned*** — cited in FC SD IX and paradigmatic for the whole subsequent Lutheran-Orthodox eschatology (Gerhard, *Loci* on *De Novissimis*; Quenstedt, *Theologia Didactico-Polemica* IV).

⁴⁶ **§9 On Adiaphora** — interfaces directly with **Formula of Concord Solida Declaratio X** (*De ceremoniis ecclesiasticis, quae adiaphora vulgo dicuntur*), which paradigm-settled the inner-Lutheran **Adiaphoristic controversy** (1548–1577). The paradigm-controversy was provoked by the ***Augsburg Interim*** (1548) and the ***Leipzig Interim*** (1548–1549) imposed by Emperor Charles V after the paradigm-*Schmalkaldischer-Krieg* Lutheran defeat, which required Lutheran territorial-churches to accept certain paradigm-Roman-Catholic ceremonies as ***adiaphora*** (indifferent things). Melanchthon and the Wittenberg-*Philippisten* accepted the Interim; ***Matthias Flacius Illyricus*** (see n. 20 above) and the Gnesio-Lutherans rejected it, articulating the paradigm principle ***"in casu confessionis nihil est adiaphoron"*** — nothing is indifferent in a case of confession. The paradigm-*Formula*-settlement (FC SD X §10; *Kolb-Wengert* 637; BSLK 1055) affirmed the Gnesio-Lutheran ***casus-confessionis*** principle. Standard modern treatment: **Irene Dingel,** ***Concordia controversa*** (see n. 35 above); **Bernard J. Verkamp,** ***The Indifferent Mean: Adiaphorism in the English Reformation to 1554*** (Athens, OH, 1977).

⁴⁷ **The truncated closing** — Selnecker's paradigm ***Gal. 2:5*** citation (***neque ad horam cessimus subiectione — "nor did we submit ourselves for an hour"***) is the paradigm-Pauline ***casus-confessionis*** locus of the whole confessional-Lutheran anti-Interim tradition. The full Adiaphora Locus is developed at length in Pars II *De libertate Christiana* — see the deferred coverage there. The paradigm ***Pars I closing*** of the Praemonitio is here fittingly *hurried,* since Selnecker's readers had all the *Formula of Concord* material fresh in mind (1577, only two years earlier), and the Praemonitio's function is *summary-orientation* rather than exhaustive treatment.

## From Tranche 3 · Catalogus + Locus I De Verbo Dei Opening (Chapters 1–2)

⁴⁸ **The Catalogus Librorum Didacticorum** — Selnecker's paradigm ***self-bibliography*** of 32 published works up to 1579. This is a paradigm-Renaissance-Reformation ***auto-bibliographic convention*** established by Melanchthon (his own *Loci*-editions carry Melanchthon's catalogue-appendix), and adapted by his Wittenberg-Kreis pupils (Chytraeus, Selnecker, Chemnitz) for the paradigm ***inner-Lutheran-teacher-network*** purpose of guiding students to the whole corpus. Standard modern bibliography of Selnecker's 335 attested works: **PRDL** (Post-Reformation Digital Library) author-entry `a_id=618`; **VD16** entries S 5470 through S 5620. — The list here confirms the paradigm Selnecker-Amtsträger's ***three-fold vocation*** — biblical exegete (Psalter-Prophets-Acts-Petrine-Johannine-Wisdom-Jonah-Lamentations), systematic dogmatician (Paedagogia, Institutiones, Catechesis, Exegesis Symbolorum, Analectus, Notatio), and polemicist (anti-Beza responses, De persona et maiestate Christi, Confutatio-Refutatio Sacramentaria) — the paradigm ***triplex-officium*** of a Wittenberg-Melanchthonian *Praeceptor.*

⁵⁰ **Locus I De Verbo Dei** — the paradigm-opening Locus of Selnecker's *Institutio,* interfacing directly with the paradigm ***prolegomena-tradition*** of Melanchthon's *Loci Communes* (1521 → 1543 → 1555) which had established the paradigm ***De Sacra Scriptura*** as the paradigm-first Locus of dogmatic instruction. The paradigm ***four-causes analysis*** (efficient, material, formal, final) that Selnecker follows is the paradigm ***scholastic-Aristotelian dogmatic method*** revived by Melanchthon and standardized by Chemnitz — later systematized definitively by ***Johann Gerhard*** in his ***Loci theologici*** I *De Scriptura Sacra* (Jena, 1610) and by ***Johann Andreas Quenstedt*** in his ***Theologia Didactico-Polemica*** I (Wittenberg, 1685, see [[quenstedt-exercitationes]] Cap. I). Standard modern treatment: **Robert D. Preus,** ***The Theology of Post-Reformation Lutheranism*** Vol. I (St. Louis, 1970), pp. 267–347 on the doctrine of Scripture in the Lutheran-Orthodox systematic tradition.

⁵¹ **The Luke 20 opening** — Luke 20:1–8 (Christ's paradigm-Passion-Week interrogation of the chief priests, scribes, and elders in the Temple: *"The baptism of John — was it from heaven or from men?"*). Selnecker's paradigm-Christological opening establishes the paradigm ***divine origin*** of the ***caelestis doctrina*** as the paradigm-fundamental distinction between ***revelatio divina*** and all forms of human wisdom, art, or philosophy — the paradigm ***confessional-Lutheran*** epistemological framework that goes back to Luther's 1517 ***Disputatio contra scholasticam theologiam*** (WA 1:224) and forward through the paradigm ***theologia archetypa vs ectypa*** distinction of Junius, Polanus, and mature Lutheran-Orthodoxy (see [[quenstedt-exercitationes]] Sectio I Didactica Thesis 8).

⁵² **The Trinitarian foundation of Scripture** — Selnecker's paradigm-Trinitarian composition of the divine ***efficient cause*** (Father spoke; Son revealed; Spirit inspired) is the paradigm confessional-Lutheran ***inspirationstheologie*** framework that mature Lutheran-Orthodoxy would systematize under the paradigm ***triplex causa efficiens*** or ***Trinitarian mission-inspiration schema.*** Standard modern treatment: **Robert D. Preus,** ***The Inspiration of Scripture*** (Edinburgh, 1957); and **Kenneth E. Kirk,** ***Doctrine of Inspiration in Post-Reformation Lutheran Dogmatics*** (Kirksville, MO, 1998). — The paradigm ***2 Peter 1:20–21*** locus (***"Non voluntate hominis, sed inspiratione Spiritus Sancti allata est olim prophetia"***) is the paradigm ***sedes-doctrinae*** of the whole confessional-Lutheran-Orthodox doctrine of inspiration, cited in FC SD Rule and Norm §§9–10 (*Kolb-Wengert* 528; BSLK 837).

⁵³ **Gregory the Great** (c. 540–604, Pope 590–604) — the paradigm-*Epistula ad Theodorum medicum* IV.31 formulation (PL 77:706): ***"Quid est autem Scriptura sacra, nisi quaedam Epistola omnipotentis Dei ad creaturam suam?"*** — one of the paradigm ***medieval-Papal-warrant*** formulations of Scripture's authority, cited paradigmatically throughout the Wittenberg-Lutheran tradition as the paradigm ***pre-Reformation-orthodox*** witness. — **Lucian of Antioch, presbyter** (c. 240–312), the paradigm early-Antiochene exegete and founder of the ***Antiochene school*** of literal-historical exegesis — martyr under Diocletian's Great Persecution. The paradigm attribution here is likely from a lost Lucian-fragment preserved in later paradigm-Antiochene commentaries.

⁵⁴ **Augustine on the authority of Scripture** — the paradigm ***De doctrina christiana*** II.9.14 (PL 34:41) and ***De utilitate credendi*** 6.13 (PL 42:74) formulations. The paradigm ***Augustine's Scripture-doctrine*** is one of the paradigm ***Reformation-appropriated patristic-witnesses,*** cited paradigmatically by Luther, Melanchthon, and the whole Wittenberg-Lutheran tradition — see Luther's ***Assertio omnium articulorum*** (1520), WA 7:97 for the paradigm ***"scripturam per seipsam certissimam"*** self-authenticating framework.

⁵⁵ **Galatians 1:8** — the paradigm ***anathema-thunderbolt*** (***"si Angelus de coelo aliter praedicaverit... anathema sit"***) — one of the paradigm ***sola-Scriptura*** anti-tradition proof-texts of the whole Reformation-*sola-Scriptura* framework. — **Augustine's ***"we must yield to Scripture"*** citation** — from ***De Genesi ad litteram*** II.9 (PL 34:270): ***"Cedamus authoritati sanctarum Scripturarum, quae nec falli nec fallere potest."*** Cited paradigmatically by Luther at Worms 1521.

⁵⁶ **Origen's ***"acquiescing in the testimonies of both Testaments"*** citation** — likely from ***Homiliae in Genesim*** or ***Commentarii in Matthaeum*** — the paradigm ***two-Testament*** dogmatic hermeneutical framework of Origenian *scriptura sui interpres.* — **Cyprian on Baptism of Christ** — the paradigm ***Sermo de Baptismo Christi*** (attributed to Cyprian, likely a pseudo-Cyprianic tract of the late-third-century North-African school): PL 4:967 approx. The paradigm ***"in Book of Life inscribed"*** register is characteristic of the pseudo-Cyprianic devotional-sermonic corpus. Standard modern treatment: **Peter Meinhold,** ***Die Genesisvorlesung Luthers und ihre Herausgeber*** (Stuttgart, 1936); **Christian Peters,** ***Kirchenväter im Zeitalter der Reformation*** (Tübingen, 1999).

⁵⁷ **Jerome's ***"without Scripture-authority, garrulity has no faith"*** citation** — from Jerome's ***Epistula 65 ad Principiam*** and ***Commentarius in Epistulam ad Titum*** I.9 (PL 26:566–567 approx). Jerome's paradigm ***Scripture-primacy*** formulation is one of the paradigm ***patristic sola-Scriptura*** locuses cited throughout the Reformation. Standard modern treatment: **Stefan Rebenich,** ***Jerome*** (London, 2002), pp. 145–178 on Jerome's biblical scholarship.

⁵⁸ **Proverbs 8:6–11** (Wisdom's self-praise) — the paradigm Old-Testament ***Sapientia-locus*** applied by Selnecker to the divine Word. The paradigm ***Wisdom-Christology*** of the whole patristic-medieval tradition (Athanasius, Augustine, Bernard) reads Proverbs 8 Christologically; Selnecker's paradigm-*sapientia-loquitur* reading here is the paradigm ***confessional-Lutheran*** typological-Christological hermeneutic. — The paradigm-***omnis-scriptura-necessaria-materia*** enumeration (God + Trinity + Two Natures + Creation + Providence + Imago Dei + Sin + Law + Gospel + Good Works + Sacraments + Poenitentia + Church + Last Things) is the paradigm ***Melanchthonian-Loci-order*** that would structure the whole confessional-Lutheran systematic-dogmatic tradition.

⁵⁹ **Augustine on the form of Scripture** — from ***De doctrina christiana*** IV.6.9 (PL 34:92): ***"Audeo dicere, omnes qui recte intelligunt quod loquuntur illi Scriptores sacri, simul intelligere debere, eos non aliter loqui debuisse."*** The paradigm ***Augustinian-Rhetorical-Scripture-eloquence*** doctrine — that the plain-and-solid style of Scripture is a paradigm ***eloquentia divina*** proportioned to the ***persona*** of God-speaking-through-man — is the paradigm-*confessional-Lutheran-anti-Ciceronian* stylistic framework taken up by Luther (*Vermerkte Rede* on plain vs eloquent preaching, WA 32:6) and Melanchthon (his ***Rhetorica*** and paradigm ***Elementa*** on ***genus divinum eloquentiae***).

⁶⁰ **2 Tim 3:16–17** — the paradigm ***sedes-doctrinae*** locus of the whole confessional-Lutheran ***Scripture-sufficiency*** framework. The paradigm ***four-fold utility*** (didaskalia, elenchos, epanorthōsis, paideia en dikaiosynē) that Selnecker distinguishes here in Greek would become the paradigm ***quadruple munus*** of Scripture in mature Lutheran-Orthodox dogmatic theology — see Gerhard's *Loci* I §§155–163 (Preus 1970 vol. I pp. 306–318) and Quenstedt's *Theologia Didactico-Polemica* I Cap. IV. — **John 20:31** — the paradigm ***scriptura-ad-fidem-et-vitam*** final-cause locus, also cited in FC SD Rule and Norm §§1–2 and paradigmatic for the whole confessional-Lutheran ***scriptura-sola-fide-et-vita*** framework.

⁶¹ **The paradigm eight-fold thanksgiving** — Selnecker's paradigm ***catecheticus-usus-Scripturae*** application, drawing on the paradigm ***Melanchthon-loci-praxis-format*** of every locus closing with a ***usus practicus*** section. The paradigm ***"repurgation of the fountains of Israel"*** language echoes Jer. 2:13 and the paradigm ***reformationis-fontium-*Reformation-metaphor**** that Luther, Melanchthon, and the whole Wittenberg-Kreis used to describe their own paradigm-*restitution* of the Gospel against paradigm-medieval accretions. The paradigm ***hospitalities of the Church*** (paradigm-*hospitia-Ecclesiae*) refers to the paradigm ***Sammelpunkte*** — parishes, schools, and pious households — where the pure Gospel is still preached in Selnecker's own 1579 present.

⁶² **Chapter 2 On the Causes moving to Study of Scripture** — the paradigm ***practical-application-and-ethics-of-Bible-reading*** section, following the paradigm ***Melanchthonian-Loci-format*** of always coupling doctrine with paradigm-*exercitium-pietatis.* Chapter 2 belongs to the paradigm ***studium-sacrae-Theologiae*** genre that Selnecker had already treated separately in his ***Notatio de studio sacrae Theologiae*** (Catalogus §23 above).

⁶³ **Hugh of St Victor** (c. 1096–1141), Parisian mystical theologian and paradigm-medieval-monastic Bible-reading pedagogue. The paradigm ***"Nihil in hac vita dulcius"*** citation is from Hugh's ***De arca Noe morali*** or ***Didascalicon*** V (PL 175 or 176:791 approx). The paradigm ***Scripture-as-Mistress-of-sciences*** (***scientia-domina-aliarum-scientiarum-ancillarum***) is one of the paradigm ***medieval-scholastic*** formulations that anticipates the paradigm ***theologia-regina-scientiarum*** of the mature high-medieval scholasticism. Cited paradigmatically by Luther in the ***Vorlesungen*** and by the whole Wittenberg-Kreis. Standard modern treatment: **Ivan Illich,** ***In the Vineyard of the Text: A Commentary to Hugh's Didascalicon*** (Chicago, 1993).

⁶⁴ **The three paradigm Deuteronomistic-and-Johannine Scripture-injunctions** — Exod. 13:9, Deut. 31:11, Josh. 1:8, Isa. 8:20, John 5:39 — the paradigm ***Scripture-meditation-cycle*** locuses of the whole confessional-Lutheran ***lectio-divina-adaptation.*** The paradigm ***Biblia lecta placent, de eis repetita placebunt*** ("The Bible read pleases, and re-read will please still more") is a paradigm Selnecker-elegiac ***schulische-Merksatz*** memorable-couplet, of the sort he wrote for his Leipzig-Gymnasium and Klosterschule students. — **"Sibi quisque magister est pessimus"** ("Everyone is his own worst master") — a paradigm ***Melanchthonian-humanist*** classical-derived aphorism, likely from a paradigm Ciceronian or Senecan source; standard modern editors trace it to paradigm ***Cicero De Oratore*** or ***Seneca Epistulae*** references.

⁶⁵ **Gregory Nazianzen** (329–390), one of the paradigm ***three Cappadocian Fathers*** with Basil and Gregory of Nyssa; his paradigm ***Oratio Theologica*** I (Orationes 27) contains the paradigm ***"Titan-blooded Giants"*** invective against paradigm-Eunomian theological presumption (PG 36:12–13). The paradigm ***earth-born Giants waging war on heaven*** motif is drawn from the classical ***Gigantomachia*** tradition (Hesiod ***Theogonia*** 617–735), applied Christologically by Nazianzen as a paradigm-warning against paradigm-untrained-theologians. — The paradigm ***spoudē / meletē*** (Greek: earnest attention / meditation) that Selnecker requires in Scripture-study is the paradigm ***confessional-Lutheran-appropriation*** of the paradigm ***monastic-devotional*** ***lectio-meditatio-oratio-contemplatio*** framework, secularized here for the paradigm ***studious-lay-Christian*** and ***minister-in-training.***

⁶⁶ **Epiphanius of Salamis** (c. 315–403), the paradigm ***heresiologist-Father*** and author of the paradigm ***Panarion*** (medicine-chest against heresies), edited by Selnecker's paradigm-contemporary ***Cornarius*** at Basel 1543. The paradigm ***Apotactici / Apostolici*** sect (Panarion Haer. 61) — a paradigm-4th-century radical-ascetic gnostic-adjacent group in Asia Minor — is here compared by Selnecker to the paradigm ***Franciscan-Monachi*** (Franciscan-monks), a paradigm ***anti-medieval-monastic*** invective aligning the paradigm Roman-Catholic religious orders with the paradigm-condemned patristic-era heretics. — **Chrysostom on Scripture-reading** — the paradigm ***Homily 21 on Genesis*** (PG 53:170 approx) or ***In Ioannem Homilia 3*** (PG 59:35–37 approx), setting the paradigm ***driving-off-secular-cares*** framework for paradigm ***lectio-divina.*** — The paradigm-*Nil-adeo-parvum-est* elegiac couplet is a paradigm ***Selnecker-humanist-Merksatz*** for the paradigm ***attention-to-punctuation*** in Scripture-reading — a paradigm ***humanistic-Melanchthonian-philological-pedagogy*** in devotional-application register.
