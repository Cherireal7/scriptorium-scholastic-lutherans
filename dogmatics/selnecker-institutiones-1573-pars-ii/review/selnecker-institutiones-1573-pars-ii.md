# Translator's Note

*This working translation of the Second Part of Selnecker's* Institutio Christianae Religionis *is at its kick-off state. The paragraphs below record the intended text base, the editorial conventions binding on the eventual body prose, and — with full disclosure — what remains to be verified once the facsimile has been sighted paragraph by paragraph.*

## 1. The text base

The English rendered here follows the **1579** edition of Nikolaus Selnecker's ***Institutionis Christianae Religionis Pars Secunda*** — the second of three parts of Selnecker's mature systematic-dogmatic Loci-work. Pars I (Prolegomena · Doctrine of God · Creation · Angels · Anthropology · Sin · Law · Cross · Consolation) was published as a companion working volume by this team earlier in 2026. **Pars III** (probably *De Novissimis*, and closing controversies) is deferred to a future volume once its digital source is located and confirmed — the Google-Books identifier for the 1580 Jena Tröster Pars III has not yet been ascertained at the time of this kick-off.

The working text-base for the present volume is the digitised **Bayerische Staatsbibliothek München** copy (StaBibl München shelfmark **Dogm. 11-2**), preserved in Google-Books scan `36616453470017`. This copy is also served under Google-Books ID `xRE8AAAAcAAJ` (direct PDF download is captcha-gated at the Google-Books endpoint; the working PDF is the BSB copy already on disk from the Pars I sourcing session of 2026-08-31). The PDF is stored locally at `sources-original/selnecker-pars-ii-1579.pdf` (30.6 MB / 695 pages of the 8vo octavo imprint), with a `pdfplumber` layout-extraction cached at `sources-original/pars2-full.txt` (1,056 KB / 24,003 lines) for reference.

The bibliographic authority for the 1579 Selnecker Pars II is **VD16 S 5566** (Jena, Jakob Tröster) — the specialist Reformation-controversy bibliography **Controversia et Confessio** (Herzog August Bibliothek, Wolfenbüttel) indexes the work under entry `fa69041c-1638-4246-99e4-4be5c37a1710`.

## 2. What the author himself supplies

Selnecker's own hand supplies the entirety of the volume — the *proem* on the Gospel ***De Evangelio,*** the six announced Loci (Justification of Man before God, Penitence, Christian Liberty, the Power of the Church, the Doctrine of the Sacraments, and the Rationale of Human Life), the internal sub-Loci which the six unfold (notably *De Regno Christi,* *De Bonis Operibus,* *De Libero Arbitrio* under Penitence; *De Praedestinatione* after Christian Liberty; *De Oratione* after the Church; *De Baptismo* and the extensive *De Coena Domini* under the Sacraments; and *De Resurrectione carnis* closing the *Ratio Vitae Hominis*), and the closing meditation ***Verba Piorum ex hac Vita in Aeternam Vitam Decedentium*** — an extract from an oration of *M. Johann Rosa* which Selnecker prints *in pia beata memoria* as the volume's *ars moriendi* coda.

His treatments quote — often at length — from Scripture (with clear preference for Isaiah, the Psalms, the Pauline corpus, and the Johannine literature), from the church fathers (Augustine, Chrysostom, Cyril of Alexandria, Basil, Gregory of Nazianzus, Cyprian, Bernard, Tauler), from Luther and Melanchthon (his *praeceptor,* whom he quotes both explicitly and idiomatically throughout), and from the Lutheran confessional documents — with special emphasis on those Formula-of-Concord Articles which Selnecker himself had helped draft: **Article III** (Justification) throughout Locus I, **Article IV** (Good Works) in the Penitence-material, **Article V** (Law and Gospel), **Article VI** (Third Use of the Law), **Article VII** (Lord's Supper) at very great length in *De Coena Domini,* **Article VIII** (Person of Christ) in the same, and **Article XI** (Predestination) in the appended tract after Christian Liberty.

All of these citations are preserved unchanged in the translation. Modern-critical equivalents (WA/LW for Luther; CR/MSA for Melanchthon; BSLK/*Kolb-Wengert* for the confessional documents; PL/PG/CCSL/SC for the church fathers; ESV numbering for scripture alongside Selnecker's own citation form on first occurrence per Locus) are consigned to the endnotes.

## 3. What the Scholastic Lutherans have added — and where

*Nothing in the body text is our own.* All editorial additions are relegated to numbered endnotes at the end of the volume. These endnotes supply:

- Standard modern citations for the Reformation-era works Selnecker cites (WA/LW for Luther; CR/MSA for Melanchthon; BSLK/*Kolb-Wengert* for confessional documents — with special attention to the *Formula of Concord* Selnecker helped draft)
- Standard modern citations for the church fathers (PL/PG/CCSL/SC volume:column; NPNF English vol:page)
- Biographical dates on first named mention of contemporaries and predecessors
- Cross-references to the paradigm *Formula of Concord* Articles III (Justification), IV (Good Works), V (Law and Gospel), VI (Third Use of Law), VII (Lord's Supper), VIII (Person of Christ), X (Adiaphora), and XI (Predestination) — all of which interface directly with Selnecker's Loci in this volume
- Cross-references to the parallel Loci corpora of the same generation: Melanchthon's *Loci communes* (1521/1543/1555), Chemnitz's *Loci theologici* (1591 posth.), Gerhard's later *Loci theologici* (1610–1622), and — for the sacramental treatments — Chemnitz's *Fundamenta sanae doctrinae de vera et substantiali praesentia,* the paradigm-Lutheran anti-Sacramentarian handbook of the same generation
- Cross-references to the parallel Loci in *Institutio Pars Prima* (companion volume, 2026)

## 4. Editorial conventions

Locked from the Praetorius trilogy and continued through Chytraeus, Kirchner, Quenstedt, Große, and Selnecker Pars I. See [[reference-scholastic-lutherans-glossary]] for the master vocabulary list and register-norms.

Highlights:

- **Register:** scholarly / academic-critical English; reverent but not archaising. Preserve substance of Selnecker's Loci-analytic style — the numbered *distinctiones,* *quaestiones,* *definitiones,* *argumenta,* *fontes solutionum,* and *usus practicus* — as the paradigm mature-Melanchthonian *Loci-format* which Selnecker inherits from his *praeceptor*.
- **Technical Latin terms:** preserved on first occurrence in each Locus in italic (*justificatio, sanctificatio, unio mystica, communicatio idiomatum, causa meritoria, causa instrumentalis, satisfactio vicaria, praesentia realis, manducatio oralis, manducatio impiorum, praedestinatio, poenitentia, contritio, absolutio, libertas Christiana, adiaphora,* etc.), glossed briefly, used in English thereafter — unless the term is itself the subject of the Locus, in which case the Latin is retained throughout.
- **Greek** appears where Selnecker quotes NT/patristic sources; preserved in polytonic script with parenthetical transliteration and English gloss on first substantive occurrence.
- **Hebrew** appears where Selnecker quotes OT/rabbinic sources; preserved in pointed Hebrew script with transliteration and gloss on first occurrence.
- **Scripture:** ESV for the English; Selnecker's own citation form (Vulgate numbering) noted parenthetically on first citation per Locus.
- **Confessional documents:** Selnecker cites the pre-*Concord* confessional writings (AC, Ap, SC, LC, SA — 1530–1537) as ***confessio nostra*** and the *Formula of Concord* (1577) as ***Formula Concordiae*** or occasionally ***nostra Concordia.*** Modern references consigned to endnotes: BSLK / *Kolb-Wengert.*
- **Named authors first occurrence:** append life-dates in the endnote, not in body.
- **Rubrication, typeface, layout:** locked via `shared/reference-custom.docx` and identical to the completed Chytraeus, Kirchner, Quenstedt, Große, and Selnecker-Pars-I volumes.
- **Ornamental divider:** `— ❦ —` (aldus leaf), per the convention adopted from Chytraeus onward.

## 5. Scope disclosure

At kick-off the present volume covers ***Pars Secunda*** in the entirety of its 695-page 1579 Jena Tröster imprint (Google-Books scan of the BSB Dogm. 11-2 copy).

**A structural feature to be noted transparently** at the outset: the 1579 Pars II imprint carries ***no separate dedicatory epistle.*** After the printed title page (p 6 of the scan) and the BSB shelfmark stamp (p 7), the volume proceeds directly into the *Proem De Evangelio* (p 8), which serves as the *praefatio ad lectorem* and as the doctrinal bridge from Pars I (which had closed with *De Consolatione*) into the six announced *ordo salutis* Loci. Pars I 1579 does carry a dedication (to the Consuls and Senators of the Republic of Leipzig, dated May 1579); Selnecker evidently regarded that Pars I dedication as functioning also for the continuation-volume, and did not add a second. This absence is documented here rather than passed over in silence.

**Paragraph-by-paragraph audit at close** — per the discipline confirmed on Selnecker Pars I and on the earlier Kirchner *Enchiridion* ([[iiif-manifest-verify]]) — every tranche of this Pars II translation will be cross-checked against the OCR line-ranges at volume close via parallel Explore agents. Section-level word-count ratios miss paragraph-level gaps, and this discipline is what turned "Pars I complete" into "Pars I *truly* complete" on 2026-09-02.

# Nikolaus Selnecker — Biographical Sketch

*(Reused from the companion Pars I volume for the reader coming to Selnecker for the first time.)*

**Nikolaus Selnecker** (also spelled *Selneccer* or *Schellenecker*) was born on **5 December 1530 at Hersbruck,** a small Franconian town near Nürnberg. His father, a jurist, brought the family to Nürnberg while Selnecker was still a child, and it was there that Selnecker received his first musical education — a formation that would define one strand of his later life-work as a paradigm-Lutheran hymn-writer.

At **Wittenberg** from 1550 he studied under **Philipp Melanchthon** — whose *Loci communes* method Selnecker would later adopt and adapt in the present *Institutio* — and took his *Magister* in 1554. From 1557 he served as *Hofprediger* to Kurfürst August of Saxony at Dresden, an office he held until 1565 when the paradigm ***Crypto-Calvinist controversy*** at the Dresden court forced his departure. He became **Professor of Theology at Jena** in 1565, then at **Leipzig** from 1568 — with interruptions during the second phase of the Crypto-Calvinist controversy 1570–1574 (when the strict-Lutherans were briefly displaced) — and finally returned to Leipzig from 1574 as *Superintendent* and *Pfarrer* at St. Thomas, an office he held (with a single year at Braunschweig-Wolfenbüttel 1571) until his death.

Selnecker's **confessional-Lutheran significance** rests on his role as one of the **six co-authors of the *Formula of Concord*** (1577). The six — Jakob Andreä (Tübingen), Martin Chemnitz (Braunschweig), David Chytraeus (Rostock), Andreas Musculus (Frankfurt/Oder), Christoph Körner (Frankfurt/Oder), and Selnecker himself (Leipzig) — worked between 1576 and 1580 to bring the *inner-Lutheran* controversies of the second half of the sixteenth century to a paradigm confessional settlement: the *Solida Declaratio* of the *Formula of Concord.* Selnecker's specific contribution was the *Solida Declaratio* Article XI (**Predestination**), whose paradigm anti-double-predestination Lutheran formulation — *God has predestined to salvation but not to damnation; the reprobate are lost by their own contumacy* — became the paradigm confessional-Lutheran position against both the Reformed *decretum absolutum* on one side and the Semi-Pelagian *praevisa merita* framework on the other.

Selnecker's **hymnody** is his second lasting legacy. Author of the paradigm ***"Ach bleib bei uns, Herr Jesu Christ"*** (*EG* 347 — the paradigm-canonical *Ausgangs-Choral* still sung in confessional-Lutheran and Reformed hymnody worldwide) and of dozens more, Selnecker's paradigm ***Christliche Psalmen*** (1587) is one of the paradigm confessional-Lutheran hymn-collections of the whole *Concord*-generation.

Selnecker died at **Leipzig on 24 May 1592,** and was buried in the *Paulinerkirche.* His epitaph, composed by his son, closed with the paradigm confessional-Lutheran memorial-verse ***Christus mihi vita, mors lucrum*** (Phil. 1:21).

The present *Institutio Christianae Religionis* is Selnecker's mature systematic-dogmatic Loci-work, composed in the years immediately after the *Formula of Concord* (1577) and issued in three Parts at Jena by **Jakob Tröster** in 1578–1580. **Pars II** — the present volume — appeared in **1579** and covers the paradigm **Reformation-era *ordo salutis*** from Justification through the *Ratio Vitae Hominis,* interfacing at every point with the Loci Selnecker had helped draft in the *Formula*'s *Solida Declaratio.*

# Proem — De Evangelio · On the Gospel

*(Pars II opens without a separate proem-heading in the imprint. The signature-heading* DE EVANGELIO *appears alone at the head of the running text on p. 8. Selnecker himself signals its function in the second paragraph — "we shall set this as a Proem to the Second Class of doctrine" — that is, as an introductory Locus prefixed to the six announced Loci which the title page catalogues.)*

## §1. The Sion-Prophecy Meditation

**The signification of the word *Evangelium* is well known.** In its generic sense it signifies ***a good and joyous message***³ — that is, a discourse that announces good and joyous things. Among the Greek writers this idiom was familiar: *"To crown* Euangelia,*"* ***εὐαγγελικῶς***⁴ — that is, "on account of good news"; and in *Isocrates:* *"to sacrifice* Euangelia,*"* — that is, to render thanks to God with a public rite of sacrifice on account of joyous tidings. And further, the word ***Evangelium*** signifies the reward given for a happy report — whence *Cicero to Atticus:* *"O sweet Letters, for which I acknowledge an Evangelium is owed"* — that is, a reward for the good news. The Germans call it ***ein Botenbrot.***⁵ And in *Homer* also ***Euangelia*** signifies the gifts or rewards which were given to those who brought glad tidings: *Odyssey* ξ [Book 14]: **Δαγγέλιόν δὲ μοι ἔσω** — *"Let there be a reward to me for the good news brought."* Afterwards ***Evangelium,*** as it were the *peculium* of Christ's Church, signifies the sum of that blessed doctrine which was not devised by men but was proclaimed to us by Jesus Christ come down from heaven, as it was foretold in Isaiah 52 — *that chapter truly of gold,* which is to be learned by the students word for word, and to be diligently commended and expounded to the youth by pious teachers. We shall set this before, as a proem to this Second Class of doctrine, with a very brief exposition.

> *"Arise, arise, put on thy strength, O Sion, put on the garments of thy beauty, O Jerusalem, holy city."*⁶

**Sion is the Church of Christ, gathered out of Jews and Gentiles.** She is here commanded to render two things. **First,** that she strenuously arm herself against her enemies, and boldly muster against them. **Second,** that she adorn herself splendidly — as a bride is adorned with jewels for the sake of the Son of God, the Bridegroom of the Church (Isa. 61); and as He himself is arrayed in priestly ornament, so are we as the Bride, adorned with jewels (Ps. 45).

But the arms are described in Ephesians 6.⁷ For since the life of the pious is a continual struggle and unceasing warfare, the pious mind always has that with which to contend. As, therefore, we ought to be *courageous* against our enemies, so it is entirely necessary that we be humble, gentle, kind, and glad toward the Bridegroom Christ — according to that word: *"Kiss the Son"*; and again in Psalm 45: *"Incline thy ear; I am thy servant."* And this Paul also intends in 2 Corinthians 10: *"The weapons of our warfare are not carnal, but mighty before God"* — that is, divinely powerful — *"to the pulling down of strongholds, while we cast down reasonings and every high thing that exalteth itself against the knowledge of God"* — (thus far Paul has expressed what Isaiah wills should be first: that the pious be equipped with arms against their enemies, the Devil, sin, the world, the flesh, heresy, the abuse of Philosophy, and so on. Now he explains the other part) — *"and bring into captivity every thought unto the obedience of Christ"* — that is, we submit ourselves wholly, we and all our thoughts, to Christ and to His Word, freely and with a spontaneous spirit. For these are the ***garments of beauty:***

**1.** Human reason — logic, physics, and ethics — that is, the universal *subjection* and, as it were, the *laying-down* at the knees of Christ; or, as Paul puts it, ***τῇ παντὸς ἢ τῷ ὅλῳ νοήματος αἰχμαλωσίᾳ εἰς τὴν ὑπακοὴν τοῦ Χριστοῦ***⁸ — in each and every article of the faith, in the words of the Institution of the Lord's Supper, and in the invocation which is our miraculous life-support.

**2.** The spontaneous obedience and alacrity of the will to dedicate itself wholly to the Son of God, according to that word: *"Draw me after Thee."*

**3.** Invocation, patience, fidelity in one's calling, the hope of deliverance, the desire of eternal life and of converse with Christ, with the Angels and with the blessed — as *Monica,* the mother of Augustine, was wont to say: ***Evolemus, evolemus*** — *"Let us fly away, let us fly away."* And Paul: *"I desire to depart and to be with Christ."*⁹

> *"For no more shall the uncircumcised and the unclean enter into thee. Shake thyself from the dust, arise and sit down, O Jerusalem; loose the bonds from thy neck, O captive daughter of Sion."*¹⁰

**This is the reason** why for those who believe in the Son *the war is to be waged with one hand,* and *peace is to be restored with the other* — why quiet and gladness are to be repaired, cultivated, and exercised in Christ. For this is truly the *office* of the pious, of which it is wont to be said: *"With one hand she wages war; the other hand renders peace."* For, since Christians are ***Kings and Priests,*** they ought to render both offices — that they be not only adorned with arms but also armed with laws, and that with one hand they build the temple, with the other bear the sword against the enemies: even as the Jewish people, returned out of the Babylonian captivity, was wont to do in the building of the Jerusalem Temple.¹¹

The reason itself is twofold. **The first** is, that "the uncircumcised and the unclean shall no more enter" — that is, false teachers shall not any more seduce the people, but shall be prostrated, vanquished, and confounded by the Word of God, and shall bear no part of the government — according to Psalm 1: *"The impious shall not stand in the judgement,"* and Psalm 9: *"The impious is trapped in the works of his own hands,"* — that is, by *meditation* (namely of the Word), not by way or by sword. And Psalm 17: *"By the word of Thy lips I have observed the ways of the plunderers"* (*Pharizim*) — that is, of those corrupting the true doctrine.

**The other reason** is, that "the bonds have been broken" — that is, the yoke of the Law has been taken away, and *there is now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus.* The Law has been abrogated, as far as concerns its malediction. *We are not under the Law, but under Grace;* and *the Law is not laid down for the just* — as we said more copiously above in the Locus *On the Law.*¹² The flaming sword (Gen. 3), the stone tablets (Exod. 34), the great fire (Deut. 18), the iron yoke (Deut. 28; Jer. 28), the yoke of burden, the rod of the shoulder, the sceptre of the exactor (Isa. 9), the shattering hammer (Jer. 23), the unbearable burden (Acts 15), the ministry of death (2 Cor. 3), — or, as this passage says, the ***bonds of the neck*** — have now ceased: that is, the *thundering of the Law* does not terrify those who believe in the Son.

> *"For thus saith the Lord: Ye are sold for nothing, and ye shall be redeemed without silver."*¹³

**Here is shown what doctrine ought to succeed the doctrine of the Law** — namely the *doctrine of the Gospel,* whose sum and perpetual voice is: *"Ye are sold for nothing, and ye shall be redeemed without silver."* That is: *unjustly were ye reduced into servitude by the fraud of the Devil, who tore you from Me and neither paid Me any price nor gave you any promise. It is just, therefore, in turn, that I recover My own thing, saith the Lord* — no price being paid to the robber.

For, as a stolen thing is not again to be bought back from the thief or the plunderer, but is restored to its owner *without price* by the righteous judge, — so God recovers the human race, paying nothing to the unjust robber, the Devil, and demanding no price from the human race, which can pay no price whatever. We are received, therefore, ***for nothing*** — not on account of our merits, but on account of the grace and faith of Christ. And this is the constant and perpetual confession of the ***Orthodox Churches:*** that *for nothing, without works of the Law, by faith alone in Christ, we are justified, reconciled to God, and saved.*¹⁴

And hence has been drawn the explication of the *Second Article of the Apostles' Symbol:* *"I believe that Jesus Christ, true God begotten of the Father before the ages, and likewise true man born of the Virgin Mary, is my Lord, who has redeemed me — a lost and damned man — and delivered me from all sins and from the power of Satan, not indeed with gold and silver, but with His own holy and precious blood."*¹⁵

And Isaiah 55: *"O all ye that thirst, come to the waters — even those who have no money — come, buy and eat; come, buy, I say, without money and without any price, wine and milk. For thus saith the Lord God: My people went down at the beginning into Egypt, that they might sojourn there; and the Assyrian without cause hath oppressed them. That is, I will free My people, as I did before deliver them out of Egypt and out of Babylon."*

# Locus I. De Iustificatione Hominis coram Deo · On the Justification of Man before God

*(Selnecker heads this Locus in the imprint:* DOCTRINA DE IVSTIFICATIONE HOMINIS CORAM DEO — *the Doctrine on the Justification of Man before God. Locus I opens on p. 38 of the 1579 imprint (OCR ln 1131), following the transition passage in which Selnecker names it "the most saving and most necessary doctrine of the Justification of man before God."*¹⁷*)*

## §1. On the Principal Significations of the Word *Iustitia*

**Passing over the heaping-up of the significations of *iustitia*** (righteousness / justice) in the principal languages — Hebrew, Greek, and Latin — we shall assign to the word ***iustitia*** a threefold use.¹⁸

**First,** the name *iustitia* is often used for ***universal justice*** — that is, for *obedience according to all the laws,* or for *congruence with the law* — which congruence is called *virtue,* as it is said:

**ἐν δὲ δικαιοσύνῃ συλλήβδην πᾶσ' ἀρετή ἐστι**¹⁹ ·
***Iustitia in se se virtutes continet omnes***
*(Justice contains in itself all the virtues.)*

And **Aristotle** calls *iustitia* the ***right use of all the virtues.***²⁰

**Second,** it is used for ***particular justice,*** which renders to each what is his own, and preserves in contracts and in dealings with others an *equality* — that is, an equality of what is *honourable* and *seemly.* These two uses are elsewhere called ***legal justices.***²¹ But in the Church they are otherwise explained than in the doctrine of Philosophy. For the *iustitia legis* requires both *interior and exterior* obedience, and *the congruence of the whole man with God.* Whereas Philosophy discourses only concerning ***external discipline and honourableness,*** and does not judge that those are *sins* which within, in the mind and heart, will and affections, do not congrue with God or with the Law.

**The third** use is proper to the ***Gospel*** — as when we say: ***"the thief on the cross is just by faith,"*** that is, ***accepted and pleasing to God, and given the remission of sins on account of Christ the Mediator.***²² Concerning certain other special significations of the word *iustitia* we have spoken in our exposition of Psalm 32.²³

## §2. What is the Righteousness of Faith or of the Gospel? — Definition

**The righteousness of faith is nothing else than** the absolution from sin and guilt, the acceptance and reconciliation of the sinner who is doing penitence before God — which is made by faith in Christ the Mediator, who died and rose for us; or, ***the remission of sins,*** ***the imputation of the righteousness or obedience of Christ,*** ***the free redemption and liberation from sin, from eternal death, from the wrath of God, from the malediction of the Law, from the tyranny of the Devil, and from damnation.*** This liberation is done *for nothing,* *out of the mere mercy of God for the sake of His Son,* without works of the Law, and without the merit of man, and is the ***adoption of those who believe*** — into the sons and heirs of God — unto life, gladness, and eternal salvation.

**In this description, such as it is, one thing is worthy of consideration:** namely, that ***iustificatio*** is set opposite to the word ***condemnatio.*** For ***to be justified*** is altogether a ***forensic word,*** and signifies to be set at the judgement-seat, ***to be absolved from the guilt of sin and from damnation.***²⁴

## From the Front Matter

¹ **English gloss of the title page.** — The 1579 title page's scripture-epigraph is drawn from **1 Peter 2:12** in the Vulgate rendering (*Conversationem vestram inter gentes habentes bonam: ut in eo quod detrectant de vobis tamquam de malefactoribus, ex bonis operibus vos considerantes, glorificent Deum in die visitationis*). Selnecker crops the verse to its exhortative first half and renders the closing phrase *in die visitationis* as *die quo idipsum manifestum fiet* — "on the day in which the same shall be made manifest" — a paraphrase common in 16th-c. Lutheran devotional prints. — Note also the two Hebrew tetragrammaton-characters between the title and the epigraph, printed twice as **יהוה יהוה** (rendered as ח ר ח ר by the OCR through Antiqua-Hebrew type-confusion); this is Selnecker's characteristic printer's-invocation of the divine Name at the opening of each of his printed works. — For the *Cum Gratia & Privilegio ad decennium* formula, cf. the parallel Chytraeus 1594 and Kirchner 1592 imprints: this is the standard Imperial-Book-Privilege wording granting ten-year exclusive-publication protection.

² **On the absence of a Pars II dedication.** — Compare the extensive dedication of Pars I *To the Consuls and Senators of the Republic of Leipzig,* signed **Leipzig, in the month of May 1579,** and the pattern in Selnecker's *Paedagogia,* *Analecta,* *Explicatio Symbolorum,* and other of the didactic-books catalogued in the *Catalogus Librorum Didacticorum* of Pars I. The multi-volume-work-with-single-dedication is a common Late-Renaissance convention (compare Chemnitz's *Loci theologici* and Gerhard's *Loci theologici*).

## From the Proem *De Evangelio*

³ ***Bonum et laetum nuntium*** — "a good and joyous message." — Selnecker deliberately places the etymology first, before any dogmatic definition, in the manner of Melanchthon's *Loci communes* (1543) *De Evangelio* opening. Cf. **CR** 21:355ff. (*Loci Præcipui Theologici* ed. 1559).

⁴ **εὐαγγελικῶς** — "as glad tidings." — Selnecker's Antiqua Greek is not always cleanly set in the 1579 imprint; several later Greek quotations in the Proem are corrupted in the OCR by Antiqua-Greek/Latin type-confusion (e.g., **Δαγγέλιόν** where **εὐαγγέλιόν** is required, and **Χριστῦ** for **Χριστοῦ** at the *αἰχμαλωσίᾳ εἰς τὴν ὑπακοὴν τοῦ Χριστοῦ* citation). The English follows Selnecker's manifest intent as attested by the standard editions of the classical loci.

⁵ ***ein Botenbrot*** — literally "a messenger's bread" — the medieval-German customary reward paid to a messenger bringing good news. — Selnecker also cites the same term in his 1587 *Christliche Psalmen* preface, where he applies it homiletically to the Gospel-preacher's calling.

⁶ **Isaiah 52:1** — Vulgate *Consurge, consurge, induere fortitudine tua, Sion, induere vestimentis gloriæ tuæ, Jerusalem civitas sancti* → ESV *"Awake, awake, put on your strength, O Zion; put on your beautiful garments, O Jerusalem, the holy city."* — Selnecker's chapter-by-chapter meditation on Isaiah 52 through 55 dominates the Proem *De Evangelio* (OCR ln 79–ln ~500) as its scriptural framework.

⁷ **Ephesians 6:11ff.** — the ***panoplia Dei,*** the *whole armour of God.* Compare Selnecker's 1582 sermon-cycle on Ephesians (in his *Postilla*).

⁸ **2 Corinthians 10:5** (Selnecker's own Greek) — **αἰχμαλωτίζοντες πᾶν νόημα εἰς τὴν ὑπακοὴν τοῦ Χριστοῦ** — the OCR-corrupted rendering *αἰχμαλωσίᾳ* has been silently restored to the standard *αἰχμαλωτίζοντες* / *αἰχμαλωσίαν* text.

⁹ **Philippians 1:23** — *cupio dissolvi et esse cum Christo.* Selnecker paraphrases *cupio emigrare* for the Vulgate's *cupio dissolvi* — a rendering common in Melanchthonian devotional Latin. The Monica-anecdote is drawn from **Augustine, *Confessions* IX.10** (*CCSL* 27:147; *NPNF* I.1:139).

¹⁰ **Isaiah 52:1–2** — continuation of the same Sion-prophecy.

¹¹ **The Two-Handed Christian: Warrior and Builder.** — Selnecker's *"Kings and Priests"* motif here draws on **1 Peter 2:9** (*regale sacerdotium*) and on the Nehemiah 4 image of the *builders with sword and trowel.* The Latin sententia *Una gerit bellum, pacem manus altera reddit* is a stock **Reformation adage** attributed variously to Luther and to Melanchthon; the precise printed source has not been located.

¹² **"as we said more copiously above in the Locus *On the Law*"** — cross-reference to the companion volume ***Selnecker, Institutio Pars I, Locus VII De Lege*** (working English edition 2026, pp. 285ff.), where Selnecker treats the triple abrogation of the Mosaic Law (ceremonial/judicial/moral) and the triple use of the Moral Law (*paedagogicus / politicus / didacticus,* with the *Formula of Concord* Article VI *tertius usus legis* interface).

¹³ **Isaiah 52:3** — Vulgate *Quia hæc dicit Dominus: gratis venundati estis et sine argento redimemini.*

¹⁴ ***Orthodoxarum Ecclesiarum.*** — Selnecker's standard reference-term for the *Concordia-Lutheran* churches, i.e., those subscribing to the 1580 *Book of Concord.* Compare his 1582 *Epitome recens historiae de Sacra Coena Christi.*

¹⁵ **Second Article of the Apostles' Symbol.** — Selnecker's paraphrase here follows the wording of **Luther's Small Catechism** on the Second Article (1529): *Ich glaube, dass Jesus Christus... mich verlorenen und verdammten Menschen erlöset hat, erworben und gewonnen, von allen Sünden, vom Tode und von der Gewalt des Teufels, nicht mit Gold oder Silber, sondern mit seinem heiligen, teuren Blut...* — *BSLK* 511 / *Kolb-Wengert* 355. Cf. also **Large Catechism** Second Article, *BSLK* 649 / *Kolb-Wengert* 434.

¹⁶ ***Basar,*** ***Bisar,*** ***Besroh*** — Selnecker unfolds the Hebrew etymology of *bāśar* (flesh, and thence *to bear news to the flesh* → *to preach good tidings*) in the Proem *De Evangelio* at OCR ln 295ff. This treatment is deferred to T2.

## From Locus I *De Iustificatione*

¹⁷ **Transition passage** (OCR ln 1125–1128, closing the pre-Iustificatio material): *"Now we shall take in hand the most saving and most necessary doctrine of the Justification of man before God, God helping us."* — Selnecker's *sedes-doctrinae* signal for the Locus.

¹⁸ **Threefold use of *iustitia.*** — This distinction is Melanchthon's, from the *Loci Præcipui Theologici* (1559) *De Iustificatione* opening — cf. **CR** 21:731ff., **MSA** II/1:328. Selnecker inherits it exactly.

¹⁹ **Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics* V.1.1129b30** — **ἐν δὲ δικαιοσύνῃ συλλήβδην πᾶσ' ἀρετή ἐστι** ("in justice all virtue is comprehended in a word"). — Aristotle attributes the sentence itself to *Theognis* fr. 147 West. The Latin verse-rendering *Iustitia in se se virtutes continet omnes* is a common Renaissance-humanist verse-hexameter; compare **Erasmus, *Adagia* III.5.62.**

²⁰ **Aristotle**, *Nicomachean Ethics* V.1 in general — where justice is treated as the *summary virtue* embracing all others.

²¹ ***Iustitia legalis*** — the scholastic category of "legal justice" as elaborated by **Thomas Aquinas, ST II-II q. 58 art. 5–6.** Selnecker's rejection of Philosophy's satisfaction with *external discipline* is Melanchthonian and reflects the *Apology of the Augsburg Confession* Article IV rejection of philosophical righteousness — cf. **Ap. IV. 12–14** (*BSLK* 160 / *Kolb-Wengert* 122).

²² **The thief on the cross** — *Luke 23:43.* Selnecker's use of the good thief as the *paradigmatic case* of *sola fide* justification is standard Lutheran doctrinal-preaching, deriving from **Luther, *Lectures on Genesis* on Gen 15:6** (WA 42:562ff. / *LW* 3:19ff.).

²³ **Selnecker's exposition of Psalm 32.** — Reference to his own earlier work: cross-reference not yet identified with certainty; probable candidate is his *Der ganze Psalter Davids* (Nürnberg 1565, expanded Leipzig 1581) or the *Psalmodia* (Wittenberg 1567). To be verified against **VD16** at the audit.

²⁴ ***Forensis vocabulum.*** — Central Melanchthon-inherited term. Selnecker's placement of the forensic character right at the head of the definition is programmatic and interfaces directly with **Formula of Concord Article III** *Solida Declaratio* — of which Selnecker was co-author — which insists on the forensic and imputative character of justification against the Osiandrian *unio essentialis / iustitia essentialis* framework. Cf. **FC SD III. 9ff., 17ff., 30ff., 62–63** — *BSLK* 916ff. / *Kolb-Wengert* 562ff. This is the *sedes doctrinae* passage of the whole Locus.
