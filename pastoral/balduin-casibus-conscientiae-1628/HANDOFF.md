## What this project is

Translating **Friedrich Balduin** (1575–1627) · ***Tractatus Luculentus, Posthumus, Toti Reipublicae Christianae Utilissimus, de Materiâ rarissimè antehac enucleatâ, Casibus nimirum Conscientiae*** (Wittenberg: Paulus Helwigius, 1628) from Latin into modern English.

**Historical significance.** This is the **first single-author Lutheran casuistry** — Balduin (Hutter's successor at Wittenberg, †1627) is the first Protestant to work moral theology in the form of *cases of conscience* in a serious systematic manner. The *Tractatus* was published posthumously the year after his death and was repeatedly reprinted through the 17th century. It fills a genre gap in our library: we have Wittenberg dogmatic (Selnecker, Kirchner), catechetical (Kirchner Enchiridion, Chytraeus), and polemical work, but no pastoral-casuistry.

## Author

**Friedrich Balduin** (Balduinus; also Bad., Baldo. — b. **17 November 1575 at Dresden**; d. **1 May 1627 at Wittenberg**). Studied at Wittenberg under **Aegidius Hunnius** and **Polykarp Leyser**; MA 1598, D. theol. 1607. Professor of Theology at Wittenberg from 1604, succeeding **Leonhard Hutter** in the senior chair after Hutter's death in 1616. General-Superintendent of the Saxon Electorate from 1614. One of the *Wittenberg orthodoxy* generation between Chemnitz and Gerhard, most closely associated with **Hunnius, Leyser, Hutter, and the young Johann Gerhard** (whose *Loci* Balduin knew). Known also for a *Commentarius in omnes epistolas Beati Pauli* (14 vols, published 1608–1623) which was one of the standard Wittenberg-Lutheran Pauline commentaries of the 17th century.

(VII) When words are ambiguous — accept per mind of swearer (Gregory 16 moral. c. 5 + c. 22 q. 5 c. 12 with Gloss *God accepts oath as swearer understands*); unambiguous words — accept in given sense, no hidden-thought twist; **anti-scholastic mental-reservation polemic** — some allow (adulteress before husband denying via tacit *should-be-shown-to-man*; robber-money-promise with tacit *if I owe*) — Balduin: impia doctrina, licentia mentiendi, sermo est character mentis; oaths admit no simulation though words sometimes may. (VIII) Oaths against piety/charity/public good — no obligation; *l. 15 ff. de conditio. instit. facta quae laedunt pietatem, existimationem verecundiam nostram, & generaliter contra bonos mores fiunt*, and *De pactis, pacta quae contra leges constitutionesve vel contra bonos mores fiunt, nullam vim habere*. (IX) Oaths per se indifferent — do not fall under oath (right-hand vs left absurd); if hidden good-ratio, obligate. (X) Oaths for something with bad end — distinguish per-se-evil (theft/adultery/homicide/unjust-war — not bound) vs good-arising-from-evil (safe-return, offspring-from-adultery, preservation-in-delict — bound as gratitude to God's benefit). (XI) Oaths on trivial/small matter — reject fictitious mortal-venial distinction per Deut 27:26 *every sin merits divine wrath and eternal malediction*; Cajetan real-integrity-vs-part-distinction (pear-apple omitted) vs Sotus lib. 8 De iust. q. 1 art. 5 + Navarrus manuali c. 12 n. 20+40 + Azorius xi c. 4 (light bond only) both wrong; every oath binds equally since invokes divine name. (XII) Coerced oaths bind — Aristotelian mixed-voluntary-involuntary per Ethic. iii c. 1; robbers example — money-promise stands under oath because (1) made to God, (2) not simply involuntary, (3) private thing to which truth preferred, (4) public good served (life-preservation); *Cicero* De officiis iii *Regulus* laudabilis-example; Aquinas ecclesial-rescission-of-force-oath rejected — Church cannot make against natural-divine right. (XIII) When licit to rescind rash oath — Herod-John Baptist-head Matt 14:9 illicit-thing-should-have-been-rescinded; David-Nabal averted by Abigail 1 Sam 25 with Augustine ser. 1 *De decoll. Iohan.* *iuravit David temere, sed non implevit, majori pietate*; David-Shimei retracted before Solomon 1 Kgs 2:9 (Exod 22:28 nemaledicas-principi); when oath fights with commonwealth-laws Rom 13:1,5; when thing becomes impossible/illicit (perpetual-celibacy vow); persons excluded (boys/mad/insane/those-sub-alia-potestate per Num 30:4 + Council of Gangra c. 15). (XIV) Oaths made from error bind if licit-and-possible — Gibeonites Josh 9 covenant kept though by imposture, confirmed miraculously per Josh 10:12 sun-standstill; Saul's violation punished with famine + 7 sons hanged 2 Sam 21. (XV) What perjury is + threefold way — per Isidore c. 22 q. 5 *mendacium confirmatum iuramento*; lie in words or in facts; three modes (asserting false as true / promising with never-intention / promising with intention but not-performing); false-god-perjury not proper (illicit-oath cannot yield perjury but abuses divine name); rash oaths not proper perjury but abuse; laws with oath — perjury unless one submits to penalty (obedience-or-penalty distinction). (XVI) How atrocious is perjury (six-fold rationale for fleeing) — (1) express prohibition Zech 5:3 + Wis 8:17 + Zech 8:17 + Lev 19:12; (2) exclusion from kingdom Ps 15:4 + Zech 5:4 + Mal 3:5; (3) OT punishments Israelites/Gibeonites 2 Sam 21 + Zedekiah 2 Chr 36 with Ezek 17:15-16 prophecy + Alcimus stroke 1 Macc 9:54 + Menelaus killed 2 Macc 4:38 + *manus Dei non manumittit periurium, si non statim, aliquando* Exod 20:7; (4) perjury heavier than homicide — violates first-table + abuses divine name + calls own damnation (Gen 9:5-6 + Gal 5:20 + 1 John 3:15); (5) gentile prodigies — Sicilian Aetnaean Palici altar per Macrobius v Saturnal. c. 19 + Virgil Aen. 9 *Sympetha* + Aristotle De mirabilibus auscultationibus fountain Acadinus + Sardinian fountains blinding per Solinus 10 + Tyanaean fountain per Philostratus Life of Apollonius i c. 4 + Stygian waters per Herodotus 6 + Pliny 21.10 + Ovid Ibin + Palemonis specus at Corinth per Pausanias Corinth. ii + Ovid perjuratam-Stygial; (6) gentile laws — Cicero De legibus ii *penalty divine exitium human dedecus violati iurisiurandi esto* + Indian finger-amputation + Scythian capital per Strabo xv + Egyptian capital per Diodorus ii c. 1; Christian canon-laws (perjurers marked infamous per c. finibus; 40-day-bread-water + 7-year-penance c. quicunque in 6 q. 1; exclusion from testimony c. praedicandum c. 2 q. 1; penalty equal to adultery-homicide-capital; cleric deposition per c. cum sinces. et c. ab homine); civil laws (Baldus l. Lucius ff. de fide — no infamia in assertorio only promissorio, especially if solemn/public; l. si major extra-judicial infamy for perjury to another's harm). (XVII) When may sworn promise not bind — Aquinas IIa IIae q. 89 art. 8 + Cajetan + Navarrus manuali c. 11 n. 7; four cases: (1) thing illicit/useless/impossible (Herod-John Matt 14:7); (2) status of things/persons changes per Seneca lib. 4 De beneficiis c. 34 conditions-remaining vs Paul-Corinth-not-coming 2 Cor 1; (3) mutual oaths broken by other party — *frangenti fidem, fides frangatur eidem*; (4) supposed-cause failing (benefit not obtained). (XVIII) Whether men can relax oaths — no, only tacit-consent of superior; Papal claim to relax subjects' oaths of princes rejected; Becanus Appendix disputationis obedientia Christ mag. exam. propos. 2 hedges then admits *si urgens necessitas boni spiritualis* — Balduin: no man has power against divine-natural right; *religio iurisiurandi* is bond of ancestors per Cicero De officiis lib. 3 *Regulus* example (fatherland/parents/love-of-own could not retract oath even to Punic tortures); illicit-matter (null from origin — no need for dispensation); *break-faith-to-ill-promised*; recipient can waive right; jurist-not-Pope should resolve doubtful cases. (XX) Coronidis loco (final conclusions) — 7 practical points: puberes not admitted per c. pueri 12 q. 5, priests before civil magistrate not required per c. non liceat 22 q. 5 except in 4 cases (goods lost, faith/religion at stake, common salvation of Rep., purge infamy); competent judge required; judge may compel witness under some conditions; oaths on fast per c. honestum 22 q. 5; judge should not compel one he knows will perjure per Abbas Panormitanus on c. clericos de habitat. clericorum (clerics-concubines-not-to-be-forced); infamous cannot swear self-clear (penitence absolves in God's forum but authority of judge remains); *iuramentum calumniae* licit — 5 requirements (just-suit-estimation, no truth-hiding, no knowing-false-proofs, no fraud-prolongation, no promise for cause of suit).
