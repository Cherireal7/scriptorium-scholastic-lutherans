# Translator's Note

## 1. The text-base

This working English translation follows the **third edition** of Kirchner's *Enchiridion*, printed at **Frankfurt am Main by Johann Spies in the year M.D.XCII (1592)**. The title page describes itself as **"Jetzt zum dritten mahl mit allem Fleiß übergangen, und an vielen Orten mercklich gebessert. Sammt einem Register, darinnen ein jeder Articul, so in diesem Buch begriffen, zu finden"** — *"now for the third time gone over with all diligence and in many places noticeably improved, together with a Register in which every Article treated in this Book may be found."* This 1592 third edition is Kirchner's mature revision of the 1583 first edition (Heidelberg, Spies), which we translated in an earlier working edition (preserved in the file `kirchner-enchiridion.1583-first-edition.md`).

The source-text used here is the Google Books digitisation of the copy held at the Bavarian State Library, Munich, from which we have extracted the per-page OCR text via **pdftotext (Poppler)** — yielding 38,735 lines of running Fraktur text across the book's 889 PDF pages. The OCR text has the usual imperfections of Google-Books-quality Fraktur (long-*s* → *f* / *ss* confusion, occasional missing spaces, ligature-recognition errors), which we correct silently against the underlying source-image where the OCR is broken; systematic gains or losses are noted in the endnotes when they touch the doctrine.

The book itself has **46 numbered Chapters** (in the Register: **I. Von der Heiligen Schrifft** through **XLVI. Vom ewigen Leben**), plus a Widmungsvorrede to the Elector Palatine (Ludwig VI), a title page, and a closing Index. The 1583 first edition had approximately the same 46-chapter structure but was materially expanded and revised in 1592.

## 2. Editorial scope of this working edition

This edition renders **all 46 Chapters in full Q&A depth, line-by-line and paragraph-by-paragraph**, matching the source Q&A structure. Nothing is compressed, summarized, or skipped. Where the earlier 1583 edition translation had rendered some chapters (XV–XXX) in compact-summary mode for pace, this 1592 edition rebuild works exhaustively through every question, every answer, every enumeration, every Bible citation, every patristic quotation.

## 3. Editorial conventions

*(Details on Latin/Greek preservation, Bible-citation normalisation, Fraktur handling, and the *Hauptstück* → "Chapter" rendering will follow the same conventions as the 1583 edition.)*

# Chapter I

### *Von der Heiligen Schrifft, oder Wort Gottes*

*On the Holy Scripture, or the Word of God*

— ❦ —

## Question 1. Whence is it proven that the Christian religion and faith alone is the true religion and faith?

**Whence is it proven that alone the Christian religion and faith is the right religion and faith?**

**First**, from **antiquity**. For no faith upon earth — however it may be named — has stood so long as this Faith. Adam and all the Fathers from the beginning of the world have had this Faith, and all the blessed will have this Faith until the end of the world:

- Gen. 3:15 — *"I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed"*, etc.
- Acts 10:43 — *"To this Jesus give all the prophets witness"*, etc.
- Rom. 4:3 — *"What saith the Scripture? Abraham believed God, and it was counted unto him for righteousness."*

**Second**, no faith upon earth has done such wonder-signs as this Faith. On this let one read all the Histories of the Old and New Testaments. The other faiths in the world have not been able to do such wonder-signs, though they attempted them.

**Third**, no faith upon earth has had so many martyrs, who so knightly fought and contended against every offence and persecution, as the Christian Faith. Many Christians from the beginning have indeed been slain over it — yet this Faith has stood against all, even when it stood alone, and though all the others opposed and assailed it.

**Fourth**, no faith from the beginning of the world has been so certainly proclaimed and prophesied — how it and other faiths in the world would fare — as this Christian Faith. The Christian Faith has sure Prophecies of how it and other faiths in the world will fare, and so on. And the histories rhyme finely with the prophecies.

**Fifth**, especially is the doctrine of the Gospel — which Christendom alone has — a doctrine of which no human reason naturally knows, and without which no lasting comfort in *Anfechtungen* can be drawn or grasped. And the experience of all the right-believing testifies that it works in them steadfast comfort, peace, and joy of heart in tribulation; so that they can rightly settle themselves in peace and conclude that with God they are well.

**Rom. 5:1** — *"Now, since we have become righteous through faith in Christ, we have peace with God, and access to grace."*

— ❦ —

## Question 2. What is God's Word, or the Holy Scripture?

**What then is God's Word, or the Holy Scripture?**

It is **a doctrine given by God himself**, in which Law and Gospel are set forth, and it is taught what God is according to his Essence, and also what his Will is toward us men.

**John 17:17** — *"Sanctify them in thy truth; thy Word is the truth."*

— ❦ —

## Question 3. Which are the chief chapters of Scripture, or of the Word?

**Which are the chief chapters of the Scripture, or of the Word?**

**Two:** the **Law** and the **Gospel**.

The **Law** commands and requires of us what we ought to do and to leave undone (Exod. 20).

The **Gospel**, however, does not preach what we are to do and to leave undone; but only bids us to hold out the lap of our garment and to take, and says:

*"See, dear man — this has God done for thee. He has given thee his Son, has had him slain for thy sake,"* etc.

**John 3:16** — *"For God so loved the world that he gave his only-begotten Son,"* etc.

— ❦ —

## Question 4. Whence does the Holy Scripture take its beginning?

**Whence does the Holy Scripture take its beginning?**

**From God himself.** For at Exod. 32:15–16 it stands thus: *"Moses turned himself, and went down from the mount, and had the two tables of the Testimony in his hand, which were written on both sides; and God had made them himself, and himself had graven the Writing therein."*

From this it clearly appears that **God himself made the first beginning** of putting his Word and Revelation into writing. And from this also the *loftiness of the Holy Divine Scripture* is to be recognised.

Moses hereafter, by God's command (as Exod. 33 shows), wrote out the whole Law, and left it to the children of Israel. And him have the Prophets, Evangelists, and Apostles followed, and so on.

— ❦ —

## Question 5. By whom was God's Word first brought forth?

**By whom was God's Word first brought forth?**

**By God himself.** As **Psalm 103[:7]** says: *"He made his ways known unto Moses, his deeds unto the children of Israel."* And **2 Tim. 3:16** — *"All Scripture given by God through inspiration is profitable for doctrine,"* etc.

— ❦ —

## Question 6. What moved him to it?

**What moved him thereto?**

**Nothing else** but his unspeakable **mercy**, according to which he willed to make himself known to us men by a certain and steadfast Word. Especially, however, since men had fallen into sin and lost God's right knowledge (Gen. 3), he did not will that we all should altogether and wholly, in such blindness and ignorance of God our Creator and of his will toward us, go down to ruin — for otherwise we should all have had to be eternally damned and lost, and remain so.

— ❦ —

## Question 7. With what men, then, is such Word of God to be found?

**With what men, then, is such Word of God to be found?**

**Alone with the true rightful Congregation of God**, to whom God himself has revealed himself through his Word and Wonder-signs.

As **Deut. 4:6–8** stands: *"This shall be your wisdom and understanding in the sight of all peoples, when they shall hear all these commandments and shall have to say: See, what a wise and understanding people is this, and a noble people. For where is so noble a people to which the gods draw so near, as the LORD our God, whenever we call upon him? And where is so noble a people that hath such righteous statutes and commandments as all this Law which I set before you this day?"*

Likewise **Psalm 147:19–20** — *"He showeth his Word unto Jacob, his statutes and his rights unto Israel; so hath he not done to any of the heathen, nor doth he let them know his rights."*

**John 10:4–5** — *"My sheep hear my voice, and a stranger they follow not."*

— ❦ —

## Question 8. What should a Christian principally seek in Holy Scripture, and to what does the Holy Scripture chiefly point?

**What should a Christian principally seek in Holy Scripture — and to what does the Holy Scripture most of all point?**

In the Holy Scripture one shall above all seek **Christ Jesus, the Saviour of the world**, together with his benefits and merit — for all Scripture principally points to and shows him.

**John 5:39** — *"They bear witness of me."*

— ❦ —

## Question 9. Which are the chief properties of the Holy Scripture?

**Which are the chief properties of the Holy Scripture? These.**

**First**, that the Scripture is the true **Mistress over every other human writing**. For the writings of all Fathers and men must be proved through her, or otherwise they do not stand.

**Ps. 19:8** — *"The Testimony of the LORD is sure."*

Whence **Augustine, *Contra Epistolam Petiliani*** says: *"Nec catholicis Episcopis consentiendum est, sicubi forte falluntur, aut contra canonicas Dei Scripturas aliquid sentiunt."* — *"One is not bound to hold with the Catholic teachers themselves, wherever they perchance are deceived or teach anything against the Holy Scripture."*¹

**Second**, that she is **more certain than all Councils** — and the Councils themselves must direct themselves after her.

**Gal. 1:8** — *"Even if an angel from heaven should preach another Gospel than that which we have preached unto you, let him be accursed."*

**Jerome:** *"Spiritus Sancti doctrina est, quae canonicis literis prodita est, contra quam si quid statuant concilia, nephas duco."* — *"It is the Holy Spirit's doctrine which has been set forth in the canonical writings; if the Councils lay down anything contrary to it, I hold it for wrong."*²

**Third**, she suffers **no human addition** — she will be alone, or nothing at all.

**Ps. 12:6** — where she is therefore called *"a pure silver."*

**Fourth**, she is **not to be learned to the end** — as the 147th Psalm says of her: *"Of his understanding there is no number"* (Ps. 147:5).

Whence every Christian, of high and of low estate, must **search day and night** in the Holy Scripture.

— ❦ —

## Question 10. Whence is it clear that the Holy Scripture is a certain doctrine on which one may and must rely?

**Whence is it then clear that the Holy Scripture is a certain doctrine on which one may and must rely?**

**First**, that she was begun to be written **by God himself**.

**Second**, that she was **inspired by God himself** (2 Tim. 3:16).

**Third**, that she has been from the beginning **confirmed with such excellent divine Wonder-works**.

**Fourth**, that the holy men who wrote her were **driven thereto by God himself**.

**2 Pet. 1:19** — *"We have a firm prophetic Word, and ye do well that ye take heed thereto as unto a light that shineth in a dark place,"* etc.

— ❦ —

## Question 11. Does the Pope not rightly say that the Scripture has her regard from the Church?

**Does the Pope not rightly say that the Scripture has her regard from the Church?**

**No.** For she has her *Authority and regard* solely and alone from God himself, who has given her. That, however, the Pope draws the regard of Scripture *upon the Church* — that is at bottom just as much as if one said the daughter were over the mother, or the wife over her husband. For the Scripture, or God's Word, is as it were a *Mother*, through which the Christian Church is begotten; and is as it were a Husband over his Wife, and not the reverse.

— ❦ —

## Question 12. Yes, they say — but the Church was before the Scripture?

**Yes, they say — but the Church was before the Scripture?**

Nothing hangs upon this — that there were believing Christians before God's Word was set down and written. For the Word was there before, though it was not straightway written. The first men, Adam and Eve, had indeed no *written* Word; but they had God's Word nonetheless, and by that Word were called into the fellowship of the faith and the Church, as Gen. 3, 4 and following show. For where God had not revealed himself to them through the Word, they would have known nothing certain of him, nor been true members of his Church.

— ❦ —

## Question 13. Is there then found in the Scripture everything necessary for salvation?

**Is there then found in the Scripture everything that is necessary to salvation?**

**The Papists say No hereto** — yet do therein altogether wrong. For **2 Tim. 3:16–17** stands clearly: *"All Scripture given by God through inspiration is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness, that the man of God may be perfect, thoroughly furnished unto all good works."*

Wherefore Christ also says (**Luke 16:29**): *"They have Moses and the Prophets; let them hear them."* Although the New Testament was not yet written at that time, yet nothing has since been written in the New Testament that had not its sure ground already in the Old — as one may perceive from the concordance of the Old and New Testaments.

And **John 20:30–31**: *"Many other signs did Jesus in the presence of his disciples that are not written in this book. These however are written, that ye may believe that Jesus is the Christ,"* etc.

Whence also **Athanasius, *Contra Gentes***, writes: *"Sufficere Scripturas divinitus inspiratas, ad omnem instructionem veritatis."* — *"That the Holy Scripture is complete to inform of all that pertains to the truth."*³

— ❦ —

## Question 14. How is a Christian to conduct himself in the exposition of Scripture, that he may know which exposition is right or not?

**How is a Christian to conduct himself in the exposition of Scripture, that he may know which exposition is right or not?**

By **this rule** shall a Christian abide, which St. Paul gives (**Rom. 12:6**): *"All prophecy or exposition shall be conformable to the faith."*

Those teachers, then, who so teach that they teach what is **conformable to the faith in Christ** (or to the chief chapters of the *Catechism*) — these we are to hear, and their teaching to receive. But whoever does not teach conformably to this — him one is neither to hear nor to see, be he Pope or whosoever he may be. For we are baptised upon Christ, that we should believe *his* Word, and are not baptised upon the teachers, or upon Popes, or upon churches. St. Paul's rule at Rom. 12:6 cannot fail us.

Whence **Augustine**, *Contra Faustum Manichaeum* lib. 11, of all teachers' books says: *"Hoc genus literarum non cum credendi necessitate, sed cum iudicandi libertate legendum est."* — *"That is: the teachers' writings one must not so read as though one had to believe everything they bring forward: but so that one may judge freely whether it is in accord with the Holy Scripture."*⁴

— ❦ —

## Question 15. Is a Christian then straightway bound to believe when only a passage from Scripture is quoted?

**Is a Christian, then, straightway bound to believe when only a passage from Scripture is quoted?**

**No.** Rather he shall give heed whether it also be *rightly quoted*. For where one will confirm anything with Scripture, that Scripture must properly serve the matter — otherwise the *devil* too quotes Scripture (Matt. 4), and likewise the *fanatics* (*Schwärmer*), though falsely. But the **best test is the Catechism**: if the exposition agrees with the chief points of the Catechism, it is to be received; if not, it is to be shunned.

— ❦ —

## Question 16. What power and effect has the Scripture, or the Word?

**What has the Scripture, or the Word, for Power and Working?**

**First**, through the Word is **forgiveness of sins dispensed** (Isa. 40).

**Second**, it **makes consciences quiet** — this human doctrines do not do (Eph. 4; 1 Tim. 1).

**Third**, it **drives away the devil**, helps, strengthens, and comforts against the world, the flesh, and every evil thought — wherefore also the first Psalm calls him blessed who day and night is occupied with God's Word.

**Fourth**, it **brings all blessedness**, both in this and in the coming life — and that so richly, that whoever has it, even in the deepest poverty and misery is glad through it, and would not give it for all the goods of the world.

**Fifth**, it **engrafts us into Christ**, so that no creature can separate us from him — neither devil, world, sects, nor the like.

**In sum:** the Holy Scripture is *profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness — that the man of God may be perfect, throughly furnished unto all good works* (2 Tim. 3:16–17).

— ❦ —

## Question 17. How comes it, then, that the Word does not everywhere bring forth fruit?

**How, then, does it come to pass, that the Word does not everywhere bring forth fruit?**

It is not the Word's fault, nor the preacher's, but of the **ground** or of the **heart**. For God's Word, where it is preached, has *three sorts of lost hearers*, and only the fourth part is truly good — that is, those who receive it with **penitent heart** and bring forth fruit in patience (Luke 8).

— ❦ —

## Question 18. May a Christian undertake anything in matters of Faith or Religion for which he has no Word of God?

**May a Christian undertake anything in matters of Faith or Religion for which he has no Word of God?**

**No.** For it stands written (**1 Pet. 4:11**): *"If any man speak, let him speak as the oracles of God; if any man have an office, let him do it as of the strength which God supplieth."* As though he would say: *All your words and works shall be grounded in God's Word. And where ye are not sure of that, then leave it alone, that ye be not harmful to yourselves and to others.*

— ❦ —

## Question 19. Do the Enthusiasts (*Schwärmer*) act rightly, who reject God's Word or the preached Word?

**Do the *Schwärmer* also act rightly, who reject God's or the preached Word?**

**No.** For Christ himself says (**John 17:20**) that we shall believe *through the Word of the Apostles*, or through the *preached Word*. That they now do not believe it all — that is not the Word's fault, but the fault of the *unbelieving heart*.

— ❦ —

## Question 20. Is it also right that the Papists give out that the Scripture is obscure?

**Is it also right that the Papists give out, that the Scripture is obscure?**

**No.** For upon earth there is no clearer book written than the Holy Scripture. She is against every other book as the sun against every other light. And **all *Patres***, Fathers or scribes, must from her explain, ground, and prove their writings and books.

Wherefore Psalm 119[:105] compares the Scripture to a Lantern, and at 2 Pet. 1:19 she is called *"a light that shineth in a dark place, until the day dawn, and the morning star arise in the hearts."*

Now where some passages are dark, they shall be explained through other clearer passages.

— ❦ —

## Question 21. But how shall a Christian bear himself when the Fathers' sayings are cast at him against the Scripture?

**But how shall a Christian bear himself, when the sayings of the Fathers are cast at him against the Scripture?**

**These two rules** shall he hold:

**First**, he shall ask whether the Fathers might not have erred. Have they erred (as the opposite party must confess) — then their sayings straightway do not avail; they must have a higher proof, that is, a *clearer passage of Scripture*. For God's Word alone is the plumb-line by which all doctrines and thoughts are to be measured.

**Ps. 19:8** — *"The Testimony of the LORD is sure, and makes the simple wise."*

**Isa. 8:20** — *"According to the Law and Testimony they shall not say this; else they shall not have the morning-red."*

**Second**, they shall say that the Fathers did not will that any should believe them where they do not bring clear Scripture.

**Augustine to Jerome:** *"I count not that thou wouldest wish thy books to be so held as though they were the books of the Prophets or Apostles; for I believe only the Holy Scripture — that it does not err. All others I read in such wise, that I do not believe it therefore true because they have said so — unless they prove it from Scripture."*⁵

— ❦ —

## Question 22. When now in matters of religion disputes fall out — who shall then be judge?

**When now disputes fall out in matters of religion — who shall then be judge?**

**God's Word alone shall be judge.** For therefore is the Scripture called *a Canon or Plumb-line* (Ps. 19; Gal. 6; Phil. 3).

Whence also **Augustine**, *De peccatorum meritis et remissione* lib. 1 cap. 22, says: *"Let us believe the Holy Scriptures, which cannot err nor lead other people astray."*⁶

— ❦ —

## Question 23. What shall a Christian then think of the Councils?

**What then shall a Christian think of the Councils?**

**Very highly and much** — yet with this understanding, that they *ground their Decrees on God's infallible Word*. For to God alone does it stand to lay down articles of Faith, and to no man. And the Councils can and shall do no more than pronounce that which is already before-hand grounded in God's Word, and show how the passages of Scripture are to be understood, which have been quoted wickedly and falsely by the impure teachers.

Whence Christ says (**Luke 24:48**): *"Ye are witnesses of all these things."*

— ❦ —

## Question 24. Whether one is bound to believe the Councils even where they bring no Word of God?

**Whether one is bound to believe the Councils even where they bring no Word of God?**

**No.** For Christ says (**John 10:4–5**): *"My sheep hear my voice, and a stranger they follow not."* And Paul (**Gal. 1:8**): *"If even an angel from heaven should preach unto you another Gospel,"* etc.

— ❦ —

## Question 25. But when the Councils are not to be further believed than according to God's Word — when then shall the disputing come to an end?

**But when the Councils are not to be further believed than according to God's Word — when then shall the disputing come to an end?**

**Answer.** When the wrong party will not let itself be shown by God's Word, but goes on obstinately in its erring opinion once set — then *God himself* at last steps in and makes an end of the strife: as happened from the beginning with Arius and other suchlike heretics.

— ❦ —

## Question 26. Whether one is not bound to believe human traditions?

**Whether one is not bound to believe human traditions?**

**No.** They may not be equated with God's Word, for they come from sinful men. So says God's Word expressly, that one honours God in vain with such doctrine as is human command (Matt. 15:9). Beside that, it also plainly teaches (1 Thess. 5:21), that one shall *prove all things*, and only *what is good* — that is, what is in accord with God's Word — is to be kept. But the touchstone is (as said) God's Word. The LORD Christ also has not said, *"I am a human doctrine or custom,"* but *"I am the Truth."*

— ❦ —

## Question 27. But does the Apostle not say, 2 Thess. 2:15: "Hold fast the traditions ye have been taught"?

**But does the Apostle not say, 2 Thess. 2:15: "Hold fast the traditions ye have been taught"?**

**Answer.** The Apostle in the said Words speaks not of human traditions *apart from God's Word* — but of the doctrine which the Apostle set forth to the Thessalonians partly in preaching, partly in his first Epistle to the Thessalonians. For the Apostles have not preached one doctrine when present, and written another when absent, and further commanded a third to the Church over and above — so that one doctrine ran against another.

— ❦ —

## Question 28. What difference is there between the doctrine of the heathen and of the Christian Church?

**What difference is there between the doctrine of the heathen and of the Christian Church?**

**Answer.**

**First**, the doctrine of the heathen is brought forth from human reason alone; the Scripture, however, is given by God himself.

**Second**, the doctrine of the heathen is only a small particle of Law and of Virtues; of the *Being* and gracious *Will* of God she knows nothing certain. But the doctrine of the Christian Church is *both of the Law and of the Gospel*, complete, and sufficient for the true knowledge of God.

**Third**, the doctrine of the heathen confirms **idolatry** and invocation of those who are no gods but only creatures. The Scripture, however, teaches only the right knowledge of God, and how one is to invoke the living God and no creature.

**Fourth**, the doctrine of the heathen is not a means through which God enlightens and converts the hearts of men — but rather an *instrument* through which Satan turns the hearts away from God. The Christian religion, however, or God's Word, is an *Instrument* through which God converts, renews, enlightens, and sanctifies the hearts unto eternal life. As Rom. 1:16 stands: *"The Gospel is a power of God unto salvation to all who believe thereon."*

— ❦ —

*[Here ends Chapter I.]*

# Chapter II

### *Von Gott*

*On God*

— ❦ —

## Question 1. How and whereby will God the LORD be rightly known?

**How and whereby will God the LORD be rightly known?**

**In and by his Word**, wherein he has revealed himself.

**Ps. 119:105** — *"Thy Word is a lantern unto my feet, and a light unto my path."*

— ❦ —

## Question 2. What is God?

**What is God?**

With this Question have the highest and wisest men on earth at all times been highly troubled — but never has any one hit upon it.

Whoever now would rightly pursue this Question, he must put all things away, and hearken only where God himself points us — namely: *"This is my beloved Son; him shall ye hear."* Therefore whoever would know what God is, let him look upon Christ, the Son of God.

For the simple, this description drawn from the *Catechism* may be held:

**God is a spiritual, single Being — threefold in Persons — who has created all things and still preserves them, who has redeemed the damned and lost men through his Son, and through the Holy Spirit sanctifies, calls into his Christendom or Church, enlightens, forgives sins, on the Last Day awakens from the dead, and gives all believers an eternal Life — but thrusts the godless into eternal damnation.**

Such description is taken from God's Word and from the *Symbolum Apostolicum*, as is easy to see.

- **John 4:24** — *"God is a Spirit."*
- **Matt. 28:19** — *"Baptise them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost."*
- **Gen. 1:1** — *"In the beginning God created heaven and earth."*
- **John 3:16** — *"So God loved the world,"* etc.
- **John 5:28** — *"The hour cometh, in which all that are in the graves shall hear the voice of the Son, and shall come forth."*

— ❦ —

## Question 3. Which are the chief properties of God? These.

**Which are the chief properties of God? These.**

**1.** That he is **Almighty**, and therefore works everything that he wills, and can help out of every need. **Gen. 17:1** — *"I am the Almighty God."*

**2.** That he is **truthful and wondrous** — holds all that he has spoken. **John 4** — *"God is truthful."* **Ps. 23** — *"The Word of the LORD is truthful."*

**3.** That he is **just and the highest Judge**, wherefore one should fear before him. **Ps. 7:12** — *"God is a righteous Judge, and a God who threatens daily; if one will not be converted, he has whetted his sword."*

**4.** That he is **merciful, patient, and of great Goodness**, etc. — wherefore the penitent shall not doubt his grace. **Ps. 103; John 3.**

— ❦ —

## Question 4. Is there then only one God?

**Is there then only one God?**

**Yes**, there is only **one** single, eternal, natural, truthful God.

- **Deut. 6:4** — *"Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God is one LORD."*
- **Isa. 44:6** — *"I am the first, and I am the last; and beside me there is no God."*
- **Ibidem** [Isa. 44:8] — *"Ye are my witnesses. Is there also a God beside me? There is no Rock; I know none."*

— ❦ —

## Question 5. How many Persons, however, are in the one Godhead?

**How many Persons, however, are in the one Godhead?**

**Three distinct Persons — Father, Son, Holy Spirit** — one right, single, natural, truthful **God**, Creator of heaven and earth: against the **Arians, Macedonians, Sabellians**, and their like heresy.

— ❦ —

## Question 6. Prove this to me from Scripture.

**Prove this to me from Scripture.**

**Gen. 1:1** — *"In the beginning **Elohim** created heaven and earth."* Now it is manifest that *Elohim* is *pluralis numeri* — signifies not one but many — and yet he does not say *"they created"* (as many), but *"he created"* (as one) in the singular: giving to understand that there is no more than one single God and Creator. But that he says *"the gods"* [Elohim] shows that in that same single Divine Being there is nevertheless a *Number*, that one calls "many" or "more than one."

**Matt. 28:19** — *"Go ye therefore and teach all nations, baptising them in the Name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit."* Here Christ says to baptise into *one* Name, since the Godhead is only one Being; and yet he names *three distinct Persons* — Father, Son, and Holy Spirit — to show that in the one Divine Being there are three distinct Persons, etc.

— ❦ —

## Question 7. Do the Jews and Turks then act rightly in rejecting this doctrine of the Holy Trinity?

**Do the Jews and Turks then act rightly in rejecting this doctrine of the Holy Trinity?**

**No.** For it will not do to judge according to reason, that inwardly in his Godhead it must be — as it seems to us — a single Person, whereof we have never seen anything, and no man can see. Since now we have Scripture's indication that in the Divine Being there are three Persons, we shall follow the Scripture (which is God's testimony of himself) and not heed what blind reason adds thereto. For **God we must believe, as he has revealed himself in his Word.**

— ❦ —

## Question 8. What difference then is there between the three Persons of the Holy Trinity?

**What difference then is there among the three Persons of the Holy Trinity?**

**The distinction of the Father** is that he has the Godhead from none, but has given it from eternity, through the eternal Generation, to the Son.

**The distinction of the Son from the Father** is that the Son is born of the Father from eternity — **Ps. 2:7**.

**The distinction of the Holy Spirit from the Father and the Son** is that the Holy Spirit *proceeds* from the Father and the Son — **John 15:26**.

Beside such **inward distinction** of the Persons there is now the **outward distinction**, in which the Son and the Holy Spirit are made manifest — the Son in the *Manhood*, for the Son alone became Man; the Holy Spirit is manifest distinctly in the form of the **Dove**, the **fiery Tongues**, the **Gifts**, the **manifold Languages**, and **Wonder-signs** — though the Manhood was made by all three Persons, and the fiery tongues and the Gifts of the Holy Spirit are the *work and creation of all three Persons*.

— ❦ —

## Question 9. Give me in a brief conception the *Summam* of this great mystery.

**Give me in a brief conception the Summa of this great Mystery.**

**Summa:** there is **one God**, **one LORD**, one Divine Majesty, Nature and Being of all three Persons — but there is manifested sometimes the Person of the Father, sometimes of the Son, sometimes of the Holy Spirit; and whichever now is manifested, it is *the one God in three Persons*, that we may rightly know the Divine Majesty, and not — as Jews, heretics, Mahomet — blindly and headlong believe as though God were no more than a single Person: which God will not have, but will be known as he has revealed himself to us. And especially is God concerned about the *Revelation and Knowledge of his Son* through the whole Scripture of the Old and New Testament.

**John 5:39** — *"Search the Scriptures, for they bear witness of me."*

— ❦ —

## Question 10. Can anyone also be saved without knowledge of this Article?

**Can anyone also be saved without knowledge of this Article?**

**Without knowledge of this Article** let no man venture to come to heaven — namely, that three distinct Persons in the Godhead are: Father, Son, Holy Spirit — and yet a *single, eternal, almighty, Divine Being*. So will God be known, and this is our Christian Faith upon which we are baptised and called (**Matt. 28:19; Mark 16:15**).

— ❦ —

## Question 11. Give me a brief report on the Person of the Father.

**Give me a brief report on the Person of the Father.**

The Father is the **first Person** of the Holy Trinity — the *Origin or Fountain* (as one may call it) of the Godhead in the Son and Holy Spirit — and the Son (when the Father is named) may not be *sundered off*, but must together be named and meant; likewise also the Holy Spirit must be named and meant with the Father and Son, because no Person outside the other can be *a separate God for himself*.

So speak **St. Paul** and **St. Peter**: *"Blessed be God, the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of all grace."* And he [the Father] himself in the Gospel ever *puts the Father foremost* and ascribes everything to him — and yet says (**John 5:23**) *"they shall honour the Son as they honour the Father."* Likewise (**John 16:15**): *"All that the Father hath is mine"* — except that the Father is the first Person from whom the Son has it, and not the reverse.

That certain sins, however, happen distinctly against the Father, against the Son, and *into* the Holy Spirit — this pertains to the **revelation of the Persons**, not to a *division of the Being*, etc.

— ❦ —
