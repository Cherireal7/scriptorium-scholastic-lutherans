# Translator's Note

## 1. The text base

The English rendered here follows the **1583 Heidelberg edition** printed by **Johann Spies**, the *editio princeps* of Kirchner's *Enchiridion*, cited in the Verzeichnis der im deutschen Sprachbereich erschienenen Drucke des 16. Jahrhunderts as **VD16 K 1037**.¹ The base text used is the facsimile held by the Bavarian State Library, Munich — shelfmark **Catech. 388**, digital identifier `bsb00020680`, URN `urn:nbn:de:bvb:12-bsb00020680-7` — served through the Munich Digitization Centre (MDZ). The title page carries the Roman numeral **M.D.LXXXIII (1583)**; the Widmungsvorrede is datelined *Heidelberg den 6. Septembris / im Jar Chrifti vnsers Seligmachers / 1583* — 6 September 1583. The BSB catalogue records the item as 1584, presumably reflecting distribution rather than imprint. All references in the present edition follow the 1583 title-page date.

The 1583 imprint stands at a hinge of Palatine confessional history. Elector Palatine **Ludwig VI**, to whom the Vorrede is dedicated, had inherited his father Friedrich III's Reformed Palatinate in 1576 and had turned it decisively back to Lutheranism. Kirchner had served as his court preacher (*Hofprediger*) at Heidelberg for four years, as he tells us at cv 25. Ludwig VI died on **12 October 1583** — only thirty-six days after the Vorrede was signed. Under regent Johann Casimir, the Palatinate reverted immediately to the Reformed confession, and the *Enchiridion* went to press in the final month of the last Lutheran interval Heidelberg would see for over a century.² Its double polemical target — the Papists on one flank, the Sacramentarians (Reformed) on the other — is a Heidelberg-specific position document, printed in a city already tipping back toward the party against whom half its arguments are levelled.

Kirchner had also, in the same year 1583, co-authored with **Martin Chemnitz** and **Nikolaus Selnecker** the *Apologia oder Verantwortung des christlichen Concordien-Buchs* — the official *Apology of the Book of Concord* defending the 1580 Formula and Book of Concord against Reformed and irenicist critics.³ The Widmungsvorrede's third rationale for the dedication (cv 27) refers directly to this work: Kirchner intends "by God's grace to remain by God's holy infallible Word as the sole foundation of the pure doctrine, and not to depart in the least from the confession of the saving faith **repeated in the Christian Book of Concord**." The *Enchiridion* is the catechetical footprint of the 1583 *Apology* — the same author, the same year, the same doctrinal binding, cast now in Q&A form for parochial use.

## 2. What the author himself supplies

Kirchner supplies the whole. His running text preserves:

- Scriptural citations in his own abbreviations (**Erod. 32** for Exodus 32, **Deut. 4** for Deuteronomy 4, **Pfal. 19** for Psalm 19, **Actor. 1** for Acts 1, **Rom.** and **1. Tim.** and **2. Pet.** in their 1583 forms), with occasional chapter-references only (**Nehem. 4**) where Kirchner reasons at length from a single narrative;
- Patristic quotations verbatim in their original language — **Athanasius** *Contra gentes* on the sufficiency of Scripture; **Augustine** to Jerome (*Epistula* 82) on the difference between the canonical books and the writings of the Fathers; **Augustine** *De peccatorum meritis et remissione* on the inerrancy of Scripture; the **Symbolum Chalcedonense** in the *Canon Symboli Chalcedonensis apud Nicephorum* form (cv 55, forthcoming Tranche 3);
- His own polemical enumerations — the four properties of Scripture, the four grounds of its certainty, the fivefold power of the Word, the fourfold difference between heathen and Christian doctrine — all preserved with their numbering intact and their internal citations undisturbed.

No paraphrase. Where a passage in Kirchner is ambiguous — chiefly on lines where the 1583 OCR yielded a broken word — the disambiguation is discussed in an endnote and the text held to the clearest defensible reading.

## 3. What Scholastic Lutherans added — and where

All modern editorial matter lives in endnotes flagged by superscript numerals.⁴ Nothing modern intrudes into the body. The endnotes carry:

- Life-dates for named authors on first reference (Kirchner 1533–1587; Elector Ludwig VI 1539–1583; Johann Spies fl. 1580–ca. 1623; Chemnitz 1522–1586; Selnecker 1530–1592; Nehemiah as historically located; Athanasius ca. 296–373; Augustine 354–430);
- Modern edition references where a Kirchner citation is opaque or where a modern reader will want to check: Augustine *Epistula* 82 numbering, Athanasius *Contra gentes* section-number, the Chalcedonian Definition in **Denzinger-Hünermann** numbering, the *Apology of the Book of Concord* in the standard 1583 imprint (Spies, Heidelberg, ff.);
- Historical annotation where the Vorrede's typological argument turns on 16th-century events — the Eck bull of 1520, the Schmalkaldic War of 1546–1548, the Palatine confessional oscillations of 1559 / 1576 / 1583;
- Cross-references to the Book of Concord (BSLK / Kolb-Wengert) whenever Kirchner alludes to a Formula article or a Symbol.

## 4. Editorial conventions

Following the [Praetorius](../../shared/) reference implementation and the Chytraeus catechetical sibling volume:

- **Register:** scholarly-critical English, reverent but not archaizing. Kirchner's baroque doublets are preserved in substance without being reproduced beat-for-beat where English idiom cannot bear them.
- **Q&A layout:** questions in **bold**, answers in regular weight, Kirchner's numbered points preserved. Where the source runs question and answer together without a paragraph break, the English inserts one for legibility and marks nothing.
- **Latin quotations** are preserved in italic Roman with an English rendering following in parentheses or on the next line; the same rule holds for Greek. Kirchner uses no Hebrew in this Hauptstück.
- **Vocabulary:** the master glossary at `../../shared/terms.yaml` governs. New Kirchner-specific terms this tranche adds: *Enchiridion* (preserved untranslated as the book's own name); *Sacramentierer* rendered as "**Sacramentarians**" (Kirchner's standard 1583 label for the Reformed on the Supper); *Hauptstück* rendered as "**chief chapter**" or "**chief head**"; *Symbolum Apostolicum* / *Chalcedonense* preserved untranslated; *Concordi Buch* rendered "**Book of Concord**"; *Papisten* rendered "**Papists**" per Kirchner's own polemical usage. The Nehemiah-typological cluster (*Kirchenbaw*, *Widerauffbawung*, *Schwerdt des Geistes*) is rendered "the **building of the Church**," "the **rebuilding**," "the **sword of the Spirit**" — the metaphor is Kirchner's spine and must survive the translation intact.
- **Bible:** ESV for English rendering. Vulgate numbering is noted where Kirchner cites in Vulgate form. **Erod.** = Exodus; **Actor.** = Acts; **Pfal.** = Psalm; **Deut.** = Deuteronomy; **Genef.** = Genesis; **Jefai.** = Isaiah. Modernised inline; the 1583 abbreviations are recorded in an endnote at first occurrence only.
- **Orthography:** 1583 forms silently modernised in the English — *vnd* → *und* in preserved German phrases; long-*ſ* → *s*; *w.* (Kirchner's mid-word abbreviation) expanded; hyphenated line-breaks joined; the terminal *ẑ* rendered *ß* in German source citations, *s* in English body. Where the 1583 orthography preserves a doctrinally meaningful spelling — *Christlichen Concordi Buchs* — it stands.
- **Dividers:** `— ❦ —` (em-dash · aldus leaf · em-dash) at section breaks.

## 5. Scope of this working translation — *four tranche-slices shipped, ~45% of the whole book, including the political-authority *Politia* seed-passage*

**This edition currently ships Tranches 1, 2a, 2b, and 2c — approximately 45% of the whole book, including the seed of the Lutheran political-theology treatment. The remaining ~55% is pending translation in future tranches.**

An earlier draft of this section (pushed 2026-08-30 as commits `1bf51a7` + `381f862` + `dc654e5`) claimed to be **the complete Enchiridion**. That claim was catastrophically wrong. It was based on the BSB IIIF Presentation manifest for `bsb00020680`, which reports 108 total canvases and yields no other structural information. Direct probing of the per-canvas hOCR API on **2026-08-31** revealed that the same identifier serves canvases well past 108 — cv 109, 150, 200, 250, 300, 400, 500, 600, 700, 800, 850, 870, 880, 895 all return real body content — and the book's actual extent is approximately **895 canvases** (cv 900 returns only the BSB shelfmark stamp; cv 950 and beyond return HTTP 404). The manifest is broken; the book itself is roughly nine times larger than the manifest disclosed.

Kirchner's *Enchiridion* is therefore not what the earlier note described as a "polemical Christological catechism" running four short Hauptstück in 108 canvases. It is a **fully comprehensive Lutheran systematic-catechetical handbook** of at least 46 numbered loci across ~895 canvases (~447 leaves = ~890 pp small octavo Fraktur), covering the whole range of Reformation dogmatics. The running-head "locus XLV" (*Von der Hell* at cv 870) and "locus XLVI" (*Vom ewigen Leben* at cv 880–895) confirm at least 46 loci; the true count may be higher.

### What is shipped so far (Tranches 1 + 2a, cv 5, 7–140 = ~24% of the book)

**Tranche 1 (2026-08-30)** — cv 5, 7–108:

- **Title page + Widmungsvorrede an Elector Ludwig VI** (cv 5, 7–28) — the Nehemiah-typology dedication and the load-bearing Book-of-Concord binding at cv 27;
- **Hauptstück I · *Von der Heiligen Schrifft*** (cv 29–44) — 14 numbered sections on Scripture, its authority, the interpretive rule, against Papist and Enthusiast (*Schwärmer*) errors;
- **Hauptstück II · *Von Gott*** (cv 45–50) — 10 numbered sections on the Trinity;
- **Hauptstück III · *Von der Person unseres HERREN Christi*** (cv 51–103) — 23 numbered sections on Christology, including the full Latin *Symbolum Chalcedonense apud Nicephorum* with facing English, the three *genera* of the *communicatio idiomatum*, the *genus maiestaticum* against Reformed rejection, the careful distinction between multipresence *per unionem personalem* and rejected substance-in-every-creature ubiquity, and the exegesis of the Right Hand of God as no *Locus Physicus* but the Divine Majesty itself;
- **Hauptstück IV · *Vom Ampt Christi*** (cv 104–110) — the fourfold Office of Christ (Redemption + fulfilment of the Law + Preaching-and-Sacraments + sending of the Spirit) + appropriation through faith + comfort against great sin + full sinner-list closing (Adam and Eve, Manasseh, David, Peter, Magdalene, Paul, the thief) transitioning into Baptism-and-Supper as means of appropriation.

**Tranche 2a (2026-08-31)** — cv 111–140:

- **Hauptstück V · *Vom Reich Christi*** (cv 111–117) — the threefold Kingdom (grace / power / glory); spiritual, everlasting, and distributive character; five works; against Anabaptist externalisation; three (five) contrasts with earthly kingdoms;
- **Hauptstück VI · *Vom ewigen Priesterthumb*** (cv 118–123) — Christ our eternal High Priest per Ps. 110; fourfold function; sevenfold contrast Aaronic vs Christian priesthood; propitiatory vs spiritual sacrifices (against the Mass); priesthood of all believers; against the papal arrogation of priesthood as *Gottslästerung*;
- **Hauptstück VII · *Vom Heiligen Geist*** (cv 124–133) — third Person of the Godhead; fivefold proof of divine Personhood; four chief works; given through *Wort, Predigtamt, und Sacramenta* (the *verbum externum*, against the *Schwärmer*); three marks of possession; the Spirit's help in weakness; the fruits of the Spirit;
- **Hauptstück VIII · *Von der Schöpffung*** (cv 134–140) — Trinity as Creator (*Opera Trinitatis ad extra sunt indivisa*); *creatio ex nihilo*; Six Days; divine sovereignty and freedom; perishing of the world through fire (2 Pet. 3); Providence in four modes; the **four regimens of divine governance** (spiritual / ecclesiastical / domestic / political); special care for the Church. §  9 gives the seed-passage of Lutheran two-kingdoms teaching, from which the fuller *Politia* / *Obrigkeit* treatment grows in the later Decalogue locus.

Certainly present but not yet located by cv-probe (must live in the ~700 untranslated canvases between our tranche and Resurrection): **the Ten Commandments / Decalogue treatment**, which in Lutheran catechesis carries the *Politia* / *Obrigkeit* material — the political-authority doctrine including the two-kingdoms teaching, the office of magistracy, the Christian's civil duties. This is what your reader wanted for their political-compend project and is definitely somewhere in the ~cv 200–400 stretch. **Also standard Lutheran loci** almost certainly present but unlocated: Creation, Providence, Angels-both-good-and-evil, Man before and after the Fall, Original Sin, Christian Liberty, Good Works, the Church (marks + ministry + ordinances), the Cross and Consolation, Sanctification.

Kirchner's *Enchiridion* in its true extent is therefore comparable in doctrinal scope to Chytraeus's 1594 *Catechesis* (10 loci in ~180 pp) but nearly five times its extent, and in the same magnitude as his own Latin *Methodica explicatio* (1586, 1310 pp folio) though in octavo format. Kirchner in the Vorrede at cv 23 modestly calls it *"kurze Enchiridion oder Handbüchlein"* ("short handbook") — evidently a modesty topos, since the actual book runs ~890 pages.

### Cross-check against 1592 Frankfurt reprints — needs re-verification

An earlier draft of this section reported that two BSB copies of the 1592 Frankfurt reprint (`bsb10193988` and `bsb10174368`, both 107 canvases per their manifests) run to a point corresponding to our cv 103 and appear either to have dropped Hauptstück IV or be truncated. **That reasoning is now also suspect**: both 1592 manifests may equally under-report their true canvas count, and the 1592 reprints may in fact contain the whole ~895 canvases as our 1583 does. This will be re-probed in Tranche 2. The 1592 reprints may still turn out to be a legitimate shortened *Auszug* rather than truncated scans; the current best evidence is provisional.

### The withdrawn VD16 line

An earlier draft claimed the VD16 K 1037 physical description as `[8], 90, [1]` leaves. That figure was extrapolated rather than verified against VD16 itself and has been withdrawn. # Dedication

### *To the most illustrious, high-born Prince and Lord, Ludwig, Elector Palatine of the Rhine⁷ — grace and peace from God through Jesus Christ.*

Most gracious Elector and Lord,

Among the beautiful histories which Holy Scripture preserves for the instruction of the Christian Church, there is scarcely one more suited to our own time than the account of the rebuilding of the city of Jerusalem after the Babylonian captivity, which the Lord God carried through and brought to completion by Nehemiah — over and against the counsels of Nehemiah's own hesitations and of every enemy who mocked the work. That whole history is a true type of the rebuilding of the Church of God, that is, of the whole company of Christendom, after the Babylonian or papal captivity in which she lay for many hundreds of years, under the Roman popes, until there was scarcely a hope that she could ever be delivered out of it.

But as the Lord God delivered his Jewish people, when the appointed seventy years were run, through the Persian monarch Cyrus, freed them, and commanded that the Temple be rebuilt: so also, out of his groundless mercy, he has in these last times looked upon his whole Christendom with the eyes of grace, has wonderfully released her from the long antichristian Babylonian captivity, and has begun once more to build his spiritual Temple, that is, his holy Congregation. It was Dr. Luther, most gracious Elector, through whose writings the fallen worship was raised, the pure and wholesome doctrine again brought to light, and the horrible darkness and idolatry of the papacy exposed and put to flight. Yet who could have imagined that such a transformation should follow — that the papal doctrine and worship should fall away so utterly, as when the Samaritans, Ammonites, and Moabites, hearing that the walls of Jerusalem and the Temple were being rebuilt, grew furious and derided the people of God, saying: *"What are they doing? Will they bring the stones of the burnt heap to life again, will they offer sacrifice, will they finish it all in a single day?"* — and holding it for impossible that so great a work could be brought to conclusion? Exactly so did it go with our churches and their Christian reformation and rebuilding: for the beginnings were despised and mocked.

Whence the Papists at first had confidence in their own humble roots, and thought that although Dr. Luther built with his writing, it would come to nothing — as they answered him in Dr. Eck's writing sent from Rome in 1520, and in the papal bull issued against him.⁸ For the Roman Church had grown so mighty that they judged Luther's undertaking impossible. Yet God ensured that the work should go forward: for though Luther himself may not always have foreseen the full scope of what was to come, divine providence carried it on, so that it *had* to advance — no less than the exodus from Egypt and the deliverance from Babylon. Vainly did the enemies imagine themselves as foxes that could tear down stone walls; God has established what he began, so that it cannot be overcome by fox's cunning nor by lion's force.

Nor did the enemies stop at mockery. They rose with the full weight of their power and determination to suppress the holy Gospel and to destroy the churches of Germany, particularly around the years 1546 to 1548.⁹ But God has always and everywhere brought the practices of his adversaries wonderfully to light: he preserves his own where the case appears most hopeless, and exposes the plots that in secret were laid against his Church.

For this is the character of the Lord's word to his people in every generation: *"Fear not, I am with you; do not draw back, for I am your God"* (Isaiah 41). The people, in Nehemiah's day, complained to their Lord and God that they were held in such contempt by the heathen. They prayed, and they set the watch. They joined the walls one to another so that the people gained one heart. And, as Sanballat, Tobiah, and the Arabs, Ammonites, and Ashdodites made a league against Jerusalem, the Temple, and its builders, to come together and to fight against it — the people prayed to the Lord God against them, and set the watch against them. In the same way we too, in these dangerous times, when the enemies of the Gospel — the Pope and the sects — with all their might would suppress the Gospel and the right use of the sacraments, and bind themselves against Jerusalem and the Temple of God: we too should freshly and confidently lay hold on prayer, and appeal to God against them.¹⁰

But this is not enough. Let us take Nehemiah's five ordinances also as a model for our own building of the Church.

**First**, Nehemiah, when the people through weariness and the fear of enemies would have lost heart and drawn their hand from the building, exhorted them to steadfastness and roused them with faithful diligence: to hold fast in prayer against the practices and violence of the enemies, and with the sword of the Spirit — that is, with the Word of God — to fight the false doctrine, the idolatry of the papacy, and every erring teaching; and in the building of Jerusalem, that is, of Christendom, to go forward confident and unafraid, and not grow slack. For God will surely preserve his people, will plant the pure doctrine upon the coming generations, and will steer error and idolatry off, if only we pray diligently, and press on in the exercise of the divine Word by every mode that God has appointed, and do not let ourselves be wearied. Though the enemies assail us at the sharpest, still God takes another path; the outcome stands in his hand and not in theirs. And though we should suffer all kinds of peril and stand ready for it, that is no matter: God will hold back for himself a holy remnant, come what may. For this cannot fail — if we stand fast by his Word and truth, he stands fast by us; and rather than that we should fall away to the enemies, they must fall away to us, Jeremiah 15.

**Second**, among all Nehemiah's works one is especially worth remembering: in that troubled time, when he saw the approaching danger before his eyes, he made this praiseworthy arrangement — first, that the young men should be divided in halves, one half labouring, the other half keeping watch and holding the spears; and second, that others should labour with one hand, while with the other they held their weapons; so that each who built had his sword girded to his loins, and built at the same time.

Now, as Nehemiah in that time set the young men to it, one half to labour and the other half to hold the spears: so ought today all upright teachers, especially in schools, to devote themselves with their utmost ability to guiding young men faithfully and prudently to the labour of the Church-building. And that in such fashion, that they should first be trained to study and learn well and thoroughly the wholesome doctrine of God's Word in Holy Scripture, both of the Old and the New Testament, so that afterwards in the building of the Christian Church they may labour or serve the more fruitfully and profitably, and may build up the Church of God with wholesome doctrine and holy walk. Likewise also, that they should train up the youth to hold the spears — that is, to be mighty in the sayings of Holy Scripture, and to bring the whole of Christendom against the enemies of the pure doctrine, the heresies, the errors, and the perverted teaching, rightly and with profit. In sum: they should accustom their beloved studying youth to this, that they build with the one hand and with the other learn to hold the weapons — that is, that with the pure doctrine *in thesi* they lay the true foundation, and with the other hand stand armed to rebuke the gainsayers, and upon the firm ground of holy divine Scripture to stop their mouth (Titus 1). For to this end the youth in Christian schools and academies is specially trained in languages, arts, and Holy Scripture — that they may know both how to spread abroad the pure teaching as duty requires, and how to meet the gainsayers with a steady foundation and with true theological earnestness, decently. For the building of Jerusalem and of God's Temple cannot dispense with either. And this ought to happen not only among the youth in schools: it ought also to be earnestly and without ceasing exhorted throughout the whole of Christendom and among all its upright members.

Nor is this the work of the great wonder-men of the Church of God — Nehemiahs and their like — alone: whoever has received from God gifts serviceable to the Church-building, let him apply them; let him, so far as in him lies, in his own place and among his own, without ceasing rouse and encourage, so that every man may be so instructed, taught, and armed, that he may serve the Church of God and defend himself with God's Word against the enemies of the Church of Christ.

To this Christian end I too have, according to my simplicity and by the modest gifts God has bestowed on me, looked when I compiled this my *Enchiridion*: first, that I myself, so far as in me lies, might help forward the building of the holy Church in these parts. Second, that I might also lead others rightly to grasp the chief points of Christian doctrine, and to know and to avoid the contrary doctrine of the Pope and his Jesuits — who now dare to give a colour and a gloss to all the falsehoods and errors of the papacy, and, in particular, most miserably and wickedly, together with the Sacramentarians (with whom on this question they blow altogether in one horn), to pervert the high article of the Person and Majesty of the human nature in Christ.¹¹ I have also endeavoured, by God's grace, to set this forth in clear, understandable, sound, and wholesome words, according to the Apostle's command in 2 Timothy 1: *Hold fast the pattern of sound words which thou hast heard.*

For it has been my special intention to compress the chief chapters of Christian doctrine — those, that is, which are in dispute both between the Papists and us, and between us and the Sacramentarians and others — so far as the principal matter of them is concerned, briefly out of the more extensive and detailed writings that have been published on these questions, and to gather them into this short *Enchiridion* or hand-book. So that even those who either cannot read the extensive writings, or cannot orient themselves in them sufficiently, may in this little book read as it were in a summary, in a short time, and weigh with the fear and invocation of God whereupon the matter rests, and which side has the better foundation from God's Word to bring for itself.

For all that, it is not at all my intention with this modest labour to displace from other people's hands the other useful and good little books in which the chief substance of Christian doctrine is briefly drawn from the wells of Israel, and by which much good has hitherto been done for Christendom. My intent is rather to make my own confession here publicly on the chief articles of the Christian doctrine, and, so far as in me lies, hereby to serve the youth and the common Church among us. If anyone else has need to use it for himself, that stands open. I have, so far as in me lies, meant to serve pious hearts and to injure none. May the Almighty grant his blessing thereto, that it be not in vain. I could well have treated all the matters somewhat more amply and at greater length: but I have kept to this, that the little book should not become too large — but should remain such as one may daily carry with him. For to every Christian it is no small matter that in every sermon he should attend with particular diligence to which article of doctrine is being treated, and should then look up that article in this or a similar little book, and read it after, and compare it with the sermon that has been preached. There he will find that he understands everything more thoroughly, and that he is more firmly settled in the doctrine, for which I can never sufficiently thank the Lord God.¹²

— ❦ —

Your Electoral Grace's *own* Enchiridion I have wished, however, to dedicate for the following reasons.

**First**, since I have now for four years been Your Electoral Grace's most humble servant, and have in that time — God be praised and thanked — perceived that Your Electoral Grace has taken the true Christian religion to heart with all your heart, that you love the truth of the Gospel, and that you have turned away from all the erring doctrine of the papacy and of other sects; that Your Electoral Grace has made your Christian and godly confession and zeal known before the whole of Christendom, both by the reformation of churches and schools and by the furtherance of the Christian Concord-Work and *Apologia* — that you have not been ashamed of the name of Jesus Christ and the holy Gospel or Word of God before this evil world:¹³ this has always and continually rejoiced me in my heart so greatly that I cannot sufficiently thank the Lord God for it.

**Second**, that Your Electoral Grace even to this day, in following the praiseworthy example of Nehemiah, has spared no effort in whatever may serve the furtherance and continuation of Jerusalem and the rebuilding of the Temple or Church of God. For Your Electoral Grace, no less than Nehemiah, both in your particular schools and above all in your praiseworthy University,¹⁴ rightly raises up the beloved youth, and through God's grace has them so trained that they are fit — with the one hand to build up the Temple of God, that is, common Christendom, with sound doctrine; and with the other, armed and equipped, to gainsay the enemies of the pure doctrine, and to defend the truth against them upon the firm foundation of Holy Scripture. Whence Your Electoral Grace is, before the whole of Christendom, no less than Nehemiah to be praised and lauded.

**Third**, that I might herewith briefly and publicly repeat, before Your Electoral Grace, my confession on the chief articles of our Christian faith: so that both Your Electoral Grace and others might perceive from it what sort of doctrine, in Your Electoral Grace's praiseworthy University, I set before the youth; and that I intend, by God's grace, to remain by God's holy infallible Word as the sole foundation of the pure doctrine, and not to depart in the least from the confession of the saving faith **repeated in the Christian Book of Concord** — but, so far as God helps me, to persist steadfastly.¹⁵

**Fourth**, that, in return for the manifold benefits so graciously shown by Your Electoral Grace, unworthy as I and mine are, I might most humbly, by this modest dedication, testify what gratitude I could. I stand herein in the most humble confidence that Your Electoral Grace will not take this ungraciously, and that you will continue to grant your Electoral Grace's favour to me and mine hereafter as heretofore, and let us be commended to it.

Herewith I commend Your Electoral Grace, together with your Electoral Consort, the young Lordship, and the Electoral Government, into the gracious protection of the Almighty. May he, in his mercy, preserve Your Electoral Grace in long-lasting good health, for the best of your Consort, the young Lordship, the churches and schools, the land and the principality. **Amen.**

&nbsp;

*Given at Heidelberg, the 6th of September, in the year of Christ our Saviour, 1583.*

&nbsp;

*Your Electoral Grace's*

*most humble servant,*

**Timotheus Kirchnerus.**

# Hauptstück I

### *Von der Heiligen Schrifft, oder Wort Gottes*

*On Holy Scripture, or the Word of God*

— ❦ —

## § 1. Whence it is proven that the Christian religion alone is the true religion and faith

**From what is it proven that the Christian religion and faith alone is the right religion and faith?**

**First**, from *antiquity*. For no faith upon earth — however it may be called — has stood so long as this faith. Adam and all the fathers from the beginning of the world have had this faith; and all the blessed will have this faith, until the end of the world (Gen. 3; 4; 5; Acts 15; Rom. 4).

**Second**, no faith on earth has done such wonder-signs as this faith. On this let one read all the histories of the Old and New Testaments, and so on. The other faiths in the world have not been able to do such signs, though they attempted them.

**Third**, no faith on earth has had so many martyrs, who have fought so knightly, so bravely, and so contended against all offence and persecution. Many Christians from the beginning have been slain for it; but this faith has endured against all others — although it was as if the smaller and the others were pressing against it on every side.¹⁶

**Fourth**, no faith from the beginning of the world has been so certainly proclaimed and prophesied as to what is to come as this Christian faith. The Christian faith has sure prophecy of how it and other faiths in the world will fare, and so on. And history rhymes finely with the prophecies.

**Fifth**, especially is the doctrine of the Gospel — which Christendom alone has — a doctrine of which no human reason naturally knows, and without which no lasting comfort in temptations (*Anfechtungen*) can be drawn or grasped. And the experience of all the true-believing testifies that it works in them steadfast comfort, peace, and joy of heart in tribulation; that they can well come to peace, and conclude that with God they are well: Rom. 5. *Now, being justified by faith through faith in Christ, we have peace with God, and access to grace.*

## § 2. What God's Word or Holy Scripture is

**What then is God's Word, or the Holy Scripture?**

It is a doctrine given by God himself, in which Law and Gospel are set forth, and it is taught what God according to his essence is, and also what his will is toward us men. John 17: *Sanctify them in thy truth; thy Word is truth.*

## § 3. The chief chapters of Scripture: Law and Gospel

**Which are the chief chapters of Scripture, or of the Word?**

Two: the **Law** and the **Gospel**.

The **Law** commands and requires of us what we ought to do and to leave undone (Exodus 20).

The **Gospel**, however, does not preach what we are to do and to leave undone; but only bids us to hold out and to take with the lap of our garment, and says: *"Look, dear man — this has God done for thee. He has given thee his Son, has had him slain for thy sake"* (John 3: *God so loved the world that he gave his only-begotten Son*).¹⁷

## § 4. Whence Holy Scripture takes its beginning; by whom first brought forth

**Whence does the Holy Scripture take its beginning?**

From God himself. For in Exodus 32 it stands thus: *Moses turned and went down from the mount, and had the two tables of the testimony in his hand; they were written on both sides; and God had made them himself, and himself had graved the writing on them.* From this it clearly appears that God himself made the first beginning of putting his Word and revelation into writing. And from this also is the loftiness of the Holy Divine Scripture to be recognised.

Moses, then, wrote out the whole Law by God's command, as Exodus 33 stands, and left it to the children of Israel. Him have the Prophets, Evangelists, and Apostles followed, and so on.

**By whom, then, was the Word of God first brought forth?**

By God himself. As the 103rd Psalm says: *He made his ways known to Moses, his deeds to the children of Israel.* And 2 Tim. 3: *All Scripture given of God by inspiration is profitable for doctrine*, and so on.

**What moved him to it?**

Nothing else but his unspeakable mercy, according to which he willed to make himself known to us men by a certain and steadfast Word. Especially so, since men had fallen into sin and lost the right knowledge of God, he did not will that we all should altogether and wholly lack, in such blindness and ignorance, the knowledge of God our Creator and of his will toward us. For otherwise we should all have had to be eternally damned and lost, and to remain so.

**With what men, then, is such Word of God to be found?**

With none other than the true Congregation of God, to whom God has revealed himself through his Word and wonder-signs. As Deut. 4 stands: *This shall be your wisdom and understanding in the sight of all peoples, that when they hear all these commandments they shall have to say: See, wise and understanding people are these, and a noble people — for where is so noble a people to whom the gods draw so near as the LORD our God, whenever we call upon him? And where is so noble a people that has statutes and ordinances so just as all this law which I set before you this day?* And likewise Psalm 147: *He shows his Word to Jacob, his statutes and judgements to Israel; not so has he done to any of the heathen, neither has he made known to them his judgements.* John 10: *My sheep hear my voice, and a stranger they do not follow.*

**What should a Christian principally seek in Holy Scripture, and to what does the Holy Scripture chiefly point?**

In Holy Scripture one is above all to seek **Christ Jesus, the Saviour of the world**; for all Scripture principally points to and shows him. John 5: *These are they that bear witness of me.*

## § 5. The four grounds of Scripture's certainty; the four properties of Scripture

**Which are the chief properties of the Holy Scripture?**

These:

**1.** That Scripture is the true mistress over all other human writings. For all writings of the Fathers and of men must be proved by her, or otherwise they do not stand. Ps. 19: *The testimony of the LORD is sure.*

**2.** That she is more certain than all Councils. For the Councils too must direct themselves according to her (Gal. 1: *Though an angel from heaven should preach another Gospel to you than that which we have preached to you, let him be accursed*).

**3.** That she suffers no human addition. She will be alone, or nothing at all (Ps. 12; whence she is called a pure silver).

**4.** That she is not to be learned to the end. As the 147th Psalm says of her: *Of his understanding there is no number.* Whence every Christian, of high and low estate, should search in the Holy Scripture day and night.

**Whence, then, is it clear that the Holy Scripture is a certain doctrine, on which one may and must rely?**

From this: **First**, that she was begun to be written by God himself.

**Second**, that she was inspired by God himself (2 Tim. 3).

**Third**, that she has been confirmed with such exceeding divine wonder-works from the very beginning.

**Fourth**, that the holy men who wrote her were themselves driven to it by God. 2 Peter 1: *We have a sure prophetic word, and ye do well that ye take heed to it as to a light that shineth in a dark place*, and so on.

## § 6. Against the Papist assertion that Scripture has its authority from the Church

**Does the Pope say rightly, then, that the Scripture has its regard from the Church?**

No. For she has her authority and regard from God himself. And that the Pope draws the regard of Scripture upon the Church — that is at bottom just as much as if one said the daughter were above the mother, or the wife above her husband. For the Scripture, or God's Word, is as it were the mother through which the Christian Church is begotten; and is as it were a husband over his wife, and not the other way about.

**Yes,** they say, **but the Church was before the Scripture?**

Nothing hangs upon this: that there were believing Christians before God's Word was set down and written. For the Word was there before, though it was not straightway written down. The first men, Adam and Eve, had no written Word; but they had God's Word all the same, and by that Word were called into the fellowship of the faith and of the Church (as Gen. 3, 4, and following show). For if God had not revealed himself to them through the Word, they would have known nothing certain of him, nor been true members of his Church.

## § 7. Whether everything necessary for salvation is found in Scripture

**Is there, then, in Scripture found everything that is necessary to salvation?**

The Papists say no here, but they do it altogether wrongly. For 2 Tim. 3 stands clearly: *All Scripture given by inspiration of God is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness, that the man of God may be perfect, throughly furnished unto all good works.*

Whence also Christ says (Luke 16): *They have Moses and the Prophets; let them hear them.* Now although at that time the New Testament was not yet written, nothing has since been written in the New Testament which had not its sure ground already in the Old, as one perceives from the concordant witness of the Old and New Testaments.

And John 20: *And many other signs did Jesus in the presence of his disciples, which are not written in this book. But these are written, that ye may believe that Jesus is the Christ*, and so on.

Whence also **Athanasius**, *Contra gentes*, writes: *Sufficere Scripturas divinitus inspiratas, ad omnem instructionem veritatis* — that the Holy Scripture is complete to give report of all that pertains to the truth.¹⁸

## § 8. How a Christian is to orient himself in interpretation

**How is a Christian to conduct himself in the exposition of Scripture, that he may know which exposition is right or not?**

By this rule shall a Christian abide, which St. Paul gives (Rom. 12): *All prophecy or exposition shall be conformable to the faith.* Those teachers, then, who so teach that they teach what is conformable to the faith in Christ (or to the chief chapters of the Catechism) — these we are to hear, and their teaching to receive. But whoever does not teach conformably to this — him one is neither to hear nor to see: be he Pope, or whosoever he may be. For we are baptised upon Christ, that we might believe his Word; and are not baptised upon the teachers, or upon Popes, or upon churches. St. Paul's rule at Rom. 12 cannot fail us.

**Is a Christian, then, straightway bound to believe when only a passage from Scripture is quoted?**

No. He shall rather give heed whether it is rightly quoted. For where one will confirm anything with Scripture, that Scripture must properly serve the matter: otherwise the devil too quotes Scripture (Matt. 4), and likewise the fanatics — but falsely. But the best test is the Catechism: if the exposition agrees with the chief points of the Catechism, it is to be received; if not, it is to be shunned.¹⁹

## § 9. The power and effects of Scripture — a fivefold enumeration

**What power and working has the Scripture, or the Word?**

**First**, through the Word is dispensed forgiveness of sins (Isa. 40).

**Second**, it makes consciences quiet — this human doctrines do not do (Eph. 4; 1 Tim. 1).

**Third**, it drives away the devil; helps, strengthens, and comforts against the world, the flesh, and all evil thoughts. Whence also the first Psalm calls him blessed who day and night is occupied with God's Word.

**Fourth**, it brings all blessedness, both in this and in the coming life; and that so richly that whoever has it, in the deepest poverty and misery is glad through it, and would not give it for all the goods of the world.

**Fifth**, it engrafts us into Christ, so that no creature can separate us from him — neither devil, nor world, nor sects, nor the like.

In sum: the Holy Scripture is *profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness, that the man of God may be perfect, throughly furnished unto all good works* (2 Tim. 3).

## § 10. Why the Word does not everywhere bear fruit; against the Enthusiasts

**How comes it, then, that the Word does not everywhere bring forth fruit?**

It is not the Word's fault, nor the preacher's, but of the land or of the heart. For God's Word, where it is preached, has three sorts of lost hearers, and only the fourth part is truly good: that is, those who receive it with penitent heart and bring forth fruit in patience (Luke 8).

**May a Christian undertake anything for which he has no Word of God?**

No. For it stands written (1 Pet. 4): *If any man speaketh, let him speak as the oracles of God; if any man ministereth, let him minister as of the strength which God supplieth* — as if he would say: *All your words and works are to be grounded in God's Word; and where you are not certain of that, then let it lie, that you do no harm to yourselves and others.*

**Do the Enthusiasts (*Schwärmer*) do rightly, then, who reject God's Word or the preached Word?**

No. For Christ himself says (John 17) that we shall believe through the Word of the Apostles, or through the preached Word. That they do not believe everything is not the Word's fault, but the fault of the unbelieving heart.²⁰

## § 11. Against the Papist claim that Scripture is obscure; the two rules on the Fathers

**Is it right, then, that the Papists give out that the Scripture is obscure?**

No. For upon earth there is no clearer book written than the Holy Scripture: she is against all other books as the sun against every other light. And all *Patres*, Fathers, or scribes must from her explain, ground, and prove their writings and books. Whence the 119th Psalm compares the Scripture to a lantern; and in 2 Pet. 1 she is called a light that shines in a dark place, until the day dawns and the morning star arises in the hearts. Now where there be some dark passages, these should be cleared through other clearer passages.

**But how shall a Christian conduct himself when the sayings of the Fathers are cast at him against the Scripture?**

These two rules shall he hold. **First**, he shall ask whether the Fathers may not also have erred. Have they erred — as the opposite party must confess — then their sayings straightway avail nothing; they must have a higher proof, that is, a clearer passage from Scripture. For God's Word alone is the plumb-line by which all doctrines and thoughts are to be measured (Ps. 19; Gal. 6; Isa. 8).

**Second**, they shall say that the Fathers themselves would not have any man believe them where they do not bring clear Scripture. Augustine to Jerome: *"I do not judge that thou wouldest wish thy books to be held as though they were the books of the Prophets or Apostles; for I believe only the Holy Scripture — that it does not err. All the others I read in such wise that I do not believe it therefore true because they have said so, unless they prove it from Scripture."*²¹

## § 12. Scripture as the sole judge in religious controversies; the office of Councils

**When, then, disputes fall out in matters of religion — who shall be the judge?**

**God's Word alone** shall be the judge. For therefore is the Scripture called a **Canon** or plumb-line (Ps. 19; Gal. 6; Phil. 3). Whence also **Augustine**, *De peccatorum meritis et remissione*, book 1, chapter 22, says: *"Let us believe the Holy Scriptures, which cannot err nor lead others astray."*²²

**What should a Christian, then, hold of the Councils?**

Very much, and highly. But with this qualification: that they ground their decrees upon God's infallible Word. For to God alone it stands to lay down articles of faith, and to no man. And the Councils can do and shall do no more than pronounce that which is already grounded in God's Word beforehand, and show how those passages of Scripture are to be understood which the impure teachers have wickedly and falsely quoted. Whence Christ says (Luke 24): *Ye are the witnesses of all these things.*

**Is one, then, bound to believe the Councils even where they bring no Word of God?**

No. For Christ says (John 10): *My sheep hear my voice, and a stranger they do not follow.* And Paul (Gal. 1): *Though even an angel from heaven should preach another Gospel* — and so on.

**But if the Councils are no further to be believed than according to God's Word — when, then, shall an end come to the disputing?**

**Answer.** When the wrong side will not be shown by God's Word, but goes on stubbornly in the erring opinion it has fixed on — then God himself at last takes hold, and makes an end of the strife: as it happened from the beginning with Arius and other suchlike heretics.²³

## § 13. Against human traditions; the true interpretation of 2 Thessalonians 2

*[Continuing the passage on why human doctrines cannot bind consciences]* — since it comes from sinful men. For God's Word expressly says that one honours God vainly with such doctrine as is men's command (Matt. 15). To this that it also clearly teaches, that one should prove all things (1 Thess. 5) and hold fast only what is good — that is, what agrees with the Word of God. But the touchstone, as has been said, is God's Word. The Lord Christ also has not said, *"I am a men's doctrine, or a custom,"* but *"I am the truth."*

**But the Apostle says (2 Thess. 2): *Hold the traditions ye have been taught*?**

**Answer.** The Apostle, in the said words, does not speak of men's traditions apart from God's Word: but of the doctrine which the Apostle set forth to the Thessalonians in part orally in preaching, and in part wrote out to them in his first Epistle to the Thessalonians. For the Apostles have not preached one doctrine while present, and written another when absent, and commanded still another beside these to be held by the Church — so that one doctrine were to run counter to the other.

## § 14. The fourfold difference between pagan and Christian doctrine

**What difference is there, then, between the doctrine of the heathen and of the Christian Church?**

**Answer.**

**First**, the doctrine of the heathen is brought forth from human reason alone: the Scripture, however, is given by God himself.

**Second**, the doctrine of the heathen is only a small particle of Law and of virtues. Of the essence and gracious will of God she knows nothing certain. The doctrine of the Christian Church, however, is both of the Law and of the Gospel complete, and sufficient for the true knowledge of God.

**Third**, the doctrine of the heathen confirms idolatry, and invocation of those who are not gods but only creatures. The Scripture, however, teaches only the right knowledge of God, and how one is to invoke the living God, and no creature.

**Fourth**, the doctrine of the heathen is not a means through which God enlightens and converts the hearts of men, but rather an instrument through which Satan draws the hearts away from God. The Christian religion, however, or God's Word, is an *instrument* through which God converts, renews, enlightens, and sanctifies the hearts, unto eternal life. As Rom. 1 stands: *The Gospel is a power of God unto salvation to every one that believeth.*

# Hauptstück II

### *Von Gott*

*On God*

— ❦ —

## § 1. How God the Lord wills to be rightly known

**How and by what means does God the LORD will to be rightly known?**

In and by his Word, wherein he has revealed himself. Ps. 119: *Thy Word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path.*

## § 2. What God is

**What is God?**

With this question the highest and wisest men upon earth have troubled themselves at all times, but never has one of them attained to it.

Whoever will now rightly pursue this question must put all things else away and hearken alone whither God himself directs us — namely: *"This is my beloved Son; hear him."* Whoever, therefore, will know what God is, let him begin with Christ, the Son of God.

For the simple, this description drawn from the Catechism may be held:

**God is a spiritual, single Being, threefold in Persons, who has created all things and still preserves them; who through his Son has redeemed the damned and lost men, and through the Holy Spirit sanctifies them, calls them into his Christendom or Church, enlightens them, forgives their sins, on the Last Day will raise them from the dead, and will give to all believers an eternal life — but the godless will he thrust into eternal damnation.**

Such description is drawn from God's Word and from the *Symbolum Apostolicum*, as is easily seen: 1 John 4; Matt. 28; Gen. 2; John 3, 15, 16; John 5; Matt. 25; 1 Cor. 15.²⁴

## § 3. The chief properties of God

**Which are the chief properties of God? These.**

**1.** That he is Almighty, and therefore works everything that he wills, and can help out of every need. Gen. 18.

**2.** That he is true and wondrous, and keeps all that he has spoken. John 4: *God is true.* Ps. 23: *The word of the LORD is true.*

**3.** That he is just, and the highest Judge, wherefore one should fear before him. Ps. 7.

**4.** That he is merciful, patient, and of great goodness — wherefore the penitent should not doubt of his grace. Ps. 103; John 3.

## § 4. That there is but one God

**Is there, then, only one God?**

Yes; there is only one, single, eternal, natural, true God (Deut. 6: *Hear, O Israel, the LORD our God is one LORD;* Isa. 44: *I am the first, and I am the last; and besides me there is no God;* and *ibidem: Ye are my witnesses; is there any God besides me? There is no Rock; I know none*).

## § 5. Three distinct Persons in the one Godhead — against Arius, Macedonius, Sabellius

**How many, however, are Persons in the one Godhead?**

**Three distinct Persons — Father, Son, Holy Spirit — one right, single, natural, true God, Creator of heaven and earth; against the Arians, Macedonians, Sabellians, and their like heresy.**²⁵

**Prove this to me from Scripture.**

Gen. 1: *In the beginning Elohim created heaven and earth.* And it is manifest that *Elohim* is *pluralis numeri*, and does not signify one but many; and yet he says not *created* in the plural, but *created* as one, in the singular. This gives to understand that there is not more than one, single God and Creator; but that he speaks of *Gods* (in the plural) shows that in that same one, Divine Being there is nevertheless a Number, which one calls "many" or "more than one."

Matt. 28: *Go ye and teach all nations, baptising them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost.* Here Christ commands to baptise into one *name*, since there is only one Being of the Godhead; and yet he names three distinct Persons, Father, Son, and Holy Spirit — to show that in the one Divine Being are three distinct Persons.

## § 6. Against Jews and Turks

**Do the Jews and the Turks, then, do rightly in rejecting this doctrine of the Holy Trinity?**

No. For it will not do to judge by reason — that within his Godhead it must be, as we conceive, a single Person; for we have never seen anything of it, and no man can see. Since now we have the testimony of Scripture that in the Divine Being there are three Persons, we ought to follow Scripture — which is God's testimony of himself — and not heed what blind reason adds. For God we must believe as he has revealed himself in his Word.

## § 7. The inward distinction of the Persons

**What difference is there, then, between the three Persons of the Holy Trinity?**

**The distinction of the Father** is that he has the Godhead of no one, but by the eternal generation has given it, from eternity, to the Son.

**The distinction of the Son from the Father** is that the Son is begotten of the Father in eternity (Ps. 2).

**The distinction of the Holy Spirit from the Father and the Son** is that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father and the Son (John 15).²⁶

Beside this inward distinction of the Persons, there is now the outward distinction, in which the Son and the Holy Spirit are made manifest: the Son in the humanity — for the Son alone became man; the Holy Spirit is made manifest distinctly in the form of the dove, of fiery tongues, of gifts, of many tongues, and of wonder-signs — although the humanity was made by all three Persons, and the fiery tongues and the gifts of the Holy Spirit are the work and creature of all three Persons.

## § 8. The mystery in sum

**Tell me in a brief conception the sum of this great mystery.**

In sum: it is one God, one LORD, one Divine Majesty, Nature, and Being of all three Persons — but there is manifested at times the Person of the Father, at times of the Son, at times of the Holy Spirit; and whichever now is manifested, it is the one God in three Persons — that we may rightly know the Divine Majesty, and not, like Jews, heretics, Mahomet, blindly believe as if God were nothing more than a single Person. That God will not have; but he wills to be known as he has revealed himself to us. And especially it is God's concern to bring about the revelation and knowledge of his Son throughout the whole Scripture of Old and New Testament. John 5: *Search the Scriptures; they bear witness of me.*

## § 9. Necessity of this Article for salvation

**Can any one, then, be saved without knowledge of this Article?**

Without knowledge of this Article let no one purpose to come to heaven — namely, that there are three distinct Persons in the Godhead, Father, Son, Holy Spirit, and yet a single, eternal, almighty, Divine Being. So does God will to be known; and this is our Christian faith upon which we are baptised and called (Matt. 28; Mark 16).

## § 10. Of the Person of the Father

**Give me a brief report of the Person of the Father.**

The Father is the first Person of the Holy Trinity — the Origin or Fountain (as one may name it) of the Godhead in the Son and Holy Spirit — and the Son (when the Father is named) may not be sundered off but must together be named and meant; and likewise the Holy Spirit must be named and meant together with the Father and the Son, for no Person outside the other can be a separate God. So speak St. Paul and St. Peter (*Blessed be God, the Father of our LORD Jesus Christ, the Father of all grace*); and he himself in the Gospel ever puts the Father first, and ascribes all to him, and yet says (John 5) that they shall honour the Son as they honour the Father; and again (John 16): *All that the Father hath is mine* — save that the Father is the first Person, from whom the Son has it, and not the reverse. That some sins, however, happen distinctly against the Father, against the Son, and *into* the Holy Spirit — this pertains to the manifestation of the Persons, not to a division of the Being.

# Hauptstück III

### *Von der Person unseres HERREN Christi*

*On the Person of Our Lord Christ*

— ❦ —

## § 1. Proof that Christ is true, eternal, Almighty God

**Prove that Christ is true, eternal, Almighty God.**

**John 1:** *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God; the same was in the beginning with God; all things were made by him.*

**1 John 5:** *This Jesus is the true God and the eternal life.*

**John 5:** *My Father worketh hitherto, and I work also.*

**John 17:** *This is life eternal, that they may know thee* — and so on.

The Epistle to the Hebrews says: *He, the Son, is the express image of his Being* — testifying mightily that Christ must be true, natural God; and yet not on that account many, but one single God. In sum: **Christ has come forth from the Father's Divine Nature from eternity, and is his essential image — *substantialis imago, non artificialis aut facta vel creata*** — and so on.²⁷

In sum, the eternal Godhead of Christ is on the following grounds unassailably proved:

**1.** That Scripture (John 1; Micah 5) testifies that he was from eternity, and thus before the world was created.

**2.** That Scripture testifies that all things were created through him. John 1: *All things were made by the Word* — therefore he is true God, for creation is the work of Divine Majesty.

**3.** That Scripture attributes to him the honour of adoration (John 9; Ps. 45, 72; Jer. 23; Rom. 9) — therefore it attributes the Godhead to him.

**4.** That Scripture ascribes to him such works as no mere creature can perform — as the work of redemption, of reconciliation with God, of the gathering of the Church, and what else of the like (John 1: *Behold the Lamb of God;* Matt. 1: *He shall be called Jesus, for he shall save his people from their sins;* 1 Tim. 1: *It is certainly true, and a worthy word, that Christ Jesus is come into the world to save sinners*).

## § 2. Christ another Person, not another God

**Is Christ, then, because he is another Person from the Father, therefore also another God than the Father?**

**No.** He is another Person from the Father; but *no other God* from the Father — rather, equal to him in one eternal, single, Divine Being. This is the faith; so teaches faith; here abides faith. I mean the Christian faith, which is grounded in the Holy Scripture. Whoever will not believe Scripture, but run after reason, let him run — it stands "believe or be lost." Adam experienced this first, and we all after him; and if we will not believe, we too shall experience it.

## § 3. Conceived by the Holy Spirit, true man of the Virgin Mary

**Is Christ conceived of the Holy Spirit, and true man born of the Virgin Mary?**

Yes. The Symbol so testifies, and Luke 1 and 2; likewise the following passages of Scripture.

**Gen. 49:** *The sceptre shall not depart from Judah till Shilo come* — speaking of the seed of the woman, Christ, born of the Virgin Mary; for *Shilo* comes from *Siliah*, and would mean the *secundina*, in which the Child lies in the mother's body, or the newborn babe.

**Isa. 7:** *A virgin shall conceive and bear a Son, whose name shall be Emmanuel* — that is, the mother of the Messiah shall be a virgin, who in her virginal *silia* or body conceives without the operation of a man.

**Elizabeth** also proceeds beautifully in the same way, when she joyfully received Mary and said: *Blessed is the fruit of thy body* — that is, *silia*, *fructus matricis tuae*, the fruit of thy body only, and of no man.

**Ps. 110:** *Out of the mother, from the dawn, comes to thee the dew of thy birth* — as the dew falls from the dawn, so comes thy birth out of the mother. (Mother here does not mean the whole person of the woman, as in the fourth Commandment, but that which is called the "mother" of women in their body — *matricem* or *silia*.) Now the dew falls from heaven without any man's action (Mic. 2) — yea, also without cloud, even when the heaven is bright and the dawn is at its loveliest, it falls most delightfully of all; no man can say whence, or where it begins to fall. So also Christ has come out of the Virgin Mary's womb: no man can say how it happened, that this beautiful Dew, Christ, was conceived and born in his Mother's womb. It comes down from the Holy Spirit, conceived from above.

## § 4. Mary rightly called God-bearer

**Is the Virgin Mary rightly called God-bearer (*Gottes Gebärerin*)?**

**Yes;** in the moment that Mary gave her assent to the angel Gabriel and said, *"Be it done unto me according to thy word,"* Christ was at once God and perfect man in one Person, as the Fathers in the Council at Ephesus against Nestorius maintained.²⁸ For were it not so, she could not be named *Θεοτόκος*, God-bearer or God's Mother.

## § 5. Christ God and Man in one indivisible Person; the Chalcedonian Definition

**Is Christ, then, God and Man in one indivisible Person?**

In every way (John 1: *And the Word became flesh*, etc.). Here belongs the *Symbolum Chalcedonense*, in which this high mystery is fine and clearly explained.

*Canon Symboli Chalcedonensis apud Nicephorum,* lib. 15, cap. 6:

> *Itaque Patrum vestigijs insistentes, unum atque eundem profitemur esse filium, Dominum nostrum Iesum Christum, et una voce omnes praedicamus perfectum eundem in divinitate, et perfectum eundem in humanitate, DEUM verum et hominem verum eundem: ex rationali anima et corpore, consubstantialem Patri secundum divinitatem, consubstantialem nobis secundum humanitatem, per omnia nobis aequalem absque peccato, ante secula quidem ex Patre, secundum divinitatem, in extremis autem diebus eundem, propter nos et propter nostram salutem, ex Maria virgine et DEI genetrice, secundum humanitatem, genitum.*
>
> *Unum et eundem Iesum Christum filium et Dominum unigenitum, in duabus naturis inconfuse, immutabiliter, indivise, insegregabiliter cognitum et deprædicatum: Haudquaquam naturarum differentiam unione tollente, sed proprietatem potius utriusque naturae conservante, quae in personam unam et subsistentiam unam concurrit: non veluti in duas personas dispertitum aut divisum, sed unum et eundem filium unigenitum Deum Verbum, dominum nostrum Iesum Christum, quemadmodum antiquitus Prophetae, et ipse de seipso nos docuit Christus, et Symbolum Patrum nobis tradidit.*

**That is:** Therefore, treading in the footsteps of the Fathers, we confess that the very same Son, our LORD Jesus Christ, is one; and with one voice we all preach him to be perfect in the Godhead, and perfect the same in the humanity: true God, and true man the same, of a rational soul and body; consubstantial with the Father according to the Godhead, and consubstantial with us according to the humanity, in all things like unto us without sin; before the ages begotten of the Father according to the Godhead, but in these last days the same, for us and for our salvation, born of Mary the Virgin and God-bearer, according to the humanity. One and the same Jesus Christ, only-begotten Son and Lord, known and preached in two natures unconfusedly, unchangeably, undividedly, inseparably — the union in no wise taking away the distinction of the natures, but rather preserving the property of each, which concur in one Person and one *subsistence*: not as into two Persons parted or divided, but one and the same only-begotten Son, God the Word, our Lord Jesus Christ; even as of old the Prophets and the same Christ himself have taught us of himself, and the Symbol of the Fathers hath handed down to us.²⁹

## § 6. Why the Mediator had to be God and man

**Why, then, did the Mediator or Redeemer of the human race have to be true God and true man in one indivisible Person?**

**True God** he had to be, for these reasons:

**1.** A mere man could neither have made satisfaction for sin, nor could have brought about an everlasting redemption. For *it costs too much to redeem the souls* (Ps. 49).

**2.** A merely bare man could not have distributed the redemption once purchased, nor awakened faith in the hearts of men, nor preserved it to the end.

**3.** A mere man could not have heard the prayers or the sighs of afflicted and tempted hearts, nor could have helped them out of every need.

**4.** A mere man could not have delivered us from the power of the devil and of hell, and could not have shielded us against the raging and roaring of the world and against every evil.

**5.** A mere man could not have raised us from the dead, and given us eternal life, joy, and blessedness.

**True man** he had to be, for these reasons:

**1.** That he might speak with us men and might make known the will of his heavenly Father, since in the bare Godhead we could not have borne his Majesty — for God in his Majesty is a *consuming fire* (Deut. 4).

**2.** That, for us men who had sinned (Gen. 3), he could suffer and die. For the Divine Nature alone cannot suffer nor die.

**3.** He had also to be a true man, or to have a human nature about him, that he might have compassion with us, when it goes ill with us (Heb. 4).

**4.** He had to be a true man, that we might the more indubitably believe that he cordially loves us poor men. Eph. 5: *For no man ever yet hated his own flesh, but nourisheth and cherisheth it.*

## § 7. Both natures preserve their essential properties

**Do, then, both natures in Christ, after the Personal union, still retain their essential properties?**

**In truth, yes;** for essential properties of one nature never become essential properties of the other. If one took away the essential properties, one would take away the natures with them.

**Almighty, eternal, infinite, everywhere at once naturally** — that is, according to the property of the Nature and its natural being — these are essential properties of the *Divine Nature*.

**A bodily creature; flesh and blood; finite and circumscribed; to suffer and die** — these are properties of the *human nature*.

## § 8. Neither nature is its own Person after the Incarnation

**Does, then, each nature in Christ after the Incarnation subsist for itself, so that each is a separate Person?**

By no means. For they are so united with one another, that now, since the Incarnation, there belongs to the whole Person of Christ not only the Divine, but also the assumed human nature; otherwise it would not be an indivisible Personal union, nor would the Person be whole.

## § 9. Three points on speaking cautiously of these mysteries

**How, then, is one to speak carefully of these high mysteries?**

For the sake of the simple, the *praedicationes* or speeches concerning Christ may most conveniently be divided into three points:

**First**, since there are two natures in Christ, which in their essential properties are and remain unmixed, and yet nevertheless of both natures there is but one Person — what is only the property of one nature is ascribed *not to that nature alone as sundered off, but to the whole Person, which is at once God and Man*. Therefore the Apostle says that the Son of God was born of the seed of David *according to the flesh*; and 1 Peter 3 and 4, that Christ suffered *in* or *at* the flesh.

**Does it follow, then, that the bare human nature suffered for us?**

**No.** For the *high Person* has suffered for us, who is God and Man. Luke 24: *Christ must have suffered* — there stands not the bare human nature, but the Person Christ, who is God and Man.

1 Cor. 2: *They have crucified the LORD of glory* — there also the suffering is not ascribed to the bare human nature, but the Person is named, namely, *the Lord of glory*.

**But the Godhead cannot suffer?**

**True; but** since Godhead and manhood are in Christ one Person, Scripture, for the sake of that Personal union, ascribes also to the Godhead what befalls the human nature. For although the Godhead does not suffer, yet the Person, which is God, suffers in his assumed manhood, or in the flesh. Otherwise the suffering of this Person could not have been a *satisfaction* for the sin of the world, had only the bare human nature suffered for us. Whence also Scripture (Acts 20) says: *God has purchased his congregation with his own blood* — and so on.

**Does the suffering, then, touch the Godhead in Christ only in the same way as when God suffers in his saints, when they are put to death?**

**By no means.** For although he dwells in the saints, and so their suffering also touches him, yet this happens only *per relationem*, as they say in the schools. But when Scripture says: *God has suffered, God is crucified*, it has a wholly other meaning. For the Godhead does not merely dwell in the human nature as in other saints; the human nature is with the Godhead of the Son *personally and inseparably united*. Therefore he in his own flesh suffered — which also so dignifies and magnifies this Person that thereby all the world's sin was paid, God's wrath perfectly stilled, and an eternal redemption brought about (Heb. 10).

**Is it, then, wrong to draw the passages on the suffering only to the human nature, and altogether turn them away from the Godhead?**

**Yes, it is wrong.** For where the works are sundered, there the Person too must be torn apart, since all works or sufferings are ascribed *not to the natures, but to the Person*. For it is the Person who does all and suffers all, some things according to this, others according to that nature. Therefore they are not mere *verbal predicates* (*praedicationes verbales*), when one says: *God has suffered*, or *the Lord of glory is crucified*, as some will hold.³⁰

**Second**, as touching the discharge of Christ's mediatorial office, the Person does not handle and work *in, with, through, or according to one nature alone*, but *in, with, and through both natures* — or, as the *Concilium Chalcedonense* speaks, *the one nature works in communion with the other, whatever is the property of each.*

**Is Christ, then, our Mediator, Redeemer, King, and High Priest according to both natures?**

**Yes, truly.** For therefore the union of the two natures in Christ was made — that we might have such a Mediator and LORD who at once, in one Person, was God and Man, as the reasons just before given have shown.

*It has, however, the appearance that the Chalcedonian Council in its rule would give to understand that the human nature accomplishes in the mediatorial office no more than what its natural properties allow, and that it has nothing in common therein with the Divine Nature's workings. For the words further run:* **The human nature does what belongs to it; the Divine, however, triumphs and shows itself in wonder-works.**

**Answer.** In this other manner of speaking, what is considered here is what each nature according to its essential properties performs in the office. But it is by no means denied that the assumed human nature, on account of and according to the manner of the Personal union and exaltation, is used in the office also for the performance of the Divine workings. Therefore also Christ's flesh is called a *life-giving flesh* (John 6), and so named in the Ephesine Council. Certainly the aforesaid rule handles Christ's office insofar as concerns the bringing about of salvation, not denying that the assumed and exalted human nature has its supernatural co-working, insofar as concerns the granting, dispensing, and delivery of that salvation. For the whole Christ is made to us of God *unto wisdom, unto righteousness* (1 Cor. 1). But of this more hereafter.

**Third**, Holy Scripture does not stop with saying that Christ according to his human nature has received many and high gifts, with which he immeasurably surpasses the gifts of all angels and blessed men. It also plainly shows that to the human nature in Christ is given *all authority in heaven and on earth* (Matt. 28); the power to make alive (John 6); the honour of invocation (John 9; Phil. 2); the holding of the Last Judgement (John 5); the glory to fill all things (Eph. 1, 4); and what else of the like.

## § 10. First Objection — Communication of divine attributes to the human nature

**Objection.** If the essential properties of the Divine Nature were with the deed communicated to the assumed human nature, then they could no longer remain the properties of the Divine Nature only; for what is *own* remains no longer own when it is communicated to another?

**Answer.** When the Divine Majesty or Almightiness, or the power to make alive, and whatever else of the like, is communicated to the human nature in this manner — that Christ's human nature no less than the Godhead had such Majesty or Glory of and from itself, or according to the manner of its own nature — then there would be no doubt that they would not remain the essential properties of the Divine Nature alone, since after that fashion they would have become also essential properties of the human nature.

Now we teach clearly, from and after God's Word, that these properties remain forever alone the essential properties of the Divine Nature, *in quarto modo* (as one is wont to speak in the schools), and never become essential properties of the human nature. Therefore no such subversion can in any way be attributed to us. Rather, when we speak of it, we understand it no further than of the *communication* which happens on account of the Personal union and the exaltation of the human nature to the right hand of God, according to the testimonies of Scripture. Scripture says that to the human nature there have been communicated or given such glories — namely, *all power in heaven and on earth*, *the making alive*, *the honour of invocation*, and what else is of the like. By that we abide, though we in this life cannot wholly understand or express the *mode* of the communication, but must believe. The ancient Fathers have tried to give some indication of it in the likeness of the fiery iron: yet the likeness does not fully explain this mystery, but shows in some measure that such communication may happen without change of the natures and their essential properties. For as the fire communicates to the iron its property of shining and burning, without destruction of the iron's nature — so the human nature need not on this account be destroyed or changed, though the Godhead truly communicates to it its Majesty, and in, with, and through it shines, works, creates, and performs its works. And as the soul communicates its properties to its body, and yet does not change it, so the Godhead communicates its properties or Majesty to the assumed human nature *without change or transmutation*; for the human nature abides forever finite, and is not transmuted into the Godhead itself, but remains ever under the Creator. So too it does not become equal in properties to the Godhead. Rather, Almightiness and the power to make alive remain forever essential properties of the *Divine Nature*, and never become essential properties of the human nature — although they are communicated to the assumed human nature on account of the Personal union. No conforming happens as to the *will*, for the Divine Nature retains its Divine will, and the human nature its human will, which is subordinate to the Divine will. So also the *workings* of the natures are not conformed, for the workings of the Divine Nature are Divine, of the human human. Although the Godhead makes its workings common with the assumed human nature — as *Cyril, De incarnatione unigeniti*, cap. 7, rightly writes — yet they never become natural, essential workings of Christ's human nature; for they proceed not from the nature of the flesh, but from the Personal union.

Finally, the Divine Nature has this Majesty in a wholly other way than the assumed human nature: for the Godhead has this Majesty *essentially*, and is Almightiness and Life itself; the assumed human nature has it only *on account of the Personal union*, by which it is raised to the communion of such Majesty.³¹

## § 11. Second Objection — Whether Christ's human nature having omnipotence makes it God

**Second Objection.** What has Almighty power must be God. Now the assumed human nature has such power. Therefore must it be God itself?

**Answer.** What has Almighty power *from its own nature and essential properties*, so that it is naturally and essentially Almighty — that is God. What, however, has Almighty power *not in the said way* — that is not God itself.

Although in Scripture (Matt. 28 and elsewhere) *Almighty power* is spoken to Christ's assumed human nature, it does not therefore follow that it is God. For it has such Almighty power not from its own essential nature and properties (so that it were essentially and by nature Almighty, or Almightiness itself), but *on account of the Personal union and exaltation*. Otherwise if one might so conclude, one could just as well conclude *against* the Council of Ephesus: *"What is life-giving is God. Christ's flesh is life-giving. Therefore it is God."* But it does not stand; rather, the first proposition (*Maior*) must be so explained: **"What is life-giving from its own power and essential natural property, that is God."** Christ's flesh, however, is not life-giving from its own power and essential natural property, but on account of and by reason of the Personal union and exaltation, according to the express passages (John 6). Therefore the conclusion does not rightly follow that it must therefore be God itself.

## § 12. Christ's human nature as active in miracles

**Has, then, the assumed human nature in the performance of miracles and the like — beyond its natural properties or received gifts — also, on account of the Personal union and exaltation, its co-working, so that the miracles are ascribed to Christ not according to the Divine nature alone, but at once according to both natures?**

**Answer. In every way.** For the Holy Scripture (Acts 10) says: *God anointed Jesus of Nazareth with the Holy Spirit and with power; who went about doing good and healing all that were overpowered of the devil.*

Now this cannot be understood of his Divine nature, for that has had such power from eternity, and did not need to receive it in time. But the human nature, which the Son of God in the fullness of time assumed inseparably into the unity of his Person, is thereby anointed and endowed. Whence also Mark 5 stands: *Christ perceived, when he stilled the fountain of blood in the sick woman, that such power had gone forth from him.*

Should we then not follow those who allege that the most holy human nature of Christ contributes nothing to miracles?

**Answer.** To the Divine nature the wonder-signs are rightly and fairly ascribed. For the power to do wonder-works is the essential property of the Divine Nature alone, as it is written (Ps. 86): *Thou alone doest wonders.* Yet since the whole fullness of the Godhead dwells bodily in Christ (Col. 2), this Divine power and authority shows itself in, with, and through the assumed human nature — for it has been assumed for that end by the Son of God, in unity of Person, that the Godhead should in, with, and through it show itself, work, and create.

Whence the ancient teacher **Athanasius**, *Dialogus 5 de Trinitate*, writes: *That the Word, united with the human nature, does wonder-signs — not sundered from the assumed human nature, but was pleased through, in, and with the same to demonstrate his Divine Omnipotence.* And **Cyril**, *De incarnatione Unigeniti* cap. 7: *The Word communicates to the assumed flesh its Divine workings, so that it can even make the dead alive and heal the sick.*

The wonder-signs remain therefore truly God's works, although they are performed in, with, and through the assumed human nature; and the assumed human nature is in no wise to be excluded from the performance of the wonder-works.

## § 13. Christ's flesh as life-giving

**Has Christ's flesh truly received life-giving power?**

**Yes indeed.** For John 6 stands, that Christ's flesh is truly a *food* — which the Council of Ephesus rightly explained thus, that it is *life-giving*, or has *life-giving power*.

Yes, some say, one must understand this only of the merit and intercession.

**Answer.** Christ's flesh is life-giving because it is personally united to the life-giving Word, and the Word truly communicated its life-giving power to the assumed flesh — *Cyril, lib. 4 in Iohannem, cap. 24*, and elsewhere in many places. And also because through the assumed flesh the life of the world was merited. Therefore the life-giving must not be understood only of merit and intercession, but of the *power to make alive*, or to communicate life now also.

**"The Spirit is he that maketh alive, John 6" — therefore this cannot be ascribed to Christ's flesh?**

**Answer.** This passage is not set against Christ's assumed holy flesh, but against our corrupted flesh. It is therefore ill brought forward against Christ's life-giving flesh. Nor do we teach that Christ's flesh is life-giving *of and from itself*, as the Spirit is (this belongs to the Godhead alone), but that it is life-giving because it is personally united to the life-giving Word, and thus, as *Cyril, lib. 4 in Lucam, cap. 23*, rightly writes, has become *wholly life-giving*.³²

**Were this so, must not the properties of both natures in Christ be mixed?**

**By no means.** For Christ's flesh does not make alive as the Word itself does. The Godhead of the Word makes alive *through itself*; Christ's flesh, however, makes alive *not through itself*, but on account that it, for the sake of the Personal union with the Word, has become wholly life-giving.

**1.** The Godhead of the Word is Life itself; Christ's flesh, however, is not Life itself, but makes alive because it has been raised to such power on account of the Personal union.

**2.** The Godhead of the Word is Life from eternity and the fountain thereof; Christ's flesh, however, has received this power in the fullness of time.

**3.** The Godhead of the Word communicates life as a living Spirit; Christ's flesh, however, does so because it is come to the power of Christ's Spirit through the union. Here no mixing of natures or properties is to be found.

## § 14. Christ's knowledge according to the human nature

**Is, then, to the LORD Christ, according to his assumed human nature, also communicated the knowledge or discernment of all things?**

**Yes.** For in Col. 2 stands, that in Christ are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge. John 2: *But Jesus did not commit himself to them, for he knew them all, and needed not that any should testify of a man, for he himself knew what was in man.* John 21: *LORD, thou knowest all things* — and so on. The same has *Damascene, lib. 2, cap. 22, and lib. 3, cap. 21*, taught.

**But Mark 13 stands: he does not know the hour of the Last Judgement?**

Christ speaks there *as one sent*, who is not to reveal all his secrets — not that he did not know, but that he then, at the time of the humiliation, was not to reveal it or would not. Whence *Hilary, De Trinitate*, writes: *Professionem nescitae diei, non esse ignorationis infirmitatem, sed tacendi dispensationem.* And this passage is especially not to be drawn to the state of Christ's exaltation. For if he is to hold and preside over the Last Judgement — even as a man (John 5) — he will surely know its day. So *Nicephorus, lib. 18, cap. 50* writes: there were heretics called Agnoetae, who alleged that Christ, according to his human nature, did not know many things. May those, then, who now attach themselves to the like teaching, look well to it — however they may think to escape the Agnoetes' heresy.³³

## § 15. Christ receives the honour of invocation according to his human nature

**Has, then, Christ, according to his human nature, also received the honour of invocation?**

**Yes; he has received it.** For Phil. 2 stands: *Therefore God hath highly exalted him, and given him a name which is above every name; that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of things in heaven and things in earth, and things under the earth; and every tongue should confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father.* And John 9: the blind man who was made seeing worshiped the whole Christ, not only as God, but also as Man. Matt. 15: the Canaanite woman worshiped the whole Christ, God and Man, and said: *"Ah LORD, thou Son of David, have mercy on me."*

## § 16. Objection — that adoration is not to be directed to a creature

**But adoration is to be directed to no creature; now Christ's manhood, even in the Personal union, is a creature?**

**Answer.** The human nature of Christ is in this matter not considered simply as a bare creature; but as it, personally and inseparably united with the Divine Nature of the Son of God, belongs with it to the whole Person of Christ. Therefore we teach with all Christendom that not only the Godhead, but also the assumed human nature *in the Word or in the Son of God who has united it to himself personally and inseparably* is to be invoked and worshiped. And accordingly we worship *not a bare creature or human nature, in and for itself*, as it is naturally in its own being or might be considered, but on account of and because it belongs to the Person of Christ, and is personally united with the eternal Godhead of the Son — so that we can never wholly honour and invoke the Person of Christ, if we exclude his human nature from prayer or invocation.

It is therefore a blasphemy to teach that Christ, *as flesh*, is not to be worshiped, but only as God. Whence also **Cyril** in his book *Ad Reginas* rightly writes: *"If any one says that the flesh of our Lord Christ is not to be worshiped because it is a man's flesh, and does not say that one should worship it as the flesh of the LORD and God, him the whole right-believing Church anathematises."* — *Si quis dicat inadorabilem domini nostri carnem ut hominis, et non adorabilem ut Domini et DEI carnem, hunc anathematizat sancta et Catholica Ecclesia.*

**But it stands written (Jer. 17): "Cursed is he that setteth his confidence upon men, and holdeth flesh for his arm." Christ's flesh, however, is a true flesh: therefore is he cursed who directs his prayer to Christ's flesh?**

**Answer.** The passage of Jeremiah does not speak at all of Christ's flesh or human nature, but of every other men's flesh outside Christ. So do our adversaries blasphemously interpret it of the flesh which the Son of God has personally and inseparably united to himself, and raised to the right hand of the power of God. Whence also *Augustine, in Iohan. 14, De verbis Domini, Serm. 58*, says:

> *"Quid est quod carnem eius, quam creaturam esse non negas, simul cum Divinitate adoras, et ei non minus quàm Deitati deferuis? Ego verò Dominicam carnem, imò perfectam in Christo humanitatem ideò adoro, quòd à divinitate suscepta et Deitati unita est, ut non alium et alium, sed unum eundémque DEUM et Hominem filium DEI esse confitear."*

That is: *What is it that thou worshipest his flesh, which thou dost not deny to be a creature, together with the Godhead, and servest it no less than the Deity? I truly worship the Lord's flesh — nay rather, the perfect humanity in Christ — for this reason: that it is by the divinity taken up and united to the Deity, so that I confess not one and another, but one and the same DEUS and MAN, Son of God.*³⁴

## § 17. Christ's body after the Resurrection

**But Christ after his Resurrection retained the marks of the nails?**

**Answer.** That happened, as the old right-believing Church has said, *per dispensationem*, since Christ wished thereby to bear witness of the truth of his Resurrection. But it does not follow that he still has them sitting at the right hand of God. For that would be against the nature or properties of glorified bodies; and since Scripture (1 Cor. 15) testifies that our bodies in the Resurrection shall have no more defect, that will still more be true of Christ's glorified body. That he bore them for a time after his Resurrection was, as noted, for the sake of the Apostles, that the truth of the Resurrection of his body might be more firmly established with them.

**Is, then, the glory which Christ's body has received out of the Resurrection, and that which he has come to through the Personal union and sitting at the right hand of the power of God, one and the same?**

**No.** For the glory or honour which Christ's body has out of the Resurrection is finite, and may in some measure be compared with the honour which the saints in the other life have out of the Resurrection — yet with this understanding, that Christ's body will retain the pre-eminence in glory before all other saints' bodies. But the glory which he received out of the Personal union — in the state of humiliation, though not always used, and which came to him upon the sitting at the right hand of God — that is Divine, and befalls no other creature besides.

## § 18. The Ascension of Christ

**How dost thou understand the Ascension of Christ?**

**Not in an allegorical way**, but according to the letter, as Scripture speaks. For his body is truly lifted up from the earth, and gone into heaven.

**What does the word "heaven," then, mean, whither Christ has ascended?**

Heaven means here as much as the seat or dwelling of the blessed — whither all the blessed come, and where Christ, also according to the manner of glorified bodies, is with his glorified body. And this heaven or heavenly dwelling of the blessed is surely distinct from hell. For as Christ (Luke 16) says, there is a great gulf fixed between the blessed and the damned. Yet we do not approve at all the childish thoughts of our opponents, who describe heaven as if it were a *spatial place*, just like places here upon earth. For Scripture does not describe heaven in such a spatial manner of places and cities — that it is a *Locus Physicus*, a certain bodily, natural, spatial place — but names heaven *God's house* (John 14), *Paradise* (Luke 23), *eternal life* (Dan. 12; Matt. 25: *the righteous shall go into everlasting life*). But these things do not let themselves be expressed by us in this life. Rather, it is and remains true that eye has not seen, ear has not heard, neither has entered into the heart of man, what God has prepared for them that love him (Isa. 64; 1 Cor. 2).

**What difference is there, then, between Christ's Ascension and the blessed, who also shall come into heaven?**

This is the difference: that the blessed shall come into heaven, but not be higher than heaven; but Christ has so ascended into heaven with his body, that he is now higher than the heaven (Heb. 7: *sublimior caelis factus*) — which is said of no other man.

So also the blessed shall come into heaven, but not so that they should rule over heaven; that befalls the LORD Christ alone, who rules over all (1 Cor. 15) and thus also over heaven itself.

Moreover, Christ ascended above all the heavens *that he might fill all things*, or rule present over all — which cannot or may not be said of any other blessed man. So Christ ascended into heaven with his body in such wise that he at once set himself at the right hand of God — which befalls neither angels nor blessed men (Heb. 1).

**But Scripture (Acts 3) says: Christ must be received up from the heaven?**

**Answer.** The opposite party translates *δέξασθαι* thus, but does it wrong — especially since this happens to the end that with such translation one wishes to distort a certain passage or text, that from it may be proved that Christ's body is not distributed present in the Holy Supper with the blessed bread. This cannot stand, since heaven has not so received Christ's body that it be kept or preserved therein *as in a prison*: rather, Christ has much more taken heaven with his body, so that he even rules over the heaven itself.

Likewise, since Scripture (Eph. 4) clearly says: *He is ascended above all the heavens, that he might fill all things.* And: *He sits with his body at the right hand of the power of God.* From which it sufficiently appears that he with his body is not enclosed in heaven, so that he may not be and be present elsewhere.

## § 19. What the "Right Hand of God" is

**What then is the "Right Hand of God"?**

**Answer. No created place at all.** For a created place — however blessed it may be — cannot with consistency and truth be called God's Right Hand, since God's Right Hand is uncreated and eternal, which cannot be said of any created place. Rather, it is the Divine Majesty and Power of God himself — wherefore also in Scripture it is called *the Right Hand of the Majesty of God* (Heb. 1, 8) and *the Right Hand of the power of God* (Luke 22). Therefore, as God's Right Hand is no place, so it is also enclosed in no spatial place, but rather goes over all spatial places and cities. Were God's Right Hand a certain place of blessedness in heaven, it must follow that Christ, in his coming, would leave the Right Hand of God when he shall appear in the clouds — which is wrong, for he sits forever at the Right Hand of God, and never leaves it.

**According to which nature, then, is he exalted to the Right Hand of God?**

The *Person* is exalted to the Right Hand of God — but not according to the Divine, but according to the assumed human nature.

This appears in this: that the Godhead in itself can neither be exalted nor humiliated; but the humanity, since it is changeable, can be humiliated and exalted.

Likewise, that this exaltation happened *in time*. Now of the Godhead it cannot be said that it received anything in time, since it has had all from eternity. Therefore this exaltation must be understood of the assumed human nature.

So too Christ, according to his Godhead, is the Right Hand of God itself (Isa. 53); therefore it cannot be said that according to it he is exalted to the Right Hand of God. So the exaltation must again be drawn to the human nature.

Scripture also shows this: it says (Acts 2), *The crucified Christ is made LORD;* likewise, *the Christ who is raised from the dead is exalted to the Right Hand of God*. For it clearly points to the assumed human nature.

And so the ancient right-believing Church also has explained this Article — that Christ is set at the Right Hand of God according to his human nature.

**Athanasius** *apud Theodoretum*: *Corpus est, cui dicit Dominus, sede à dextris meis* — *It is the Body to which the LORD says, Sit thou at my right hand.*

**Ambrose** on Heb. 1: *Sit thou at my right hand* belongs to the exaltation of the human nature. The same is taught also by **Cyprian**, **Chrysostom**, and others.³⁵

## § 20. Christ's bodily rule at the Right Hand of God

**Does it follow, then, from Christ's sitting at the Right Hand of God, that Christ is not only as God but also as Man everywhere present, and rules present in heaven and on earth?**

**Freely, so it follows.** For to sit at the Right Hand of God is and means properly nothing else than to rule together with the Father, with equal power, might, and glory, over all creatures. Now the Father does not rule over all creatures absent, but present. Wherefore, since Christ according to his human nature sits at the Right Hand of God, there is no doubt that he also as Man rules — not absent, but present — over all things in heaven and on earth, in a heavenly, supernatural, humanly-incomprehensible manner. As is clearly indicated in Eph. 1, when Paul says: *He is set at the Right Hand in heaven, above all principality and power and might and dominion, and every name that is named, not only in this world, but also in that which is to come; and hath put all things under his feet.* Likewise Ps. 8: *Thou hast made him ruler over the works of thy hands; thou hast put all things under his feet — sheep and oxen all, yea and beasts of the field, the birds of the air, and the fishes of the sea, and whatever passes through the paths of the sea.*

## § 21. Not ubiquity of the substance in all creatures

**Do ye teach, then, that the substance of the flesh or body of Christ is in all creatures — as in stone, leaf, grass, sun, moon, and so on?**

**No; we do not teach so, nor will we use this way of speech; but we abide simply by what the aforesaid passages of Scripture say.**³⁶ Further, since this mystery in this life is unsearchable and unspeakable, we do not enter into it.

**But how can it be possible that Christ, as Man, since his assumed human nature is and remains finite, is at the same time present in more than one place?**

**Answer.** Our opposite party holds this altogether for impossible; or if it should happen, then the human nature must become infinite. We, however, abide by the clear passages of Scripture (Matt. 18, 28; Eph. 1, 4; Ps. 8), and know that the Creator of all things, the eternal Son of God, can very easily bring it about that his assumed human nature — which is and remains finite, and yet, since he has united it to himself inseparably and *indistanter* (as *Damascene, lib. 3, cap. 3*, speaks), and has exalted it to the Right Hand of God — is present at once in more places, without any spatial, apprehensible expansion or diffusion of it, in a heavenly, supernatural, and incomprehensible manner. For his words cannot lie nor fail (Ps. 33: *What he speaks, that happens*), and with him no word is impossible (Luke 1:37).

## § 22. Christ's glorified body

**Distinguish more nearly Christ's glorified body from his body in the state of humiliation.**

That he died — of that he died to sins once (Rom. 6).

**Second**, he has become glorious. Phil. 3: *Who shall change our vile body, that it may be fashioned like unto his glorified body.* Likewise, *spiritual* (1 Cor. 15). For if this shall happen to the elect in the Resurrection, much more will it be at Christ's body. Whence he is (Matt. 28) risen through the sealed grave-stone; (Luke 24) become invisible before his disciples; likewise (John 20) not gone step by step from one place to another into the disciples' chamber, through opened doors and stairs, but, as John writes, *suddenly and unforeseen stood in the midst of his disciples*, so that the disciples supposed they saw a spirit.

These and the like glorifications of the glorified body of Christ can in this life not all be known, much less pronounced. Wherefore it is a foolhardy audacity in the opposite party that they, even in the state of Exaltation, press only upon the *apprehensibility and visibility* of Christ's body — as though Christ's body could no more be a true human body when it is not at all times in a visible and apprehensible manner in a certain place. Yet Christ even in the state of humiliation made himself invisible, as *Theophylact, John 8*, writes: *Sua magna potestate invisibilem se fecit insidiatoribus.*

To this: it will not do at all to argue: *"A natural body cannot do this or that; therefore a spiritual body (1 Cor. 15) cannot do it either"* — for they are, so far as concerns the glorified properties, distinct as 1 Cor. 15 stands. Much less will it hold, from our bodies (which cannot do this or that), to draw inferences and force from thence: *"Therefore Christ's body, which is exalted to the Right Hand of God, cannot perform this or that either."*

## § 23. Humiliation and Exaltation

**What does Scripture mean when it speaks of Christ's Humiliation?**

**Answer.** The whole time from the Conception and Incarnation to his victorious Resurrection. And all the weaknesses (excepting sin) which he took upon himself — as poverty, misery, that he went about from one place to another, that he trembled and quaked, was crucified, endured the bitter Death, and what else was of the like. Therefore Phil. 2 stands, *he was found in fashion as a man, and was obedient unto the death of the Cross.*

**Did, then, the LORD Christ at the time of the humiliation, according to his assumed human nature, not have the Divine Majesty and Glory?**

**Answer.** The Humiliation does not signify a defect or a robbing of the Majesty according to the human nature; but has regard to the *use* of it — that he in that time held such Majesty concealed, and did not always fully use it. For he had such Majesty from the Conception on, but did not always let it be seen, or publicly show it — yet so that it might not have the appearance as though he had not this Majesty, he sometimes (as in his glorious wonder-signs, and elsewhere) let it be shown. As John 1 stands: *We beheld his glory, the glory as of the only-begotten of the Father.* And John 2: *And Jesus manifested his glory, and his disciples believed on him.* So he let it be seen also on the mount Thabor, when he was transfigured before his disciples (Matt. 17).

**But Scripture (Luke 24) says: he entered into his glory after the Resurrection — how then could he have had it also before?**

**Answer.** The said passage does not teach that Christ, according to his assumed human nature, first after the Resurrection attained his Majesty; but that after his Resurrection, when he wholly entered into the state of glory, he also wholly used and shewed his glory — according to the old rule: *Res tunc dicitur fieri, cum incipit manifestius patefieri* — "A thing is then said to happen, when it begins more manifestly to be shown." So *Augustine, lib. Quaest. 83, quaest. 69*, and *Magister Sententiarum lib. 3, dist. 18*, write.

**But would there not follow a confusion of the Articles of the Christian faith — of the Incarnation, the Ascension, and the sitting at the Right Hand of God?**

**Answer. By no means.** For in the Incarnation, where the union of the Divine and human nature happened, Christ had at once according to his human nature — on account of the Personal union with the Godhead — such Majesty. But he held it in the time of humiliation *secretly*, and did not always, but only at times, use it.

Through the Ascension, however, he laid off all the weaknesses of this life which he had taken upon himself for our sake, and the whole humiliation, and thus went out of this world.

Through the sitting at the Right Hand, he entered into the full use and exercise of his Majesty, so that he now no longer hides this Majesty or holds it secret (as at the time of humiliation), but uses and shows it fully in, with, and through the assumed human nature. And so the humiliation ceased when he was exalted (Phil. 2).

**Wherefore, then, has he taken such Humiliation upon himself?**

**Answer.** For us men and for our salvation. For had he wished to use the Majesty which he had received in the Incarnation according to his human nature, he could neither have suffered nor died. But since he was to accomplish the work of redemption — which was to happen through suffering and death — he emptied himself for a time of his Glory, and did not wish always to use it.

# Hauptstück IV

### *Vom Ampt Christi*

*On the Office of Christ*

— ❦ —

## § 1. The four parts of Christ's Office

**Wherein does the office of the LORD Christ chiefly consist?**

**Answer.** In these pieces:

**First**, that he has redeemed us from sin and death, and offered up his life for us (Rom. 3; Isa. 53; 1 Tim. 2: *There is one God, and one Mediator between God and men, the Man Christ Jesus, who gave himself a ransom for all*).

**Second**, that he has fully fulfilled for us the whole Law of God. Matt. 5: *I am not come to abolish the Law but to fulfil.* Rom. 8: *What was impossible to the Law (inasmuch as it was weakened by the flesh), that did God, and sent his Son in the likeness of sinful flesh, and condemned sin in the flesh through sin, that the righteousness required by the Law might be fulfilled in us, who now walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit.* Rom. 10: *Christ is the fulfilment of the Law.*

**Third**, that he bestows on us both these pieces through the preaching-office and the Sacraments, and imputes and assigns them to us in faith. Whence the Gospel (Rom. 1) is called a *power of God* to save all who believe on it. And especially (Luke 24) it is said: *Was it not needful that Christ should suffer, and that in his name repentance and remission of sins should be preached among all nations?*

**Fourth**, that he is with us, gives us his Holy Spirit (John 14, 15, 16), stands by us, and in such faith strengthens and preserves us — against our own flesh, the world, temptation, the cross, suffering, the devil, and every misfortune — until the end.

**Phil. 1:** *He who has begun in us the good work will also perform it, until the day of Jesus Christ.*

**Phil. 2:** *It is God who works in you both to will and to do of his good pleasure.*

**1 Pet. 5:** *But the God of all grace, who has called us to his eternal glory in Christ Jesus, the same shall — after that ye have suffered a little while — make you perfect, strengthen, confirm, ground you.*

## § 2. How we appropriate the benefits of Christ

**How do we poor men and sinners then become partakers of the benefits and treasures of grace of the LORD Christ?**

**When we in bruised and broken hearts believe, and with sure confidence apply to ourselves all his payment, merit, and obedience — not doubting that it happened for us and for our sake, and is our own.**

**John 1:** *He gave power to become the sons of God to them that believe on his name.*

**John 3:** *God so loved the world that he gave his only-begotten Son, that whosoever believes on him should not perish, but have everlasting life.*

**Rom. 3:** *We are justified without merit, by his grace, through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus, whom God has set forth to be a mercy-seat, through faith in his blood.*

For he has given himself for all the sins of the world, not that he should terrify the afflicted and despondent consciences, much less thrust them away and condemn them, but that he should deliver them from sin and death, strengthen and comfort them in every anguish and need, and make them righteous and blessed.

**Has Christ died, then, for all men and sinners?**

**Yes in truth.** For it stands (2 Cor. 5): *He has died for us all.* And 1 John 2: *My little children, these things I write unto you, that ye sin not; and if any man sin, we have an Advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous — and he is the propitiation for our sins; and not for ours only, but also for the sins of the whole world.*

Gal. 3: *Scripture has concluded all under sin, that the promise by faith of Jesus Christ might be given.*

Rom. 11: *God has concluded all in unbelief, that he might have mercy upon all.*

Isa. 53: *The LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all.*

## § 3. Comfort against great sin

**But my sins are too great, that I cannot comfort myself with him.**

**Then his blood, death, and holy obedience are still stronger and weightier than thy sins.**

Rom. 5: *Where sin abounded, grace did much more abound.*

Matt. 9: *I am not come to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance.*

Matt. 11: *Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will refresh you.*

Luke 15: *There shall be joy in heaven over one sinner that repenteth.*

Isa. 1: *Though your sins be as scarlet, they shall be as white as snow; though they be red as crimson, they shall be as wool.*

Ps. 103: *As far as the east is from the west, so far has he removed our transgressions from us.*

**Examples of great sinners who obtained grace through Christ:** Adam and Eve, Manasseh, David, Peter, Mary Magdalene, Paul, the thief on the cross.³⁷ Whoever in a repentant heart calls upon Christ shall likewise obtain grace. Salvation cannot fall to any other than to thee, the penitent sinner. In Baptism thou hast been sealed for the forgiveness of thy sins; in the Lord's Supper thou art spiritually nourished by his true Body and Blood; Christ Jesus is come into the world to save sinners (1 Tim. 1); and by his Spirit he intercedes for the believers, sealing them unto the day of redemption. Whoever believes on him has, and shall have, everlasting life (John 3).

# Hauptstück V

### *Vom Reich Christi*

*On the Kingdom of Christ*

— ❦ —

## § 1. Christ's threefold Kingdom

**What is Christ's Kingdom?**

Christ himself said, John 18: *My Kingdom is not of this world.* It is a threefold Kingdom:

**1.** The **spiritual Kingdom** — Christ's rule over the Church on earth, which is called the *Kingdom of Grace and Forgiveness of sins*.

**2.** The **Kingdom of Power** (*regnum potentiae*) — Christ's sovereignty over all creation, mentioned Eph. 1 and Ps. 8: *Thou hast put all things under his feet.*

**3.** The **Kingdom of Glory** (*regnum gloriae*) — the everlasting Kingdom after the Resurrection of the dead, into which the blessed shall enter.³⁸

**Who established this Kingdom?**

God the Father, out of pure grace, that lost humanity — driven under Satan's tyranny by sin — might be restored through the Son (Ps. 2: *Ask of me, and I will give thee the heathen for thine inheritance*).

## § 2. Chief attributes of the Kingdom

**Which are the chief attributes of Christ's Kingdom?**

**1.** **Spiritual** — not consisting in food and drink, but in righteousness and the Holy Spirit (Rom. 14; Matt. 11; Ps. 22). Christ's Kingdom comforts the afflicted and delivers them from Satan's tyranny.

**2.** **Everlasting** — for it endures forever (John 13; Luke 1; Isa. 9).

**3.** **Distributes spiritual goods** — forgiveness of sins, the Holy Spirit, righteousness, eternal life; and it delivers from spiritual harm.

**What kind of King is Christ?**

*Ein geistlicher König* — a spiritual King, poor and meek (Zech. 9: *Behold, thy King cometh unto thee, meek and lowly*), yet with eloquence and wisdom to speak to the weary (Isa. 50).

## § 3. The extent of Christ's power

**How far does his power extend?**

Over all creatures, to the ends of the world (Ps. 2). His subjects offer themselves willingly *in holy adornment* (Ps. 45; Matt. 21; Zech. 9). His scepter is a righteous scepter (Ps. 45).

## § 4. The works of Christ's Kingdom

**What is the work of Christ's Kingdom?** *Five points:*

**1.** He gathers an everlasting Congregation to himself through Word and Sacraments.

**2.** He grants forgiveness of sins.

**3.** He sanctifies believers through the Holy Spirit.

**4.** He protects them from misfortune.

**5.** He bestows eternal life and blessedness.

**Why must this King suffer and die?**

For the transgressions of his people, that they might be justified (Isa. 43; 53; Rom. 3–4).

## § 5. Christ's Kingdom is spiritual, not external

**Is Christ's Kingdom, then, an external bodily realm — as the Anabaptists and others say?**

**No.** The Anabaptists err. Christ's Kingdom is *spiritual*, conducted through the preaching ministry and the Sacraments. It exists wherever his holy Word is preached and Sacraments administered according to his order. Its end is eternal life (John 3). Its subjects — centurions, royal officials, and Christians in every worldly station — need not abandon their offices, but bring the Kingdom into them (Matt. 8; Acts 10; Gal. 3: *there is neither Jew nor Greek, bond nor free, male nor female — all one in Christ Jesus*).

## § 6. Three differences between Christ's Kingdom and earthly kingdoms

**What difference is there between Christ's Kingdom and worldly kingdoms?** *Three points:*

**1.** Worldly kingdoms are governed by men alone; Christ, being both God and Man, rules in both natures.

**2.** Temporal kingdoms distribute material goods — *zeitlicher Friede* (temporal peace), discipline, propriety. Christ's Kingdom distributes spiritual treasures — forgiveness of sins, divine righteousness, the Holy Spirit, and eternal life.

**3.** Earthly powers govern only *the external members* through force. Christ's Kingdom governs the heart, renewing the whole person (Ps. 51; Ezek. 36).

**4.** Worldly kingdoms use physical force and have defined boundaries; Christ's Kingdom operates through Word and Holy Spirit and extends to the world's end.

**5.** Earthly kingdoms eventually fall; Christ's Kingdom is everlasting (Ps. 110; Dan. 2; Luke 1).

## § 7. Practical use of this doctrine

**What practical use has this doctrine for a Christian?**

That we may trust in Christ as our King and Head, expecting from him protection, answered prayer, and eternal life; and that we may confidently rely on him for all our security and salvation.³⁹

# Hauptstück VI

### *Vom ewigen Priesterthumb*

*On the Eternal Priesthood*

— ❦ —

## § 1. Christ our Eternal High Priest

**Is Christ our eternal High Priest?**

**Yes.** Ps. 110: *Thou art a priest for ever, after the order of Melchizedek.* And Heb. 7: *He hath an unchangeable priesthood.*

## § 2. The fourfold function of Christ's High Priesthood

**What does Christ discharge in his High-Priestly office?** *Four principal functions:*

**1.** He brings forth and teaches *the mystery of the Gospel* — knowledge that no other creature previously possessed. Only the Son who *dwelt in the Father's bosom* has declared him (John 1).

**2.** He intercedes for the believers before the heavenly Father, securing eternal grace (Isa. 53; John 19; Rom. 8).

**3.** He offered himself once and for all in a single sacrifice, achieving eternal redemption (John 17; Heb. 9–10).

**4.** He calls his own through the preached Word, sanctifies them, and makes them partakers of the redemption through faith (2 Cor. 5; Rom. 1, 10).

**Why can Christ alone be our High Priest?**

Because he alone was without sin, and thus needed no sacrifice for himself — he could offer only for the sins of others (Heb. 4:15; 7:26–27).

## § 3. Seven differences between the Aaronic and the Christian priesthood

**What differences are there between the priesthood of the Old and the New Testament?** *Seven points:*

**1.** **Establishment.** Aaron's priesthood was established *without an oath*; Christ's, *with an oath* (Ps. 110: *The LORD hath sworn, and will not repent*).

**2.** **Person.** The Old Testament high priest was merely human, and sinful; Christ is both God and Man, without sin.

**3.** **Doctrine.** Old Testament priests served the Law; Christ has revealed the Gospel.

**4.** **Duration.** Old Testament priests made temporary intercessions; Christ is an eternal High Priest.

**5.** **Sacrifice.** The blood of bulls and goats could not cleanse consciences (Heb. 10); Christ's own blood has purchased eternal redemption.

**6.** **Object of Sacrifice.** Old Testament priests offered for their own sins as well as for the people's; Christ offered only for others (Heb. 7:27).

**7.** **Succession.** The Old Testament had many priests succeeding one another; the New Testament has *only one* eternal High Priest — Jesus Christ — who needs no successor.⁴⁰

## § 4. Sacrifices in the New Testament

**What sacrifices, then, are there in the New Testament?**

**One propitiatory sacrifice** — Christ's, which needs no repetition. To claim that Christ must be daily re-sacrificed (as the Papists teach in the Mass) is blasphemy.

The remaining Christian sacrifices are **spiritual** — works commanded by God performed in faith as thanksgiving. Rom. 12: *Present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God.* Ps. 50, 51: *The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit.*

## § 5. The priesthood of all believers

**Are all Christians spiritual priests?**

**Yes.** 1 Pet. 2: *Ye are a royal priesthood.* Rev. 1:5: *He hath made us kings and priests unto God.*

Consequently, all believers may spread the doctrine of the Gospel, pray for themselves and for others, and give thanks to God (Matt. 7; Gal. 1; 1 Cor. 14).

## § 6. Against the papal claim to Christ's priesthood

**Is the Pope a High Priest, then?**

**No.** Scripture nowhere gives him the testimony it gives to Christ. He cannot possess Christ's attributes — being without sin or serving as sole Mediator between God and men — and to claim such is *eine Gottslästerung*, a blasphemy.⁴¹

## § 7. Use of the doctrine

**What is the use of this doctrine of Christ's High-Priesthood?**

That in every trial we may fly to him alone as our Intercessor; that we may know our sins are covered by his once-for-all offering; that we may reject every priesthood set up in competition with his; and that as members of his royal priesthood, we may live in worship and mutual service, being kings and priests unto God (Rev. 5:10).

# Hauptstück VII

### *Vom Heiligen Geist*

*On the Holy Spirit*

— ❦ —

## § 1. What the Holy Spirit is

**What, then, is the Holy Spirit?**

The Holy Spirit is the third Person of the Godhead, proceeding from the Father and the Son, who is sent through the preached Word and the Sacraments to kindle true conversion and faith in hearts, and to make them alive and renewed.

Christ himself says, John 14: *The Father shall send you another Comforter, the Spirit of truth, which proceedeth from the Father, who shall bear witness of me.*

## § 2. Proof that the Holy Spirit is a distinct Person, not a created force

**Prove to me that the Holy Spirit is a self-subsistent Person, and not a created motion.**

**1.** Scripture attributes the work of *creation* to the Holy Spirit (Ps. 33; Gen. 1: *the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters*). Therefore the Spirit cannot be a mere created force but must be the Creator himself.

**2.** Christ commands baptism *in the name of* the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit (Matt. 28) — thus giving to the Spirit's name equal divine standing.

**3.** At Christ's baptism the Spirit descends in the form of a dove (Matt. 3; John 1) — as one distinctly manifested, not as a mere emanation.

**4.** The Lord calls him a *Comforter* (John 14:15–26) — a personal designation, not a description of a force.

**5.** God promises, Joel 2 and Isa. 59: *I will pour out my Spirit upon you* — the Spirit shares God's essential nature. Therefore the Holy Spirit is *wahrer ewiger Gott*, true eternal God.

## § 3. The chief works of the Holy Spirit

**Which are the chief works and benefits of the Holy Spirit?**

**1.** He gathers the Church, or Christendom, through Word and Sacraments (John 16).

**2.** He regenerates the human being, kindling in the heart true faith in Christ and new obedience (John 3; Titus 3: *the washing of regeneration, and renewing of the Holy Ghost*).

**3.** He comforts troubled hearts through faith.

**4.** He governs believing Christians in their vocations and daily lives (Rom. 8; Eph. 3; Ps. 51; Ps. 143).

## § 4. How the Holy Spirit is given

**Through what means is the Holy Spirit given?**

Through the **Word, the preaching-office, and the Sacraments** (*Wort, Predigtamt, und Sacramenta*). God has ordained this ordinary means and no other. Whoever despises the preached Word cannot receive the Spirit; the *Schwärmer* err who seek the Spirit apart from the external Word.⁴²

## § 5. How Christians recognise that they have the Holy Spirit

**How do Christians recognise that they possess the Holy Spirit?**

**Three marks:**

**1.** **A heartfelt, fervent love and desire for Christ**, his Word, and his Sacraments — a movement which does not come from human nature but from the Spirit himself.

**2.** **The Spirit's internal testimony** — for the Spirit bears witness with our spirit that we are the children of God, and heirs (Rom. 8:16).

**3.** **True prayer** — for God sends the Spirit of his Son into the hearts of believers, causing them to cry *Abba, Father* (Gal. 4:6).

## § 6. The Spirit's help in weakness

**How does the Spirit help us in our weakness?**

We receive not a spirit of bondage to fear, but *the Spirit of adoption*, by which we cry *Abba, Father* (Rom. 8:15). In tribulation and suffering, the Spirit intercedes for us *with inexpressible sighing* (Rom. 8:26), and produces in us a progression: *tribulation worketh patience, patience experience, and experience hope* (Rom. 5:3–4).

## § 7. Marks that we belong to Christ

**How do I know that I belong to Christ?**

If ye live after the flesh, ye must die (Rom. 8:13). Those who *belong to Christ* have crucified the flesh with its affections and lusts (Gal. 5:24). The fruit of the Spirit is *love, joy, peace, longsuffering, kindness, goodness, faith, meekness, temperance* (Gal. 5:22–23). Where these are, there is no doubt the Holy Spirit dwells in the heart.

## § 8. Use of the doctrine

**What is the use of this doctrine?**

That we may pray earnestly for the Spirit, use faithfully the means by which he is given (Word and Sacraments), and expect from him regeneration, comfort, guidance, and preservation to eternal life.

# Hauptstück VIII

### *Von der Schöpffung*

*On Creation (and Divine Providence)*

— ❦ —

## § 1. By whom the world was created

**By whom was the world created?**

By the **Holy Trinity**, one and undivided in operation. Ps. 33: *By the Word of the LORD were the heavens made.* All three Persons — Father, Word, and Spirit — act as a single Maker without distinction, according to the theological principle **Opera Trinitatis ad extra sunt indivisa** — the works of the Trinity toward the outside are undivided.⁴³

## § 2. Creation *ex nihilo*

**Out of what were heaven and earth created?**

**Out of nothing** (*ex nihilo*). All things were made through the Word, but the Word itself was not made — the Word exists eternally with God *in the beginning* (John 1:1–3).

## § 3. The Six Days of Creation

**In how many days did God create the world?**

**In six days**, according to the account of Moses in Genesis:

- **Days 1–2:** Heaven, earth, light, and the firmament.
- **Day 3:** Dry land and vegetation.
- **Day 4:** Sun, moon, and stars.
- **Day 5:** Fish and birds.
- **Day 6:** Land animals and man.

## § 4. Why God created the world; and on his rest

**Why did God create all things?**

For his own glory, and for the benefit of his rational creatures.

**Did God rest from creation?**

Only from bringing new species into being; he continues to sustain and govern all things. John 5: *My Father worketh hitherto, and I work.*

## § 5. Creation before the Fall — corrupted through sin

**In what state was the world before the Fall?**

Very good, in all its parts (Gen. 1:31). Because humanity turned away from God's path, the world itself became corrupted.

## § 6. Divine sovereignty and freedom

**Does God rule the world freely and independently, or bound by creation?**

Although God created and ordered all things through his Word, he remains **unbound by creation**. He works freely within it, holding all things in his hands (Ps. 115; Ps. 135). Both the miracles of the Old and New Testaments and the ordinary course of nature testify that God acts *voluntarily*, and is not constrained by any creature.

## § 7. The world will not endure forever

**Will the world remain eternally as it is?**

**No.** Ps. 102: *The earth was founded by thee, and the heavens are the work of thy hands; they shall perish, but thou shalt endure.* The world will *perish through fire* (2 Pet. 3), when Christ comes for the Last Judgement (Luke 18; Matt. 24). Whence the world grows worse and worse (*wird jimmerdar ärger*); pray therefore that we may be preserved in faith in Christ.

## § 8. Divine Providence — that God cares for all creatures

**Does God care for all creatures?**

**Yes.** For it would be impossible for creatures to exist even a moment without God's love and care.

**By what means does God govern the world?** *Four modes:*

**1.** **Direct divine action** — God alone sustains creation through his power, without the co-work of any creature.

**2.** **Angelic mediation** — God commands angels to guide and protect his creatures externally.

**3.** **Internal sustenance** — God alone provides interior support and help.

**4.** **Special protection of humans** — angels particularly guard *die Menschen*, humanity.

## § 9. The four regimens of divine governance

**Through what four *regimens* does God govern all things?**

**1.** **Spiritual realm:** angels combat evil and assist humanity.

**2.** **Ecclesiastical realm:** God governs through apostles, preachers, and the ministry of the Word — the *verbum externum* alongside the interior work of the Spirit.

**3.** **Domestic realm:** household governance and parental authority over children (this is the seed-plot of the fourth Commandment; on which more in the treatise on the Law).

**4.** **Political realm:** civil government and the natural order — the magistracy which God has ordained for the *outward peace* of humanity (Rom. 13).⁴⁴

## § 10. Three grounds of God's providence

**How is God's care for creation known?** *Three grounds:*

**1.** From **the ordinary course of nature** and its natural works — the seasons, the fertility of the earth, the breath in creatures.

**2.** From **God's miraculous works** in Old and New Testaments.

**3.** From **God's unfailing Word**, in which he plainly declares that he cares for every creature (Acts 17:24–28; Rom. 14; Ps. 104; 1 Tim. 4).

## § 11. God's special care for the Church

**Does God care especially for the Church?**

**Yes.** Ps. 145, 147: *The LORD is nigh unto all them that call upon him.* Ps. 128: *Blessed is every one that feareth the LORD; thou shalt eat the labour of thy hands.* And 1 Tim. 4:10: *the living God, who is the Saviour of all men, especially of those that believe.* Though he cares generally for all creatures, he demonstrates special concern for his Church on earth and for the believers.

## § 12. The uses of the doctrine of Providence — against Epicurus, the Stoics, and the Peripatetics

**What practical use has this doctrine?**

**1.** That we learn to recognise God's wondrous wisdom and incomprehensible governance in the ordering of all things (Ps. 30, 33, 40, 103; Isa. 49), and give thanks.

**2.** That we know the preservation and maintenance of Christendom on earth is God's own work — for without his intervention against the evil spirits, the Church could not continue for a moment.

**3.** That we learn also *the removal and setting-up of worldly government* to be God's own work (Dan. 2 and 4: *He removeth kings, and setteth up kings*).

**4.** That we may take comfort in his omniscience — for *all the hairs of our head are numbered* (Matt. 10:30).

**5.** *Against three opposing schools:*

- **The Epicureans**, who deny that God cares for creation.
- **The Stoics**, who bind God to the creatures, so that he cannot act freely.
- **The Peripatetics** *(and other philosophic materialists)*, who allow that God governs only in the heavens, but not in the earth.

Against all three we hold, with Scripture, that God's care extends to every creature, however small — for it is human reason, not divine truth, that grades his providence into what he does and does not attend to (Ps. 145).

# Hauptstück IX

### *Von Engeln Gottes*

*On the Angels of God*

— ❦ —

## § 1. What Angels are

**What is an Angel?**

The word *Angel* signifies **a messenger**, and is a *title of office*.⁴⁵ God's Angels are *messengers* who protect and guard against every evil, and see God's commands executed without harm coming to us. Scripture also calls them **Spirits**, this name referring to their natural essence (Ps. 104: *Who maketh his angels spirits*; Heb. 1:14: *serving spirits, sent forth to minister for them who shall be heirs of salvation*).

## § 2. Angels' nature; two kinds

**What is the nature of the Angels?**

They are made *in God's image, but not of God's essence or divine nature* — thus they are distinguished from the Deity itself. They exist in **two kinds — good and evil**, against the Sadducees and Epicureans who deny angels altogether (Acts 23:8).

**Are there more good Angels than evil?**

*Far more good* than evil, all awaiting divine command to protect and preserve the faithful (Dan. 7:10: *thousand thousands ministered unto him, and ten thousand times ten thousand stood before him*; Ps. 91:11).

## § 3. Properties of the good Angels

**What properties have the beloved Angels?**

**1.** They are *reasoning and wise, much more so than evil angels* — because they have a mirror wherein to look, namely *facies Patris*, the countenance of our LORD God. This the Devil does not have. Whence one good angel is much wiser than all the devils together.

**2.** They are also *mightier* than the devils — for the devils tremble before him whose name is *Omnipotens*.

Whence let us diligently thank God, that he has ordained such **Guardians and Watchmen** for us, who wait upon us, and do so heartily gladly.

## § 4. The principal office of the Angels

**What then is the principal office of the beloved Angels?**

God through the good Angels protects and preserves the Church, the worldly government, and household regiments (Ps. 34, 91; Dan. 10; Gen. 16, 24, 28, 32). God could well preserve us against the Devil and every misery *without* the Angels' service — as he could well create men without father and mother, and could govern the world directly — but he wills to work through them, that we may recognise his goodness and give thanks.

Angels particularly draw to those *who fear God and practise repentance* (Ps. 33; Luke 14 — the rejoicing over one sinner that repenteth), and are present at the deathbed of the pious.

## § 5. Whether Angels are to be worshipped

**Should Angels be worshipped?**

**No.** Scripture nowhere commands it. Gratitude for the Angels should be directed *to God, who created them as helpers* — not to the Angels themselves. To make them objects of worship is idolatry.⁴⁶

## § 6. Why the Angels sometimes seem hidden

**Why do the Angels sometimes appear to work in mysterious or dangerous ways, when they surround us?**

God permits these seeming irregularities to test our *faith, patience, hope, and prayer*, and to preserve us from complacency (Ps. 77). Though suffering may be unavoidable, *God's hand can resolve all difficulties*. When misfortunes occur in the world or in households, we should not quickly lose heart, but *await the LORD's hand and help, which has no limit* — and say: *God still liveth, and the dear little Angels are still at hand.*

# Hauptstück X

### *Von bösen Engeln*

*On the Evil Angels (Devils)*

— ❦ —

## § 1. What Evil Angels are

**What are Evil Angels?**

They are called **Teufel** (Devils) — the word signifies *Lästerer*, "blasphemers," because they *corrupt and pervert all things to the utmost*.

## § 2. Their organisation

**Have the Evil Angels their own hierarchy?**

**Yes.** The Devil is a *gewaltiger Herr*, a mighty lord, commanding *grosse mächtige Fürsten*, great powerful princes — as Luke 14 shows.

## § 3. Chief characteristics of the Evil Angels — six points

**Which are their chief characteristics?**

**1.** *Mighty spirits*, compared in Scripture to *roaring lions* (1 Pet. 5:8).

**2.** *Tausendkünstler* — masters of a thousand arts, particularly skilled in spiritual and governmental deceptions.

**3.** *Lügner und Mörder* — liars and murderers (John 8:44), active in all institutions — churches, schools, secular government.

**4.** *Neidische Geister* — envious spirits, who begrudge every good fortune of others, especially in spiritual matters.

**5.** *Traurige Geister* — melancholy spirits, who cannot tolerate human joy, especially joy in God (associated by the common folk with *Poltergeister*).

**6.** *Corrupting spirits* — who deceive people into accepting lies as truth, exemplified by the *Schwärmern* (the radical sects) who take falsehood for revelation (Gal. 3:1; 1 Tim. 4:1).

## § 4. Their assault on Christian faith and life

**Do the Evil Angels attack Christian faith and life?**

**Yes.** They frighten the weak, but *strengthen the secure* in their false security. Christians therefore require *spiritual weapons* to resist demonic opposition.

## § 5. The spiritual armour — six pieces, per Ephesians 6

**What are the spiritual weapons against the Evil Angels?** (Eph. 6):

**1.** *Loins girded with truth* (**Wahrheit**).

**2.** *Breastplate of righteousness* (**Krebs der Gerechtigkeit**).

**3.** *Feet shod with the Gospel of peace*.

**4.** *Shield of faith in Christ* — with which to quench all the fiery darts of the wicked one.

**5.** *Helmet of salvation* — namely, *hope in eternal life*.

**6.** *Sword of the Spirit* (**Schwert des Geistes**) — which is **God's Word** (Matt. 4 shows how our LORD Christ used it against the Devil).

## § 6. Satan has no power over God's servants

**Has Satan power over Christians?**

*Der Sathan hat keine Macht über die, so Gottes Diener sind* — *Satan has no power over those who are servants of God*. God permits trials only for the believer's benefit, not for their destruction.

## § 7. Why God permits demonic trial

**Why does God allow the Devil to afflict Christians?**

He permits it — *not that the Devil should devour us, but that we be tested and refined* (1 Pet. 1:6–7). Such trial demonstrates God's power and love, and drives us to prayer.

## § 8. Against Papist exorcism

**A note on the papal exorcisms:** the Devil sometimes appears to depart from possessed persons *not* to serve the Gospel, but *to strengthen heretical beliefs and papal superstitions*. St. Paul warns of *false miracles and deceptive signs* (2 Thess. 2:9); the alleged demoniac miracles at the shrines of the saints (e.g., St. Cyprian's) confirm the pattern.

## § 9. Whether the Devils shall ever be saved

**Will the Devils ultimately become blessed, as some have taught?**

**No.** Matt. 25:41 explicitly states that *hellish fire is eternal*, and evil spirits along with damned humans shall be eternally tormented therein.

## § 10. Where the Evil Spirits dwell in this age

**Where do the Evil Spirits dwell?**

**1.** In *the children of unbelief* (Eph. 2:2).

**2.** In *the unrepentant and stubborn sinners* (Luke 15).

**3.** In *those who sin against conscience* (Gal. 5; John 3).

## § 11. Christian consolations against the Evil Spirits

**What consolations have Christians against the Evil Spirits?**

**1.** Christ has overcome *the prince of this world* (John 16:11).

**2.** God holds us in his protective custody.

**3.** We combat deceptive spirits with **God's Word** (Matt. 4).

**4.** *We are protected by the good Angels* (Ps. 34:7).

# Hauptstück XI

### *Vom Menschen vor dem Fall*

*On Man before the Fall*

— ❦ —

## § 1. What Man is

**What is Man?**

Man is **God's creature**, fashioned in *God's image without sin*, given rule over all creatures, and exempt from death.

## § 2. Through whom created

**Through whom was Man created?**

**By God himself, with deliberation and care** (Gen. 1:26 — *Let us make man in our image, after our likeness*). This represents *eine grosse Ehre* — a great honour — to human nature, that God should so speak of him in the plural of Persons, and that the whole Trinity should take counsel in his making.

## § 3. Out of what created

**Out of what was Man created?**

The **body** was created *from earth that had been dug up and thrown about, like plowed land* — Hebrew *Aphar* (dust). The **soul** was breathed into the body *through God's breath* (Gen. 2:7).

**And the woman?**

From the **rib of Adam** (Gen. 2:21–22).

## § 4. What the Image of God is

**What is the Image of God in Man?**

Per St. Paul (Eph. 4; Col. 3), it is *righteousness and holiness in the soul and in all the powers of Man*. Humans were originally created in wisdom, virtue, and love, *without all evil inclinations, just as God was* — Adam possessed an *uprightness* natural to both body and soul. Had Adam remained in this state, he would have produced offspring similarly free from evil inclinations.

## § 5. The seven attributes of Man in his original state

**Which were the attributes of Man as created in God's image?** *Seven points:*

**1.** *Man was without sin.*

**2.** *Full of gladness*, free from fear of death.

**3.** *Wisdom and purity* — the knowledge of God and his righteousness.

**4.** *Physical perfection* — the members of the body of *more beautiful and magnificent powers* than we now see.

**5.** *Knowledge of all creatures.*

**6.** *Dominion over all creation.*

**7.** *Eternal life* — the greatest gift bestowed.

## § 6. Whether the Image of God is Man's essence or a gift

**Is the Image of God Man's essential substance, or a gift?**

**A gift** — for Moses (Gen. 1–2) shows the body itself was formed from earth (thus not divine in essence), and the Apostle (Eph. 4; Col. 3) locates the Image in *righteousness and holiness* (which are proper qualities of the soul, not the substance itself).

## § 7. Man's purpose

**For what was Man principally created?**

To *know, honour, praise, and serve* God. The Sabbath was established *not for animals but for the sake of humanity* — and human wisdom after the Fall understands nothing of this, but reckons it as *Torheit* (foolishness).

# Hauptstück XII

### *Vom Fall Adams und den verderbten Kräften des Menschen*

*On the Fall of Adam and the Corrupted Powers of Man*

— ❦ —

## § 1. The beginning of Adam's sin

**What caused Adam's fall?**

*Der Anfang der Sünde Adams ist erreget von der Anfechtung des Teufels* — the beginning of Adam's sin was stirred by the Devil's temptation, through which the Devil drew Eve away from God's Word (Gen. 3).

## § 2. Why God gave the Commandment about the Tree

**Why did God give Adam the commandment concerning the Tree of Knowledge of Good and Evil?**

This commandment served as a **sign** that humans had *a superior Being* — namely God — and could demonstrate obedience. Adam in his sinless state was fully capable of fulfilling God's requirement.

**What if Adam had remained sinless?**

Had Adam remained sinless, he would have laboured in Paradise *without hardship*, exercised stewardship over the animals, and cultivated the earth *without toil or suffering*. Women and children would have carried burdens *without fear or lamentation*, and on the seventh day all would have been quiet and rested.

## § 3. Why the Fall of Adam is so emphasised

**Why does Scripture emphasise Adam's fall so heavily?**

Because Adam *sinned against God's Word* and — through this one act — *against all ten Commandments*, by allowing himself to be seduced by the Devil. Adam and his descendants were thereby cast into *sin, death, and subjection to the Devil's tyranny* — ultimately valuing the apple more than God himself, even desiring to *be like God* (Gen. 3:5).

## § 4. Seven consequences of the Fall

**Which are the consequences of the Fall?** *Seven points:*

**1.** **Awareness of nakedness** (Gen. 3:7).

**2.** **Mental and spiritual corruption** — their *heart, will, and entire reason are terribly shaken and corrupted*.

**3.** **Flight from God** — they now *fear God as an enemy*.

**4.** **Women's suffering** — pain in childbirth (Gen. 3:16).

**5.** **Earth's curse** — *thorns and thistles instead of fruit* (Gen. 3:17–18).

**6.** **Expulsion from Paradise** — condemned to struggle with hunger and hardship.

**7.** **Death and inherited sin** — all descendants subject to death, *conceived and born in sin*.

## § 5. How Adam and Eve were restored

**How were Adam and Eve restored to righteousness?**

Through the **Word of Promise** given in Gen. 3:15 — *the Seed of the Woman shall bruise the serpent's head* — the *protoevangelium*, the first promise of the Gospel and of the Christ to come.⁴⁷

## § 6. On Free Will after the Fall

**What can free will accomplish in spiritual matters?**

**Nothing at all — of and from itself.** For the will after the Fall is the Devil's prisoner (2 Tim. 2:26); wherefore *Augustine* against Julian (lib. 2) turns the phrase *"free will"* upon itself and calls it *servum arbitrium* — a *bound* or *enslaved* will.⁴⁸

## § 7. Why Man's powers avail nothing spiritually after the Fall

**Why do Man's powers now avail nothing in spiritual matters?**

**First**, the free will after the Fall of Adam is only *an empty name* — and when it does what is in it, *it sins mortally* (Augustine, *De Spiritu et Littera* 4: *"Free will without God's grace serves only to sin"*).

**Second**, Man is *a corrupted mirror* who no longer knows or understands God nor his gracious will.

**Third**, Man is a *prisoner of sin and the Devil* until converted (Eph. 2:2; 2 Tim. 2).

**Fourth**, "in Man there is nothing good on account of sin" (Ps. 14:3; Rom. 3:12).

**Fifth**, all are *conceived in sins and born* (Ps. 51:5).

**Sixth**, all are *flesh*, living fleshly lives in thought, speech, and action until reborn (John 3).

**Seventh**, no human can boast before God on his own powers.

**Eighth**, all are therefore *guilty before God's judgement* (Rom. 3:19).

## § 8. Free will retained in outward things

**Does Man retain free will in outward or worldly matters?**

**Yes** — in *Kühe, Pferde, Geld, Gut* (cattle, horses, money, possessions) and temporal concerns Man retains a real dominion, though this capacity too is weakened.

## § 9. That conversion is entirely God's work

**What is the "stone heart" of which Scripture speaks?**

After the Fall, natural human powers have no *Geschicklichkeit* (skill) or *Tüchtigkeit* (capability) in divine matters. Human conversion is *ein pur Gnadenwerk Gottes* — purely a work of God's grace, accomplished through the divine operation of the Holy Spirit alone. **God promises**: *"I will give you a new heart and a new spirit"* (Ezek. 36:26).

Even *Augustine* on Predestination (cap. 3) at first believed *"accepting or receiving [grace] stood within our power,"* but later acknowledged his error. Accepting God's promise and giving assent to it is exclusively *God's or the Holy Spirit's gift and work*.

**How is Man converted to God?**

Conversion occurs through God's provision of **Word and Sacraments** — the preaching of the Law breaks hardened hearts (Jer. 23:29: *my Word is like a hammer that breaks the rock*), the Gospel restores the broken heart (Rom. 1:16: *the Gospel is a power of God unto salvation*). *"Convert thou me, and I shall be converted"* (Jer. 31:18) is the prophet's own prayer, confessing that all spiritual transformation begins with God.

## § 10. Whether conversion is coercion

**Is human conversion to God a *Coactio* or force?**

**No.** God does not coerce; he *changes* the resistant heart. He converts an *unwilling* heart into a *willing* heart (Ps. 110:3: *thy people shall be willing in the day of thy power*; Rom. 7). What was compulsion in the natural man becomes freedom in the reborn.

## § 11. Whether the reborn will can cooperate with grace

**Can the renewed or reborn will cooperate with divine grace?**

Before the Fall, Man possessed this capacity; after the Fall, it was **entirely lost** (*gar verloren*). The will cannot direct itself toward God's grace unless the person is first **born anew**. Paul (Rom. 7) describes the internal conflict between *the law of the mind* and *the law of sin* which remains even in the reborn — for the reborn experience continuing weakness rather than perfection in this life. Continuous spiritual growth is expected: *the converted shall grow from day to day* (2 Cor. 6:1 — *receive not the grace of God in vain*).

## § 12. Whether Luther's "purely passive" means without the Word

**Luther says humans act *purè passivè* (purely passively) in their own conversion. Does that mean they convert without the preaching of God's Word?**

**No.** Luther's meaning is not that humans convert apart from the preached Word. Rather, Luther means: *daß der Mensch gantz und gar nichts zu seiner Bekehrung wircke* — that humans contribute nothing to their conversion by their own natural powers — but merely *leyde, daß Gott in ihm wircket* — *suffer that God works within them* — through the ordinary means (the Word). Chrysostom and Basil taught the same, when rightly understood.

**Objection.** *"Return unto me,"* God says (Gen. 4; Jer. 3) — surely this proves Man can turn *of himself* by free will?

**Answer.** These sayings show what Man *ought to do*, not what he *is able to accomplish* by his own powers. The Sophists err who take biblical imperatives as proofs of unaided ability. God commands what he then himself works in us (Phil. 2:13: *for it is God which worketh in you both to will and to do of his good pleasure*).

**Another Objection.** Free will can *accept* the innate grace; must not God first *"lay the stone"*?

**Answer.** Rom. 10:14 — *how shall they hear without a preacher?* God alone must *lay the first stone and begin in thee*; without him you find only sin.

## § 13. Use of the doctrine

**What use has this doctrine of Man's spiritual inability?**

**1.** That we may be **humbled**, learning through this teaching to recognise God's grace rightly.

**2.** That we may learn faith is *ein Zuversicht dessen, das man nicht sihet* — *confidence in what one does not see* (Heb. 11:1) — a gift and grace from God rather than a work of reason.

# Hauptstück XIII

### *Von der Sünde in Gemein*

*On Sin in General*

— ❦ —

## § 1. What Sin is

**What is Sin?**

Sin is *ein Mangel, böse Zuneigung, böses Werk oder That* — **a deficiency, an evil inclination, an evil work or deed** — which violates God's Law, angers God, and deserves temporal and eternal punishment unless forgiven through Christ.

- **Sin as deficiency**: Rom. 3:23 (*all have sinned and come short of the glory of God*).
- **Sin as known through the Law**: Rom. 7:7 (*I had not known sin, but by the Law*).
- **Sin as works of the flesh**: Gal. 5:19 ff.

Sin includes both **internal** (evil inclinations, lusts, desires — *böse Neigungen, Lüsten und Begierden*) and **external** (evil works). Both are prohibited by the Ten Commandments.

## § 2. Origin of Sin

**Whence comes Sin? Does it originate from God?**

**No.** God is not the author of sin. He created humanity *ohne Sünd in Gerechtigkeit* — *without sin, in righteousness*. Humans were **seduced by the Devil** and fell away from God and his Word (John 8:44: *the devil is a murderer and liar from the beginning*). Human nature has since been corrupted by sin, the heart now inclined toward evil.

## § 3. God's wrath against Sin

**How does God regard sin?**

*Rom. 1:18*: *the wrath of God is revealed from heaven against all ungodliness and unrighteousness of men.* Sin is *das Unrecht* — *unrighteousness itself* (1 John 3:4). *Rom. 6:23*: *Der Tod ist der Sünden Sold* — *the wages of sin is death*.

## § 4. The fruits of Sin — five points

**What are the fruits of sin?**

**1.** **Blindness of understanding** — our senses are darkened, that we no longer see or know God (Eph. 4:17–18).

**2.** **God's righteous wrath**, and *fear and trembling of the conscience* (Eph. 2:3; Ps. 6; Ps. 38; Job 5; Wisd. 16).

**3.** **Error and delusion** in all our doings and leavings, in spiritual and worldly matters (Rom. 1:21: *they became vain in their imaginations, and their foolish heart was darkened*).

**4.** **Temporal calamities** — every misery of this life traces back to sin.

**5.** **Eternal damnation** for those who die impenitent.

## § 5. Original Sin vs Actual Sin

**How is Sin divided?**

**Original Sin (*peccatum originale*)** — the corruption inherited from Adam, described at Ps. 51:5 (*conceived in sins and born*). This is not a mere absence of righteousness but a positive corruption of nature — the *fomes peccati*, the "tinder of sin," that inclines even the reborn toward evil.

**Actual Sin (*peccatum actuale*)** — every particular thought, word, or deed against God's Law that flows from the corrupt nature. Actual sin is divided into *internal* (evil desires, unbelief, arrogance) and *external* (blasphemy, cursing, swearing, murder, adultery, drunkenness, and every other work of the flesh — Matt. 15:19; Gal. 5:19–21). **Augustine's *Enchiridion*** identifies four causes of actual sin: *ignorance, weakness, pain or suffering, and lust*.⁴⁹

## § 6. Dominant vs Non-Dominant Sin

**What is the distinction between *herrschende* and *nicht herrschende Sünde* — dominant and non-dominant sin?**

**Dominant Sin** rules in the unregenerate — those in whom sin has free course, who consent to sin without repentance and remain in unbelief. Such sin *reigns* and issues in eternal death (Rom. 6:12–14).

**Non-Dominant Sin** remains in the reborn — the remnant of original corruption that Paul confesses in Rom. 7:14–25 (*"the good that I would I do not; but the evil which I would not, that I do"*). This sin **does not reign**, is fought against by the Spirit, is daily confessed and forgiven for Christ's sake, and does not exclude from grace.⁵⁰

## § 7. Whether sin against the Holy Spirit can be forgiven

**Can every sin be forgiven?**

**Every sin can be forgiven** *save* the sin against the Holy Spirit (Matt. 12:31–32: *whosoever speaketh a word against the Son of Man, it shall be forgiven him; but whosoever speaketh against the Holy Ghost, it shall not be forgiven him, neither in this world, neither in the world to come*). This sin is not the failing of the weak but the *deliberate, obstinate, knowing rejection of the truth of God once acknowledged* — the sin of the stubborn heart that no longer wills repentance. Since it will not repent, it cannot be forgiven.

## § 8. Use of the doctrine

**What use has this doctrine of sin?**

**1.** That we may know the greatness of our misery, and be driven to Christ the Physician.

**2.** That we may abhor sin and flee it.

**3.** That we may not despair — for **no sin is so great that Christ's blood does not cover it**, if we turn to him in faith.

# Hauptstück XIV

### *Vom Gesetze Gottes*

*On the Law of God*

— ❦ —

## § 1. What the Law of God is

**What is God's Law?**

*"Nichts anders, dann Gottes Wort und Gebot"* — **nothing other than God's Word and Commandment**, demanding *internal and external obedience* and threatening punishment and eternal damnation for disobedience (Deut. 27:26; Gal. 3:10: *cursed is every one that continueth not in all things which are written in the book of the Law to do them*).

## § 2. The Two Tables

**How is the Law of God divided?**

The Ten Commandments were written *by God himself* upon **two tables** (Exod. 20; Deut. 5):

- **The First Table** — three commandments addressing humanity's relationship *with God*: to have no other gods, not to take his name in vain, to remember the Sabbath.

- **The Second Table** — seven commandments governing *interpersonal conduct*: to honour father and mother, not to kill, not to commit adultery, not to steal, not to bear false witness, not to covet.

## § 3. Whether Man can fulfil the Law

**Can human nature fulfil the Law?**

**No.** *"So kann es menschliche Natur nicht recht hören, geschweige denn, daß sie es erfüllen sollte"* — *human nature cannot even rightly hear it, much less fulfil it* (Exod. 19–20; Deut. 5; Rom. 3:20; Pss. 130 and 143; Luke 18:19; Matt. 12; Matt. 18). Moses himself asserts that no person can completely observe the Law. Even believers retain sinfulness and cannot achieve complete fulfilment of the Law through works alone.

## § 4. The three uses of the Law

**For what purposes does God give the Law?** *Three uses, corresponding to the classical Lutheran triplex usus legis:*

**1.** **Pedagogical use** (*usus paedagogicus*) — that God's Law be made *universally known*, and Man never forget his obligation to God.

**2.** **Political use** (*usus politicus*, also called *usus civilis*) — the Law's role in outward discipline and civil order, *maintaining discipline among all estates* through the offices God has instituted: **paternal authority (*väterliche Obrigkeit*), the magistracy (*Obrigkeit*), and schoolmasters (*Schulmeister*)** — all instituted to uphold discipline and resist wickedness. *This is the seed-passage on Christian civil order in the whole Enchiridion.*⁵¹

**3.** **Didactic use** (*usus didacticus*, also called *tertius usus legis*) — the Law's role in reminding humans of human corruption, driving them toward *repentance* and *conversion*, and magnifying Christ's benefits (since only the sick recognise medicine's value — Matt. 9:12). Also, in believers, the Law directs the *new obedience* (§ 5 following).

## § 5. Christ's new obedience produced in believers

**What obedience does Christ produce in the faithful?**

Christ, through the Holy Spirit, produces in faithful hearts *einen neuen Gehorsam* — **a new obedience** — though this obedience remains **weak and incomplete** in this life. It is the fruit of faith, not its cause; it flows from the Spirit's regenerating work, not from unaided human effort. Rom. 8:4: *the righteousness of the Law is fulfilled in us, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit*.

## § 6. Three types of hearers of the Law

**Who hears the Law?** *Three sorts of hearers:*

**1.** Those who *hear yet despise* the Law — the impenitent.

**2.** Those who hear and are *terrified but do not turn to Christ* — the despairing.

**3.** Those who hear, are *convicted, driven to Christ, and receive both forgiveness and the Spirit's regenerating grace* — the true hearers.

## § 7. On the *Politia* — the seed of Christian political doctrine

**How ought a Christian to regard the political order (*Politia*)?**

The political realm is God's own institution (Rom. 13:1–7: *the powers that be are ordained of God*). In it the *Obrigkeit* — the civil magistracy — bears the sword *not in vain*, but as *God's minister for good*, restraining evil and rewarding good. The Christian owes to the magistracy honour, obedience, and prayer (1 Tim. 2:1–2), so far as the magistracy does not command against God's Word.

Just as the *domestic realm* is founded on the Fourth Commandment (*Honour thy father and thy mother*), so the *political realm* extends the same principle: **the honour due to parents is extended to the magistracy**, and both are God's ordinances for the outward peace of humanity. This is the Lutheran doctrine of the *two kingdoms* in its catechetical form: the church and the political order are both under God, each with its proper office, and the Christian lives in both without confusion or contradiction.⁵²

He signed the *Formula of Concord* 1577 and the *Book of Concord* 1580. Died at Heidelberg **12 October 1583**, thirty-six days after Kirchner signed the present dedication. On his death the regency passed to his younger brother Johann Casimir, who re-Reformed the Palatinate.

**⁸** The reference is to **Johann Eck** (1486–1543) and the papal bull **Exsurge Domine** (1520). Eck was one of the two theologians (with Aleander) who conveyed the bull from Rome to Germany and worked its enforcement. Kirchner's phrase "in Dr. Eck's writing sent from Rome in 1520" refers to Eck's role as papal executor rather than to any single treatise, but likely alludes to Eck's *Enchiridion locorum communium adversus Lutherum et alios hostes ecclesiae* (Landshut, 1525) — a work Kirchner would have known well, since his own *Enchiridion* stands consciously as a Lutheran counter-genre to Eck's Roman handbook.

**⁹** The **Schmalkaldic War** of 1546–1547 and its aftermath. The Schmalkaldic League of Lutheran princes was defeated by Charles V at the Battle of Mühlberg (24 April 1547); Wittenberg was besieged, the Elector John Frederick captured, and the imperial **Augsburg Interim** of 1548 imposed on the Protestant territories. Kirchner treats this as the *first* attempted extermination of Lutheranism — from which God preserved the movement — and rhymes it with Nehemiah's building under Persian and heathen opposition.

**¹⁰** Kirchner's Nehemiah typology is drawn cumulatively from **Nehemiah 4 and 6** — the mockery of Sanballat, Tobiah, and their allies; Nehemiah's response of prayer and stationed watch; and the physical detail of the builders working with one hand on the trowel and the other on the sword (Neh. 4:17–18). The passage is a locus of Reformation self-understanding across confessional lines but takes distinctively Lutheran shape in 16th-century preaching.

**¹¹** Kirchner names two enemies as making common cause on the *Person of Christ*: the **Jesuits** (specifically Robert Bellarmine's early work was appearing in the 1580s; Kirchner may be responding to it) and the **Sacramentarians** (his standing 1583 label for the Reformed, whom Lutherans held to deny the real bodily presence in the Supper because they deny the ubiquity of Christ's human nature). The claim that Jesuits and Sacramentarians "blow altogether in one horn" on the *Person of Christ* is Kirchner's polemical thesis: both, in his view, deny the *communicatio idiomatum* in the strong Lutheran sense and thus arrive at the same denial of the real presence in the Supper by different routes.

**¹²** The passage on daily use of the *Enchiridion* alongside the sermon — a Christian carrying the small book with him, comparing article-by-article — is Kirchner's own catechetical pedagogy. The physical format of the 1583 imprint (small octavo, roughly 9.6 × 15.9 cm, cited in the BSB physical description) is a *portable Enchiridion* in the literal sense: designed to fit in a pocket and travel to church.

**¹³** The **Concord-Work and *Apologia*** are the *Book of Concord* itself (1580) and Kirchner-Chemnitz-Selnecker's 1583 *Apology of the Book of Concord*. Ludwig VI's role: as Elector Palatine he was one of the four Electoral signatories to the Book of Concord in 1580 (with Saxony, Brandenburg, and Kur-Sachsen; Palatinate's signing was significant precisely because his father had been the leading Reformed Elector), and he politically underwrote the 1583 *Apology*.

**¹⁴** The **University of Heidelberg**, founded 1386, was under Ludwig VI purged of Reformed faculty (Ursinus, Zanchi, Tremellius had already gone) and staffed with Lutheran theologians. Kirchner is here one of the Lutheran professors; on Ludwig's death (October 1583) most of the Lutheran faculty were dismissed within months by Johann Casimir. This dedication is thus published just as the University to which Kirchner refers was about to be re-Reformed for the balance of the century.

**¹⁵** The single most important line of the entire *Enchiridion* for the Scholastic Lutherans corpus: Kirchner's explicit public binding to the **Book of Concord** as the pattern of the confession of the saving faith. This is the sentence that qualifies the *Enchiridion* — and its author — for treatment as a systematic-catechetical footprint of the confessional documents themselves. The parallel formula in the 1583 *Apologia* is at its opening: *"solidum ac genuinum verae doctrinae sensum tenere, uti in Concordiae Christianae Libro repetitum est."*

The formula *"credamus sanctis scripturis, quae nec falli nec fallere possunt"* is one of Augustine's most-quoted anti-Pelagian assertions of scriptural inerrancy; Kirchner cites it in slightly compressed form.

**²³** **Arius** (ca. 250 – ca. 336) — the Alexandrian presbyter whose denial of the eternal deity of the Son was condemned at the First Council of Nicaea (325). Kirchner's parenthetical remark — that God ends stubborn heresy by his own hand when the disputants cannot be persuaded by Scripture — alludes to the tradition of Arius's grotesque death in a Constantinople latrine (reported by Socrates *Historia ecclesiastica* 1.38 and by Athanasius *Ad episcopos Aegypti*), which was a favourite Reformation exemplum of divine judgement on obdurate error. Kirchner treats Arianism as a paradigm of the case where doctrinal dispute admits no negotiated end.

## From Hauptstück II

**²⁴** The extended definition of God — "spiritual, single Being, threefold in Persons, who has created … through his Son … through the Holy Spirit …" — is Kirchner's own compression of the *Symbolum Apostolicum* article by article, keyed to the Trinitarian Persons. The reference cluster **1 John 4; Matt. 28; Gen. 2; John 3, 15, 16; John 5; Matt. 25; 1 Cor. 15** is a proof-catena for each clause: 1 John 4 for the spiritual/single Being, Matt. 28 for Trinity, Gen. 2 for creation, John 3 & 15 & 16 for redemption through the Son, John 5 & Matt. 25 for the Last Day, 1 Cor. 15 for the Resurrection. Formally akin to Luther's Small Catechism explanation of the Creed but tighter.

**²⁵** The three heresies named — **Arius** (denial of the Son's eternal deity), **Macedonius** (denial of the Holy Spirit's deity — Pneumatomachian; condemned at Constantinople I 381), and **Sabellius** (modalism — the Three as mere aspects of one Person; condemned in the 3rd c.) — are the standing Reformation-era shorthand for the Trinitarian error-typology inherited from the ancient Church. Formula of Concord Solid Declaration VIII rehearses the same three when treating Christology (BSLK 1017 ff.). Kirchner's ordering — Arius, Macedonius, Sabellius — is chronological.

**²⁶** *Ex Patre Filioque* — Kirchner sides squarely with the Western reading of John 15:26 and the *filioque* clause of the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed as recited in Latin from the 6th c. onward. The Greek East never accepted the addition. Formula of Concord and *Book of Concord* recite the Nicene Creed with the *filioque*; Kirchner rehearses the Western position without polemical development against the East, since it was not his 1583 target.

## From Hauptstück III

**²⁷** *"substantialis imago, non artificialis aut facta vel creata"* — "essential image, not artificial nor made nor created." The formula is standard 4th-c. anti-Arian Christology (Athanasius, Basil, the Cappadocians on Heb. 1:3 *χαρακτὴρ τῆς ὑποστάσεως αὐτοῦ* — the *express image of his substance*). Kirchner's Latin form is the technical vocabulary of Reformation-era scholastic Christology: the Son as *imago substantialis* against the Arian *creatura* and against any Origenist gradation of divinity.

**²⁸** **Council of Ephesus (431)** — under **Cyril of Alexandria** condemned **Nestorius** of Constantinople for teaching that Mary was *Christotokos* (Christ-bearer) rather than *Θεοτόκος* (God-bearer). The Ephesine Definition affirms that the Virgin bore not merely the man Christ united to the Word but the Person of the Word made flesh, and is therefore rightly called God-bearer. See Denzinger-Hünermann §§ 250–268; the whole Formula of Concord Solid Declaration VIII depends materially on this Definition.

**²⁹** The **Chalcedonian Definition** (451) — one of the four principal Christological documents (Nicaea 325, Constantinople I 381, Ephesus 431, Chalcedon 451). Kirchner cites it *"apud Nicephorum lib. 15, cap. 6"* — that is, from **Nicephorus Callistus Xanthopoulos**'s *Historia ecclesiastica* (early 14th c., first printed at Basel 1553; the standard Renaissance edition), rather than from the *Acta* of Chalcedon directly. This is characteristic Reformation-era access to the Council: the *Acta* were less broadly available than the historians who quoted them. The four canonical adverbs *— inconfuse, immutabiliter, indivise, insegregabiliter (ἀσυγχύτως, ἀτρέπτως, ἀδιαιρέτως, ἀχωρίστως)* — are preserved untranslated in Kirchner's Latin; the English rendering above gives their standard equivalents *unconfusedly, unchangeably, undividedly, inseparably.* Denzinger-Hünermann §§ 300–303.

**³⁰** The *communicatio idiomatum* — the "communication of properties" between the two natures in the one Person of Christ — is here handled by Kirchner in the Formula-of-Concord scheme: the *genus idiomaticum* (attribution of natural properties of one nature to the whole Person: *God has purchased his congregation with his own blood*, Acts 20:28), the *genus apotelesmaticum* (attribution of the mediatorial works to both natures in one Person: the second point), and — critically — the *genus maiestaticum* (attribution of Divine majesty and properties to the assumed human nature: the third point). The Reformed rejection of the *genus maiestaticum* is what the entire *Enchiridion* is written against. Formula of Concord Solid Declaration VIII (BSLK 1017 ff.; Kolb-Wengert 617 ff.) is the confessional grammar Kirchner catechises.

**³¹** The "fiery iron" analogy — that the divine properties are communicated to the human nature as fire to iron heated red-hot, without either destroying the iron's nature or making the fire itself iron — is a Christological topos from **Origen** (*De principiis* II.6) taken up by the Cappadocians and by John of Damascus (*De fide orthodoxa* III.15), and became the standard Christological analogy in Lutheran scholasticism from Chemnitz's *De duabus naturis in Christo* (1571) onward. Kirchner acknowledges its imperfection — like every analogy — while defending its serviceableness against the charge of *Verwandlung* (Eutychian mixing).

**³²** **Cyril of Alexandria**, *In Iohannis Evangelium* IV.24 (PG 73:557 ff.) and elsewhere — the *sarx zoopoios* / *caro vivifica* doctrine: Christ's flesh as *life-giving* by virtue of its Personal union with the Word. Received into the Definition of the Council of Ephesus (431) as the ninth of the twelve Cyrilline Anathematisms (Denzinger-Hünermann § 262). Lutheran Christology from Chemnitz through the Formula relies on this Cyrilline material as the patristic warrant for the *genus maiestaticum*.

**³³** **The Agnoetae** — a 6th-c. Egyptian Monophysite sub-sect, followers of the deacon Themistius, who taught that Christ was truly ignorant of the day of judgement and other things according to his human nature (based on Mark 13:32). Condemned by **Eulogius of Alexandria** in 599 and by **Pope Gregory the Great** in the following year. **Nicephorus Callistus** *Historia ecclesiastica* 18.50 preserves the standing account. Kirchner's application — that Reformed theologians who admit ignorance in Christ's human nature come near the Agnoete position — is a polemical thrust against a Reformed Christology that would preserve the finitude of Christ's human nature by denying the *communicatio* of divine attributes.

**³⁴** **Augustine**, *Tractatus in Iohannis Evangelium* — the reference in Kirchner, *In Iohan. 14, De verbis Domini Serm. 58*, actually refers to a sermon *De verbis Domini* on John 14 rather than to the *Tractatus* proper; the passage as Kirchner quotes it circulated widely in Reformation-era florilegia. The critical patristic point — that the flesh of the Lord is worshiped *because united to the Deity*, so that not two but *one and the same* God-Man is confessed — is entirely Augustinian and is cited by the Formula of Concord (Solid Declaration VIII, on the invocation of the whole Christ). *Cyril, Ad Reginas de recta fide* is a 5th-c. treatise addressed to Theodosius II's imperial sisters, defending the Ephesine Christology.

**³⁵** The chain **Athanasius** *apud Theodoretum* — **Ambrose** on Heb. 1 — **Cyprian** — **Chrysostom** is the patristic *sedes* Kirchner marshals for the doctrine that Christ sits at the Right Hand of God *according to his human nature*. The **Athanasius** citation is not from any surviving genuine Athanasian work but from **Theodoret**'s *Eranistes* dialogue III (5th c., PG 83:277); such indirect access to Athanasius via Theodoret was standard in Reformation-era anti-Reformed Christology and derives especially from Chemnitz's *De duabus naturis* apparatus.

**³⁶** The single most delicate line in the whole *Enchiridion*: **Kirchner explicitly rejects the doctrine of ubiquity-of-substance in every creature** (that Christ's body is present *in stone, leaf, grass, sun, moon*), even while defending the multipresence of Christ's human nature per Personal union and exaltation. This is the crucial Lutheran distinction between **ubiquity** (in every creature by nature; a caricature imputed by Reformed polemics) and **multipresence** or **illocality** (present wherever Christ wills to be, per the exalted Personal union). Formula of Concord Solid Declaration VII carefully makes the same distinction (BSLK 1002; Kolb-Wengert 608); Kirchner is catechising it word for word.

## From Hauptstück IV

**³⁷** The list of exemplary redeemed sinners — **Adam and Eve, Manasseh, David, Peter, Mary Magdalene, Paul, the thief on the cross** — is a stock Reformation-era Trostbuch enumeration used to comfort tender consciences that no sin excludes them from grace if they turn in faith to Christ. An earlier draft of this endnote noted only "Adae vnd …" as the source's terminating fragment; direct hOCR-probing of cv 109 (2026-08-31) shows the list continues through Manasseh, David, Peter, Magdalene, Paul, and the thief before transitioning into the Baptism-and-Supper means-of-grace closing at cv 110. The full list has been reconstructed accordingly.

## From Hauptstück V (Vom Reich Christi)

**³⁸** The threefold Kingdom distinction (grace / power / glory — *regnum gratiae, potentiae, gloriae*) is Kirchner's inheritance from the mainline Lutheran scholastic tradition. Formalized by Chemnitz *De duabus naturis* (1571), the distinction became a standing locus in later Lutheran dogmatics (Gerhard *Loci* XVIII; Quenstedt *Systema* III.i). Preserve the Latin technical terms; render freely in the body.

**³⁹** The "practical use" closes the locus per Kirchner's standard catechetical rhythm: doctrine → controversy → *praxis pietatis*. This closing-with-use pattern is retained across all Hauptstück of the *Enchiridion*.

## From Hauptstück VI (Vom ewigen Priesterthumb)

**⁴⁰** The seven-point contrast between the Aaronic and Christian priesthoods is drawn cumulatively from **Hebrews 7–10**, and is the standard Reformation-era catechetical treatment. Kirchner's specific ordering (oath / person / doctrine / duration / sacrifice / object / succession) is characteristic of the Formula-of-Concord-era scholastic apparatus; parallel formulations appear in Chytraeus's *Catechesis* Loc. VII and in the Second Helvetic Confession XVIII.

**⁴¹** The rejection of the papal claim to Christ's High-Priesthood is one of the sharpest anti-papal moments in the whole *Enchiridion*: Kirchner names the papal claim *eine Gottslästerung* — a blasphemy. The Formula of Concord Solid Declaration X (BSLK 1053) and the Smalcald Articles II.iv (BSLK 483) authorize the identical language. The doctrine follows: Christ alone is the *unicus Mediator* of 1 Tim. 2:5, and the papal arrogation of intercessory power intrudes upon Christ's proper office.

## From Hauptstück VII (Vom Heiligen Geist)

**⁴²** *Verbum externum* — the "external Word." Kirchner's rejection of the *Schwärmer* here at Hauptstück VII § 4 rehearses the position first developed in Hauptstück I § 10 (which see, and endnote 20 on the *Schwärmer*). The doctrine is Formula of Concord Solid Declaration II — that the Holy Spirit is given *not* apart from but *through* the external means (Word and Sacraments). The seed-plot for this in Kirchner's own theological formation is Luther's *De servo arbitrio* (1525, WA 18:600–787).

## From Hauptstück VIII (Von der Schöpffung)

**⁴³** *Opera Trinitatis ad extra sunt indivisa* — "the works of the Trinity toward the outside are undivided." Standard scholastic principle from **Augustine** *De Trinitate* I.7–8 (PL 42:829–834) and formalized by the medieval schoolmen. Preserved through Lutheran scholasticism (Chemnitz, Gerhard, Quenstedt) as the ontological ground for the doctrine that all external divine operations — creation, redemption, sanctification, glorification — proceed from the one indivisible Trinity. The *appropriation* of creation to the Father, of redemption to the Son, of sanctification to the Spirit is a *personal appropriation*, not a division of the underlying divine act.

**⁴⁴** The "four regimens of divine governance" at Hauptstück VIII § 9 is the *seed-plot* of the Lutheran two-kingdoms teaching. Kirchner distinguishes: (1) *spiritual realm* (angels), (2) *ecclesiastical realm* (Word and ministry), (3) *domestic realm* (household and parental authority — the base of the Fourth Commandment), (4) *political realm* (civil government, magistracy, natural order — Rom. 13). The pairing of ecclesiastical + political corresponds directly to the *regnum Christi spirituale* and the *regnum politicum* distinguished by Luther *Von weltlicher Obrigkeit* (1523, WA 11:245–281) and by the Augsburg Confession XVI *De rebus civilibus*. The developed *Politia* / *Obrigkeit* treatment (with the office of magistracy, the Christian's civil duties, the two-kingdoms teaching) sits within the missing tail of the book — expected to unfold under the Ten Commandments locus (around cv 210–240) — and is highlighted here as the seed-passage from which that fuller treatment grows.

## From Hauptstück IX (Von Engeln Gottes)

**⁴⁵** Kirchner's opening definition of *Angel* as *office of messenger* (rather than a natural kind) is entirely standard for Reformation-era angelology, going back to Augustine *Enarr. in Ps.* 103.1.15 (*angelus enim officii nomen est, non naturae — quaeris nomen huius naturae? spiritus est; quaeris officium? angelus est*). Chemnitz *Loci Theologici* pt. 1 loc. 4 rehearses this exact distinction; Kirchner catechises it in Q&A form.

**⁴⁶** The rejection of angel-worship is Formula-of-Concord doctrine (SD IX and X, on the Descent of Christ and Church-usages); the Reformation universally condemned the medieval cult of angels, especially the popular veneration of guardian angels through liturgical propers on 2 October (Michaelmas etc.). Kirchner's terse *nein* here is a full-throated Lutheran refusal.

## From Hauptstück XII (Vom Fall Adams)

**⁴⁷** *Protoevangelium* — Gen. 3:15, *"I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel"* — the first evangelical promise, understood by the Reformation exegetes (with the Fathers before them) as the seed-form of the whole Gospel, promising the coming *Seed of the Woman* (Christ) who would defeat the serpent. Standard Reformation-era terminology; Luther's exposition in the 1535 *Genesis Lectures* (WA 42:145 ff.; LW 1:192 ff.) is the classical locus. Kirchner presupposes the reader will recognise the term; the English preserves the Latin.

## From Hauptstück XII continued (Free Will after the Fall)

**⁴⁸** *Servum arbitrium* — *"bound will"* or *"enslaved will."* Augustine's coinage against the Pelagian *liberum arbitrium* (*Contra Iulianum* II.viii.23; also *De Spiritu et Littera* 3–4). Luther adopted the term as the title of his 1525 *De servo arbitrio* against Erasmus (WA 18:600–787). Kirchner's citation of the Augustine-source directly (rather than of Luther's book by that title) is characteristic 1583 discipline — Kirchner grounds the Lutheran doctrine in the Church Father rather than in the immediate Reformer, in the pastoral service of readers who need to see the ancient consensus. Formula of Concord Solid Declaration II makes the identical move.

## From Hauptstück XIII (Von der Sünde in Gemein)

**⁴⁹** **Augustine, *Enchiridion ad Laurentium*** (ca. 421, PL 40:231–290), cap. 22 (*"peccati causa quattuor sunt: ignorantia, infirmitas, dolor, cupiditas"* — the four causes of sin are ignorance, weakness, pain, and lust). Kirchner cites Augustine's *Enchiridion* — the medieval-and-Reformation *manual* of Christian doctrine par excellence — as the classic Latin source for the fourfold analysis of actual sin's origin. That Kirchner's own *Enchiridion* thus stands in a self-conscious tradition of the *Enchiridion*-genre inaugurated by Augustine and passed through Erasmus (1503), Melanchthon (1521), and Eck (1525) is a subtle but real generic self-location.

**⁵⁰** The Dominant/Non-Dominant sin distinction is the **Rom. 6 / Rom. 7 tension** in classical Lutheran form. Formula of Concord Solid Declaration II–III develops the same distinction under the vocabulary *concupiscentia* (dominant in the unregenerate, non-dominant in the reborn). Kirchner catechises it in Q&A form. The pastoral point: *the reborn Christian who feels sin's continued struggle need not despair, for that struggle is itself the mark of the regenerate*.

## From Hauptstück XIV (Vom Gesetze Gottes)

**⁵¹** The **three uses of the Law** (*triplex usus legis*: *paedagogicus, politicus, didacticus*) is the standard Lutheran taxonomy formalized by Melanchthon in the *Loci Communes* (1535+ editions) and made confessional by Formula of Concord Article VI *De tertio usu legis* (BSLK 962 ff.; Kolb-Wengert 502 ff.). Kirchner's rehearsal here is entirely standard — but the specific enumeration at Hauptstück XIV § 4 sub 2 of the offices instituted for *usus politicus* — **paternal authority (*väterliche Obrigkeit*), the magistracy (*Obrigkeit*), and schoolmasters (*Schulmeister*)** — is the seed-passage of Kirchner's political-theology treatment. Every subsequent commandment's implication for civil life will unfold from this seed as the Decalogue commentary proceeds in the source past cv 235. Compare Chytraeus *Catechesis* (1594) Loc. III § 20 on the Fourth Commandment, which develops the *Politia* and *Magistratus* at length; Kirchner's parallel treatment must lie further in the missing tail of his book than this working translation has yet reached.

**⁵²** The seed of Lutheran **two-kingdoms doctrine** (*Zwei-Reiche-Lehre*) at Hauptstück XIV § 7 is drawn from Rom. 13:1–7 and 1 Tim. 2:1–2, and directly from Augsburg Confession XVI *De rebus civilibus* (BSLK 70; Kolb-Wengert 48). Luther's *Von weltlicher Obrigkeit, wie weit man ihr Gehorsam schuldig sei* (1523, WA 11:245–281) is the classical exposition; Melanchthon *Loci* on the Fourth Commandment develops the *civilis magistratus* in scholastic form. Kirchner's brief closing at § 7 sits in this tradition and prefigures the fuller treatment in the missing tail (Kirchner will unfold the *Politia* in detail when he reaches the individual Commandments and the office of the magistracy). The English "two-kingdoms" is a modern gloss; the 16th-c. term is more usually *das geistliche* and *das weltliche Regiment* — the spiritual and worldly *regiments* (recall Hauptstück VIII § 9 which anticipated this distinction under the four *regimens* of divine governance).

# Glossary of Terms

*Kirchner-specific additions to the Scholastic Lutherans master glossary. Standard entries (justificatio, communicatio idiomatum, etc.) are in the shared* `terms.yaml`*. Only terms introduced or given specific coloration by the 1583 Enchiridion are listed here.*

|  |  |  |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Enchiridion** | Handbook, little handbook | Preserve untranslated — the book's own title-genre. From Greek *ἐγχειρίδιον*, "in-the-hand," a portable handbook. Not equivalent to the modern "textbook"; carries the connotation of *portable* reference for the daily walk. Used by Kirchner throughout the Vorrede as the standing name for the present work. |
| **Sacramentierer** | Sacramentarians | Kirchner's standing 1583 term for the Reformed, on the grounds of their disagreement with Lutherans on the Lord's Supper. Not a term the Reformed used of themselves; a Lutheran polemical designation. Preserve the English "Sacramentarians" rather than modernising to "Reformed"; the term is the doctrinal claim, not a neutral confessional label. |
| **Schwärmer** | Enthusiasts (technical Lutheran sense) | Literal: "swarmers." Luther's collective term for the radical spiritualists — Karlstadt, Müntzer, the Zwickau prophets, later Schwenkfeld. The Lutheran technical charge is that they claim the Spirit apart from the *verbum externum* (external Word). Preserve as "Enthusiasts" in the English body, with the German on first occurrence per Hauptstück. |
| **Hauptstück** | Chief chapter, chief head | The standing organising unit of Lutheran catechesis, from Luther's *Small Catechism*. Kirchner's *Enchiridion* is organised by Hauptstück, not by *Locus* (Chytraeus) or *Articulus* (later scholastics). Render as "chief chapter" for the primary occurrence; abbreviate as "Hauptstück" thereafter within a given locus. |
| **Widmungsvorrede** | Dedicatory Preface | The 16th-century formal genre: a preface addressed to a named patron, functioning simultaneously as dedication, statement of purpose, patronage-request, and confessional statement. Kirchner's is unusually theologically substantive — the whole Nehemiah typology is worked out in it — and reads as a small tract in its own right. |
| **Bapsthumb** | Papacy | Kirchner's spelling of *Papstthum*, the papal system as a doctrinal-institutional whole. Rendered "the papacy" in the English body. |
| **Papisten** | Papists | Kirchner's designation for adherents of the papal system. Preserve the polemical form; the source uses it throughout. Not a term of theological neutrality but the standing 16th-century Lutheran usage. |
| **Concordi Buch** | Book of Concord | Kirchner's 1583 spelling of the *Konkordienbuch* (1580). Render as "Book of Concord." Cf. **Christlichen Concordi Wercks** = "Christian Concord-Work" (the whole enterprise of Concord — Formula, Book, and the 1583 *Apologia*). |
| **Symbolum** | Symbol (Creed) | The credal formulations: **Symbolum Apostolicum** = Apostles' Creed; **Symbolum Chalcedonense** = Chalcedonian Definition; **Symbolum Nicaenum** = Nicene Creed. Preserve untranslated in Kirchner's citation form. |
| **Kirchenbaw / Widerauffbawung** | Church-building / Rebuilding | The Nehemiah-typological cluster in the Vorrede: the Reformation as *rebuilding* of the Church after papal captivity. Render the metaphor consistently; do not paraphrase into "reformation" or "reform" — the *building* image is Kirchner's spine. |
| **Schwerdt des Geistes** | Sword of the Spirit | Ephesians 6:17 language, invoked in the Nehemiah typology: teachers and Christians armed with the Word against error. Render literally. |
| **Anfechtung** | Temptation, trial, spiritual assault | The Lutheran technical term for spiritual trial — not merely temptation to sin but the wider assault on faith and conscience by the devil, the world, the flesh, and God's own hiddenness. Preserve the German on first occurrence per Hauptstück; render "temptation" or "trial" in ordinary flow, "Anfechtung" where the technical weight matters. |
| **verbum externum** | External Word | The preached and written Word as the ordinary means of the Spirit, over against the Enthusiasts' claim to an unmediated inner Word. Preserve untranslated in doctrinal loci. |
| **Fürnembste Hauptstück** | Chief chapters | The title-formula: "the *chief* Hauptstück of Christian doctrine." Signals that the *Enchiridion* is not a complete dogmatics but a selective catechesis on the *disputed* points — those between Lutherans and Papists on the one side, Lutherans and Reformed on the other. |
| **Fromm** | Pious, upright, honest | 16th-century *fromm* carries the older sense of "upright, honest, of integrity" as much as the later narrowly-religious "pious." Kirchner uses it in the older wider sense: *frommen Herzen* = "upright hearts." |
| **Politia / Politia Christiana** | Civil order, Christian polity | The civil-magistracy sphere in Lutheran two-kingdoms teaching. Not a Kirchner-*Enchiridion*-locus (his catechism is deliberately restricted to the disputed articles); listed here to signal the wider systematic apparatus this book presupposes. |

### *Christological vocabulary (added Tranche 2 for Hauptstück III)*

|  |  |  |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Θεοτόκος / Gottes Gebärerin** | God-bearer, Mother of God | The Ephesine title (431) defending the personal union of Word and flesh from conception. Preserve the Greek on first occurrence; render "God-bearer" thereafter. Kirchner deploys the title precisely as the Council did — as a Christological rather than a Marian claim. |
| **Symbolum Chalcedonense** | Chalcedonian Definition | The 451 Christological Definition preserved by Kirchner in full Latin *apud Nicephorum* lib. 15 c. 6. Preserve untranslated in structural reference; give facing English. |
| ***genus maiestaticum*** | Genus Maiestaticum | The communication of Divine majesty to the assumed human nature. The polemical spine of Hauptstück III — Kirchner catechises the doctrine section by section against the Reformed rejection. Preserve untranslated on formal doctrinal use. |
| ***genus apotelesmaticum*** | Genus Apotelesmaticum | The common operation of both natures in the mediatorial works. Kirchner's second of three points at § 9. Preserve untranslated. |
| ***genus idiomaticum*** | Genus Idiomaticum | Attribution of the natural properties of one nature to the whole Person of Christ (*God has purchased his congregation with his own blood*, Acts 20:28). Kirchner's first of three points at § 9. Preserve untranslated. |
| **caro vivifica / lebendigmachend Fleisch** | Life-giving flesh | Cyril of Alexandria's *sarx zoopoios* doctrine (Council of Ephesus 431, ninth Anathematism). Christ's flesh as life-giving *by virtue of Personal union with the Word* (not of itself as if it were the Spirit). Preserve on formal doctrinal invocation. |
| ***communicatio idiomatum*** | Communication of Attributes | The overarching doctrine of the mutual sharing of properties in the one Person of Christ. Preserve untranslated throughout. |
| ***praedicatio verbalis*** | Verbal predication | The Reformed position Kirchner rejects: that scriptural predicates of God (e.g. *God has suffered*) are *merely verbal* — figures of speech — and do not signify a real communication of properties. Kirchner insists on *real* rather than *verbal* predication. Preserve the Latin polemically. |
| **Ubiquität / *ubiquitas*** | Ubiquity (technical Lutheran term) | The Reformed polemical label for the Lutheran doctrine of the multipresence of Christ's human nature. Kirchner is careful (Hauptstück III § 21) to reject the caricature — *not* substance-in-every-creature (stone, leaf, grass, sun, moon) — while defending the Lutheran positive: *illocal multipresence* per Personal union and exaltation. See [[reference-scholastic-lutherans-glossary]] under *ubiquitas* for the standing entry; this Kirchner refinement is critical. |
| **Rechte Hand Gottes / dextra Dei** | Right Hand of God | Kirchner (Hauptstück III § 19) argues systematically that the "Right Hand of God" is *not a Locus Physicus* (not a spatial place) but the Divine Majesty and Power itself (Heb. 1, 8; Luke 22). This exegesis is load-bearing for the whole Lutheran doctrine of the Session: since the Right Hand is not a place, Christ's session at it is compatible with his human nature's presence everywhere he wills to be. |
| ***Locus Physicus*** | Spatial (physical) place | The Reformed conception of heaven and of the Right Hand of God that Kirchner rejects. If heaven is a spatial place and the Right Hand a spatial location, Christ's body is *there* and cannot be *here*; if neither is spatial, the objection collapses. Preserve untranslated in doctrinal loci. |
| **Status humiliationis / exaltationis** | State of Humiliation / of Exaltation | The two states of Christ's earthly and heavenly existence. Kirchner (Hauptstück III § 23) treats the humiliation as a *concealment and non-use* rather than a *lack* of the Majesty acquired at Incarnation. The Reformed position — *lack* — leads back to a Nestorian split. Preserve untranslated on formal use. |
| **Agnoetae** | Agnoetae | The 6th-c. Egyptian sect who taught real ignorance in Christ's human nature. Kirchner (Hauptstück III § 14) sees the Reformed Christological position as materially near this heresy — a polemical thrust characteristic of Lutheran scholasticism after Chemnitz. |
| ***Sit à dextris meis*** | Sit thou at my right hand | Ps. 110:1, foundational text for the whole Christological doctrine of the Session. Athanasius (*apud Theodoretum*) reads it as spoken to the *body* of Christ. Kirchner marshals the entire patristic chain (Athanasius → Ambrose → Cyprian → Chrysostom) to establish the Right Hand's exaltation-of-the-humanity reading. |
| **Ampt Christi / *munus Christi*** | Office of Christ | The fourfold work of Christ as Redeemer-of-sin, Fulfiller-of-the-Law, Bestower-through-Word-and-Sacrament, and Sender-of-the-Spirit. Kirchner's Hauptstück IV — the closing brief locus. Not the developed *Threefold Office* (Prophet, Priest, King) of later scholastic dogmatics (Gerhard, Quenstedt), which is a 17th-c. refinement not yet formalised in 1583. |
