# Translator's Note

## 1. The text-base

This working English translation follows the **posthumous Latin edition** of Kirchner's methodical systematic, printed at **Leipzig by Johann Beyer in the year M.D.XCV (1595)**, under the full title ***Methodica Explicatio Praecipuorum Capitum Doctrinae Coelestis, ita conscripta ut non solum brevem hypothesin veritatis Christianae contineat, verum etiam solidam refutationem pugnantium opinionum complectatur*** — *"A Methodical Exposition of the Chief Chapters of the Heavenly Doctrine, so composed as to comprise not only a brief hypothesis of Christian truth, but also a solid refutation of the opinions that oppose it."*

The title-page adds a **crucial second sub-title:** ***"CVI ADDITA EST VITA ET curriculum ministerii eiusdem D. Timothei Kirchneri piè defuncti: Et in locis singulis ad marginem notatio, ex quibus D. Lutheri scriptis, in quibus plenius explicantur, ij sint desumpti: locorum item Scripturae toto hoc libro explicatorum, et capitum totius libri . . . index"* — *"To which is added the Life and Course of the Ministry of the same D. Timotheus Kirchner, piously deceased; and at each single Locus a marginal notation, showing the places in D. Luther's writings from which they are drawn; likewise an Index of the Scripture-passages here expounded, and of the Chapters of the whole book."***

The source-text used here is the Google Books digitisation of a library copy (48.6 MB PDF), from which we have extracted the per-page OCR text via **pdftotext (Poppler)** — yielding 51,945 lines of running late-humanist Latin. The OCR has the usual imperfections of Google-Books-quality 16th-century Latin (long-*s* → *f* / *ss* confusion, Greek-letter fallouts, marginal-note interpolations into the main text, dropped diacritics), which we correct silently against the underlying source-image.

## 2. What this book actually is

Kirchner himself, in the preface *ad Christiano Lectorem*, tells the story of the book's origin: *"Three years ago I published my German Enchiridion, and dedicated it to Elector Palatine Ludwig VI, at his own instigation. Since the publication of that book pleased many pious lovers of truth, they urged me to attempt something similar in the Latin tongue. Though I would gladly have satisfied their desire long ago, from the time I was compelled to leave the Palatinate so many most difficult and heavy hindrances called me away from this labour that, contrary to my expectation, I was not able to put my hand to it. At last, seeing that on account of other far graver occupations I could not perform what I wished and willed, I assigned it to my beloved son-in-law **Master Johann Fladung**, at that time pastor and superintendent of the church of Saalfeld, that in his spare hours he should convert the said Enchiridion into the Latin tongue. This labour he willingly undertook, and in a short space of time, by God's grace, brought it to a happy completion. On rereading his version and attending more carefully to each single point, it seemed good to me not only to alter certain things which stood in the German exemplar and had been already converted, but also to add other matters not to be despised to the earlier ones, so that the explication of the matters in this language might be somewhat fuller. It also came into my mind that I should be doing a not unwelcome service to the studious minds of piety if I added to each single matter the principal ***objections of the Adversaries with their solutions***, whereby the wits might be exercised and the judgment of the learners in matters of religion might be formed. By this reason it happened that the book grew not only in quantity, but was also rendered almost a new work; a thing which the reader will easily perceive from a collation of the German Enchiridion with this little book."*

The reader must therefore be told plainly: **this Methodica is not a mechanical Latin translation of the 1583 or 1592 Enchiridion.** It is:
- Kirchner's own **Latin-language remake**, based on Fladung's initial translation, which Kirchner then revised;
- **expanded** at many points where the Latin explication is fuller than the German original;
- **carrying at each Locus a formal apparatus of Objections + Solutions** which the German edition did not have;
- and (Fladung's second contribution, added in the son-in-law's own preface *ad Lectorem*) **anchored by marginal cross-references to specific volumes and pages of Luther's Jena and Wittenberg tomes** — turning the whole book into an indexed Luther-anthology arranged by systematic locus.

Kirchner died on 21 September 1587 (see the elegiac funeral poem by **Nicolaus Selnecker**, printed in the front-matter of the 1595 edition); the book therefore appeared eight years posthumously, seen through the press by Fladung.

## 3. Editorial conventions

**Locus-numbering.** Kirchner's 46 *Loci* are rendered as **Loci I–XLVI** in Roman numerals, following his own Index at the end of the volume. Where our English *Enchiridion* rendered the German *Hauptstücke* as *Chapters*, this Latin edition uses **Locus** throughout — matching the Latin *loci-communes* tradition of Melanchthon, Chemnitz, Selnecker, and Gerhard which is Kirchner's own idiom in this Latin work.

**Question format.** Every *Quaestio* is rendered as a numbered `Question` heading; the bolded restatement of the question precedes the answer, following the same convention as our Enchiridion volume.

**Objections apparatus.** Kirchner's ***OBIECTIONES*** — the formal apparatus of adversary syllogisms with responses that he added to the Latin edition — are rendered as numbered `Objection` sub-headings at the end of each Locus, following the exact syllogistic form of the source (major, minor, ergo — then ***Respondeo***).

**Luther marginalia.** Fladung's marginal cross-references to Luther (e.g. *T. 1 p. 80*, *T. 3 p. 68*, *Postilla Domestica Jena*) are preserved as **inline citations in italics within square brackets** at the point where they appear in the source margin, so that the reader can follow the Luther-anchor without leaving the page. Fladung's own note on the editions he consulted (Jena Latin tomes 1–4, Jena German tomes 1–8, Wittenberg Postilla Ecclesiastica 1562, Domestica Jena 1579) is preserved in his preface *ad Lectorem*.

**Latin and Greek.** The Latin of the *Methodica* is preserved *verbatim* in italics wherever Kirchner is quoting a Father, a Council, a syllogistic proposition, or a technical scholastic distinction (***sola Scriptura***, ***causa efficiens***, ***per verbum***, ***synergia***, ***libertas christiana***, ***iudicium contradictionis***); each is followed by a plain-English gloss on first occurrence. Greek words retained by Kirchner in Roman characters (***Scheol***, ***Gehenna***, ***catechumenus***) are preserved without transliteration.

**Bible citations.** Book abbreviations are normalised to the modern English standard (Rom., 1 Cor., Matt.); chapter and verse numbers are silently corrected against the underlying reference where Kirchner's citation differs from modern versification. The Latin *verf.* (verse) is expanded to *v.* in English.

**Bold and italics.** **Bold** marks Bible citations, Kirchner's enumerated *primo / secundo / tertio* points, and the load-bearing thesis in each answer. *Italics* mark Latin quotations, Greek transliterations, and technical scholastic vocabulary retained in the original tongue. Section dividers use the aldus-leaf ornament — ❦ —.

**Polemical labels.** As in our Enchiridion volume, Kirchner's polemical distinctions — ***Pontificii***, ***Sacramentarii***, ***Anabaptistae***, ***Novatiani***, ***Chiliastae***, ***Sadducaei***, ***Enthusiastae***, ***Ebionitae***, ***Marcionitae*** — are preserved as he uses them, and rendered by the natural English equivalents (**Papists**, **Sacramentarians**, etc.).

**Silent OCR corrections.** Long-*s* → *f* and *ss* → *β* confusions, dropped Greek diacritics, marginal-note text interpolated into the main body by the OCR flow, and line-break hyphen artefacts from Poppler's extraction are silently corrected against the source-image. Substantive editorial decisions that touch the doctrine are noted in the endnotes.

## 4. Reader navigation

The book contains **46 Loci** across the whole span of Christian doctrine, following the same order as the German *Enchiridion*: from **Sacred Scripture** (Locus I) and the **Doctrine of God** through the person and offices of **Christ**, the **Holy Spirit**, **Creation**, the **Fall**, **Sin**, **Law and Gospel**, **Justification**, the **Sacraments**, the **Church**, the **Antichrist**, and the **Last Things** — closing with **Eternal Life** (Locus XLVI). A full **Table of Contents** follows this Note, grouped into six parts. Each Locus opens with a divider ornament and closes with the same before the next Locus begins; the formal **Objections** appear at the end of each Locus in Roman numerals. The **Endnotes** appear as a numbered section following Locus XLVI. This volume is a **companion** to the Scholastic Lutherans edition of the German *Enchiridion* (1592) — the two together give Kirchner in both his tongues, with the Latin adding a formal *disputationes*-style apparatus and a page-anchored Luther-index that the German lacks.

# Locus I. Of Sacred Scripture, or the Word of God

### *De Sacra Scriptura seu Verbo Dei*

— ❦ —

## Question 1. From what foundations is it proved that the Christian religion and faith alone is the true religion and faith?

**From what foundations is it demonstrated that the religion and faith of Christians alone is the true religion and faith?**

**I. From antiquity.** For no religion on this earth, of whatever name, has such authority as the Christian religion attains by antiquity and duration. This faith **Adam** and all the **Patriarchs** embraced; and this same faith **all** who shall be heirs of blessedness will retain **until the end of the world**.

**Gen. 3:15** — *"I will put enmities between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; she [or: he] shall crush thy head, and thou shalt lie in wait for her heel."*

**Acts 10:43** — *"To him [Jesus] all the Prophets bear witness, that all who believe in him shall receive remission of sins through his name."*

**Rom. 4:3** — *"For what saith the Scripture? Abraham believed God, and it was reputed to him unto justice."*

**II.** **No religion in the world has been so confirmed and established by so many illustrious and truly divine works and miracles as the Christian faith** — as the histories of the Old and New Testaments abundantly witness. Besides this, no religion, though it has attempted them, has been able to bring forth similar works.

**III.** **No religion has been sealed with the blood of so many true martyrs** — who suffered for truth and righteousness. None has endured and conquered through so many perils and persecutions except the Christian. For though from the beginning of the world many have been slain for the sake of this religion, the religion has not been *cut off*; and though it has been assaulted by all other sects, it has never been *conquered*.

**IV.** **No religion besides the Christian has sure predictions and prophecies concerning things to come.** For whatsoever concerning its own future and that of other sects, whatsoever concerning **Christ**, the **Church**, and the political mutations of empires has been foretold in Sacred Scripture many ages back, the outcome has confirmed and daily confirms. To all the oracles and predictions of the sacred letters the deeds and the events have answered, and answer still. **Isa. 43:11–12** — *"I am, I am the Lord, and there is no Saviour besides me: I have announced, and I have saved,"* etc.

**V.** **Above all**, the doctrine of the **Gospel** — proper to Christians, unknown to men of all other sects — proves and demonstrates the truth and certitude of the Christian religion. For without this Gospel-doctrine, whoever live in the world are ***without God***, and ***have no hope*** (**Eph. 2:12**). And this Gospel-doctrine, they who embrace it by true faith, by the witness of the experience of all the faithful, have **solid, immovable, and efficacious consolation, tranquility, and peace of heart in all temptations and afflictions**; whence they easily come to rest, and gather that they are accepted to God in Christ. **Rom. 5:1–2** — *"Being therefore justified by faith, let us have peace with God, through our Lord Jesus Christ, by whom also we have access through faith into this grace, wherein we stand, and glory in the hope of the glory of the sons of God."*

— ❦ —

## Question 2. What then is the Word of God, or Sacred Scripture?

**What then is the Word of God, or Sacred Scripture?**

**It is a doctrine given by God himself, in which — by the ministry of Law and Gospel — we are instructed concerning God, both as to his Essence and as to his Will toward us men, and are sanctified unto eternal life.** [*Luther: T. 1, p. 80; T. 5, p. 471; T. 5, p. 314*]

**John 17:17** — *"Sanctify them in truth; thy word is truth."*

— ❦ —

## Question 3. What are the chief chapters of Sacred Scripture, or of the Word?

**What are the chief chapters of Sacred Scripture?**

**Two: the Law and the Gospel.** [*Luther: T. 3, p. 165; T. 3, p. 68*]

The **Law** teaches and prescribes what we ought to do and to omit.

The **Gospel**, however, is not concerned with things to be done and omitted, but **freely offers the remission of sins and reconciliation with God, on account of Christ**. **John 3:16** — *"So God loved the world, that he gave his only-begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have life everlasting."*

— ❦ —

## Question 4. Whence does Sacred Scripture take its origin?

**Whence does Sacred Scripture take its origin?**

**From God himself.** For at **Exod. 32:15–16** we read: *"And Moses returned from the mount, carrying the two tables of the testimony in his hand, written on both sides, and made by the work of the Lord; the writing also of God was graven in the tables."*

Whence it is clear that God himself first ***consigned his word and revelations to letters***, from which also we understand the dignity and majesty of the Sacred Letters. Moses hereafter, by God's command (as we read at **Exod. 34**), consigned the whole Law to letters and left it to posterity; and him the Prophets, Evangelists, and Apostles have followed.

— ❦ —

## Question 5. By whom was the Word of God first made known?

**By whom was the Word of God first made known?**

**By God himself.** [*Luther: T. 8, p. 134; Genesis T. 3, p. 1; Genesis T. 4, pp. 119, 120*] As **Ps. 103:7** says: *"He made known his ways to Moses, his wills to the children of Israel."* And **2 Tim. 3:16** — *"All Scripture divinely inspired is profitable to teach, to reprove, to correct, to instruct in justice, that the man of God may be perfect, furnished to every good work."*

— ❦ —

## Question 6. What moved God to this self-manifestation?

**What moved God to this self-manifestation?**

**Nothing other than his inexhaustible and ineffable mercy**, on account of which he willed to make himself known to us by a sure and certain Word — especially since men, having fallen into sin, had lost the true knowledge of God (**Gen. 3**), and he willed not to leave them in those shadows and in ignorance of his essence and of his will; for otherwise all men would have had to perish eternally in those shadows.

— ❦ —

## Question 7. Where and among whom may this Word of God be found?

**Where and among whom may this Word of God be found?**

**Only in the true Church of God**, to which God himself has made himself known by his Word and by illustrious miracles. [*Luther: T. 7, p. 93; Genesis T. 3, p. ; T. 3, p. 303*]

**Deut. 4:6** — *"This is your wisdom, and understanding in the sight of peoples: that hearing all these precepts, they may say — Behold a wise and understanding people, a great nation. Nor is there any other nation so great, that hath gods so nigh them, as our God is present to all our petitions. For what other nation is there so renowned, that hath ceremonies and just judgements, and all the law which I set forth this day before your eyes."*

And **Ps. 147:19–20** — *"Who declareth his word to Jacob, his justices and his judgements to Israel. He hath not done in like manner to every nation, and his judgements he hath not made manifest to them."*

**John 10:27, 5** — *"My sheep hear my voice; but a stranger they follow not, but flee from him, because they know not the voice of strangers."*

— ❦ —

## Question 8. What must a Christian man chiefly seek in Scripture? And what is the chief scope of Sacred Scripture?

**What must a Christian man chiefly seek in Scripture, and what is the chief scope of the Sacred Scripture?**

**In Sacred Scripture, Christ Jesus, the Saviour of the world, is above all to be sought — with his merit and benefits.** [*Luther: T. 7, p. 187; T. 8, p. 258*] For to him the whole Scripture points us; to him the whole Scripture bears witness. **John 5:39** — *"Search ye the Scriptures; they are they that give testimony of me."*

— ❦ —

## Question 9. What are the chief properties of Sacred Scripture?

**What are the chief properties of Sacred Scripture?**

**These:**

**I.** That Sacred Scripture is the **Lydian stone**, the **certain rule**, and the **infallible canon of all human writings** — apart from which, unless they be approved by it, they are nothing. [*Luther: T. 3, p. 165; T. 1, p. 402; T. 4, p. 20*]

**Ps. 19:8** — *"The testimony of the Lord is faithful, giving wisdom to little ones."*

Whence **Augustine, *Contra Epistolam Petiliani***: ***"Nec catholicis Episcopis consentiendum est, sicubi forte falluntur, aut contra canonicas Dei Scripturas aliquid sentiunt."*** — *"Neither with the Catholic Bishops themselves is one to consent, wherever they perhaps are deceived, or teach anything contrary to the canonical Scriptures of God."*

**II.** That Scripture is **more sure and firm than all councils**. For the councils themselves must be instituted according to Scripture, and depend upon it. [*Luther: T. 7, p. 255*]

**Gal. 1:8** — *"But though we, or an angel from heaven, preach a gospel to you besides that which we have preached to you, let him be anathema."*

Whence **Jerome**: ***"Spiritus Sancti doctrina est, quae canonicis literis est prodita; contra quam si quid statuant concilia, nefas duco."*** — *"It is the doctrine of the Holy Spirit which is set forth in the canonical letters; against which if the councils establish anything, I count it profane."*

**III.** Scripture ***admits no syncretism or human mixture***; it wills to be either alone or nothing. [*Luther: T. 1, p. 503, f. b.; T. 5, p. 391; T. 3, part 1, p. 120*]

Whence **Ps. 12:7** — *"The silver examined by fire, proved, and purged sevenfold."*

**IV.** Scripture is of ***unsearchable and inexhaustible wisdom***. [*Luther: T. 5, p. 440; T. 5, p. 125*] As **Ps. 147:5** — *"Great is our Lord, and great is his power; and of his wisdom there is no number."*

Whensoever any Christian therefore, of whatever condition or dignity, ought to search it and turn it over day and night by hand. Whence **Chrysostom, *Colossians* 3**, says: *"Hear, I entreat you, all secular ones: procure for yourselves Bibles, the pharmacies of the soul; or if nothing else, then at least acquire the New Testament, the Acts of the Apostles, the Gospels — as continual and diligent teachers. If molestation shall happen, look into these as into a pharmacy: from here take for yourself consolation, whether some damage happen, or death, or loss of household. Yea, not only look in, but pass all over again and again, and hold them in mind. For hence is the cause of all evils, that the Scriptures are ignored,"* etc.

— ❦ —

## Question 10. Whence, therefore, is it clear that Sacred Scripture is a doctrine certain and firm, in which we may and must safely trust?

**Whence is it clear that Sacred Scripture is a certain and firm doctrine, on which we may and must safely rest?**

**From these:** [*Luther: T. 4, p. 120, f. b.*]

**I.** That it was first ***consigned to letters by God himself***.

**II.** That it is ***divinely inspired*** — **2 Tim. 3:16**.

**III.** That from the beginning it was ***confirmed by so many illustrious and truly divine miracles***.

**IV.** That the ***holy men of God who wrote it were impelled by God himself*** — **2 Pet. 1:19**: *"And we have the more firm prophetical word, whereunto you do well to attend, as to a light that shineth in a dark place,"* etc.

From which it is evident that Scripture is by far to be preferred to the writings of the Heathen.

— ❦ —

## Objections

### Objection I. The most ancient religion of all is most to be believed. The religion of the Heathen is the most ancient of all. *Ergo*, it is most to be believed.

**Respondeo**: The **minor is manifestly false**. For the doctrine of the Church alone is the most ancient — inasmuch as God himself, in Paradise, immediately after the creation and the fall of the first parents, made it known (**Gen. 3**). Neither is there any more ancient history in the world which sets forth the creation of all things than the history of **Moses**. And collation shows that Moses and the Prophets are more ancient than the Greeks; for **Herodotus** begins there where the Prophet **Jeremiah** ends, as at **Jer. 44** it is permitted to see and to read. Whence **Justin Martyr, in his *Apology* to Antoninus**: ***"Moses is more ancient than all the Greek writers."*** And **Africanus** in **Eusebius, *De Praeparatione Evangelica* lib. 10**: ***"Whatever among the Greeks excels in the praise of antiquity, all that follows Moses by a long interval."***

### Objection II. The universal doctrine of virtue is comprehended in the Attic and Roman laws and in the writings of the philosophers. *Ergo*, the Heathen religion is true, and to be received by all.

**Respondeo**: The **antecedent is most false**. For the Attic and Roman laws and the writings of the philosophers scarcely contain a *shadow* of the doctrine of the virtues. [*Postilla domestica, T. 2*] For first, of the true recognition and invocation of God they teach nothing. Of the true fear of God, of the love of God, of faith in God, and of hope — they have **not even a word**.

Next, though they contain certain things concerning the virtues of the **Second Table**, all is nevertheless *maimed and imperfect* if compared with Sacred Scripture. Add to this that they are utterly ignorant of the doctrine of sin and of the redemption of the human race through Christ, of the free remission of sins, and the like.

### Objection III. Does not the Roman Pope rightly say, that Scripture has its authority from the Church?

**Minime** — By no means. For Scripture has its authority ***only from God***. But that the Roman Pontiff refers the authority of Scripture back to the Church, is *just as if you were to prefer the daughter to the mother, and the wife to the husband*. For Scripture is as it were the **mother** from whom the Church is begotten; and as it were the **husband** whose wife the Church is — and not the contrary.

**But (they urge) the Church was before the Word of God was consigned to letters?**

Although there were faithful men before the Word of God was consigned to letters, it matters nothing. For the Word of God was **prior to the faithful themselves** — although it was not yet committed to letters. The first parents did not indeed have the Word *written*, but they had nevertheless a **certain Word of God** through which they were called to the fellowship of faith and of the Church, as at **Gen. 3:4** is to be seen. For unless God, through his Word, had made himself known to them chiefly after the fall, they would have known nothing certain of him, nor been living members of the Church. *For it is through the Word that we believe* — **John 17**.

### Objection IV. Do all things which are necessary to salvation, then, contain and are found in Scripture?

**The Papists deny this — with the greatest injury and contumely to Scripture — but wrongly.** For at **2 Tim. 3:16–17** it is expressly written: *"All Scripture divinely inspired is profitable, that the man of God may be perfect, furnished unto every good work."* Whence also **Christ, Luke 16:29**, says: *"They have Moses and the Prophets: let them hear them."*

Although the New Testament was at that time not yet written, yet **nothing has been written in it afterward which does not have its own immoveable foundation in the Old Testament** — as is clear from the harmony and consent of the Old and New Testaments.

And at **John 20:30–31** the Evangelist says: *"Many other signs also did Jesus in the sight of his disciples, which are not written in this book; but these are written, that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that believing you may have life in his name."*

Hither belongs the voice of **Cyril on John**: *"Not all things which the Lord did are written, but what the writers thought sufficient both for morals and for dogmas."* [*Cyril: on the sufficiency of Scripture*]

And **Augustine, *In Ioannem*, Tractate 49**: *"That Christ did many things which are not written, but that they were chosen which should be written, which seemed to suffice for the salvation of believers."*

Whence also **Athanasius, *Contra Gentes***, writes: ***"Sufficere Scripturas divinitus inspiratas ad omnem instructionem veritatis."*** — *"That the Scriptures divinely inspired are sufficient for every instruction of the truth."*

### Objection V. If Sacred Scripture were sufficient, there would be no need of Apostolic traditions, of the decrees of councils, of the writings of the Fathers, and of the declaration of the Catholic Church. But there is need of traditions, etc. *Ergo*, Sacred Scripture is not sufficient.

**Respondeo**: The **minor is false** — namely, that Scripture should be completed out of traditions, out of the decrees of councils, out of the writings of the Fathers, and out of the Church's declaration.

For as concerns the ***traditions*** which the Papists peddle under the name of the Apostles: **first**, it is agreed that they are *uncertain*, and cannot with firm reasons be shown to have been left by the Apostles. **Second**, it is manifest that the traditions the Papists press very often *conflict with the writings of the Apostles themselves*. **Third**, whatsoever they be, they are to be examined by the *norm of Scripture*, and where they conflict with it, simply rejected. For at **Col. 2:8** the Apostle bids us take heed that we be not led astray by vain deception, according to the tradition of men. **Fourth**, it is in itself most unworthy to feign that human traditions are the *completion* of the divinely inspired Scripture. **Fifth**, from **Irenaeus** and others it is agreed that of old the heretics also turned to the accusation of the Scriptures, and, as though they were not sufficient, appealed to traditions. And **Tertullian *Against Hermogenes*** says: ***"Scriptum esse doceat Hermogenis officina: si non est scriptum, timeat vae illud adijcientibus aut detrahentibus, destinatum."*** — *"Let the workshop of Hermogenes teach that it is written; if it is not written, let him fear that woe which is destined for those who add or take away."*

As concerns the ***councils***: it is certain that they are to be heard no further than while they confirm their decrees out of the ***fountains of Israel***, or out of the Sacred Letters. Whence **Jerome to the Galatians** wrote: ***"Spiritus Sancti doctrina est, quae canonicis literis prodita est; contra quam si quid statuant concilia, nefas duco."*** — *"It is the doctrine of the Holy Spirit which is set forth in the canonical letters; against which if the councils establish anything, I count it profane."* [*See: doctrine of councils; is it to be received?*] For if we ought to believe the councils apart from Scripture, in vain would Scripture be called ***Canonical***.

And **Chrysostom, Homily 13 on 2 Corinthians**, says: *"Since we have the most exact balance, the gnomon and rule — the assertion of the divine laws — let us abandon what seems best to this or that man, and inquire all things from the Scriptures."*

In the same manner concerning the ***Fathers***, it is certain that with their writings they cannot complete and perfect Sacred Scripture. For there is a vast difference between ***Canonical letters*** and the writings of the Fathers. So **Augustine, *Epistle 48 to Vincentius***, writes: *"This kind of letters is to be distinguished from the authority of the Canon. For they are not so read as if testimony were brought forward from them, that one might not think otherwise, if perhaps they knew otherwise than the truth demands."* And ***Epistle 3 to Fortunatus***: *"Nor may we hold the disputations of any men whatsoever, however Catholic and praiseworthy, as the Canonical Scriptures — that it should not be lawful, saving that honour which is due to those men, to disapprove or reject anything in their writings, if perhaps we find that they thought otherwise than the truth requires, either by divine help understood by others, or by ourselves. Such am I in the writings of others; such do I wish the understanders of my own to be."*

Concerning the ***Church*** it is agreed that she is a Church only ***upon condition*** — namely, so long as she hears and follows the Word of God (**John 10:5, 27**). *"My sheep hear my voice, and follow not a stranger."* How, therefore, could Sacred Scripture be completed and perfected by her?

Great indeed is the authority of the Church, but she extends herself no further than as she follows the Word of God, or Sacred Scripture. Hence **Ambrose, *Upon Luke***: *"If there is any Church which rejects faith, and does not possess the foundations of the Apostolic preaching, she must be forsaken."* And **Augustine, *De Unitate Credendi*, ch. 2**: *"In the words of the Lord, who is the truth, the Church is to be sought."*
