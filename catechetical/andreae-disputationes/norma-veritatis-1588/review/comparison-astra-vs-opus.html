Respondent: Master Jakob Liebler, Minister of the Church at Regensburg — 10 January 1588.

Theological Theses concerning the Rule of Truth (norma veritatis), and the Judge (iudex) of Controversies in Religion.

Concerning which, divine grace assisting, under the presidency of the reverend and most renowned man, Jakob Andreae, Doctor of Sacred Theology, most worthy Provost of the Church and Chancellor of the University of Tübingen, his honoured preceptor, Master Jakob Liebler, Minister of the Church at Regensburg, shall answer in the Gymnasium of Regensburg, upon the tenth day of January, 1588.

A Disputation concerning the Rule of Truth and the Judge of Controversies in Religion.

Under the presidency of the Reverend and most illustrious man Jakob Andreae, Doctor of Sacred Theology, most worthy Provost and Chancellor of the Church and Academy of Tübingen, his revered teacher; at the Gymnasium of Regensburg, M. Jakob Liebler, Minister of the Church at Regensburg, will respond on the tenth day of January, 1588. Regensburg: printed by Johann Burger.
Quaestio Seeing that, from the days of the Apostles even unto this present day, divers heresies and contentions have exercised the Church (Ecclesia) of God, it is not without cause demanded: What ought to be the sole rule of truth (regula veritatis) and the competent judge in controversies of religion? Seeing that from the very days of the Apostles down unto the present day sundry heresies and contentions have exercised the Church, not without cause is it asked: what is that one rule of truth (unica regula veritatis) and the fitting judge in controversies of religion?
Thesis 1
Scripture as sole norm		 1. That Holy Scripture, of the Old and New Testament, is the sole rule of truth and norma, according to which all controversies in the doctrine of religion are to be judged, is amongst all the truly godly a matter placed beyond all doubt. 1. That the Sacred Scripture of the Old and New Testament is the sole rule and norm of truth (unica regula et norma veritatis), out of which all controversies in the doctrine of religion are to be judged: this is placed beyond all doubt among all truly godly men.

For if it was lawful to inquire into the doctrine of the Apostles, which was undoubted, and to take judgment from the Prophetic writings, much more must all the writings of all doctors, in all ages, be subject unto the writings of the Prophets and Apostles as unto their judge; according to that saying of Paul, Gal. 1:8: “If an angel from heaven preach another Gospel,” &c. 3. Thesis 7 (Opus) / 8 (Astra)
Church has no absolute power		 8. For the Church hath no absolute power (absoluta potestas), but a power bound and constrained by the Word of God; according to that saying of Paul, Gal. 1:8: “If we ourselves (the Apostles) should preach otherwise, we should be accursed.” 7. For the Church hath no absolute power, but a power bound and constrained unto the word of GOD; according to that saying of Paul: “If we ourselves (the Apostles) should preach otherwise, we would be anathema” (Gal. 1:8).
Thesis 8 (Opus) / 9 (Astra)
Judgment of Church ⇒ human dream unless from Word		 9. Ergo, unless every judgment of the Church be subject unto the Word of the Lord, drawn therefrom, and confirmed thereby, it is to be received as a human dream, and by no means as the judgment and decree of the Church. 8. Therefore every judgment of the Church, unless it hath been subjected to the word of the Lord, and drawn out of it, and confirmed by it, is to be received for a human dream (humanum somnium), and in no wise for a judgment or decree of the Church.

 10. The same also is the judgment of blessed Augustine, and of the other sounder Fathers and Doctors of the Church. Augustine saith: “But now neither ought I to bring forth the Nicene Council, nor thou the Council of Ariminum, as though thereby to prejudge the cause. Neither am I bound by the authority of this council, nor thou by that of the other. The very same is likewise the judgment of St. Augustine and of the rest of the more sincere Fathers and Doctors of the Church. Augustine [Contra Maximinum II.14]: “But now,” saith he, “neither ought I to bring forth the Council of Nicaea, nor thou that of Ariminum, as though about to prejudge the case. I am not held by the authority of this [Arian] bishop, nor thou by that of the other. By the authorities of the Scriptures — not those proper to either party, but common witnesses to both — let matter be conferred with matter, cause with cause, reason with reason.”

Again: “For I confess unto thy charity that to those books of Scripture alone, which are now called Canonical, have I learned to render this fear and honour, that I most firmly believe none of their authors to have erred in writing aught. And if I meet with anything in those writings which seemeth contrary to truth, I shall nothing doubt but that either the copy is faulty, or the interpreter hath not attained unto the meaning of that which was spoken, or I myself have failed to understand it. But others I so read that, howsoever greatly they excel in holiness and learning, I account not a thing true because they themselves have so judged, but because they have been able to persuade me, either by those Canonical authors or by probable reason, that it is not contrary to truth. Neither do I suppose, my brother, that thou art of any other mind. Assuredly, I say, I deem not that thou wouldest have thy books read as those of the Prophets and Apostles, concerning whose writings it is wicked to doubt that they are free from all error. Far be this from thy godly humility and thy true judgment concerning thyself.” [Aug., Epist. 19 ad Hieron.] 		10. Item [Epist. 82 ad Hieronymum]: “For I confess unto thy charity, that unto those books of the Scriptures alone which are now called Canonical have I learned to yield this fear and honour: that I most firmly believe that no author of them, in writing anything, hath erred. And if in them I shall find anything which seemeth contrary to truth, I shall not doubt but that either the codex is faulty, or the translator hath not followed what was said, or that I myself have not at all understood. But other authors I so read that, however much they may prevail in sanctity and doctrine, I do not therefore hold it true because they themselves thought so, but because they have been able to persuade me — whether by those Canonical authors, or by probable reason — that it doth not depart from the truth. Nor do I judge, my brother, that thou dost think aught otherwise; nor, I say, do I suppose that thou wouldest have thine own books read as those of the Prophets and Apostles, of whose writings — that they are free from all error — to doubt is impious. Far be this from a pious humility and a truthful estimation of thyself.”
Thesis 11
Colloquy of Worms condemned		 11. Wherefore we affirm that the utterance put forth by the Papist disputants in the Colloquy of Worms was blasphemous and damnable, wherein they said that Holy Scripture is not the voice of the judge, but the matter in controversy (materia litis). 11. Therefore we affirm that the voice of the colloquists at the Colloquy of Worms, put forth by the Papists, is blasphemous and damned — wherein they said: “Sacred Scripture is not the voice of the judge, but the matter of the suit” (non vocem iudicis, sed materiam litis).

Thesis 13
Whosoever refuses Scripture		 13. Whosoever, therefore, acknowledgeth not Holy Scripture alone (sola Scriptura) as the sole and most sufficient judge of controversies in religion, but refuseth it, of him we pronounce that he distrusteth his own cause, and that with him no disputation whatsoever ought to be held in the matter of religion. 13. Whosoever, therefore, doth not acknowledge Sacred Scripture alone (sola Scriptura) for the one and most sufficient Judge of the controversies of religion, but refuseth it — him we pronounce both to distrust his own cause, and that with such a one in the business of religion there is by no means any disputing to be had.
