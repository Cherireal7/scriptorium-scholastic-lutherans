# Theological Disputations

## Disputation I

### Theological Theses on the Norm of Truth and the Judge of Controversies in Religion

To be discussed, by the favor of divine grace, under the presidency of the reverend and most distinguished **Jakob Andreae**, Doctor of Sacred Theology, most worthy Provost and Chancellor of the Church and University of Tübingen, his honored teacher;

in the Gymnasium at Regensburg,

**Master Jakob Liebler**, minister of the Church of Regensburg, will respond on the tenth day of January.

**1588**

---

## Disputation

### On the Norm of Truth and the Judge of Controversies in Religion

### Question

Since various heresies and disputes have troubled the Church of God from the time of the apostles down to the present day, it is not without cause that the question is asked: What ought to be the one rule of truth and the competent judge in controversies of religion?

### 1

That Holy Scripture, comprising the Old and New Testaments, is the one rule and norm of truth, by which all controversies concerning the doctrine of religion are to be judged, is established beyond every doubt among all who are truly godly.

### 2

For every human being is a liar; God alone, however, is truthful in his Word.¹

### 3

Thus we read concerning the Bereans that, after receiving with all eagerness the Word of the Lord preached by Paul and Silas, they searched the Scriptures daily to see whether these things were so, just as the apostles had preached them.²

### 4

For if it was permissible to investigate the teaching of the apostles—which was beyond doubt—and to draw a judgment from the prophetic writings, it is much more necessary that the writings of all teachers in every age be subjected to the writings of the prophets and apostles as to a judge, according to Paul’s declaration: “Even if an angel from heaven should proclaim another gospel,” and so forth.³

### 5

To this belong the illustrious testimonies of the prophets and apostles:

> “To the law and to the testimony! If they do not speak according to this Word,” and so forth.⁴

Likewise:

> “Your Word is a lamp to my feet.”⁵

Likewise:

> “This is my beloved Son, with whom I am well pleased; listen to him.”⁶

Likewise:

> “We possess the more firmly established prophetic Word, to which you do well to pay attention as to a lamp shining in a dark place, until the day dawns.”⁷

### 6

For although certain difficult and obscure things occur in some passages of Scripture, the doctrine of salvation is nevertheless explained through other, clearer passages in that same Scripture.

### 7

Nor does this judge detract in any way from the Church’s judgment; rather, it confirms the Church’s authority. Concerning that authority Christ says: “If he refuses to listen even to the Church, let him be to you as a Gentile and a tax collector.”⁸

### 8

For the Church does not possess absolute power, but power bound and constrained by the Word of God, according to Paul’s declaration: “Even if we ourselves”—that is, the apostles—“were to proclaim the gospel otherwise, we would be accursed.”⁹

### 9

Therefore, unless every judgment of the Church is subjected to the Word of the Lord, derived from it, and confirmed through it, that judgment must be received as a human dream and by no means as the judgment and decree of the Church.

### 10

This is also the opinion of Doctor Augustine and the other more sincere Fathers and Doctors of the Church. Augustine says:

> But now neither ought I to bring forward the Council of Nicaea, nor ought you to bring forward the Council of Ariminum, as though either would prejudge the case. I am not bound by the authority of the latter, nor are you bound by that of the former. By the authorities of the Scriptures—not witnesses peculiar to either party, but witnesses common to both—let matter be compared with matter, cause with cause, and reason with reason.¹⁰

Likewise, in Epistle 19 to Jerome:

> For I confess to your Charity that I have learned to render this reverence and honor only to those books of the Scriptures which are now called canonical: I firmly believe that none of their authors erred in writing anything. And if in those writings I encounter something that appears contrary to the truth, I do not hesitate to conclude that either the manuscript is faulty, or the translator has not grasped what was said, or I myself have failed to understand it. I read other authors, however, in such a way that, however greatly they excel in holiness and learning, I do not consider something true merely because they themselves thought it so, but because they have been able to persuade me—either through those canonical authors or by a probable argument—that it does not depart from the truth. Nor, my brother, do I suppose that you think otherwise. I certainly do not imagine that you wish your books to be read like those of the prophets and apostles, concerning whose writings it is impious to doubt that they are free from every error. Far be this from your godly humility and truthful estimation of yourself.¹¹

### 11

We therefore affirm that the statement issued by the papal party’s delegates at the Colloquy of Worms is blasphemous and condemned, namely, their assertion that Holy Scripture is not the voice of the judge, but the subject matter of the dispute.

### 12

We have no less doubt that the opinion of Cardinal Cusanus is blasphemous and execrable when he says:

> Do not let it trouble you that the Holy Scriptures are adapted to the times and understood in various ways, so that at one time they are interpreted according to the prevailing universal rite; when the rite is changed, their meaning is changed again.

Likewise:

> The Scriptures, even though begun and continued for the good government of the Church, cannot possibly derive from the Church’s essence.

Likewise:

> When the judgment of the Church is changed, the judgment of God is also changed.¹²

### 13

Therefore, whoever does not acknowledge Holy Scripture alone as the one and all-sufficient judge of controversies in religion, but rejects it, we declare both that he distrusts his own cause and that no disputation whatsoever concerning religion is to be held with him.

## Disputation II

### On the Authority, Origin, Cause, Certainty, Perfection, Sufficiency, and Clarity of Holy Scripture of the Old and New Testaments

Held in the year 1588, with **Master Christoph Scholtius of Breslau in Silesia** responding.

#### Dedication

To the reverend and most distinguished men, **Lord Isaiah Heidenreich**, Doctor of Sacred Theology, and **Master Johann Fleischer**, pastors of the Church of Breslau, friends and brothers worthy of honor in Christ, **Jakob Andreae, Doctor of Sacred Theology**, sends greeting.

Reverend and most distinguished men, brothers worthy of honor in the Lord: When that most accomplished young man from Breslau, Master Christoph Scholtius, came to our university, he commended to me not only your piety and zeal for propagating the heavenly truth in its sincere and uncorrupted doctrine, but also the supremely faithful instruction of the Gymnasium in which he had been educated from boyhood and, by God’s grace, had brought his studies to such a point that I might entrust to him a theological disputation in which the powers of his intellect could be tested. I did this chiefly so that the illustrious senate of your city might understand the progress of his studies—a senate whose generosity in promoting the liberal studies of youth has long been celebrated.

This devotion to piety is not merely most worthy of praise; it is also exceedingly necessary for the Church and for our posterity, whatever that future posterity may be. For after the Roman Antichrist saw his kingdom being overthrown and greatly weakened by our schools, which had been established in a godly manner, he was roused by rivalry and, through a new kind of men under the name of the Society of Jesus, strives in as many schools as possible to root out the saving doctrine of Jesus Christ, our only Savior, and to inflict the greatest possible harm upon our neighboring churches. Although they boast of some success in establishing papal impiety, and although their learning is greatly extolled and commended, the facts themselves nevertheless teach that, in painting and encrusting the manifest errors of the pontiffs, they place their foundations not in Holy Scripture but in philosophical subtleties and, above all, in the authority of the Church, which they impiously proclaim to stand above Holy Scripture. They call Scripture uncertain, doubtful, obscure, defective, mutilated, and—as the papal delegates at the Colloquy of Worms in 1557, convened by the authority of the emperor and all the estates of the Empire, said in these very words—“not the voice of the judge, but the subject matter of the dispute.”

Doctor Paul Eber of blessed memory and I, who had been appointed notaries of that colloquy, wrote this impious and blasphemous statement of the papal delegates down with our own hands; and at the Diet of Augsburg in 1559 we heard it recited before the public assembly of all the estates of the Empire, not without the greatest horror.

What surer sign is there of the Savior Christ’s approach toward the final judgment, when his own Word is no longer regarded on earth as the voice of the judge? Thus it is written concerning Noah’s contemporaries: “My Spirit shall not contend with humanity forever.” For just as the extreme impiety of that age brought on the flood, so too such blasphemous declarations will hasten the coming of the Son of Man to judgment. Christ himself, in his dreadful utterance against such blasphemous people, demonstrated that he is judge in his Word, as it is written: “The one who rejects me and does not receive my words has a judge; the Word that I have spoken will judge him on the last day.” Here let the new advocates of the papal kingdom experience whether Scripture is the voice of the judge or merely the subject matter of the dispute.

May the Lord preserve you and your churches unharmed for as long as possible. Amen.

#### Thesis 1

Although in the first disputation against the decrees of the Council of Trent concerning the authority of councils it was demonstrated from the unanimous consensus of the prophets and apostles—and therefore of the entire purer Church—that the prophetic and apostolic writings contained in the books of the Old and New Testaments are the one measuring line, norm, rule, and canon of judgment and truth, according to which all controversies in religion are to be examined, judged, and decided, it has seemed good to propose another disputation on the authority of Holy Scripture. For the ministers and slaves sworn to the pope at the Council of Trent loudly denounce Scripture as uncertain, obscure, mutilated, defective, imperfect, and therefore insufficient for deciding the disputes that have arisen in religion, because, they say, it does not contain everything pertaining to faith and to the conduct of a godly life.

#### Thesis 2

At the outset we must place in the ark of our discussion what Saint Peter left written in his second epistle: “Prophecy was not at any time brought forth by human will, but holy men of God spoke as they were inspired by the Holy Spirit.”

#### Thesis 3

From this it is evident that the prophetic and apostolic Holy Scripture has one author, namely, the Holy Spirit, upon whom all its authority depends as upon truth itself beyond doubt.

#### Thesis 4

For although Scripture has various human writers, since its many books are found to have been written by more than one person, it is certain that they were all governed by one and the same Holy Spirit, whose utterances they followed rather than dreams of their own.

#### Thesis 5

Concerning Scripture, so that the whole matter may be established, nothing is found in the divinely inspired writings concerning later times before the tablets of the Decalogue were inscribed by the finger of God. By this fact the dignity and authority of Holy Scripture are most splendidly illustrated and confirmed.

#### Thesis 6

Some authors relate that certain books were written before the flood and before the tablets were marked by the hand of God. Yet by that very fact the Lord willed to detract from confidence in them, because they were not transmitted to posterity, so that greater authority might attach to writings known to be inspired by God, who made the beginning of sacred writing with his own finger.

#### Thesis 7

As for the teaching of religion concerning the true worship of God: although it was preserved and propagated for 2,461 years by the living voice without written texts—the patriarchs’ years of life having been prolonged for that reason through so long a period—God nevertheless willed to inscribe the Ten Words upon tablets with his own hand and commanded Moses to write the rest from his mouth. This was done by the infinite wisdom of God for the benefit of the Church.

#### Thesis 8

For although the heavenly doctrine had been handed down by the living voice, as if from hand to hand, from the beginning of the world through later patriarchal times, the purity of God’s Word and of divine worship was not faithfully preserved because of the corruption of human nature. Rather, the doctrine itself was corrupted, adulterated, and finally almost entirely obliterated. God restored it in Noah’s time by the living voice after it had collapsed, and confirmed it with stupendous miracles; for this reason Noah was called by the apostle a herald of righteousness.

#### Thesis 9

Before the dispersion of Noah’s posterity, he instructed not only his sons but also his grandsons and all who were descended from them in true religion with great piety and faith, and for the 350 years that he lived after the flood diligently impressed upon them the sincere worship of God. Yet how negligently it was afterward preserved by his descendants—or by the posterity of Shem alone, among whom the seat of the true Church then existed—is clearly attested by the history in which it had to be preserved especially as among God’s own peculiar people.

#### Thesis 10

For Terah, the father of Abraham, had been manifestly defiled by idolatry when scarcely two hundred years had elapsed since the confusion of languages.¹³

#### Thesis 11

From this it is clearly apparent how slippery and uncertain was the custody and preservation of the heavenly doctrine when it was placed in unwritten traditions.

#### Thesis 12

Therefore, when the traditions of true doctrine had been adulterated and corrupted, God renewed their purity for the patriarch Abraham through particular revelations.

#### Thesis 13

From that time until the death of the twelve patriarchs, the sons of Jacob, the doctrine remained uncorrupted. Yet afterward the people followed the customs of the nations with a powerful inclination of their own: thus, by the time of the exodus from Egypt, they had fallen into manifold idolatry.

#### Thesis 14

When the Israelites had been led out, they were so addicted to idolatry that, paying no regard to the miracles the Lord had shown at their departure, and scarcely enduring Moses’ very brief delay, they were not afraid to commit dreadful idolatry in the sight of God by setting up a golden calf for worship.

#### Thesis 15

When the light of the purer doctrine was therefore gradually being extinguished through traditions, it pleased God to establish another means by which the purity of the heavenly doctrine might be propagated and preserved: writings approved and confirmed by divine authority and testimony.

#### Thesis 16

Although the writings of Moses obtain authority from the Holy Spirit, their author, apart from all human testimony, nevertheless, so that provision might be made for human weakness, by God’s command the tablets of the Decalogue, engraved by God’s hand, were placed in the ark of the covenant; and the writings of Moses, composed under divine inspiration, were laid beside that same ark. Their custody was entrusted not only to the priests, but a copy was also delivered to the kings so that they might be the keepers of both the first and second tables. Their words were written for the common people as well upon posts, doorways, and doorframes.

#### Thesis 17

Since six hundred thousand people could bear witness to the truth of the books of Moses, the prince of the prophets among the Israelite people, their truth was never called into doubt.

#### Thesis 18

The same was the authority of the other prophets, who succeeded Moses among the people of Israel at intervals of time. Their books also were laid before the Lord, where the ark of the covenant was, and were diligently preserved.¹⁴

#### Thesis 19

However many hearers the individual prophets had of their sermons, so many witnesses of the truth they had—not only among the people of their own age, but also among posterity.

#### Thesis 20

The case of the New Testament writers, the evangelists and apostles, is altogether the same. They described in good faith the Gospel history and within it the life, doctrine, and miracles of Christ. Even the Jews of our own age, blinded enemies though they are, cannot deny that history; rather, against their own will they are compelled to bear witness to the truth of the events, although they do not place faith in Christ’s doctrine.

#### Thesis 21

But the testimony of the true Church, in whose time the apostles’ writings were published and approved, is far greater. As Augustine writes, those writings deserved to be approved not only by the Church of Christ but also by the apostles themselves.

#### Thesis 22

For what the apostles attested with signs, through which they confirmed their writings, the churches also confirmed, by whom those writings were diligently preserved.

#### Thesis 23

The remaining churches likewise unanimously affirmed the same writings. The same doctrine contained in the writings of the evangelists and apostles had been handed down orally to them by the apostles themselves or had been described by them.

#### Thesis 24

The most ancient ecclesiastical writers appealed to the testimony of those churches against the heretics. As Tertullian attests, the heretics either did not receive certain Scriptures, or, if they did receive them, interfered with them by additions and subtractions so as to arrange them according to their own purpose.

#### Thesis 25

Irenaeus confirms the same thing when he writes in this manner: “When they are refuted from the Scriptures, they turn to accuse the Scriptures themselves, as though they were not correctly constituted, or were not authoritative, or were variously expressed, and as though the truth could not be discovered from them.”

#### Thesis 26

In order that Scripture might be vindicated from this stain against the heretics who attempted to weaken its authority, sincere teachers appealed to the earliest churches, those planted either by the apostles themselves or by apostolic men. As Tertullian says, “They are all first, and all apostolic, while they all prove their unity,” for in them was found the same doctrine that the writings of the evangelists and apostles contained.

#### Thesis 27

Thus against the heretics both the truth and the authority of the Scriptures were sufficiently asserted by the testimony of those churches, which faithfully affirmed and guarded without writing, as though handed down from hand to hand, the same doctrine. Every teaching that agreed with those apostolic churches, the mothers and originals of the faith, was considered true and placed beyond all controversy in the true Church of God and the apostolic Church.

#### Thesis 28

To this is now added the consensus of all ages throughout the entire world: the writings already mentioned, both of the Old and of the New Testament, have always been regarded as authentic and true, and it has always been judged impious to entertain even the slightest doubt concerning them.

#### Thesis 29

It can therefore easily be understood that in establishing the canon of the sacred books the Church never possessed an unrestricted will, but was bound to antiquity and therefore to the infallible testimony of the earliest churches. By that testimony all other doctrines were, as Tertullian again bears witness, to be prejudged as false when they savored against the truth of the churches, the apostles, Christ, and God.

#### Thesis 30

For this reason the Gospels published under the names of certain apostles—Matthew, Thomas, Bartholomew, James, Philip, Peter, and Andrew—were repudiated by the Church. Some did not agree with the mother and original churches, whose truth was beyond doubt; others were known to be the works of apostles in title only, although such writings still existed in the churches in Tertullian’s time.

#### Thesis 31

From this it is also apparent in what sense the apostle called the Church the pillar and foundation of the truth.¹⁵

#### Thesis 32

For the truth contained in Holy Scripture has all its own authority from the Holy Spirit, by whose inspiration it was published, and with respect to itself it does not need human testimony at all. Yet with respect to posterity, this service is owed especially to the mother and original churches: that they were witnesses beyond doubt of the truth of the apostolic doctrine, propagated partly in writings and partly by the living voice and oral discourse.

#### Thesis 33

Even if the same thing can be granted to the apostolic churches that remained the mother and original churches for the course of many centuries, we can nevertheless call them the pillar and foundation of the truth for no other reason than that we received from them, as though handed down from hand to hand, the sacred books, confirmed by the testimony of the original and mother Church. For the corruption of the truth crept into the Church and grew through the centuries following the apostles until the formal Antichrist arose, concerning whom the prophets, Christ, and the apostles had foretold.

#### Thesis 34

Once the canon of the sacred books of the Old and New Testaments had therefore been established as the one rule, measuring line, and norm of the truth of heavenly doctrine—most certain and beyond doubt—their authority was rightly judged supreme in the Church, and every human authority of fathers and councils was subjected to it.

#### Thesis 35

Against that authority neither the universal Church, nor the fathers, nor any councils can establish anything, as it is written: “Even if we ourselves or an angel from heaven should proclaim to you a gospel contrary to what we proclaimed to you, let him be accursed.”¹⁶

#### Thesis 36

To this pertains Augustine’s statement against Cresconius: “We do no injury to Cyprian when we distinguish any writings of his whatsoever from the canonical authority of the divine Scriptures. For it was not without cause, and with such saving vigilance, that the ecclesiastical canon was established. To it belong certain books of the prophets and apostles which we dare not judge at all; according to those books, however, we may judge freely concerning the other writings of believers or unbelievers.”

#### Thesis 37

Although the canon of the prophetic and apostolic sacred books is placed beyond every hazard of doubt, other writings were inserted among them which are called Hagiographa, Ecclesiastical books, and, by Jerome, Apocrypha. It was doubted whether they were certainly apostolic, since they did not bear the name and title of their authors. They therefore did not obtain authority equal to the former books in the primitive Church. As Jerome bears witness, they were read in the Church for the edification of the people, but not for confirming the authority of ecclesiastical dogmas.

#### Thesis 38

Because the papal doctors assembled at Trent were by no means ignorant of this, their rashness is rightly condemned for attributing to these Apocrypha authority equal to that of the prophetic and apostolic writings received in the canon beyond doubt.

#### Thesis 39

Accordingly, the doctors of the primitive Church judged piously and correctly that, for the rule of faith and of the truth of heavenly doctrine to be certain and in no way doubtful, ecclesiastical dogmas must be confirmed from the canonical books alone.

#### Thesis 40

Tertullian, moreover, teaches the fullness of Scripture when he writes against Hermogenes: “Let the workshop of Hermogenes prove that it is written. If it is not written, let him fear that woe appointed for those who add or take away.”

#### Thesis 41

Since the certainty of Holy Scripture and of the canonical books is thus established, the impiety of the Council of Trent is also rightly execrated, for it has attempted to persuade the Church that not everything necessary for eternal salvation is contained perfectly and sufficiently in the canonical Scriptures.

#### Thesis 42

For the purpose of the whole ecclesiastical ministry consists chiefly in these four heads: the explanation of the Church’s dogmas, the refutation of errors, instruction in life and morals, and the correction of those things lacking in life and morals according to the prescription of the divine will. It is most certain and manifest that all these things are contained in Holy Scripture not merely sufficiently, but superabundantly.

#### Thesis 43

The question whether Christ did not reveal everything to the apostles but instead left many things to be revealed to the Church of God in the course of time by the Holy Spirit—things unknown to the apostles—is not new, nor did it first arise from the papists.

#### Thesis 44

Indeed, this was the same and far more ancient declaration of the heretics, concerning which Tertullian testifies: “They are accustomed to say that the apostles did not know everything,” being driven by the same madness by which they turn matters upside down. They admit that the apostles knew everything, but say they did not hand everything down to everyone. In either case they expose Christ to reproach, as though he had sent out apostles who were either insufficiently instructed or too simple. For who of sound mind can believe that those whom the Lord gave as teachers were ignorant of anything—those whom he kept individually in his companionship, discipleship, and common life, to whom he said that knowledge of the mysteries had been given, and to whom he afterward promised the Spirit of truth, who would teach them all truth? He thereby showed that they were ignorant of nothing.

#### Thesis 45

There are open testimonies that plainly refute this false opinion of the heretics and papists:

> “But as for you, continue in what you have learned and firmly believed, knowing from whom you learned it, and how from infancy you have known the sacred writings, which are able to instruct you for salvation through faith in Christ Jesus. All Scripture is divinely inspired and useful for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for instruction in righteousness, that the man of God may be complete, prepared for every good work.”¹⁷

> “I did not shrink from declaring to you the whole counsel of God.”¹⁸

> “These things I write to you who believe in the name of the Son of God, that you may know that you have eternal life.”¹⁹

> “These things are written so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name.”²⁰

> “The law of the Lord is perfect.”²¹

From these it is completely established that the heavenly doctrine of truth has been perfectly handed down in Holy Scripture to all.

#### Thesis 46

The papists, flatterers though they are, try to evade Paul’s most evident words when they say that Scripture is indeed useful and valid for this purpose, but is by no means sufficient by itself. Yet the testimonies we have just adduced plainly demonstrate the contrary.

#### Thesis 47

Augustine says: “Among the things plainly set down in Scripture are found all those things that contain faith and the morals of life, namely, hope and love.”²²

Athanasius says: “The holy and divinely inspired Scriptures are sufficient for every instruction in the truth.”

Chrysostom says: “Whatever is sought for salvation, all of it is fully contained in the Scriptures.”

Cyril says: “It is necessary for us to follow the divine letters and in no respect to depart from their prescription.”

#### Thesis 48

It would be absurd for one and the same part of Christian doctrine to be repeated six hundred times in the sacred writings while another part, no less necessary for salvation, was entirely omitted and passed over.

#### Thesis 49

The clarity of Holy Scripture is no less than its perfection, as it is written: “Your Word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my paths”; “The commandment of the Lord is radiant, enlightening the eyes”; and, “We have the more firmly established prophetic Word, to which you do well to pay attention as to a lamp shining in a dark place, until the day dawns and the morning star rises in your hearts.”²³

#### Thesis 50

The primitive fathers of the purer Church testify to the same thing with one voice. Lactantius easily holds first place among them. These are his words: “Could God, the maker of mind, voice, and tongue, not speak plainly? Indeed, by his providence he willed those things which are divine to be free from disguise, so that all might understand what he himself was speaking to all.”

Chrysostom says: “All things are clear and plain from the Holy Scriptures. Whatever things are necessary are manifest.”

Likewise: “Let us not hold the opinions of many, but let us inquire into the matters themselves. For it is absurd in monetary affairs not to trust others but to count and calculate the coins themselves, yet in matters far more important simply to follow the judgment of others—especially when we possess the most exact scale and rule, the assertion of the divine laws. Therefore I beg and entreat you all to disregard what seems good to this or that person and to inquire into all these things from the Scriptures.”

Likewise, on Matthew 24: “Knowing that there would be such confusion of affairs in the last days, the Lord therefore commands that Christians who are in Christianity and wish to receive the firmness of the true faith should flee to nothing unless to the Scriptures. Otherwise, if they look to other things, they will be scandalized and perish, not understanding which is the true Church, and through this they will fall into the desolation of abomination that stands in the holy places of the Church.”

#### Thesis 51

When Peter writes concerning Paul’s epistles that there are certain things in them difficult to understand, which the unlearned and unstable distort—**οἱ ἀμαθεῖς καὶ ἀστήρικτοι στρεβλοῦσιν**—as they do the other Scriptures, to their own destruction, this must be understood not only of the more elevated kind of discourse but also of the matters themselves. Carnal people who have never been illumined by the brightness of the Holy Spirit do not perceive them, but twist them according to their own perverse and preconceived judgment about the matters.

#### Thesis 52

Even if a passage that possesses some obscurity occurs in one part of Scripture, several clearer passages are found in that same Scripture by which every obscurity is dispelled.

#### Thesis 53

Therefore, it is falsely alleged by the papists that Holy Scripture has a waxen nose. It is, and remains, an immovable rule of steel—the truth of the words and testimonies of the Holy Spirit, whose meaning is easily apprehended by those who understand them.

#### Thesis 54

For this purpose above all, knowledge of languages, especially Hebrew and Greek, is fitted, so that in more obscure passages we may return to the sources and decide controversies. The canonical books of the Old Testament were written in Hebrew, and those of the New Testament in Greek.

#### Thesis 55

Since this is so and the matter itself confirms it, the second decree of the fourth session of the Council of Trent is rightly condemned. It decreed that the old and commonly used edition of the Bible, used in the Church for many centuries and concerning whose author there is controversy, should be held as authentic in public readings, disputations, sermons, and expositions, and that no one should dare or presume to reject it under any pretext.

#### Thesis 56

By these words they first indirectly condemn the translation of the Bible into vernacular languages other than Latin. This betrays the spirit of Antichrist, who attempts to retain the common people of Christians in perpetual ignorance of the divine will and law.

#### Thesis 57

How great, however, is the impiety and ingratitude toward God in wishing to deprive the Church of the use of languages commended by Saint Paul to the Corinthians! In this way it will no longer be free to believe the purest sources themselves rather than troubled streams.

#### Thesis 58

The audacity and profanity of **Gregory of Valencia**, the Ingolstadt Jesuit, must therefore be detested and abominated. In his *Analysis of the Catholic Faith* he was not ashamed to burst forth in these words: “Moreover, from what has been said it is understood that those authors are in no way to be heard who still contend, after the Council of Trent, that the common edition can be emended in certain places, as far as the meaning itself is concerned, through the Hebrew and Greek codices as though through sources, as they call them. This is not permitted. Rather, the Greek and Hebrew codices, if they disagree with our edition, must be corrected and emended through our edition. For the Church approved this edition throughout, not those codices—although neither did it reject them except where they contradicted our edition.” Thus far the Spaniard.

#### Thesis 59

In this matter the Areopagite contradicts them, not so much with the consenting votes of the fathers and doctors of the purer ancient Church as with their own decrees. There this opinion of Augustine is cited: “As the trustworthiness of the books of the Old Testament must be examined from the Hebrew volumes, so the truth of the New requires the norm of the Greek language.”

#### Thesis 60

What deserves the greatest consideration is that when Leo X approved the translation of the New Testament adorned by Erasmus as though it were a son, that same translation was afterward placed in the catalogue of heretical books and is now condemned by the Council.

#### Thesis 61

Equally worthy of rebuke is their claim that the interpretation of Holy Scripture belongs to them alone with their petty sacrificers. They decree that no one may interpret Scripture contrary to the sense which the Roman Church holds and has held, and that it belongs to that Church alone to judge concerning the true sense and meaning of the Scriptures.

#### Thesis 62

For prophecy, or the interpretation of the Scriptures, is a peculiar gift of God. It is not bound to one particular Church, but is common to churches dispersed throughout the world. The apostle prescribes its manner to the Corinthian church when he says: “Let two or three prophets speak, and let the others judge. But if a revelation is made to another sitting by, let the first be silent. For you can all prophesy one by one, so that all may learn and all may receive consolation; and the spirits of prophets are subject to prophets.”²⁴ By these words the apostle by no means teaches that an interpretation of the Scriptures must be sought across the sea, but commends it to the prophets of the individual provinces.

#### Thesis 63

The interpreters gathered at Trent also attribute more to the interpretations of the fathers than the fathers ever claimed for themselves when they decree and sternly command that no one dare interpret Holy Scripture contrary to the unanimous consensus of the fathers.

#### Thesis 64

Jerome writes to Minerius and Alexander: “My purpose is to read the ancients, to test each thing, to retain what is good, and not to depart from the faith of the catholic Church.”

Augustine, on Psalm 57, says: “Let our documents be removed from the midst; let the book of God come forth into the midst. Hear Christ speaking; hear the truth speaking.”

Likewise, against Cresconius: “I am not bound by the authority of this epistle of Cyprian, because I do not regard the letters of Cyprian as canonical, but examine them from the canonical writings. What agrees in them with the authority of the divine Scriptures I receive with his praise; what does not agree I reject with his peace.”

Likewise, in Epistle 19 to Jerome: “Nor, my brother, do I suppose that you think otherwise. I certainly do not imagine that you wish your books to be read like those of the prophets or apostles. Far be this from godly humility and a truthful estimation of yourself.”

#### Thesis 65

The apostle of the Gentiles prescribes the most exact rule, lest anyone wander dangerously in interpreting the Scriptures, when he says: “If anyone has prophecy, let it be according to the grace given to him—that is, according to the analogy of faith.”²⁵

#### Thesis 66

For even if an interpreter does not always attain the proper and genuine sense of every passage of Scripture, provided that he teaches nothing contrary to the faith, he is free from every danger.

#### Thesis 67

The analogy of faith is the simple text of the Christian Catechism: the Decalogue, the Apostles’ Creed, the Lord’s Prayer, Baptism, the Lord’s Supper, and Absolution. These seven heads are rightly called the core, marrow, and, as it were, soul of the sacred books, because everything a Christian must know for eternal salvation is contained in them, though scattered throughout Holy Scripture.

#### Thesis 68

Saint Augustine nowhere attributes to himself the praetorian authority that would permit no one to depart from his interpretation. Rather, by his example in the books he wrote *On Christian Doctrine*, he beautifully instructs interpreters how more obscure passages are to be explained by clearer ones, and how the true meaning is to be drawn according to the analogy of faith from the wording of Scripture, from the circumstances, and from comparison of passages.

#### Thesis 69

What else would result but that the assembly of the faithful would be brought into perpetual doubt concerning the true and genuine meaning of Holy Scripture if it had to be sought not from Scripture alone but from the unanimous consensus of the fathers—fathers who differ and contend not only among themselves but sometimes even with themselves?

#### Thesis 70

Therefore, since in the interpretation of Holy Scripture neither royal nor papal authority possesses force, but certain rules dictated to all interpreters by the Holy Spirit must be followed, this decree of the Council concerning the interpretation of Scripture is rightly condemned in the Church as false, impious, and tyrannical.

#### Thesis 71

From all these things it is manifest that the Roman Antichrist, together with the fathers of his Council of Trent, most shamefully deceived the Roman emperor, all kings, princes, and estates of the entire Christian world, and especially of Germany. In their first decree of the fourth session they so splendidly and magnificently promise a purification of the Church from all errors and the preservation of the purity of the doctrine of the Gospel; but in their very first approach and vestibule they shamefully set forth the contrary. They by no means receive Holy Scripture as the one and only norm of truth. For teaching the truth of the Gospel they disgracefully accuse it of being defective and mutilated, and for deciding controversies in religion they call it insufficient, uncertain, and obscure—indeed, with blasphemous mouth they call it not the voice of the judge but the subject matter of the dispute.

Finally, they summon people away from the sources to troubled streams; away from the original and mother languages to the one common translation, corrupted in very many places and openly opposed to the sources. In all disputations, readings, sermons, and expositions they invoke it as the sole authentic text, and decree that no one dare or presume to reject this common translation under any pretext. Human traditions pertaining to faith and morals, in use in the Roman Church today, they command to be received with equal devotion and reverence as though orally dictated by Christ or the Holy Spirit and preserved by continual succession in the catholic Church. They impose the penalty of anathema so that all may understand that they have never contemplated any correction of errors, abuses, and human traditions—traditions introduced into the Church not only without the Word of God but even against the express Word of God, concerning which the complaints of so many centuries exist—but instead defend them tenaciously with sword and fire. We shall treat these matters in the next disputation, concerning traditions in the Church.

This continuous volume uses two principal source bases. Disputation II and the disputations that follow it use the posthumous collected *Disputationes theologicae* (Montbéliard and Frankfurt, 1593), Staatsbibliothek zu Berlin, PPN **766549208**. Library OCR, where available, is used only as a finding aid; the wording is checked against the page images. Typographical abbreviations are silently expanded, and biblical quotations are translated from Andreae’s Latin rather than replaced wholesale with a modern English version. Each work’s thesis structure, emphases, citations, and polemical language are preserved.

The title identifies Jakob Andreae as *praeses* and Jakob Liebler as *respondens*. In an early-modern academic disputation these roles do not map neatly onto modern assumptions about sole authorship: the theses were publicly defended by Liebler under Andreae’s presidency and doctrinal authority. The translation therefore names both men rather than silently assigning every line to only one of them.
