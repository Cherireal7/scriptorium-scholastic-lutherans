# Translator's Note

*This working translation is at its kick-off state. The paragraphs below record the intended text base, the editorial conventions binding on the eventual body prose, and — with full disclosure — what remains to be verified once the 1556 imprint has been sighted paragraph by paragraph.*

## 1. The text base

The English will render the **1556 Basel** *editio princeps* of Matthias Flacius Illyricus's *Catalogus testium veritatis*, printed *in officina Ioannis Oporini* (the Basel workshop of Johannes Oporinus). This is the first edition — the one composed and signed by Flacius himself, before the enlargements of the 1597 Frankfurt recension of Simon Goulart, before the 1666–67 Frankfurt two-volume expansion, and before the intervening reprints of 1562. The choice is deliberate: what is translated here is the Flacian text, not the tradition of the book.

The scan and its OCR are to be sighted, verified, and registered in the working notes (`notes.md`) before any body prose is committed. The section immediately preceding the paratexts will be updated with the exact repository identifier (Internet Archive item, VD16 shelf-mark, or comparable) once fixed.

## 2. What the author himself supplies

Flacius's own hand supplies the paratexts (dedication, if present, and *Praefatio ad lectorem*), the framing introductions to each witness, and the arrangement of the material. The bulk of the volume, however, is not Flacius's prose but the *witnesses' own* — long Latin excerpts from patristic, medieval, and late-medieval writers, quoted at length so that the witnesses may speak for themselves. All of these are preserved.

One authorial fact deserves early acknowledgement: much of the physical work of source-hunting — travel to cathedral and monastery libraries, transcription from manuscripts — was done by Flacius's collaborator **Marcus Wagner**. Flacius composed and signed the volume; Wagner gathered a large share of what fills it. The distinction matters because it is stated in the paratexts, and to translate honestly is to preserve it.

All of Flacius's own citations — to scripture, to the church fathers, to conciliar canons, to earlier chroniclers — are preserved unchanged in the body. Modern equivalents (PL / PG / CCSL / CCCM volume-and-column, ESV chapter-and-verse alongside Vulgate numeration on first occurrence per section) are consigned to the endnotes.

## 3. What the Scholastic Lutherans have added — and where

*Nothing in the body text is our own.* All editorial additions are relegated to the numbered endnotes at the end of the volume. These endnotes supply, and only supply:

- Standard modern citations for the patristic and medieval sources Flacius quotes (PL / PG / CCSL / CCCM volume:column; SC / GCS where a critical text has since superseded Migne)
- Standard modern citations for scripture (ESV numbering, with the Vulgate reference in parentheses on first occurrence in each section)
- Biographical dates on first named mention of each witness
- Modern place-names and modern spellings for medieval toponyms and personal names
- Brief technical notes where the Latin carries a distinction the English cannot preserve, or where the confessional stakes of an entry require historical context (particularly at the entries on movements — Waldensian, Cathar, Hussite — whose theological status in later Lutheran and Reformed consensus differs from what Flacius's own criterion of *"reclamarunt Papae"* implies)

Body prose contains no modern scholarship, no editorial voice, no bracketed insertions. Everything is either Flacius or Flacius's witnesses.

## 4. Editorial conventions

- **Register:** scholarly / academic-critical English. Reverent but not archaising.
- **Greek** is preserved in polytonic script, with parenthetical transliteration and English gloss on first substantive occurrence — e.g. **παπισμός** (*papismos*, "papism," "the papal system").
- **Hebrew** appears rarely in this work; when it does, it is preserved in pointed Hebrew script, with transliteration and gloss on first occurrence.
- **Latin technical terms** — *reclamare*, *papatus*, *antichristus*, *hierarchia*, *primatus* — are preserved in italic on first occurrence in each section, glossed, and rendered in English thereafter, unless the term is itself the subject under discussion, in which case the Latin form is retained.
- **Scripture:** ESV for the English rendering. Vulgate numeration and Flacius's own citation form are noted parenthetically on first citation per section.
- **Long-*s*** (ſ) is silently modernised to round *s*. Sixteenth-century *v/u* and *i/j* variation is normalised to standard modern Latin orthography in citation. Printer's abbreviations (macron for *n*/*m*, *q̃* for *quam*, *p̄* for *per*, and the standard Antiqua ligatures) are silently expanded.
- **Witness names.** Where a witness bears a Latinised humanist form (*Berengarius Turonensis*), the standard modern name is used in the body (*Berengar of Tours*), with the Latin preserved on first occurrence in italic. Where a witness is known only by his Latin humanist name, that name is retained.
- **Confessional restraint.** The tone remains that of a translator, not a partisan. Flacius's own polemical edge — sharp, at times sarcastic — is preserved in his prose; it is neither softened nor amplified in the English.
- **Rubrication, typeface, layout** — locked via `shared/reference-custom.docx` and identical to the Praetorius trilogy and the parallel Chytraeus translation.

## 5. Scope disclosure — the tranche structure

At approximately 1,200 folio pages, the *Catalogus* is not a single volume's work. Its translation is planned in seven tranches, documented in the working notes: (0) this kick-off scaffold, (1) the paratexts, (2)–(6) the witnesses in five chronological blocks from the sub-apostolic era through the Reformation eve, (7) the index and final apparatus. The chronological blocking is an operational convenience; Flacius's own internal division of the 1556 imprint — whether by books, by centuries, or as a single running catalogue — will be read off the source and this Translator's Note updated accordingly, along with the Contents above.

At this kick-off state, no body prose has yet been committed. What is complete is the scaffolding: the intended text base, the editorial conventions, and the release plan. Everything else remains to be built, one tranche at a time.
